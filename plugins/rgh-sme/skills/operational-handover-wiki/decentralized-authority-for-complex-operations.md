Organizations instinctively centralize decision-making authority during high-stakes situations, routing every call up a hierarchy for approval. This works for simple, predictable tasks, but it breaks down under complex, nonroutine conditions: the center cannot receive and process real-time field information fast enough, so decisions arrive too late or get made on stale information.

## The Peripheral Power Pattern

For operations involving genuine complexity or unpredictability, authority should be pushed to the periphery — the frontline operators closest to the unfolding situation — rather than held at the center. This requires two things to work safely, not one:

- **Grant frontline autonomy**: Operators need explicit permission to act on incomplete information and adapt to conditions the plan didn't anticipate, without waiting for sign-off from above.
- **Centralize support, not control**: The center's job shifts from approving every decision to providing the resources, logistics, and cross-team communication channels that let frontline decisions succeed (e.g. a scaled-up coordination channel, rapid resupply, liaison contacts) — administrative protocol enforces accountability and information-sharing, not command-and-control over each choice.

Centralizing control without this shift produces a bottleneck: authorities who insist all field decisions route through central approval end up blocking valid, urgent local action while working from a picture of the situation that is already out of date.

## Applying This to Handover

When a system or operational responsibility is handed to a new team, decide up front which classes of decisions the new operators are authorized to make independently under pressure, versus which require escalation — and make sure that authorization is explicit and documented, not assumed. An operator who was not involved in building the system cannot safely act at the periphery if they don't know the boundary of their own authority. This complements the escalation guidance in [Playbook Maintenance Tension](playbook-maintenance-tension.md), whose minimal playbook template includes explicit escalation contacts for when frontline mitigation is not enough.
