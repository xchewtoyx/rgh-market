Dekker's seven reasons why a focus on 'human error' misleads organisations:

1. **Targets the wrong entity**: firing or reprimanding an operator leaves
   every error-producing condition intact for the next operator
   ([blame also suppresses reporting](blame-suppresses-reporting.md)).
2. **Feeds on pseudoscientific labels**: "human error" gets replaced by
   equivalent jargon — ["loss of situation awareness",
   "complacency"](complacency-and-loss-of-situation-awareness.md), "unsafe
   acts" — or blame just shifts to other humans (supervisors, managers).
3. **Depends on hindsight**: declaring an action erroneous requires knowing
   the outcome; without it, the same actions read as normal, adaptive, or
   heroic ([hindsight bias](hindsight-bias.md)).
4. **Tabulates subjective judgments**: error-measurement tools count how
   often an observer made a negative judgment about someone else's
   performance, nothing more.
5. **Reifies error as a behavioural category** (Shorrock, 2013): treating
   error as a distinct psychological kind invites media and investigative
   reductionism.
6. **Produces flawed statistics**: aggregating disparate post-hoc
   attributions yields meaningless metrics ("85% of accidents are caused by
   human error"), treating context-bound events as identical countable
   units.
7. **Obscures how safety is created**: it ignores that operators
   continuously negotiate resource constraints and [conflicting
   goals](goal-conflicts-and-production-pressure.md), successfully producing
   safety in the vast majority of operations — the
   [Safety-II](safety-i-and-safety-ii.md) observation.

Together these explain why error taxonomies, error-rate KPIs, and
zero-error targets ([Vision Zero](vision-zero.md)) do not measure or manage
anything real: the unit being counted is an attribution, not a behaviour
(see [error as symptom](human-error-as-symptom.md)). This is a specific
case of the general systems-theory mechanism of [goal displacement in
safety metrics](goal-displacement-in-safety-metrics.md): any feedback loop
optimises its literal target, so an error-rate metric that is easy to count
but is not the same thing as safety will reliably be optimised at safety's
expense.
