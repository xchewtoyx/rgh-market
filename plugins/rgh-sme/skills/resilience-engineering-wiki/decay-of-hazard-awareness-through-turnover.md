---
type: concept
title: Decay of Hazard Awareness Through Workforce Turnover
description: >
  Successfully reducing a hazard's frequency over years leaves the active
  workforce with no personal experience of it, because the people who
  remember it firsthand have been promoted away from day-to-day operations —
  a demographic mechanism distinct from ordinary complacency.
sources:
  - title: "Resilience Engineering: Concepts and Precepts"
    resource: "Resilience Engineering: Concepts and Precepts (Hollnagel, Woods, Leveson), ch. 20"
---

Technical and procedural defences that successfully reduce a hazard's
frequency — fewer chemical fires, fewer near-misses of a given kind — carry
a specific side effect of their own success: over years, the "old hands" who
lived through the hazard's frequent occurrence get promoted into management
and move away from day-to-day operations, while the active front-line
workforce fills in behind them with younger personnel who have zero personal
experience of the hazard ever materialising. The organisation's aggregate
knowledge of the hazard has not been lost from any record — it has simply
migrated out of the population doing the work that the hazard actually
threatens.

This is a **demographic** mechanism, distinct from the psychological
complacency [chronic unease](chronic-unease.md) is built to counter: it is
not that the current workforce has grown overconfident from watching a good
safety record accumulate, it is that they never had the direct experience
that would generate the felt sense of the hazard's reality in the first
place. A workforce in this state is genuinely unprepared for subtle systemic
breakdowns leading toward the hazard, not because it has stopped being
vigilant, but because it has nothing in its own experience to recognise the
breakdown against.

The consequence compounds with [distancing through
differencing](distancing-through-differencing.md): a precursor incident is
exactly the kind of substitute experience that could rebuild this lost
awareness in a workforce that never lived through the hazard directly, and
distancing is precisely the mechanism that discards that substitute before
it can do so. Protecting institutional hazard awareness therefore cannot
rely on personnel experience alone renewing itself — it requires
deliberately transmitting the *felt* reality of a hazard (not just its
documented existence) across a generational turnover that formal procedure
and incident databases alone do not carry.
