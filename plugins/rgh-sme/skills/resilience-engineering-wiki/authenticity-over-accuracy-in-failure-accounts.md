---
type: concept
title: Authenticity Over Accuracy in Failure Accounts
description: >
  Because no account of a complex-system failure can be an objective mirror
  of what "really" happened, judge an account by whether it authentically
  conveys the insiders' lived experience, and deliberately keep multiple
  such accounts alive rather than converging on one official story.
sources:
  - title: "Drift into Failure: From Hunting Broken Components to Understanding Complex Systems"
    resource: "Drift into Failure (Dekker), ch. 7"
---

"Accuracy" presumes a real, pre-existing account of events that a good-enough
investigation can eventually mirror correctly — a [Newtonian-Cartesian
assumption](newtonian-cartesian-accident-thinking.md) that a complex system
cannot satisfy, because no describer stands outside the system they are
describing, and the system's actual state can never be exhaustively
captured even in principle. Demanding an "accurate" account of a complex
failure sets a standard that cannot be met, and every account that claims to
meet it is quietly asserting authority it does not have — implicitly
branding every other account as mistaken or dishonest.

**Authenticity is the workable alternative standard.** An account is
authentic if it convincingly conveys that its author genuinely engaged with
how the situation looked and felt to the people inside it at the time —
"true to the phenomenology of being there" — regardless of whether it can
be verified as the single correct reconstruction of events. This is the
[local rationality principle](local-rationality-principle.md) applied to
the write-up itself, not just to the investigative stance: an authentic
account can do more real accountability work than a nominally "accurate"
formal report, because it lets outsiders actually grasp what it felt like to
act inside a web of relationships where any one decision controlled almost
nothing but influenced almost everything.

The practical implications for anyone producing or commissioning a
post-failure account:

- **Do not treat competing accounts as a sign someone is lying.** Different
  participants genuinely occupied different local vantage points; divergent
  accounts are what you would expect from a complex event, not evidence of
  bad faith.
- **Value the diversity of accounts as a resource**, analogous to
  biodiversity, rather than a problem to be resolved into one official
  narrative — different framings surface different candidate countermeasures
  that a single converged story would hide (see [go up and out, not down and
  in](go-up-and-out-not-down-and-in.md) for the parallel point about
  investigative direction).
- **Keep judgment revisable.** Because a complex system's conditions are
  irreversible, some effects of an earlier, later-corrected judgment stay
  irreversible regardless — which is a reason to judge cautiously and stay
  open to revision, not a reason to avoid judging at all.

The underlying reason multiple stories can be simultaneously legitimate
rather than merely partial is the same one behind the [root-cause
fallacy](root-cause-fallacy.md): causes and narratives about complex-system
failure are constructed by the describer's purposes and position, not
discovered whole.
