A [brutal audit](brutal-audit.md) tests whatever mindful infrastructure was
(or was not) built before chaos arrived. **Conscious audits** are the
deliberate, ongoing counterpart: nine self-assessment questionnaires
(mostly 1–3 Likert; three use Agree/Disagree with more "Disagree" meaning
more need for mindfulness) that map onto [mindfulness in high-reliability
organizing](mindfulness-in-high-reliability-organizing.md) and should repeat
regularly — slippage in scores signals drift.

| Audit | Focus | Maps to |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 5.1 What Do You Count On? | General mindful infrastructure; **enlarge** rather than "focus" attention | Starting baseline |
| 5.2 Vulnerability to Mindlessness | Triggers for automatic pilot, old categories, ignored discrepancies | Inverse of mindfulness |
| 5.3 Where You Need to Be Most Mindful | Perrow: tight coupling + interactive complexity | Risk context |
| 5.4 Preoccupation with Failure | Detection, near misses, bad-news reporting | [Preoccupation with failure](preoccupation-with-failure.md) |
| 5.5 Reluctance to Simplify | Requisite variety, skeptics, mutual respect | [Reluctance to simplify](reluctance-to-simplify.md) |
| 5.6 Sensitivity to Operations | Front-line contact, current-situation picture | [Sensitivity to operations](sensitivity-to-operations.md) |
| 5.7 Commitment to Resilience | Training, action repertoire, informal networks | [Commitment to resilience](commitment-to-resilience.md) |
| 5.8 Deference to Expertise | [Migrating decisions](migrating-decisions.md), expertise over rank | [Deference to expertise](deference-to-expertise.md) |
| 5.9 Mindfulness Organizing Scale | Compact composite | [Mindfulness Organizing Scale](mindfulness-organizing-scale.md) |

Audit 5.1's rationale: HROs are not better at predicting what will go wrong
— they share a clearer sense of **what needs to go right** and what might
signal unraveling, so correction starts sooner. Paul Schulman's nuclear and
electrical-grid studies: reliability rises with the number of people
concerned about misidentification, misspecification, and misunderstanding.
The authors deliberately say **enlarge** rather than focus — "Focus excludes;
enlarge includes" — and treat the word "focus" as a red flag during audits.

Audit 5.2 probes [mindlessness](mindfulness-in-high-reliability-organizing.md)
triggers: weak stimuli plus powerful expectations; repetitive daily
situations; distraction, hurry, overload; and situations where people **see
problems but cannot act** — Ron Westrum: organizations willing to act on
surprises are also willing to acknowledge and think about them. A **limited
action repertoire** forces old categories onto new problems so people can act
at all ("when all you have is a hammer...") — e.g. firms that decouple
authority from responsibility and hold frontline staff accountable without
giving them authority mindlessly reimpose that pattern on fast-moving events.

Audit 5.3: mindfulness cannot comprehend the incomprehensible, but it aids
**early anomaly detection** before tightly coupled, interactively complex
systems spiral — more Disagree on slack, rerouting, and sequential-linearity
items means more mindfulness is required.

Audit 5.4 encodes the maxim **"it's good to feel bad and bad to feel good"**
— feeling good risks relaxed attentiveness and undetected accumulating
problems.

Audit 5.5: diverse views concentrate at the bottom where people have least
power to make signals stick — survivable only with interpersonal skill,
mutual respect, and shared conviction that mindfulness matters; otherwise
skeptics get "dismissed with speed and enthusiasm."

These instruments differ from [structural audits versus culture
assessment](structural-audits-vs-culture-assessment-for-resilience.md):
they measure enacted attention and organizing practices, not bowtie diagrams
or formal management-system compliance. HROs spend most of their time
**reaccomplishing** mindful infrastructure because mindful processes unravel
quickly.
