---
type: concept
title: Sharp End and Blunt End
description: >
  Practitioners in direct contact with the safety-critical process (sharp
  end) work inside constraints created by the organisational layers above
  (blunt end) — and reactions to failure are biased toward blaming the sharp
  end.
sources:
  - title: The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error'
    resource: "The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error' (Dekker), ch. 2"
---

The **sharp end** is where people touch the safety-critical process directly:
pilots, surgical teams, control-room operators, on-call engineers. The
**blunt end** is the organisational structure above them — executives,
budget-setters, vendors, regulators — which shapes, resource-constrains, and
pushes trade-offs down onto sharp-end work (see [goal conflicts and
production pressure](goal-conflicts-and-production-pressure.md)). Sharp-end
practitioners inherit the blunt end's decisions as their working conditions:
the tools bought, the staffing funded, the procedures written, the pressures
applied.

**Proximal bias**: reactions to failure naturally target whoever was closest
in time and space to the mishap — the sharp end. This is convenient for the
blunt end: a blameworthy-operator narrative converts systemic vulnerabilities
into isolated human lapses and spares the organisation expensive, disruptive
change. Three cases show the pattern:

- **Ladbroke Grove (1999)**: the driver was blamed for passing red Signal
  109 — a signal with severe visual clutter, numerous prior SPADs, standing
  union complaints, and an automatic braking system cancelled for cost.
- **November Oscar (B747)**: a captain prosecuted, demoted, and fined for a
  tight approach flown with an old autopilot, a sick crew, and bad weather;
  the prosecution protected the airline's brand and drove him to suicide.
- **RAF Chinook, Mull of Kintyre (1994)**: deceased pilots found grossly
  negligent, suppressing 160 prior recorded uncommanded flying-control
  movements in the type.

The two ends also work on different timescales. The sharp end's job is
**synchronic** — resolving *the actual*, right now: assess the situation,
work out what it demands, decide, act, in time. It can do this either
proactively, by falling back on a predefined solution someone anticipated
(a trained procedure, an emergency checklist), or reactively, by improvising
a novel solution on the spot when no predefined one fits — see [the boundary
of potential variability](boundary-of-potential-variability.md) for when a
situation forces the second mode. The blunt end's job is **diachronic** —
establishing, now, and maintaining, into an unknown future, the resources
(competence, equipment, staffing) that give the sharp end anything to fall
back on when the moment arrives. A blunt end that only manages the present
moment's budget is not doing its actual job, which is always about a future
it cannot fully specify.

Investigative counter-move: whenever a finding names a sharp-end act, trace
the [latent conditions](swiss-cheese-model.md) behind it and ask what
authority and resources the actor actually had ([authority–responsibility
mismatch](authority-responsibility-mismatch.md)). Blaming the proximal
person is not only unjust but self-defeating — it [suppresses the
reporting](blame-suppresses-reporting.md) the blunt end needs to see its own
contribution.
