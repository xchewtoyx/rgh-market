  Reason's Tripod method for proactively auditing the latent organisational

  accident-causation chain that is actually predictable and controllable
  before an event happens.

Accidents result from a combination of active failures and latent
conditions — the same split [the Swiss cheese model](swiss-cheese-model.md)
draws between sharp-end unsafe acts and dormant blunt-end vulnerabilities.
Tripod (Reason, developed originally for oil and gas operations) targets
only the latent half deliberately, on the grounds that it is the only half
sufficiently predictable and controllable *before* an event: an active
failure is, by definition, something that has already happened by the time
anyone can act on it, while a latent condition sits waiting and can in
principle be found and fixed while it is still just a latent condition.

Tripod evaluates **basic risk factors (BRFs)** — upstream organisational
processes that generate latent conditions — through regular audits and
routine front-line feedback, rather than waiting for an incident to surface
them. The processes it targets are the ones organisations tend to treat as
purely administrative rather than safety-relevant: procurement procedures,
maintenance management, and how communication interfaces between roles or
units are defined. This is a genuinely proactive move: none of these three
requires an accident, or even a near-miss, to audit.

Tripod's BRF auditing is one concrete implementation of the **upward-force**
half of [safety-control upward and downward
forces](safety-control-upward-and-downward-forces.md) — it measures the
organisational conditions that generate risk, not the capabilities that
counteract it. A full proactive risk-monitoring architecture needs both
halves: Tripod-style latent-condition auditing for what is accumulating, and
a separate, positive measure of the organisation's adaptive capacity for
what is pushing back against it.
