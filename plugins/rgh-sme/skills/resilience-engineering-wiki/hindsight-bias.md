Dekker's rule for investigators: *the more you react to failure, the less you
will understand it.* The reaction "how could they have been so stupid?" is
not analysis; it is hindsight bias speaking.

Hindsight bias (Fischhoff, 1975): knowledge of the outcome leads observers to
dramatically overestimate how predictable and preventable an event was —
while remaining unaware they are doing it. Hindsight collapses the complex,
branching, highly uncertain timeline people actually navigated (the "zigs and
zags") into a simple linear narrative of a binary choice: to err or not to
err. Data that only the outcome revealed as critical gets recast as obvious
at the time — as when an A320 manufacturer claimed a crew "should have known
they were in open descent mode".

The Clapham Junction inquiry (Hidden, 1989) states the warning canonically:
*"There is almost no human action or decision that cannot be made to look
flawed and less sensible in the misleading light of hindsight."* Dekker's
Oedipus metaphor makes the deeper point: Oedipus acted without error given
everything he knew; only the messenger's later news redefined his rational
actions as terrible mistakes. Outcome knowledge changes the *description* of
the act, not the act.

The bias is amplified by a structural time differential: investigators spend
months analysing, with pause and replay, what operators decided in seconds
while doing other work — any comparison of the two performances is
comparing unlike tasks.

Because hindsight bias makes stopping at a single culpable individual feel
like a complete explanation, who conducts the investigation and from what
[epistemic distance](epistemic-distance-in-post-accident-learning.md) they
work is itself a countermeasure — a reviewer working only from a
skeletal "first story" has the least resistance to the bias available.

Countermeasures:

- Adopt the inside-the-tunnel perspective of the [local rationality
  principle](local-rationality-principle.md): reconstruct what was known,
  salient, and competing for attention at each moment, without the ending.
- Watch for the bias's companions: [outcome bias](outcome-bias.md) (judging
  decision quality by outcome severity), [counterfactual
  reasoning](counterfactual-reasoning.md) ("if only they had…"), and
  [judgmental language](judgmental-language.md) ("complacency",
  "misdiagnosis").
- Remember that in hindsight every [normalised
  deviation](normalization-of-deviance.md) looks like an obvious warning
  ignored; at the time it was the norm. Retrospective culpability judgments
  built on this bias are why [culpability-tree just-culture
  schemes](just-culture.md) misfire.
