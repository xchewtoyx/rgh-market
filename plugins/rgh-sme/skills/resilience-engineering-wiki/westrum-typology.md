Ron Westrum's typology (1988, 2014) came out of human-factors research in
high-risk sociotechnical environments (aviation, healthcare, nuclear power).
Its central claim: organizational culture dictates **information flow**, and
information flow determines safety, resilience, and operational performance
— because everything an organisation can learn about its risks travels as
information someone chose to share (or [was afraid
to](blame-suppresses-reporting.md)).

| Dimension | Pathological (power-oriented) | Bureaucratic (rule-oriented) | Generative (performance-oriented) |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Primary focus | personal power, threat, fear | rules, turf, process | the mission |
| Information flow | withheld, hoarded, distorted | strict channels | shared transparently across boundaries |
| Cooperation | low | modest | high, cross-functional |
| Messengers | **shot** | neglected | **trained** — welcomed to surface risks |
| Responsibilities | shirked | narrow, siloed | shared collectively |
| Cross-silo bridging | discouraged | tolerated under control | actively cultivated |
| Handling failure | scapegoating | procedural "justice" | **inquiry** (blameless post-mortem) |
| Handling novelty | crushed | treated as a problem | implemented, rapidly evaluated |

Notes for use:

- The "messengers" and "failure" rows are the typology's diagnostic core:
  how the last bearer of bad news was treated tells you which column you
  are in, and predicts whether [reporting
  survives](blame-suppresses-reporting.md) and whether failure handling
  looks like [restorative inquiry or retribution](just-culture.md).
- The bureaucratic column shows that blamelessness alone is not the bar —
  rule-bound "procedural justice" still neglects messengers and starves
  information flow while accumulating [safety
  clutter](safety-clutter.md); silos are the **structural secrecy** that
  hides cross-departmental [drift](drift-into-failure.md) (the bridging
  row).
- Generative culture is measurable psychometrically (Likert-scale
  agreement with the indicators above); its prerequisites are mutual trust,
  transparent information flow, and the ability to rapidly reverse
  decisions on new evidence. Google's Project Aristotle independently
  converged on the same finding: team **psychological safety** matters more
  than individual talent.
- The predictive claim is empirical, not aspirational: Westrum found
  generative culture a top predictor of patient safety in healthcare
  (BMJ Quality & Safety, 2004), and the State of DevOps research found the
  same for software delivery and organizational performance.
- Culture here is a pattern of response and flow, not an attitude
  possession — the [interpretivist
  view](safety-culture-is-what-an-organization-does.md) — and the lever for
  changing it is changing behaviour, not minds (see that note's
  behavioural-lead mechanism).
