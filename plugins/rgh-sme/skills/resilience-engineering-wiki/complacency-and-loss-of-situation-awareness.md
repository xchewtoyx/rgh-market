type: concept
title: Complacency and Loss of Situation Awareness Are Pseudo-Explanations
description: >
  Two popular investigation labels that restate the outcome as if it were a
  cause — an abstract construct cannot cause events, and both labels collapse
  under circularity.
sources:
  - title: The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error'
    resource: "The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error' (Dekker), ch. 4"
---

Both labels are [judgmental language](judgmental-language.md) dressed as
findings, and both should be eliminated from analyses.

**Against "loss of situation awareness":**

1. **Construct fallacy** (Billings, 1996): situation awareness is an abstract
   construct; constructs cannot cause physical events.
2. **Hindsight substitution**: "loss of SA" merely measures the gap between
   what the observer knows now and what the operator knew then — it is
   [hindsight bias](hindsight-bias.md) given a technical-sounding name.
3. **Cartesian epistemology fallacy**: it assumes an objective ground truth
   against which minds are graded as imperfect mirrors; all human
   perspectives are inherently local and partial ([local
   rationality](local-rationality-principle.md)).
4. **Legal scapegoating**: courts and coroners use the label to convert
   cognitive trade-offs into criminal breaches of a "duty of care".

**Against "complacency":**

1. **Undefined optimum**: proving complacency would require an established
   optimal monitoring/sampling rate to deviate from (Moray & Inagaki, 2000);
   no study has produced one.
2. **Investigative cop-out**: the label obscures the systemic drivers of
   attentional allocation — workload, interface design, [competing
   goals](goal-conflicts-and-production-pressure.md).
3. **Circularity**: "Why did they lose SA? Because they were complacent. How
   do we know they were complacent? Because they lost SA."

A related test for any post-incident psychological label is [unfalsifiable
bias attribution](unfalsifiable-bias-attribution.md): if the opposite
decision followed by a bad outcome would just as easily have earned some
other bias label, the label is being generated by the outcome, not by
anything analysable in the decision.

The repair in each case is the same: replace the label with an account of
what the person was attending to and why that allocation of attention made
sense — the analysis exemplified in [cognitive
fixation](cognitive-fixation.md) cases, where every cue supported the
"unaware" assessment. An error label ends analysis exactly where it should
begin ([error as symptom](human-error-as-symptom.md)).
