title: Emergence — Resultant vs Emergent Phenomena
description: >
  A resultant property is predictable directly from its parts; an emergent
  one is not — and accidents in complex sociotechnical systems are emergent,
  which is why an organisation with zero individually error-prone people can
  still produce a catastrophic organisational accident.
sources:
  - title: "Drift into Failure: From Hunting Broken Components to Understanding Complex Systems"
    resource: "Drift into Failure (Dekker), ch. 6"
---

George Henry Lewes's 19th-century distinction separates two ways a whole can
relate to its parts:

- **Resultant** phenomena are predictable directly from the properties of
  their constituents — the heat of a substance from how fast its molecules
  move.
- **Emergent** phenomena are not predictable from the parts at all: table
  salt's taste and crystalline structure bear no resemblance to the
  properties of sodium (a violently reactive metal) or chlorine (a poisonous
  gas); wetness exists only at the aggregate level, not in any single water
  molecule.

Emergent, distributed order does not require a central controller: slime
mould with no nervous system can solve a maze; ant colonies build structured
nests from millions of individuals each following a handful of simple local
rules, with no organiser anywhere in the system. This is **organised
complexity** — organised despite there being no organiser.

**Accidents in sociotechnical systems are emergent, not resultant.** They
are not predictable from, or explainable by, the state of the individual
people or components involved — they are one possible outcome of components
doing their entirely *normal* work and interacting in ways nobody
specifically arranged. This has a sharp practical consequence for how an
organisation should read its own safety record: because organisational
accident risk is not a resultant sum of individual accident-proneness,
**an organisation where every individual is fully rule-compliant and has
zero personal incident history can still be heading for a catastrophic
organisational accident** — and conversely, individually risky behaviour
does not automatically add up to organisational catastrophe either.
[Zero-tolerance campaigns against small individual
deviations](vision-zero.md) that justify themselves by pointing at
large-scale disasters are assuming a resultant relationship that emergence
theory specifically rules out — the two scales are not connected by a
straight line in either direction.

This is the conceptual foundation under [systems-theoretic accident
models](systems-theoretic-accident-model.md)'s claim that safety is an
emergent, whole-system property, under [drift into
failure](drift-into-failure.md) treating drift as an emergent property of
adaptation rather than a designed-in weakness, and under [Safety-II's](safety-i-and-safety-ii.md)
observation that success and failure emerge from the same everyday
performance variability rather than from different populations of good and
bad actors.

[Bedau's taxonomy of emergence](bedau-taxonomy-of-emergence.md) refines this
resultant-vs-emergent split into three graded classes and maps them
specifically onto organisational resilience.
