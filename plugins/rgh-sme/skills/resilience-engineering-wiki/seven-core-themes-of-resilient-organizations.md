Wreathall and Merritt (2003) synthesised roughly 65 organisational
safety/performance models (following Reason) into seven recurring themes
that distinguish resilient organisations across operational levels. This is
a separate, independently-derived framework from [Woods's eight
criteria](eight-criteria-of-organizational-resilience.md) — the two overlap
in substance but were built from different source material for a different
purpose (this one underlies a specific proactive-measurement toolset; Woods's
is an audit checklist) — and are worth holding side by side rather than
merged, since a system can score unevenly across either one independently.

1. **Top-level commitment** — senior management recognises human-performance
   issues, follows through on action, and actively balances short-term
   production pressure against chronic safety protection (see [goal
   conflicts](goal-conflicts-and-production-pressure.md)).
2. **[Just culture](just-culture.md)** — open reporting of concerns and
   vulnerabilities up the hierarchy without fear of retribution for
   non-culpable error, while still refusing to tolerate genuinely culpable
   behaviour.
3. **Learning culture** — whether the organisation responds to safety events
   and near-misses with denial, or with genuine repair and structural
   reform.
4. **Awareness** — continuous data collection giving management real-time
   insight into frontline performance, workplace problems, and the
   degradation of defences.
5. **Preparedness** — proactively anticipating human-performance issues and
   operational disruptions before they arrive ("being ahead" of problems).
6. **Flexibility** — adapting to novel or complex problems without
   disrupting core function; requires empowering frontline personnel and
   first-line supervisors to decide locally rather than routing decisions
   through a management bottleneck. The 1997 Millstone nuclear plant
   regulatory fines are the cited case of the opposite: decision-making
   "stiffness" and production fixation persisting despite safety warnings.
7. **Opacity (and observability)** — organisational awareness of its own
   safety boundaries and proximity to the edge of degraded defences,
   requiring wide, open sharing of safety information across all
   hierarchical levels — the same property Weick calls "collective
   mindfulness" (see [mindfulness in high-reliability
   organizing](mindfulness-in-high-reliability-organizing.md) and [informed
   culture](informed-culture.md)).

Themes 4–6 (awareness, preparedness, flexibility) parallel [the four
abilities of resilient performance](four-abilities-of-resilient-performance.md)
(monitoring, anticipation, response) closely enough to read as the same
underlying capability restated at organisational-audit granularity rather
than as independent themes — the difference in framing is source and use
case, not substance. Theme 7 names the same organisational transparency
[chronic unease](chronic-unease.md) depends on to keep leadership close
enough to the sharp end to hear bad news.
