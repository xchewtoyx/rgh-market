Drift into failure is the accident model in which risk is a *gradual erosion
of safety margins* rather than an event. Turner's man-made disaster theory
(1978) showed that disasters incubate: small, unnoticed departures from rules
and design assumptions accumulate over months or years before the surprise.
Vaughan (1996) supplied the mechanism — [normalisation of
deviance](normalization-of-deviance.md) — and Dekker (2011) generalised it:
each small departure produces an immediate efficiency or production reward
under [goal conflict](goal-conflicts-and-production-pressure.md) and no
immediate safety consequence, so the departure becomes the new baseline, and
the next departure starts from there.

Dekker names five recurring features that make drift the shape it is:
[scarcity and competition](rasmussen-boundary-model.md) squeeze operations
toward the workload and safety boundaries; [decrementalism](decrementalism.md)
means each step is too small to be worth remarking on; [sensitive dependence
on initial conditions](sensitive-dependence-on-initial-conditions.md) lets a
mundane early decision gate decades of downstream consequence;
[unruly technology](unruly-technology.md) gives ambiguous field data that
gets reinterpreted rather than treated as warning; and [protective
structures can themselves fuel drift](protective-structures-can-fuel-drift.md)
rather than arrest it.

Drift is the process of tuning a system, through repeated locally successful
trade-offs, until it is [optimised at the edge of
chaos](edge-of-chaos-and-phase-transitions.md) — the point of maximum
adaptability where a small perturbation usually produces a small response
but can, rarely and unpredictably, produce a disproportionate one. The
single strongest available lever against all five features at once is
[diversity as a safety resource](diversity-as-a-safety-resource.md): a wider
range of perspectives in the room is what generates the alternative
readings of a weak signal that a homogeneous group would wave through.

What makes drift dangerous is that it is invisible from inside:

- Every step is locally rational and locally successful; nobody experiences
  themselves as taking more risk (see [local
  rationality](local-rationality-principle.md)).
- Past success is read as evidence of safety, breeding the overconfidence that
  the [Heinrich triangle myth](heinrich-triangle-myth.md) institutionalises
  ("our injury numbers are great, so we are safe").
- The system migrates toward its safety boundary without crossing any visible
  line, so there is no alarm to respond to — only a trend to notice.
- Improvements are absorbed rather than banked: by Hirschhorn's law of
  systems development, systems always operate at capacity, so technological
  safety buffers are routinely exploited to push operations closer to the
  performance envelope's edge.

Drift's procedural manifestation — practice departing incrementally from
written rules — is [procedural drift](procedural-drift.md).

Two caveats keep the metaphor from being taken too literally. First, the
safety boundary being drifted toward is not a measurable physical distance —
it is an emergent description constructed after the fact, the same way
[Rasmussen's boundary](rasmussen-boundary-model.md) is only ever visible as
a trend, never as a coordinate. Second, "drift" implies one uniform motion,
but real organisations do not drift as a whole: separate subsystems drift
non-linearly, at different rates and sometimes in different directions, and
whether the *aggregate* system is safe depends on how well those subsystems
stay cross-functionally coupled and synchronised — a subsystem that looks
stable in isolation can still be the one whose drift, uncoordinated with a
neighbour's, produces the eventual surprise (see [working at
cross-purposes](working-at-cross-purposes.md)). No external force pushes any
of this; drift is entirely the accumulated latent effect of ordinary daily
[efficiency–thoroughness trade-offs](efficiency-thoroughness-trade-off.md),
which is also why "work to rule" — withdrawing exactly those daily
trade-offs — reliably breaks a system rather than making it safer.

Linking the micro level (an individual [sacrifice
judgement](sacrifice-judgements.md) or ETTO at a single sharp-end decision
point) to the macro level (aggregate organisational drift measured over
years) remains a genuinely open theoretical and practical challenge — it is
one thing to show each local trade-off was reasonable and another to show
how thousands of such trade-offs sum into a specific organisation-wide
trajectory, which is exactly why [meta-control of the risk
model](meta-control-of-risk.md) rather than tracking any single decision is
the more tractable lever.

Countermeasures follow from the mechanism, not from any single event:
cultivate [chronic unease](chronic-unease.md), and study how *normal work*
actually gets done — where margins are being consumed, which workarounds have
become standard — the [Safety-II](safety-i-and-safety-ii.md) programme.
After an accident, drift explains why the [chain-of-events
model](chain-of-events-model.md) misleads: there is no discrete cause to find,
because the "cause" was years of ordinary, rewarded work.
