---
type: concept
title: Risk Homeostasis
description: >
  A system tends to drift back toward its habitual level of risk acceptance
  even after an intervention adds safety margin, because the same efficiency
  pressure that consumed the margin before is still present afterward.
sources:
  - title: "Drift into Failure: From Hunting Broken Components to Understanding Complex Systems"
    resource: "Drift into Failure (Dekker), ch. 5"
---

An intervention that widens a safety margin — moving the safety boundary
further out, or pulling normal operations further away from it — looks like
a durable fix. Risk homeostasis is the finding that it usually is not: the
same [efficiency and production
pressure](goal-conflicts-and-production-pressure.md) that consumed the
previous margin is still acting on the system after the intervention, and
will consume the new margin too, restoring the organisation's habitual level
of risk acceptance.

The implication is that margin-adding interventions which leave the
underlying pressure untouched are borrowed time, not a fix — they reset the
counter on [drift into failure](drift-into-failure.md) rather than stopping
it. A durable intervention has to change the behaviour-shaping conditions
that generate the pressure in the first place (staffing levels, incentive
structure, schedule realism), and make the ongoing migration back toward the
boundary visible at every level of management that can act on it — not just
to the sharp-end operators who are, by [local
rationality](local-rationality-principle.md), the least able to see it as a
trend rather than a sequence of individually reasonable choices. Simply
layering a safety campaign or a "try harder" exhortation on top of unchanged
pressure is exactly the intervention risk homeostasis predicts will fail.
