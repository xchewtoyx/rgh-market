---
type: concept
title: Migrating Decisions
description: >
  In HROs under pressure, decisions move up and down the hierarchy to wherever
  the person with the most event-specific knowledge happens to be, decoupling
  authority from rank.
sources:
  - title: Managing the Unexpected
    resource: "Managing the Unexpected (Weick, Sutcliffe), ch. 5"
---

Migrating decisions — perhaps the most cited HRO property — was first
identified by Karlene Roberts, Susanne Stout, and Jennifer Halpern studying
carrier flight operations. During non-routine problems, [deference to
expertise](deference-to-expertise.md) replaces routine deference to rank:

"Decisions are pushed down to the lowest levels in the carriers as a result
of the need for quick decision making. Men who can immediately sense the
potential problem can indeed make a quick decision to alleviate the problem
or effectively decouple some of the technology, reducing the consequences
of errors in decision making. ... Decisions migrate around these
organizations in search of a person who has specific knowledge of the event."

Expertise is not necessarily matched with hierarchical position, so
hierarchy-bound organizations are seldom positioned to know all they can
about a problem. On carriers, a squadron boss who knows his pilots' quirks
may momentarily override higher-ranking tower officers when deciding how to
land a squadron member who has lost hydraulics.

James Reason equates organizational flexibility with this shifting-authority
structure — information flows more freely when hierarchies flatten and rank
defers to technical expertise. Bristol Royal Infirmary lacked this: centers
with lower pediatric cardiac mortality "had a better record of rescue of the
complications" — recognizing and treating them earlier for the same
severity score.

Managing the unexpected requires creating **flexible decision structures**
that let decision-making migrate to whoever has the most relevant expertise
when problems occur, valuing expertise and experience over rank. See
[communication forcing functions for distributed
authority](communication-forcing-functions-for-distributed-authority.md)
for the coupling mechanism that makes decentralisation work rather than
merely relocating coordination failure.
