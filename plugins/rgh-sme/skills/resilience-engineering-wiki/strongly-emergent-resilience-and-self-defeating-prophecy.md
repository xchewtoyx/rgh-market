**Strongly Emergent Resilience (SER)** is Pariès's name for organisational
resilience properties that cannot be reduced to individual agent traits even
in principle — the organisational-resilience instance of [strong
emergence](bedau-taxonomy-of-emergence.md) rather than [weak
emergence](nominally-and-weakly-emergent-resilience.md). Organisational
culture, collective consciousness, and social feedback loops (media
coverage or polling that alters the behaviour of the very population being
polled) are the paradigm cases: no amount of complete knowledge about
individual members explains the phenomenon, because the phenomenon is
partly constituted by self-reference.

**Retroactive causality** is the specific mechanism that makes this class
distinct from ordinary complexity: a *representation of the future* modifies
*present* behaviour, running causality in a direction ordinary systems
thinking does not usually model. An organisation's absolute belief that a
given risk has been eliminated is not neutral information sitting alongside
the risk — believing it induces the complacency that reintroduces the very
risk the belief claimed was gone, so the belief about the future state
causally produces a present state that falsifies it.

**The heuristics of fear** (Dupuy, 2002) is the deliberate countermeasure:
effective safety management maintains active risk awareness during periods
of apparent calm by treating the *absence* of danger as insufficient
grounds for complacency — a formalised version of the same posture as
[chronic unease](chronic-unease.md), grounded here in the retroactive-
causality mechanism specifically rather than in organisational psychology
alone.

**The safety paradox**: a functioning safety culture is structurally a
**self-defeating prophecy**. It works by constructing a genuinely compelling
present vision of a possible future disaster, specifically in order to
trigger the actions that prevent that disaster from ever occurring — so its
success is invisible by construction, indistinguishable from a
disaster that was never going to happen anyway. The sharpest formulation of
what this demands of the people doing the work: **the ultimate achievement
of a safety whistleblower is to be proven wrong** — raising an alarm loud
enough to change behaviour and avert the disaster it warned of means the
alarm will retrospectively look, to anyone judging it by outcome alone,
like it was never necessary. This is [hindsight bias](hindsight-bias.md)
and [sacrifice judgements](sacrifice-judgements.md)'s "true test of safety
culture" restated at the level of an entire organisation's relationship to
its own warnings, not just one operator's: an organisation that only
rewards warnings vindicated by a disaster that actually happened is
punishing exactly the warnings that worked.
