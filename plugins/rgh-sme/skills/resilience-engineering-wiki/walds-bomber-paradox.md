type: concept
title: Wald's Bomber Paradox
description: >
  Reported incidents are like bullet holes on returning bombers — markers of
  survivable damage — while catastrophic vulnerability hides in normal work
  that never generates a report.
sources:
  - title: The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error'
    resource: "The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error' (Dekker), ch. 7"
---

Statistician Abraham Wald analysed bullet holes on Allied bombers returning
from missions. Commanders wanted armour where the holes clustered. Wald's
insight: returning bombers show holes only where damage is *survivable*; the
planes hit in the clean areas — engines, cockpit — never came back. Armour
belongs where the returning planes show no holes.

Applied to safety data in ultra-safe systems:

- **Formally reported incidents are holes on returning planes**: markers of
  survivable, recoverable trouble — evidence the system absorbed a hit. This
  is why reporting rates correlate *negatively* with fatality risk (see the
  empirical refutations in the [Heinrich triangle
  myth](heinrich-triangle-myth.md)).
- **Catastrophes come from the unreported region**: disasters are preceded
  not by reported near-misses or rule violations but by *normal work* —
  daily workarounds, jury-rigged fixes, and unwritten adaptations that never
  generate a report because nothing seems wrong ([work-as-done that
  work-as-imagined never sees](work-as-imagined-vs-work-as-done.md), the
  accumulation pattern of [drift into failure](drift-into-failure.md)).

Consequences for what to monitor:

- Do not steer safety effort by the density of existing reports; the
  incident database maps where the system survives, not where it dies.
- The high-signal activity is studying how normal, successful work is
  actually done — the [Safety-II](safety-i-and-safety-ii.md) programme —
  because that is the only place pre-catastrophic drift is visible.
- Beware the related **decoy phenomenon**: resources drawn to high-profile,
  well-reported risk categories while unmonitored failure paths grow (see
  the [ultra-safe plateau](amalberti-safety-continuum.md)).
