---
type: concept
title: Judgmental Language
description: >
  Evaluative vocabulary — "complacency", "inattention", "misdiagnosis",
  "failure to" — judges performance after the fact in place of explaining it,
  and should be purged from findings.
sources:
  - title: The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error'
    resource: "The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error' (Dekker), ch. 2"
---

Words like "failure", "misdiagnosis", "complacency", and "inattention" feel
like findings but are verdicts: they characterise performance from the
outcome backwards rather than explaining it forwards. Labelling a medical
event a "misdiagnosis in spite of adequate data" ignores the actual question:
why did the diagnosis make sense to the physician given the cues that were
available and salient at the time ([local
rationality](local-rationality-principle.md))? "Adequate data" is itself a
[hindsight](hindsight-bias.md) judgment about which data mattered.

The exemplar of non-judgmental analysis is TSB Canada's Swissair 111 report
(1998). The MD-11 crew, after detecting what they perceived as minor
air-conditioning smoke, did not fly an immediate emergency descent into
Halifax but prepared for an overweight landing and secured the cabin. Rather
than writing "the crew failed to descend immediately", the TSB reconstructed
the inside-tunnel perspective: given the limited cues, an unstabilised
emergency landing at night at an unfamiliar airport without approach charts
appeared to pose a *greater* risk to passengers than proper preparation. The
same behaviour that judgmental vocabulary would render as delay becomes, in
context, a defensible risk trade-off.

Working rules:

- Grep your own findings for evaluative terms ("complacent", "inadequate",
  "failed to", "should have") and replace each with a description of what the
  person was actually doing and attending to, and why.
- "Complacency" and "loss of situation awareness" are especially insidious:
  they restate the outcome (they didn't notice X) as if it were a cause, the
  same move as treating [error as a cause rather than a
  symptom](human-error-as-symptom.md).
- Judgmental language usually arrives packaged with
  [counterfactuals](counterfactual-reasoning.md); edit both out together.
