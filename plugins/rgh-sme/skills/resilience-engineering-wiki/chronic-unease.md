Chronic unease is the deliberate refusal to be reassured. Because [drift into
failure](drift-into-failure.md) produces no alarms — every eroding step is
locally successful — high-reliability organisations (HROs) guard against it
with a standing disposition of doubt:

- **Reject past success as evidence of safety.** A long run without incidents
  is exactly what the incubation period of a disaster looks like (the
  [Heinrich triangle myth](heinrich-triangle-myth.md) shows why good injury
  numbers prove nothing about catastrophic risk).
- **Welcome minority viewpoints and doubt.** The person who is uneasy about a
  practice everyone else has [normalised](normalization-of-deviance.md) is the
  organisation's early-warning sensor; treating dissent as disloyalty disables
  it. That unease is often [expectancy violation rather than an articulable
  finding](expectancy-violation-as-anomaly-detection.md) — demanding a stated
  reason before acting on it filters out exactly the signal this mechanism
  produces best.
- **Keep leadership present at the sharp end.** Bad news must be heard where
  it originates. Leaders who only see dashboards see
  [work-as-imagined](work-as-imagined-vs-work-as-done.md); hearing how work
  actually gets done requires being there and being safe to talk to (which
  depends on a [just culture](just-culture.md) — see [blame suppresses
  reporting](blame-suppresses-reporting.md)).

The wider HRO canon (Weick & Sutcliffe, *Managing the Unexpected*) frames the
same posture as five principles — see [mindfulness in high-reliability
organizing](mindfulness-in-high-reliability-organizing.md). James Reason's
[informed culture](informed-culture.md) makes the same point at the
organizational level: "It is hard, even unnatural, for individuals to remain
chronically uneasy, so their organizational culture takes on a profound
significance. Individuals may forget to be afraid, but the culture... provides
them with both the reminders and the tools to help them remember."

Chronic unease is a property of attention and posture, not of paperwork: it
cannot be delegated to audits or metrics, and adding compliance artefacts to
manufacture it produces [safety clutter](safety-clutter.md) instead. Its
concrete organisational expression in mature systems is the set of [armor
strategies against drift](armor-strategies-against-drift.md).

A related but distinct threat to the same posture is demographic rather than
psychological: even a workforce that genuinely wants to reject past success
as proof of safety may lack the lived experience to do so, if [hazard
awareness has decayed through
turnover](decay-of-hazard-awareness-through-turnover.md) faster than the
organisation renewed it.
