Erik Hollnagel's contrast between two definitions of safety:

| Dimension | Safety-I (traditional) | Safety-II (resilience) |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Safety is… | absence of negative events ("zero harm") | presence of positive capacities to succeed |
| Study… | why things go wrong (lagging incidents) | why things normally go right (normal work) |
| The human is… | a bounded, unreliable component to constrain | a flexible source of system resilience |
| Management goal | reduce behavioural variability, enforce SOPs | understand and support adaptive workarounds |

The pivotal claim is that success and failure flow from the *same* source:
everyday performance variability. Workers continuously adapt — bridging gaps
in procedures, absorbing [goal
conflicts](goal-conflicts-and-production-pressure.md), compensating for design
flaws — and these adaptations produce success almost all the time and failure
occasionally. Suppressing variability to prevent the failures also removes
the adaptive capacity that produces the successes. This aligns Safety-II with
the [new view of human error](old-view-vs-new-view-of-human-error.md), and
Safety-I with the old view's human-as-liability premise.

Operationally, Safety-II redirects attention from incident counts (whose
predictive worthlessness the [Heinrich triangle
myth](heinrich-triangle-myth.md) establishes) to studying
[work-as-done](work-as-imagined-vs-work-as-done.md): which workarounds have
become load-bearing, where margins are being consumed, what makes hard cases
go well. That is also the observational programme that makes [drift into
failure](drift-into-failure.md) visible before an accident does.

Safety-II complements rather than replaces Safety-I: incidents still deserve
investigation — but with the understanding that the behaviour visible in a
failure is the same variability that usually delivers success.

[FRAM](functional-resonance-analysis-method.md) is the method built directly
on this premise: it models a system's normal, everyday performance
variability across functionally coupled parts, and treats a catastrophic
outcome as several individually-unremarkable variabilities combining, not as
one function breaking.

At the organisational level, the same shift in emphasis is [resilience as
adaptive capacity](resilience-as-adaptive-capacity.md): a set of ongoing
capabilities to recognise, steer away from, and recover from the edges of
safe operation, rather than a static safety margin.
