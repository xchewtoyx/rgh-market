---
type: concept
title: Garden Path Fallacy and Snap-Back
description: >
  A person explaining away one piece of disconfirming evidence at a time can
  drift arbitrarily far from the correct understanding of a situation
  without any single step feeling like a red flag — the individual-cognition
  mirror of organisational normalisation of deviance.
sources:
  - title: "Sources of Power: How People Make Decisions"
    resource: "Sources of Power (Klein), ch. 5"
---

A [mental simulation](recognition-primed-decision-model.md) or working
theory of a situation is flexible enough to absorb almost any contradictory
evidence by revising itself slightly: a discrepancy gets explained away as
noise, an unreliable report, or a special case, and the underlying theory
survives intact. Marvin Cohen's **garden path fallacy** names what happens
when this repeats: a chain of individually-reasonable explaining-away steps
carries a person's understanding steadily further from what is actually
happening, with no single step ever feeling like the moment to stop and
reconsider — two ship captains each read the other's anomalous course
change through a familiar, non-alarming prior pattern ("ships correct
themselves") right up until collision, because each individual anomaly, in
isolation, was too small to reclassify as a warning.

This is the cognitive-level counterpart of [decrementalism and normalisation
of deviance](decrementalism.md): the organisational mechanism operates on
practice and procedure over months or years, while the garden path fallacy
operates on a single person's situation understanding over minutes, but the
structure is identical — small, locally reasonable accommodations, none of
which individually crosses a threshold worth alarming about.

**Snap-back** (Cohen) is the fallacy's self-correcting counterpart:
explaining away *mild* discrepancies is often the right call, since evidence
itself is frequently unreliable — but past some threshold of accumulated
explaining-away, the working theory becomes so convoluted holding it
together that confidence collapses all at once, and the person goes back
and re-examines everything they had previously dismissed. The catch is
timing: nothing forces the re-examination to happen before the accumulated
strain reaches that threshold, so by the time snap-back fires, the
[expectancy-violation](expectancy-violation-as-anomaly-detection.md) alarms
that would normally have caught the problem earlier may already have been
individually talked down.

The practical implication is to track the *running tally* of
explained-away anomalies explicitly, not just each one in isolation — a
mounting count of "special case" dismissals is itself the signal to seek an
alternative explanation, even before any single dismissal looks
unreasonable on its own. This is one more argument for [studying normal
work](studying-normal-work.md) and comparing against original assumptions
rather than the most recent accepted explanation, and for treating a rising
count of ambiguous-threat dismissals ([ambiguous
threats](ambiguous-threats.md)) as data in its own right.
