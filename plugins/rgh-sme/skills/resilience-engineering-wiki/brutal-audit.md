Pat Lagadec: "The ability to deal with a crisis situation is largely
dependent on the structures that have been developed before chaos arrives.
The event can in some ways be considered as an abrupt and brutal audit: at
a moment's notice, everything that was left unprepared becomes a complex
problem, and every weakness comes rushing to the forefront."

Unexpected events "audit" an organization's resilience: they test how much
it stretches without breaking and how well it recovers. Audits can be mild or
brutal; unrecognized mild audits often turn brutal. Examples cited across
industries include Intel's Pentium chip, Coca-Cola's formula change, Perrier
benzene contamination, Menu Foods pet food poisonings, CDC West Nile
misdiagnosis, and excess pediatric cardiac deaths at Bristol Royal
Infirmary.

A mild audit stays mild when organizations make [strong responses to weak
signals](strong-response-to-weak-signals.md). A brutal audit exposes what
[confirmation-seeking in expectations](confirmation-seeking-in-expectations.md)
and [normalization of deviance](normalization-of-deviance.md) concealed.
The period right after an audit resembles the period after battle: naked
truths lie around for the taking, but candor soon gives way to normalizing
stories that protect reputations — and learning stops once official stories
get straightened out. HROs stretch these moments of candor.

Winston Churchill's post-Singapore reflection yields **Churchill's protocol**
— four audit questions after any disruptive event: Why didn't I know? Why
didn't my advisors know? Why wasn't I told? Why didn't I ask? The ferry
*Herald of Free Enterprise* (1987) violated two at once: Standing Order
01.09 assumed silence meant readiness without requiring those who know to
report or the master to ask. Key diagnostic: **what does "no news" mean** in
a mindful organization — going well, going poorly, or unclear?

Conscious audits (questionnaires on the five HRO principles) should repeat
ongoing; slippage in scores signals drift away from [mindfulness in
high-reliability organizing](mindfulness-in-high-reliability-organizing.md).
Weick and Sutcliffe provide nine instruments — two general (mindful
infrastructure, mindlessness vulnerability), one on coupling/complexity
context, five mapped to individual HRO principles, and the compact
[Mindfulness Organizing Scale](mindfulness-organizing-scale.md) — detailed
in [conscious mindfulness audits](conscious-mindfulness-audits.md). HROs
spend most of their time reaccomplishing mindful infrastructure because
mindful processes unravel quickly.
