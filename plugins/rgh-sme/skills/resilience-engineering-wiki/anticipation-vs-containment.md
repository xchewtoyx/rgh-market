title: Anticipation Versus Containment
description: >
  Weick and Sutcliffe's split of HRO mindfulness into three anticipation
  principles that slow escalation of weak signals and two containment
  principles that limit damage once surprise has begun.
sources:
  - title: Managing the Unexpected
    resource: "Managing the Unexpected (Weick, Sutcliffe), ch. 1, ch. 4, ch. 5"
---

The five [high-reliability organization](high-reliability-organizations.md)
principles group into two clusters within [mindfulness in high-reliability
organizing](mindfulness-in-high-reliability-organizing.md):

**Anticipation** (Principles 1–3) — tracking the development of unexpected
events before they overwhelm the system:

1. [Preoccupation with failure](preoccupation-with-failure.md)
2. [Reluctance to simplify](reluctance-to-simplify.md)
3. [Sensitivity to operations](sensitivity-to-operations.md)

To anticipate is to foresee an eventual unchecked outcome from small
discrepancies — taking a small cue and imagining a scenario where it signals
a larger situation. Escalation of small discrepancies is slowed by
anticipation and stopped by containment.

**Containment** (Principles 4–5) — preventing unwanted outcomes *after* an
unexpected event has already begun to unfold:

4. [Commitment to resilience](commitment-to-resilience.md)
5. [Deference to expertise](deference-to-expertise.md)

Unexpected events begin to unfold before they are noticed, so reliability
depends on how well prepared the organization is to be mindfully *reactive*.

Organizations that mismanage the unexpected tend to: ignore small failures,
accept simple diagnoses, take frontline operations for granted, neglect
resilience capabilities, and defer to authorities rather than experts. Most
(planning-obsessed, rational) organizations score worse on containment than
anticipation — resilience and flexible decision processes are typically
undervalued.

Reliable outcomes require sensing the unexpected in a *stable* manner while
dealing with it in a *variable* manner. At Diablo Canyon, operators
continually altered actions to deal with surprises but did not alter their
mindful processes of understanding, evidence collection, detection,
evaluation, and revising — those mindful processes were the stable routines
that triggered variable activities. Most organizations do the opposite: they
keep activities constant and vary their mindfulness.

Anticipation favors "think then act"; containment favors "act while thinking"
or "act in order to think more clearly" — empirical fighting rather than
pure planning.
