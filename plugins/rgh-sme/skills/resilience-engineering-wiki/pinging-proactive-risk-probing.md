  Deliberately probing for early physical and behavioural indicators that a

  that this only works when done by people embedded in the work, not by
  off-site specialists monitoring from a distance.
sources:
  - title: "Resilience Engineering in Practice"

Pinging (Wreathall & Merritt, 2003) is the proactive practice of probing for
changes in a project's risk profile before those changes accumulate into a
loss — the deliberate-search counterpart to [faint
signals](proactive-monitoring-control-model.md), which describes signals
arriving on their own. Rather than waiting for a formal report or an
outcome metric to move, pinging means actively checking a broad set of
physical and behavioural indicators that a situation is drifting: multiple
competing issues piling up, stalled progress, schedule slippage, a shift in
a project manager's mood, specialty staff staying on site longer than
planned, sudden labour shortages, high personnel turnover, spikes in
unplanned emergent work, minor safety or quality incidents, routine tasks
(permits, checks) being skipped or delayed, weather or holiday stress,
communication breakdowns, fatigue from a long-running effort, and degrading
site housekeeping. None of these is individually alarming; together they are
exactly the kind of accumulating pattern [decrementalism](decrementalism.md)
predicts stays invisible to comparison against the immediately preceding
state.

**Who does the pinging changes whether it works, independent of the
indicator list itself.** A first implementation trained off-site support
staff already in frequent phone or email contact with field sites to
recognise overload and refer struggling projects up — and it failed. Off-
site staff either lacked the situational depth to recognise systemic
overload from a distance, or, when they did notice something, immediately
tried to solve the localised problem within their own narrow functional
role rather than escalating the risk signal itself. The fix was structural,
not a training fix on the same design: shift to organisation-wide networked
pinging, training every role actually present on site — clerks, craft
workers, project managers, directors — to notice a shifting **local
climate**, rather than concentrating detection in a specialist role
physically and organisationally removed from where the signal originates.
See [error-likely climate versus safety
culture](error-likely-climate-vs-safety-culture.md) for what "local climate"
means as a target of detection distinct from organisational culture.

The general lesson: a proactive-monitoring design choice that looks neutral
— *who* is trained to watch for a leading indicator — can determine whether
the same indicator list succeeds or fails, because detection depends on
being close enough to the work to interpret an ambiguous signal correctly,
not merely on having a channel to receive it.
