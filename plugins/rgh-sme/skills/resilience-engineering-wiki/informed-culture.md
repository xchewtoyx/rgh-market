An informed culture is "one in which those who manage and operate the system
have current knowledge about the human, technical, organizational, and
environmental factors that determine the safety of the system as a whole."
James Reason argues sustaining wariness requires collecting and disseminating
information about incidents, near misses, and system vital signs — but
candid reporting requires trust, which is hard to develop, easy to destroy,
and hard to institutionalize.

"It is hard, even unnatural, for individuals to remain chronically uneasy, so
their organizational culture takes on a profound significance. Individuals may
forget to be afraid, but the culture... provides them with both the reminders
and the tools to help them remember" — the same function [chronic
unease](chronic-unease.md) serves at the individual level.

Reason's four subcultures required for an informed culture (assumptions,
values, and artifacts must line up around all four):

1. **Reporting culture** — what gets reported when people make errors or
   experience near misses. Requires open communication; at Bristol Royal
   Infirmary, an "oral culture" with no meeting minutes and a "club culture"
   around senior surgeons blocked reporting.
2. **[Just culture](just-culture.md)** — how blame gets apportioned when
   something goes wrong. Reason: when the line between acceptable and
   unacceptable behavior is clear, only about 10% of unsafe acts fall into
   the punishable category, leaving 90% reportable without fear; when the
   line is unclear, that 90% gets concealed.
3. **Flexible culture** — how readily people adapt to sudden increases in
   pressure, pacing, and intensity — equivalent to [migrating
   decisions](migrating-decisions.md) and [deference to
   expertise](deference-to-expertise.md).
4. **Learning culture** — how adequately lessons get converted into
   reconfigured assumptions, frameworks, and action.

Bristol Royal Infirmary violated all five HRO principles through its
reporting practices: failures not made salient, deaths simplistically labeled
anomalies, procedures not tight, recoveries missing, no experts deferred to.
The inquiry board described cultures of blame, fear, paternalism ("Professionals
know best, so don't ask questions"), and justification — closed to revision,
failure-averse, focused on uncertainty reduction through justifying current
outcomes.

An informed culture is a content model for [mindfulness in high-reliability
organizing](mindfulness-in-high-reliability-organizing.md) — the specific
approaches, priorities, and practices that make wariness sustainable.
[Safety culture is something an organization does, not
has](safety-culture-is-what-an-organization-does.md): organizations "act
their way into what they become."
