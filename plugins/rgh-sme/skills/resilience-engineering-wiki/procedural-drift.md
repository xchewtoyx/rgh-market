title: Procedural Drift
description: >
  The incremental, locally rewarded departure of actual practice from written
  procedure, driven by overdesigned rules, production goals, and invisible
  safety margins.
sources:
  - title: The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error'
    resource: "The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error' (Dekker), ch. 4"
---

Practical drift (Snook, 2000; Dekker, 2011) is [drift into
failure](drift-into-failure.md) in its procedural form: actual practice
departs incrementally from official procedure. Three drivers combine:

- **Overdesigned rules**: procedures written for a linear, predictable,
  controllable world that operations don't inhabit ([procedures as
  resources for action](procedures-as-resources-for-action.md)), so some
  adaptation is mandatory from day one.
- **Production goals**: each departure buys immediate efficiency under
  [production pressure](goal-conflicts-and-production-pressure.md).
- **Unquantifiable safety margins**: the safety cost of a departure is
  invisible — nothing bad happens — so there is no feedback to arrest the
  slide.

Repeated success without adverse consequences then converts departures into
local "compliant behaviour": [normalisation of
deviance](normalization-of-deviance.md). The written procedure and the
practised procedure part company gradually, and audits against the written
version measure a fiction ([work-as-imagined vs
work-as-done](work-as-imagined-vs-work-as-done.md)).

This is the single-workgroup version of drifted practice. When *several*
separate units each drift from shared procedure in different, mutually
invisible directions, the relevant risk is [structural secrecy and
cross-unit practical drift](structural-secrecy-and-practical-drift.md): the
danger surfaces only when the units' divergent practices are forced to
combine under time pressure.

Implications:

- A large rule/practice gap is a *finding about the rules and the pressures*
  as much as about the practitioners; closing it by enforcement alone pushes
  practice underground and blinds the organisation.
- Because drifted practice is the local norm, post-incident "violation"
  findings are [hindsight](hindsight-bias.md) applied to yesterday's normal
  work.
- Detection requires periodically re-anchoring to original design
  assumptions and studying normal work — the same prescription as for drift
  generally: [chronic unease](chronic-unease.md).
