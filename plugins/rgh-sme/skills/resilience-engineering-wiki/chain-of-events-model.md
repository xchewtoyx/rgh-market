title: Chain-of-Events Accident Model
description: >
  The oldest accident model treats failure as a linear domino sequence
  triggered by a weakest link — usually a human unsafe act — and buys its
  simplicity with severe analytic sacrifices.
sources:
  - title: The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error'

---

The chain-of-events (domino) model originates with H. W. Heinrich in the
1930s, who asserted that 88% of accidents are caused by worker "unsafe acts".
Failure is modelled as a linear sequence of causes; safety management means
finding and removing the weakest link — almost always the human — and adding
linear rules to keep the sequence from starting. Its intuitive appeal traces
to [Newtonian-Cartesian accident
thinking](newtonian-cartesian-accident-thinking.md): the assumption that
systems decompose into parts joined by linear, proportional cause-effect.

The model's analytic sacrifices:

- **Linear, proportional causality**: small causes cannot produce large
  effects, so the model cannot represent how trivial variations combine into
  catastrophe in complex systems.
- **Arbitrary chain start**: the investigator must pick where the chain begins,
  and that choice (usually the last human act before the outcome) is a
  judgment, not a discovery.
- **No simultaneity**: events that interact in parallel cannot be represented
  in a sequence.

Heinrich's companion claim — that minor incidents and fatalities sit in a fixed
ratio, so suppressing the base of the triangle prevents the apex — is
separately refuted (see [the Heinrich triangle
myth](heinrich-triangle-myth.md)).

Chain-of-events thinking survives because it yields cheap, satisfying
conclusions: a root cause, a culprit, a closed case. Its successor,
the [Swiss cheese / barrier model](swiss-cheese-model.md), relaxes the single
chain into layered defences but keeps much of the same logic; fully
non-linear alternatives are the [systems-theoretic
models](systems-theoretic-accident-model.md) and [drift into
failure](drift-into-failure.md). The habit of stopping analysis at a single
originating cause is the [root-cause fallacy](root-cause-fallacy.md).
