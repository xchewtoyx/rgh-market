Behavior-based safety (BBS) programmes rest on 1930s behaviorist psychology:
the mind is a black box; safety is produced by observing worker behaviour and
shaping it with rewards and sanctions. This re-enshrines Heinrich's "88% of
accidents are unsafe acts" premise — the [Old View](old-view-vs-new-view-of-human-error.md)
operationalised — assigning responsibility for risk to front-line personnel
while ignoring the [systemic goal
conflicts](goal-conflicts-and-production-pressure.md) that shape their
behaviour.

The measurable damage is to reporting:

- **Climate of fear**: factory surveys find up to 70% of workers afraid to
  report injuries, anticipating manager inquisitions and peer humiliation.
- **Rate-based incentives**: prize schemes (gift certificates, cash bonuses,
  boat raffles for injury-free periods) create fierce peer pressure against
  reporting — your report costs your crew its bonus.
- **Stigmatisation**: yellow-vest requirements for injured workers,
  mandatory post-incident drug testing — treating the injured as liabilities.
- A 2012 GAO study confirmed that post-incident drug testing and rate-based
  bonus policies directly suppress injury and illness reporting.

The result is the standard [blame-suppresses-reporting
loop](blame-suppresses-reporting.md) with an incentive engine attached: the
numbers improve while the risks persist unrecorded — and since low minor-
incident counts do not predict catastrophe anyway ([Heinrich triangle
myth](heinrich-triangle-myth.md)), the programme optimises a meaningless
metric at the cost of organisational learning. BBS is the flagship product
of the [functionalist view of safety
culture](safety-culture-is-what-an-organization-does.md); its target-setting
sibling is [Vision Zero](vision-zero.md).

The carrot-and-stick premise is also motivationally backwards: operators do
not need external incentives to value their own safety, and campaigns of
posters, stickers, and sanctions *undermine* intrinsic motivation. What
sustains it (Pink, 2009) is autonomy (choice over task execution and local
problem-solving), mastery (skill progression), and purpose (connection of
daily work to mission) — conditions a [line-grounded safety
function](safety-department-staff-trap.md) supports and a behaviourist
programme erodes.
