---
type: concept
title: Choke-Point Detection
description: >
  Actively summing several individually-tolerable risk factors into a
  cumulative judgment of shrinking margin, rather than checking each factor
  separately against its own limit, is a distinct and not automatically
  triggered skill.
sources:
  - title: "Sources of Power: How People Make Decisions"
    resource: "Sources of Power (Klein), ch. 8"
---

A **choke point** is a vulnerability in one's own plan: a small additional
factor that would hurt, the mirror image of a small change that would help.
Spotting one before it becomes a problem buys lead time to act (lining up a
backup supplier on a rumour of a strike, before any actual disruption).

The harder version of this skill is recognising a *compounding* choke point:
several factors that are each individually within normal operating limits —
worn brakes, extra weight, a flickering indicator of uncertain significance,
wet weather, a shorter-than-usual runway — but which together erode the
safety margin far more than any one of them would suggest on its own.
Trained professionals with identical access to the same individual facts can
still differ sharply on whether to treat the *combination* as a reason for
nonroutine action: one person totals the factors into "this is now a
problem," while another checks each factor against its own separate
threshold, finds none individually violated, and defaults to continuing —
the mechanism behind [plan-continuation
bias](plan-continuation-bias.md).

The practical implication is that summing compounding risk factors is not
automatic even for experienced people, so it is worth deliberately
prompting for out loud ("what else is stacked against us right now?") rather
than assuming that everyone with the same information has already done the
addition. This is one concrete form the [studying normal
work](studying-normal-work.md) question "what do you consider dodgy about
the operation right now" can take in the moment, not just retrospectively.
