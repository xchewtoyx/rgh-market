---
type: concept
title: The Authority–Responsibility Mismatch
description: >
  It is unfair and analytically wrong to hold practitioners accountable for
  outcomes they lacked the authority or resources to control — especially
  after the organisation itself demanded the efficiency trade-off.
sources:
  - title: The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error'
    resource: "The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error' (Dekker), ch. 1"
---

Accountability is only meaningful where authority and resources existed to
influence the outcome. Operators constantly perform [efficiency–thoroughness
trade-offs (ETTOs)](efficiency-thoroughness-trade-off.md) (Hollnagel, 2009)
because the organisation demands efficiency; when an accident then prompts
the same organisation to demand
retrospective thoroughness, the demand is hypocritical — it punishes the
trade-off it commissioned (see [goal conflicts and production
pressure](goal-conflicts-and-production-pressure.md)).

**ValuJet 592 (1996)** is the cautionary case. After un-capped oxygen
generators ignited a cargo-hold fire, maintenance mechanics were criminally
prosecuted. The systemic reality: SabreTech work cards used confusing
terminology ("expired" vs "expended"), the organisation never supplied the
protective shipping caps, and the mechanics worked under severe schedule
pressure and language barriers. The prosecutions [stifled
reporting](blame-suppresses-reporting.md) without fixing a single supply-chain
defect.

Investigative implications:

- Before attributing responsibility to a sharp-end actor, establish what they
  actually controlled: information available, time available, resources
  supplied, authority to stop or escalate (the [sharp end inherits what the
  blunt end creates](sharp-end-and-blunt-end.md)).
- A [just culture](just-culture.md) reframes accountability as
  forward-looking — who takes responsibility for fixing the systemic issue —
  and as the practitioner's right to give their account without career
  jeopardy, rather than as retrospective punishment.
