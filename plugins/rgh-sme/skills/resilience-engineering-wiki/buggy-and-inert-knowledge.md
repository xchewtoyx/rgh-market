---
type: concept
title: Buggy and Inert Knowledge
description: >
  Two training-shaped failure patterns — mental models that are wrong (buggy)
  and knowledge that is correct but fails to activate in real operational
  context (inert).
sources:
  - title: The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error'
    resource: "The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error' (Dekker), ch. 4"
---

**Buggy knowledge** is an erroneous or incomplete mental model produced by
flawed training or experience: pilots relying on jet blast from a preceding
aircraft to clear runway snow, or Feltovich's (1989) medical misconceptions
confusing blood flow rate with blood volume. The operator applies the model
correctly; the model is wrong.

**Inert knowledge** is correct theoretical knowledge — demonstrably present,
verified on classroom tests — that fails to be *accessed* in dynamic
real-world contexts, because the context of learning does not match the
context of application. Knowing-that does not become knowing-when.

Both patterns relocate the finding from the person to the training system:

- "They knew better" is ambiguous between the two, and in the inert case is
  literally true yet explanatorily empty — possession of knowledge is not
  availability of knowledge under workload and time pressure.
- The fix is a [blunt-end](sharp-end-and-blunt-end.md) redesign of training
  toward the operational contexts where the knowledge must fire, not
  retraining-as-punishment for the individual (compare the
  person-task-system mismatch view in the [accident-proneness
  myth](accident-proneness-myth.md)).
- In analysis, buggy/inert knowledge is a candidate answer to the [local
  rationality question](local-rationality-principle.md): the action made
  sense inside the mental model the system had installed.
