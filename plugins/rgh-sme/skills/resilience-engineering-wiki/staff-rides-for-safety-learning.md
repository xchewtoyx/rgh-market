**Staff rides** are case studies conducted on site where an event occurred,
championed in wildland fire by the NWCG Leadership Development Program and
Dave Thomas. Participants stand in decision-makers' shoes rather than
conducting tactical fault-finding. Sample questions: "What would I have done
in this person's place?" "How detailed should guidance from a superior be?"
"Can a senior leader use a competent but overzealous subordinate?" "What
explains repeated organizational success or failure?"

The Cerro Grande prescribed burn was itself informed by a staff ride before
the authors' account. Staff rides increasingly use the five HRO principles
as an interpretive lens — a [small win for mindful
organizing](small-wins-for-mindful-organizing.md) that spreads
[preoccupation with failure](preoccupation-with-failure.md),
[reluctance to simplify](reluctance-to-simplify.md), and related practices
through vicarious example rather than abstract policy.

Peer-review near-miss reports (e.g. Balls Canyon, 2006) attempt to bridge
After Action Reviews and formal investigations: a story built around
preoccupation with failure that surfaces faint signals of error before they
escalate. Question sets probe planned intent, situational knowledge seen
versus unseen, actions taken and omitted, and redeployable attention — all
presuming [just culture](just-culture.md) candor is not punished.

This is the safety-science theory behind field-based learning: understanding
why people acted as they did requires reconstructing context on the ground,
not extracting blame from a desk — the same orientation [speaking for the
dead](speaking-for-the-dead.md) demands in investigation.
