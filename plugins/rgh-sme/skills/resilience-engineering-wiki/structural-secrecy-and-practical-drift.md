type: concept
title: Structural Secrecy and Cross-Unit Practical Drift
description: >
  Separate units each drift their local practice away from shared formal
  procedure in mutually invisible ways, and the divergence only becomes
  dangerous when an unplanned tightly-coupled moment forces the
  never-reconciled practices to combine.
sources:
  - title: "Drift into Failure: From Hunting Broken Components to Understanding Complex Systems"
    resource: "Drift into Failure (Dekker), ch. 5"
---

**Structural secrecy** (Vaughan) names how organisational structure itself —
size, geographic spread, specialisation, formalised reporting channels — can
undermine anyone's ability to see what is really happening elsewhere in the
organisation. Bad news stays localised and gets treated as normal by the
people closest to it; more formal communication can perversely make this
worse ("efforts to communicate more can result in knowing less"), because
formal channels strip out exactly the contextual detail that would let a
distant reader recognise a problem.

**Practical drift** (Snook) is what structural secrecy enables at scale: each
local unit develops its own locally efficient routines, adapted to its own
task and legitimised by unremarkable repetition, because centrally dictated
rules rarely fit every unit's actual situation. This differs from
[procedural drift](procedural-drift.md) within a single workgroup in a
specific way: here, *multiple* units each drift from the shared formal
baseline in *different, mutually invisible* directions, with no mechanism
forcing them to notice how far apart they have grown.

The danger is not the divergence itself. In **loosely coupled** situations —
where there is time to reflect, pause, or improvise — divergent local
practices can coexist indefinitely without harm. The danger appears when a
situation suddenly **tightly couples**: time-critical, fixed-sequence, no
room for substitution, and several previously-separate units' drifted
practices must combine correctly on the first try, with no chance to
discover mid-task that their assumptions about each other no longer match.
Structural secrecy and practical drift then reinforce each other going
forward: not knowing what other units actually do enables further local
drift, and the resulting divergence further reduces any unit's reason to
communicate with the others.

The design implication: units that will ever need to combine under time
pressure need a standing mechanism for surfacing and reconciling drifted
local practice *before* the tightly-coupled moment arrives — after it
arrives is too late, because [local rationality](local-rationality-principle.md)
guarantees each unit's own practice looked sound from the inside right up to
the point of collision.
