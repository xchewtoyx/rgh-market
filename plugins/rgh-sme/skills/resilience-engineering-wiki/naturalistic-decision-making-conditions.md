description: >
  The cluster of features — time pressure, high stakes, ill-defined goals,
  missing information, and dynamic change — that define real sharp-end
  decisions and explain why they cannot be judged against laboratory-style
  rational-choice standards.
sources:
  - title: "Sources of Power: How People Make Decisions"
    resource: "Sources of Power (Klein), ch. 1"

Naturalistic decision-making (NDM) research studies experienced people
making high-stakes decisions in the field — firefighters, pilots, nurses,
military commanders — rather than novices solving artificial tasks in a lab.
The settings NDM studies share a specific cluster of features that together
explain why [rational-choice](rational-choice-explanation-of-failure.md)
standards cannot be the yardstick for judging decisions made there:

- **Time pressure**: fireground commanders make roughly 80% of decisions in
  under a minute.
- **High stakes**: a poor decision can cost lives, not just money.
- **Ill-defined goals**: the decision-maker often does not yet know what
  outcome is even being pursued — a commander arriving at a fire may not
  know whether the goal will be extinguishment, containment, or
  search-and-rescue until well into the response.
- **Inadequate or ambiguous information**: missing, unreliable, or
  contradictory data is the norm, not an exceptional failure condition.
- **Poorly defined procedures**: real settings frequently require inventing
  or modifying a procedure on the fly, rather than selecting from a
  pre-specified menu.
- **Dynamic conditions**: the situation itself changes mid-decision — one
  fireground study found the situation changed an average of five times per
  incident, sometimes requiring the commander to re-understand the whole
  scene from scratch.
- **Team coordination**: most high-stakes naturalistic decisions are made by
  a crew, not a lone individual, adding coordination demands on top of the
  decision itself.

This cluster is the concrete, empirical content behind [why it made sense to
act as they did](local-rationality-principle.md): these are the actual
conditions [sharp-end](sharp-end-and-blunt-end.md) practitioners operate
under, which is exactly what a laboratory decision task, or a boardroom
retrospective, strips away. Judging a sharp-end decision against a standard
that assumes complete information, ample time, and a well-defined goal is
judging it against a situation the decision-maker never actually faced.
