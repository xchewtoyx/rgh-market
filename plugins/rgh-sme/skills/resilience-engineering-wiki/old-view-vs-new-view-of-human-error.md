**The Old View (Bad Apple theory)** holds that complex systems are inherently
safe and failures are introduced by unreliable, erratic humans — hence claims
like "human error causes more than two-thirds of accidents". Its theoretical
engine is the [rational-choice / amoral-calculator
explanation](rational-choice-explanation-of-failure.md) of failure. Its
standard countermeasures all fail for identifiable reasons:

1. **Terminating Bad Apples** — signals everyone else to hide mistakes
   ([blame suppresses reporting](blame-suppresses-reporting.md)) while
   leaving the error traps in place.
2. **More rules and procedures** — ignores the inevitable gap between
   [work-as-imagined and work-as-done](work-as-imagined-vs-work-as-done.md)
   and drives real practice underground.
3. **Vigilance slogans and try-harder memos** — assume attitude was the
   problem, displaying ignorance of the actual operational constraints.
4. **Replacing humans with automation** — does not eliminate human work but
   transforms it, creating new cognitive burdens, mode confusion, and novel
   breakdown pathways.

The individual-defect premise behind the Old View was empirically discredited
as early as the 1940s (see [the accident-proneness
myth](accident-proneness-myth.md)).

**The New View (systemic/resilience view)** holds that systems are *not*
inherently safe: safety is continuously created by practitioners negotiating
resource constraints and [goal
conflicts](goal-conflicts-and-production-pressure.md). "Human error" is [a
symptom, not a cause](human-error-as-symptom.md) — a starting point for
investigation, never a conclusion. As James Reason put it, operators are
"the inheritors of system defects created by poor design, incorrect
installation, faulty maintenance and bad management decisions … adding the
final garnish to a lethal brew whose ingredients have already been long in
the cooking."

The diagnostic contrast:

| Old View | New View |
| :-- | :-- |
| Asks *who* is responsible | Asks *what* is responsible |
| Error is the cause | Error is a symptom of deeper trouble |
| Error is random, unreliable behaviour | Error is systematically connected to tools, tasks, context |
| Error is an acceptable conclusion | Error is the starting point |
| States what people failed to do / should have done | Asks [why it made sense](local-rationality-principle.md) to do what they did |

The New View's investigative method is the [local rationality
principle](local-rationality-principle.md); its organisational counterpart is
[Safety-II](safety-i-and-safety-ii.md), which treats the human as a source of
resilience rather than a component to constrain. The systematic case against
managing by error counts is in [why error counting
misleads](why-error-counting-misleads.md).

Whether a given individual or organisation has actually moved from the Old
View to the New View is not a settled, binary fact even where training has
been delivered — see [cultural variation in resilience
uptake](cultural-variation-in-resilience-uptake.md) for empirical evidence
that practitioners routinely hold both views simultaneously and apply
whichever fits the moment.

Red flags that an organisation is trapped in Old View thinking:

- Safety investigations and disciplinary proceedings run by the same
  department or personnel.
- Behaviour-modification programmes, or safety conferences focused on
  personal attitude, motivation, and individual mindfulness (see
  [behaviour-based safety](behavior-based-safety.md)).
- Employees terminated or penalised for safety infractions.
- HR manages safety, treating incident investigations as individual
  performance reviews.
