When time is scarce, Audit 5.9 of the [conscious mindfulness
audits](conscious-mindfulness-audits.md) offers a more economical
alternative to running all eight preceding questionnaires. The **Mindfulness
Organizing Scale (MOS)** is a nine-item 1–3 Likert instrument (sum range
9–27; >17 strong practices, 11–17 moderate, <11 actively improve) probing
whether a unit:

- maps talents and specialized knowledge,
- discusses mistakes, alternative work methods, and activities that must not
  go wrong,
- pools collective expertise when crises emerge, and
- draws on colleagues' unique skills when resolving problems.

Tim Vogus (Vanderbilt) studied medication misadministrations and patient
falls in hospital nursing units and found units scoring higher on the MOS
reported **significantly lower** rates of both — external validity against
real safety outcomes, not merely face validity of the items.

The MOS compresses the five HRO principles into observable team practices:
shared knowledge of who knows what, talk about failure and prevention,
sensitivity to what could go wrong, and rapid expert pooling under pressure —
the same infrastructure [mindfulness in high-reliability
organizing](mindfulness-in-high-reliability-organizing.md) describes at the
conceptual level. Use it for recurring conscious audits; use the full audit
battery when diagnosing which principle is slipping.
