---
type: concept
title: Decrementalism
description: >
  Drift proceeds through steps small enough that each one is beneath the
  threshold of remark, so the slide is structurally invisible to the people
  living through it, not merely psychologically overlooked.
sources:
  - title: "Drift into Failure: From Hunting Broken Components to Understanding Complex Systems"
    resource: "Drift into Failure (Dekker), ch. 2"
---

Decrementalism is the step-size mechanism underneath [normalisation of
deviance](normalization-of-deviance.md): drift does not happen through one
large, noticeable relaxation of a standard but through a long sequence of
small ones, each measured against the immediately preceding step rather than
the original standard.

A worked example makes the arithmetic concrete: an aircraft-part inspection
interval stretched from 300 hours to 2,550 hours over three decades, never in
one jump, always in increments of a few hundred hours tied to unrelated
maintenance-schedule reorganisations. No single increment looked like a
meaningful change from the one before it — the gap between yesterday's
accepted practice and today's was too small to be worth remarking on or
reporting. Yet the cumulative effect changed the system's sensitivity to a
single missed step by almost an order of magnitude: miss one lubrication on
a 350-hour cycle and there is no problem; miss one on a 2,550-hour cycle and
the consequence is severe. The vulnerability was manufactured by the
*organisation of the interval itself*, not by any single actor's carelessness.

The consequence for detection is structural, not motivational: decrementalism
means comparing today's practice to yesterday's practice will never surface
drift, because every consecutive pair looks fine. Detecting it requires
comparing current practice against the *original* assumption or design
margin — the same discipline [chronic unease](chronic-unease.md) and
[studying normal work](studying-normal-work.md) prescribe for other reasons.

Decrementalism's small step size is also its intervention opportunity: small
steps happen frequently, and asking "why did we just make that decision, and
what might it mean elsewhere?" about a small, not-yet-owned step provokes
far less defensiveness than challenging a large decision the organisation
has already committed to — and reversing a small step is comparatively
cheap. Deliberately building that question into the normal rhythm of
operational review is a workable lever precisely because decrementalism
supplies so many opportunities to ask it.

A system-dynamics reading of the same phenomenon names the underlying
structural cause: [feedback delay masks accumulating
risk](feedback-delay-masks-accumulating-risk.md) — the gap between a
risk-increasing step and its visible consequence is what lets each step be
compared only to the one before it rather than to the accumulating total.
