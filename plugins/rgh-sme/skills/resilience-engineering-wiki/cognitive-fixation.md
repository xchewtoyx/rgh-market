title: Cognitive Fixation and Thematic Vagabonding

  Under dynamic fault management, operators either settle on one plausible
  hypothesis that shields itself from contrary evidence or jump between
  explanations with every new cue — and only hindsight says which was wrong.
sources:

Sensemaking in dynamic worlds is a continuous loop (Neisser, 1976):
assessment directs action, action changes the world and generates new cues,
cues revise the assessment. Committing to an action simplifies diagnosis by
committing the operator to a hypothesis — which then generates
self-justifying explanations for subsequent cues.

Two failure shapes fall out of this loop under uncertainty:

- **Cognitive fixation**: settling on a plausible explanation from initial
  ambiguous data; the activated diagnostic knowledge then shields the
  hypothesis from contradictory evidence.
- **Thematic vagabonding**: jumping erratically between explanations driven
  by the latest alarm or cue, never forming a coherent mental model.

These are the two ends of a trade-off, not two errors: only
[hindsight](hindsight-bias.md) reveals whether the operator "should" have
held a stable hypothesis or abandoned it. Both occur under **dynamic fault
management** — the intense demand of keeping the process going (flying the
aircraft, keeping the patient stable) while simultaneously diagnosing an
unexpected anomaly.

The Azores Air Transat case (2001) shows fixation as a product of the
evidence, not of carelessness: an A330 lost both engines mid-Atlantic to a
fuel-hose leak, after the crew fixated on a computer-gauge-anomaly
explanation. Every available cue supported the fixation — no automated leak
caution fired, fuel-flow readouts looked normal, cabin crew saw no leak in
the darkness, and oil-cooling warnings added confounding noise. Explaining
the fixation from these cues is the [local
rationality](local-rationality-principle.md) analysis; calling it
[complacency or loss of situation
awareness](complacency-and-loss-of-situation-awareness.md) is its refusal.

A related fixation on the *plan* rather than the *diagnosis* is
[plan-continuation bias](plan-continuation-bias.md).
