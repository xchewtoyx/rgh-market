---
type: concept
title: The Fallacy of the Quick Fix
description: >
  The four standard post-incident fixes — reprimand, retrain, new procedure,
  more automation — treat systemic vulnerabilities as isolated defects and
  leave the conditions for the next event intact.
sources:
  - title: The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error'
    resource: "The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error' (Dekker), ch. 8"
---

Four fixes recur after almost every incident, and all four are false:

1. **Reprimanding operators** — destroys trust and [stops organisational
   learning](blame-suppresses-reporting.md). Dekker: "Reprimanding Bad
   Apples is like peeing in your pants: it feels warm for a moment, but soon
   gets cold, uncomfortable, and makes you look like a fool."
2. **Retraining individuals** — recasts a systemic vulnerability as one
   person's skill deficit, though every other operator faces the identical
   trap (and the deficit is often [buggy or inert knowledge the training
   system itself produced](buggy-and-inert-knowledge.md)).
3. **Writing new procedures** — plugs one isolated hole while increasing
   procedural complexity, reducing transparency, and breeding further
   non-compliance ([safety clutter](safety-clutter.md) feeding [procedural
   drift](procedural-drift.md)).
4. **Adding automation/technology** — introduces new cognitive demands, mode
   error traps, and unforeseen failure pathways rather than removing the
   human contribution.

Each quick fix targets an explanatory surface detail; none touches a
structural lever (see [explanatory vs change
factors](explanatory-vs-change-factors.md)). Their shared appeal is that
they are cheap, fast, visible, and spare the [blunt
end](sharp-end-and-blunt-end.md) — which is exactly why they signal an
investigation that stopped at [error-as-cause](human-error-as-symptom.md).
A related cheap layer, adding a second human check, fails for its own
reasons ([social redundancy fallacy](social-redundancy-fallacy.md)).

The hard systemic fixes that actually move safety:

- Rebalance production-versus-safety [goal
  trade-offs](goal-conflicts-and-production-pressure.md).
- Reverse [practical drift](drift-into-failure.md) before margins erode
  completely.
- Align economic incentive structures with safety objectives.
- Get leadership engaged with sharp-end operational realities ([studying
  normal work](studying-normal-work.md)).
- Recalibrate the organisation's risk models to match its actual complexity
  ([your accident model shapes what you
  find](accident-model-shapes-what-you-find.md)).
- Match operator responsibility with requisite authority ([the
  authority–responsibility
  mismatch](authority-responsibility-mismatch.md)).
