title: Your Accident Model Shapes What You Find
description: >
  The accident model an investigator holds determines what counts as a cause,
  what evidence is gathered, and which countermeasures look reasonable — so the
  model must be chosen consciously, not inherited by default.
sources:
  - title: The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error'
    resource: "The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error' (Dekker), ch. 5"
---

An accident model is the (often implicit) theory of how failures happen that an
investigator or organisation brings to an event. It is not neutral scaffolding:
it defines what risk *is*, what the major threat to safety is, and therefore
what the fix looks like. Investigators find what the model tells them to look
for.

Four model families, compared on those axes:

| Model | Risk is… | Threat is… | Fix is… |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| [Chain of events](chain-of-events-model.md) | the weakest link in a linear sequence | unreliable human operators | remove/replace the weak link; add rules |
| [Barriers / Swiss cheese](swiss-cheese-model.md) | a trajectory of uncontained hazard | weak or porous defence layers | plug holes; add redundant layers |
| [Systems theory](systems-theoretic-accident-model.md) | loss of control over component interactions | complexity, opacity, [goal conflicts](goal-conflicts-and-production-pressure.md) | enforce safety constraints; make trade-offs visible |
| [Drift into failure](drift-into-failure.md) | gradual erosion of safety margins | success breeding overconfidence and [normalised deviance](normalization-of-deviance.md) | cultivate [chronic unease](chronic-unease.md); study normal work |

The practical consequence: an organisation that never examines its accident
model will keep producing the same class of countermeasure (typically
"remove the human weak link") regardless of what actually happened. Choosing a
richer model is the prerequisite for the [new view of human
error](old-view-vs-new-view-of-human-error.md), and for asking why it made
sense for people to do what they did rather than who slipped.

The general version of this claim has a name: **What-You-Look-For-Is-
What-You-Find** (WYLFIWYF), with the corollary **What-You-Find-Is-
What-You-Learn** (WYFIWYL) — an organisation can only learn about the
dynamics it actively went looking for, so the model chosen at the start of
an investigation constrains not just its findings but everything the
organisation subsequently learns from it (see [three conditions for
effective learning](three-conditions-for-effective-learning.md)).
