title: Training Managerial Resilience Skills
description: >
  Managerial resilience's non-technical skills can be formally trained by
  importing practices from other high-risk operational domains, but training
  alone fails without an organisational culture that rewards the upward
  challenge it teaches.
sources:
  - title: "Resilience Engineering: Concepts and Precepts"
    resource: "Resilience Engineering: Concepts and Precepts (Hollnagel, Woods, Leveson), ch. 14"

If [diagnosis, decision-making, and assertiveness](middle-managers-as-safety-buffer.md)
are the three specific non-technical skills managerial resilience requires,
they turn out to be trainable competencies imported wholesale from other
high-risk operational domains, not innate management aptitude:

- **Incident command training** — adapting the dynamic decision-making and
  real-time risk-assessment techniques emergency services use for commanders
  operating under genuine time pressure and incomplete information, applied
  to managers who otherwise only practise decision-making in the far less
  time-compressed rhythm of ordinary business operation.
- **Crew Resource Management (CRM) and assertiveness training** — developed
  in aviation specifically after fatal accidents in which a co-pilot
  recognised the danger but failed to challenge the captain effectively.
  CRM explicitly teaches risk sensitivity, dynamic decision-making, and
  structured upward assertiveness: how to challenge a captain — or a boss —
  without being either passive (staying silent) or aggressive (triggering
  defensive shutdown), sometimes formalised as reframing a subordinate
  role as "monitoring and challenging" rather than merely executing. CRM
  (now mandatory for all pilots) originated after Tenerife, the 1982 Air
  Florida Potomac crash, and similar cases where copilots recognised danger
  but could not effectively challenge captains; it instructs crews to speak
  up and captains to listen. Flight 1549's captain had long trained pilots
  in CRM at US Airways — the recorded exchange is often cited as CRM and
  [threat and error management (TEM)](threat-and-error-management.md)
  working as intended under genuine surprise.

**Training alone is not sufficient.** Both methods demonstrate the same
dependency: upward-assertiveness training only changes behaviour where the
surrounding organisation actually accepts and rewards being challenged from
below. A manager trained to assert and a subordinate trained to challenge
still need [a safety culture](sacrifice-judgements.md) in which senior
figures do not punish the challenge, and [psychological
safety](chronic-unease.md) in which raising a safety concern or halting
production carries no retaliation or career cost — without that enabling
context, the trained skill has nowhere safe to be exercised, and reverts
under real pressure to whatever the untrained default was. This is the same
structural dependency [middle managers as a safety
buffer](middle-managers-as-safety-buffer.md) documents at the level of
individual disasters: the *Piper Alpha* Claymore case shows a supervisor who
had the assertiveness to ask six times and an organisation that simply
declined to be moved by it.
