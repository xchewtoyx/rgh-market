Reluctance to simplify is the second [high-reliability
organization](high-reliability-organizations.md) anticipation principle.
Organizing necessarily simplifies — Hari Tsoukas: formal organization
"subsumes heterogeneous particulars under generic categories" — but early
warning signs live in the details that disappear once lumped into generic
categories. HROs simplify slowly, reluctantly, and mindfully.

At Cerro Grande, burn boss Mike Powell used the wrong complexity scale (a
1-2-3 scale posted on the internet instead of the National Park Service's
1-3-5 scale), rating the burn low-to-moderate when the correct scale would
have rated it moderate-to-high — affecting both his qualification to direct
the burn and the resource levels required. Ratings were completed weeks
before the burn and never reevaluated on burn day. Planners also
underestimated intensity in at least one of three complexity categories.
People are prone to interpret new data in ways confirming existing
expectations — "it is hard to spot signs that burning is unwise when twenty
people are standing around ready to start."

**Label hazards.** NASA's "in-family" versus "out-of-family" distinction
shows how reporting words determine meaning: "reportable problems" must be
"previously experienced," so labels limit what people see and report.
Benjamin Whorf's "empty" drum: an "empty" gasoline drum is filled with
vapor and more explosive than a full one — the word masks the danger. ValuJet
592 crashed when "empty" oxygen generators in the cargo hold ignited.

**Resisting simplification.** Nuclear workers walk down entire systems
before shutdown rather than trusting outdated drawings. At Diablo Canyon,
department members meet to question each other's interpretations when facing
failure. Teams with diverse expertise and generalist experience recombine
knowledge into novel combinations better than homogeneous teams.

Reuben Baron and Stephen Misovich's **shareability constraint**: individual
sensemaking starts through direct acquaintance; naming impressions for
coordination shifts to knowledge by description and loses distinctive
details. HROs should be reluctant not only to simplify but, to a degree,
reluctant to coordinate — since early detection depends on names imposed on
experience. Gerardo Patriotta: our universe is "preinterpreted" — we see what
our stock of interpretations allows.

**Carrying categories lightly** means both believing and doubting a
category: differentiate into subcategories, treat categories as blind spots,
question supporting assumptions, and examine poorly fitting examples. Less
[mindful practice normalizes; more mindful practice
anomalizes](anomalizing-vs-normalizing.md) — preserving unique features that
slow normalization.

When an event is "recognized" as similar to a past one, that recognition
should trigger concern, not comfort — superficial similarity can mask deeper,
potentially fatal differences. Columbia's foam strike was classified "almost
in-family" and treated as largely understood.

**Small-win examples.** After Cerro Grande, Karl Brauneis restored sequence
to the Ten Standard Fire Orders — engage 1 through 10, disengage 10 back to
1 — converting a simplified assortment into a more nuanced ordered process
([small wins for mindful
organizing](small-wins-for-mindful-organizing.md)). Bandelier's
post-Cerro Grande interagency network reduced agency silos and increased
requisite variety; the network as a whole, not any single organization,
maintains the big picture during large prescribed burns — the same strategy
Diablo Canyon used to mitigate adverse events.
