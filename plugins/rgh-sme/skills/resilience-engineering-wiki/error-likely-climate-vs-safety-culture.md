title: Error-Likely Climate vs Safety Culture
description: >
  Reason's distinction between organisational culture (slow-moving,
  organisation-wide) and local workplace climate (fast-changing, shaped by
  immediate leadership and conditions) — a site can sit inside a strong
  overall safety culture while its local climate is acutely error-likely.
sources:
  - title: "Resilience Engineering in Practice"
    resource: "Resilience Engineering in Practice (Hollnagel, Pariès, Wreathall), ch. 7"
---

Reason (2009) separates two things that get conflated under "safety
culture": **culture** is upstream and slow-moving — the organisation-wide
pattern [safety culture is something an organisation
does](safety-culture-is-what-an-organization-does.md) describes, built up
and changed only through sustained daily practice across the whole
organisation. **Climate** is local and fast-changing — shaped by whichever
specific management, resources, and workforce happen to be assembled on a
given site or shift right now, and capable of shifting far faster than the
culture surrounding it.

The practical consequence is that a healthy overall safety culture is not a
guarantee against a locally toxic climate — a single outage crew can be
operating inside an acutely **error-likely climate** even while the parent
organisation's culture, measured any other way, looks generative. A
turbine-outage field-service context identified eight recurring high-risk
climate patterns worth naming as a checklist in their own right, because
each is locally detectable and locally fixable independent of any
organisation-wide culture initiative: top-down, intimidating leadership
style; leadership closed to listening to or answering worker questions;
leadership disengaged and physically absent from the work; unclear site
roles and responsibilities; destructive shift-versus-shift competition;
lack of crew teamwork; a customer or client overly intrusive into technical
scope; and leaders unfamiliar with current practice or resistant to outside
help.

The design implication follows directly: because climate is local and fast,
detecting it requires a mechanism that operates at the same grain and speed
— frontline workers empowered to report an error-likely climate as it forms
(anonymously if needed) or to request an embedded specialist, rather than
waiting for a slower, organisation-wide culture survey to eventually pick it
up. This is the same organisational-design lesson [pinging](pinging-proactive-risk-probing.md)
demonstrates from the detection side: a signal that is local and fast-moving
needs a detector that is local and fast-moving too.
