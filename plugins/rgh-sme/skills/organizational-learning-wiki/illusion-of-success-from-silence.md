---
type: concept
title: Illusion of Success from Silence
description: >
  When fear blocks bad news from traveling upward, organizations can appear
  successful for years while strategic failure accumulates unseen.
sources:
  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 3"
---

Low [psychological safety](psychological-safety.md) can produce an illusion
of success. Targets are met, metrics look healthy, and senior leaders remain
"blissfully unaware" while front-line signals of trouble are suppressed,
ignored, or punished.

Volkswagen's diesel fraud held for roughly a decade while US sales targets
were hit early. Wells Fargo opened millions of unauthorized accounts while
cross-sell metrics soared. Nokia's middle managers fed executives an
overly optimistic picture of feature-phone capability while the smartphone
shift accelerated. Federal Reserve Bank of New York regulators deferred to
banks they were meant to supervise.

Each organization had deep expertise and articulate goals. What was missing
was a climate where people could speak truth to power — pushing back, sharing
data, and reporting what was really happening in engineering labs or sales
fields so strategy could adapt.

Leaders who welcome only good news create fear that blocks them from hearing
the truth. Early information about shortcomings can nearly always mitigate the
size and impact of later failures — and sometimes prevent them altogether.
Connects to [dangerous silence](dangerous-silence.md) when the withheld
information concerns physical harm, and to [strategy as hypothesis](strategy-as-hypothesis.md)
when missed targets should trigger strategic revision rather than harsher
enforcement.
