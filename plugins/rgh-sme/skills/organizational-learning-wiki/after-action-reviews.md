**After Action Reviews (AARs)**, adapted from U.S. Army practice, connect
[integrating learning and work](integrating-learning-and-work.md) in the simplest
form with three questions:

- What happened?
- What did we expect?
- What can we learn from the gap?

Army framing: moving "from a culture of reports to a culture of review."
Management commitment is essential for AARs to take root.

After the August 2003 blackout, DTE Energy ran AARs within 24 hours; staff
spontaneously took "AAR Observations" notes mid-crisis without prompting. Four
embedding strategies:

1. Leadership by request and example — value deep learning over one-time events.
2. Treat day-to-day events and crises alike as learning opportunities.
3. Grassroots exposure — introduce as a safe learning space; don't mandate
   perfection.
4. A cadre of trained facilitators steering teams toward high-yield applications.

Guiding question for local ownership: "If you could improve performance in one
area that would make a significant difference for the enterprise, what would
that be?" AAR philosophy makes the work team its own first, best customer for
learning.

AARs appear in [learning infrastructure beyond training](learning-infrastructure-beyond-training.md)
as one of four Army infrastructure components (alongside training, practice
simulations, research, and doctrine) and complement [tacit knowledge through storytelling](tacit-knowledge-through-storytelling.md)
as a review ritual that moves know-how socially rather than through databases
alone.
