description: >
  Three failure types — preventable, complex, and intelligent — each requiring
  different leadership framing and response.

How leaders present failure shapes whether people report it. Edmondson's
typology distinguishes three types:

**Preventable failures** — deviations from known processes producing unwanted
outcomes ("process deviation"). Common in production lines and basic utilities.
Never good news; often tied to behavior, skill, or attention gaps. Leaders
celebrate "good catch" reporting that prevents them.

**Complex failures** — novel combinations in familiar contexts ("system
breakdown"). Common in hospital care, NASA programs, nuclear plants. Vigilance
can sometimes prevent them; analysis from diverse perspectives is essential.

**Intelligent failures** — novel forays into new territory ("unsuccessful
trial"). Common in drug development and new product design. Must be celebrated
to encourage thoughtful experimentation.

An important [framing task](setting-the-stage-for-candor.md): all three will
happen; regardless of type, the primary goal is to learn. Managers surveyed
worldwide estimate only 1–4% of failures *should* be blameworthy, yet 70–90%
are *treated* as blameworthy — driving
[epidemic silence](epidemic-of-workplace-silence.md).

Productive responses differ by type: training and system redesign for
preventable; cross-disciplinary analysis for complex;
[failure parties](failure-parties.md) and next experiments for intelligent.
