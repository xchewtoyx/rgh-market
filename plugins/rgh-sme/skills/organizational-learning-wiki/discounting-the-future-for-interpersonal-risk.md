---
type: concept
title: Discounting the Future for Interpersonal Risk
description: >
  A bias toward immediate interpersonal safety that underweights delayed but
  important consequences of staying silent.
sources:
  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 1"
---

When someone hesitates and then stays silent, they often
[discount the future](interpersonal-risk-taking-at-work.md): a delayed,
important issue (patient safety, a failed acquisition, a process flaw) loses
against an immediate, salient one (a boss's possible negative reaction).

Edmondson likens this to procrastination or eating an extra slice of cake — a
general human bias, not a character flaw. Research finds 85% of respondents
reported at least one occasion of feeling unable to raise an important concern
with their boss.

Silence feels rational in the moment even when the speaker later regrets it —
as when a CFO stayed quiet amid colleagues' enthusiasm for an acquisition that
later failed, afraid of being "the skunk at the picnic."
