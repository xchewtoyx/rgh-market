type: concept
title: Working Across Difference
description: >
  Embracing diversity as a strategic necessity for learning — not political
  correctness — because real community requires being stuck with one another.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 14"
---

The shadow side of [learning communities](learning-communities.md) is clique or
cult — gravitating toward the like-minded and excluding others. Embracing
diversity is framed as a **strategic necessity**, not political correctness or
sentiment.

Margaret Wheatley on early Internet communities: they function as "anticommunities"
because "zero cost of exit" (disconnecting when disagreement arises) means people
mostly just agree with each other — "real community is something that can only
happen when we are stuck with one another."

**Roca** (Chelsea, MA youth service): core strategy is "building bridges across
boundaries" via networks extending from at-risk kids to police, courts, schools,
and social services. Molly Baldwin: seeing the system from other stakeholders'
viewpoints revealed that her own protective prejudice toward kids was partly
self-protective, and helped others examine their biases too.

**Unilever/Oxfam** cross-sector case: a joint study of poverty-reduction efforts
in Indonesia despite reputational risk to both sides. Andre van Heemstra: internal
diversity efforts must be matched by external diversity partnerships as "a key
lever for strategic change." Barbara Stocking: campaigning alone is insufficient
for systemic problems beyond one company's control.

Ilean Galloway on the "next stage of diversity": increasingly networked, global
work means collaborating routinely with very different people. With business-case
groundwork for diversity already laid in many organizations, the real challenge
shifts to individual employees' choices of who they work with — categorical
box-checking approaches miss the more personal, developmental work of learning
to understand, communicate, and relate across difference.

Pairs with [balancing inquiry and advocacy](balancing-inquiry-and-advocacy.md)
and [mental models as organizational routine](mental-models-as-organizational-routine.md)
(surfacing assumptions held by different stakeholders).
