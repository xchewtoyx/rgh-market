title: Balancing Feedback
description: >
  Goal-oriented self-correction that maintains a target — the source of stability
  and the hidden resistance that frustrates organizational change efforts.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 5"
---

**Balancing (stabilizing) feedback** operates wherever there is goal-oriented
behavior — the system self-corrects to maintain a goal or target, explicit (a
firm's desired market share) or implicit (an unspoken norm, or a bad habit
maintained despite disavowing it). Examples: filling a glass of water, steering
a car, hiring to a target headcount.

Balancing processes are hard to manage because the goal is often **implicit and
unrecognized** — resistance looks like it "comes from nowhere." Senge's second
law of [systems thinking](laws-of-systems-thinking.md): "the harder you push,
the harder the system pushes back" — **compensating feedback** where well-
intentioned interventions call forth offsetting responses. A manager cuts
staff under budget pressure, but costs don't fall because the implicit goal
(amount of work expected) reasserts via overtime or consultants. A business owner
trying to reduce burnout via memos and shortened hours fails because an unwritten
norm ("real heroes worked seventy hours a week") was established by the owner's
own long-hours behavior.

Balancing loops are harder to *see* than [reinforcing feedback](reinforcing-feedback.md)
because they maintain the status quo rather than producing dramatic change —
"it often looks like nothing is happening." Lewis Carroll's Queen of Hearts:
"all the running you can do to keep in the same place" signals a balancing loop
nearby. Leaders attempting change often fight an unrecognized balancing process;
until the implicit goal is identified, "the change effort is doomed to failure."

**Resistance to change is neither capricious nor mysterious** — it almost always
stems from threats to entrenched norms tied to established power relationships.
Artful leaders look for the source of resistance rather than pushing harder.
Pairs with [limits to growth archetype](limits-to-growth-archetype.md) (balancing
loop limiting reinforcing success) and [shifting the burden archetype](shifting-the-burden-archetype.md).
