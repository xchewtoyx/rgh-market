title: Practice Fields for Management Teams
description: >
  Virtual worlds where management teams can experiment, replay decisions, and
  develop joint learning skills — the rehearsal space most teams lack.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 11"
---

A group of talented individual learners will not necessarily produce a learning
team — any more than talented athletes automatically form a great sports team.
[Team learning](team-learning.md) requires **practice fields**: constructed
representations of real-world complexity where experimentation is safe.

Donald Schon's reflective-practitioner framing treats practice as experimentation
in a virtual world — an architect's sketchpad where moves reveal that "building
shapes do not fit the slope" without irreversible cost. Sports teams and
orchestras rehearse: slow motion, isolated sections, repeated replay.
Advertising creative teams brainstorm and test in storyboards before client
presentations.

Management teams, by contrast, have abstract intellectual debates. Members
learn each other's opinions but lack storyboards or rehearsal — decisions on
specific situations are made under time pressure, each final as soon as made,
with no experimentation and no stepping back to reflect on how to decide better
together. The near-total absence of meaningful practice is probably the
predominant factor keeping management teams ineffective as learning units —
why [delusion of learning from experience](delusion-of-learning-from-experience.md)
persists for the most important decisions.

Basic conditions for a dialogue-practice session:

1. All members who need one another to act are present together.
2. Ground rules of [dialogue](dialogue-versus-discussion.md) are explained.
3. Ground rules are enforced — if anyone cannot [suspend assumptions](suspending-assumptions.md),
   the team names that it is now discussing, not dialoguing.
4. The team raises the most difficult, subtle, conflictual issues essential to
   its work.

Practice fields pair naturally with [systems thinking as learning language](systems-thinking-as-learning-language.md):
teams learn archetype vocabulary through use together, not lecture alone.

Senge's chapter 14 reinforces a recurring principle: **no practice, no learning**
— no sports team or orchestra would perform without rehearsing, yet organizations
routinely skip this for management work. Classrooms (passive, right-answer-oriented)
are poor practice environments; [practice fields](practice-fields-for-management-teams.md)
or rehearsal halls let people actively try, fail, and retry before "performance
fields" where results matter. Microworlds and simulations (e.g., Ford develops
these) are an evolving, more sophisticated form.

Roger Saillant distinguishes **"the camp"** from **"the hierarchy"**: the formal
management system (measures, roles, accountabilities, goals) versus time to talk
and connect more deeply, including difficult issues and emotions. A Northern
Ireland plant split along Catholic/Protestant lines used camp meetings to surface
what people valued beneath the conflict — mostly their children's futures.

Harley-Davidson separates the "management system" from **"the swirl"** — ongoing
organization-wide debate, experimentation, and testing of ideas. An idea crossing
**"the Wall of Fire"** becomes an organization-wide commitment; many ideas stay
in the swirl for years even with senior champions. Pushing for official top-down
adoption risks superficial buy-in without a way to legitimate exploration of
radical ideas — sometimes better to let them stay in the swirl.
