  Recurring structural patterns — built from reinforcing loops, balancing
  loops, and delays — that give teams a shared language for diagnosing
  organizational learning failures.

**Systems archetypes** are generic structures that recur across management
situations — "basic sentences" built from [reinforcing feedback](reinforcing-feedback.md),
[balancing feedback](balancing-feedback.md), and [delays in systems](delays-in-systems.md).
If loops and delays are the nouns and verbs of systems thinking, archetypes are
the plot lines recast with different characters.

Recognizing archetypes reveals leverage in difficult challenges and lets
experienced managers articulate patterns they already sense intuitively
("There it is again"). Mastery is necessary — though not sufficient — for
systems thinking to become a daily practice rather than a one-off diagramming
exercise.

Archetypes appear across biology, psychology, economics, and management.
Used as an [organizational learning language](systems-thinking-as-learning-language.md),
they depersonalize conflict: conversation shifts from "who failed" to "what
structure is operating," enabling inquiry without innuendo of incompetence.

Nine archetypes appear in Senge's treatment; foundational pairs include
[limits to growth](limits-to-growth-archetype.md) and
[shifting the burden](shifting-the-burden-archetype.md), which combine in
[growth and underinvestment](growth-and-underinvestment-archetype.md).

Connects to [mental models as organizational routine](mental-models-as-organizational-routine.md):
entrenched assumptions thwart structural insight unless surfaced; archetypes
help correct systematic flaws in how teams read cause and effect.
