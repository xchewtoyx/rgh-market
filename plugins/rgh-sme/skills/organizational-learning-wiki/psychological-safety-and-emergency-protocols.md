title: Psychological Safety and Emergency Protocols
description: >
  Protocols and training only work when interpersonal fear does not crowd out
  speaking up — psychological safety is a precondition, not a substitute.
sources:
  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 6"
---

US Airways Flight 1549 illustrates fearless communication as a decision-making
input under extreme time pressure. Captain Sullenberger's immediate "My aircraft"
handoff, the first officer's trained confirmation, air traffic control's reflex
to verify intentions, and disciplined essential-only questioning each depended
on a climate where crew members could speak, listen, and act without
[interpersonal risk](interpersonal-risk-taking-at-work.md) blocking protocol use.

Edmondson cautions against dismissing the case as "just" protocol execution:
"the existence of procedures does not ensure their use." Without psychological
safety, [micro-assessments of interpersonal risk](micro-assessment-of-interpersonal-risk.md)
crowd out proper responses and people fail to recognize the implications of
hesitation in moments they could have spoken up.

**Cockpit Resource Management (CRM)** — now mandatory for pilots — originated
after Tenerife and other tragedies where co-pilots could not insist captains
change course. CRM instructs crews to speak up when something seems wrong and
captains to listen. It has spread to medicine for the same reason.

Psychological safety does not imply excessive talking. For critical seconds
Sullenberger and Skiles worked silently, concentrating on tasks and visual
cues — contrast with low-safety management meetings where indirect argument
and postponed decisions consume far more time.

The lesson generalizes beyond emergencies: candor under pressure is a
deliberate leadership and training outcome, not a natural act in hierarchies.
