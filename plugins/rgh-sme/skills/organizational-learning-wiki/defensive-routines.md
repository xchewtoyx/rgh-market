---
type: concept
title: Defensive Routines
description: >
  Entrenched habits that protect people from embarrassment of exposing their
  thinking — blocking learning while feeling like protection of views or relations.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 11"
---

Chris Argyris: "We are programmed to create defensive routines... and cover
them up with further defensive routines." Defensive routines protect from the
embarrassment of exposing the thinking behind our views — not, as we tell
ourselves, protection of our views or social relations.

Common forms: saying "that's a very interesting idea" with no intention of
taking it seriously; confronting someone to squash an idea; sheltering someone
from criticism to avoid difficult issues; changing the subject under guise of
good manners.

Many cultures treat "not knowing" as weakness, so managers become skilled at
*appearing* to know. Argyris: "We are the carriers of defensive routines, and
organizations are the hosts."

Defensive routines are **self-sealing** — they obscure their own existence.
Confronting someone as "defensive" backfires. Effective approach: self-disclosure
plus inquiry — "I notice I am feeling threatened by this proposal. Could you
help me see where this uneasiness is coming from?"

In a shifting-the-burden structure, defensive routines are the symptomatic
solution that prevents the fundamental solution of mutual inquiry — producing
[skilled incompetence](skilled-incompetence.md). With shared vision and
commitment to truth about current reality, defensive routines can signal that
learning is not occurring.
