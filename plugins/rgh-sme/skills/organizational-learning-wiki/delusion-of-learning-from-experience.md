---
type: concept
title: Delusion of Learning from Experience
description: >
  A learning disability where the most important organizational decisions have
  consequences beyond anyone's direct experience or learning horizon.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 2"
---

Direct experience is powerful for learning crawling and walking — but breaks
down when consequences of an action fall outside a person's "learning
horizon" in time and space.

"We learn best from experience but we never directly experience the consequences
of many of our most important decisions." R&D choices shape marketing and
manufacturing for years; leadership promotions shape climate for years — with
the least opportunity for trial-and-error learning.

Breaking organizations into functional divisions was meant to manage scope but
creates stovepipes that cut off cross-functional contact — making analysis of
the most important problems perilous or impossible.

One of seven [learning disabilities](seven-learning-disabilities.md); why
practice fields and [team learning](team-learning.md) matter for rehearsal
management teams usually lack.
