type: concept
title: Deep Learning Cycle
description: >
  Five interlinked elements — beliefs, practices, skills, relationships, and
  awareness — that jointly develop organizational capacity rather than one-off results.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 14"
---

Senge distinguishes **surface learning** (producing a result once) from **deep
learning** (developing the capacity to reliably produce a quality of results —
"becoming a bicycle rider," not just riding once).

The **deep learning cycle** has five elements that continuously reinforce one
another; intervention can start at any point:

1. Beliefs and assumptions
2. Established practices
3. Skills and capabilities
4. Networks of relationships
5. Awareness and sensibilities

Beliefs shape practices, which shape skills; practices and skills shape
relationships and awareness; experience ("seeing is believing") reinforces
beliefs. Culture is not static — daily interaction continually reinforces all
five, and they can change together, albeit slowly.

Coherent strategies for intervening in the cycle combine three elements:

- **Guiding ideas** — purpose, vision, values.
- **Theory, tools, and methods** — explicit models of how things work plus
  practical means to apply them (Buckminster Fuller: you cannot change how
  someone thinks directly, but a tool whose use leads them to think differently).
- **Innovations in organizational infrastructure** — formal roles and management
  structures that shape how energy and resources flow.

Grounded in structuration theory: structure influences behavior (leverage lies
in underlying structure, not events), and structures arise from cumulative
participant action — "we shape our structures and then they shape us." The
Boston cow-paths question for organizations: do people repeat unexamined patterns
like cows, or step back to see and choose deeper patterns?

(infrastructure aligns resources with the cycle) and [mental models as organizational routine](mental-models-as-organizational-routine.md)
