title: Grassroots Learning Initiatives
description: >
  Starting organizational learning where you are with whoever is present — without
  waiting for top-management sponsorship to drive every needed change.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 14"
---

Counters the belief that nothing can happen without top-management sponsorship.
Ilean Galloway: waiting for top managers to drive every needed change means
waiting a long time. She drew on U.S. civil rights and feminist movements
(grassroots, not official-led) and publisher John H. Johnson — "There is no
defense against an excellence that meets a pressing public need."

**The "impossibles"**: Galloway deliberately targets problems the organization
has given up on. "The fact that I can't get support tells me it is the right
project." Cites Einstein: "the consciousness that created the problem cannot be
the one that solves it." Approach: probe what the problem reveals, enroll willing
collaborators incrementally. Example: an Intel consultant partnered with a senior
engineer to cut mean time-to-solve for technical problems from months to four
weeks using rapid learning cycles of testing and reflection.

Roger Saillant at Ford's Chihuahua plant (1986) found local workers cared about
their community but had never had real responsibility. He dismissed a senior-
technician group that tried to install their own appointee against his open
promotion system. After a storm before a Ford HQ visit, an hourly worker promoted
under the new system was at 2am catching roof leaks — "It was now their plant."
The plant launched six weeks early and became the top plant of its type worldwide.

Grassroots success still requires [becoming bicultural](becoming-bicultural-innovator.md)
to spread beyond local pockets and [connecting innovation to organizational core](connecting-innovation-to-organizational-core.md)
so change finds fertile soil rather than triggering backlash.
