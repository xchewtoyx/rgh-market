Team learning is "the process of aligning and developing the capacity of a team
to create the results its members truly desire." It builds on shared vision and
personal mastery, but talent and shared vision alone are not enough — many
teams share a vision yet fail to learn.

It is a **collective** discipline: meaningless to say "I am mastering team
learning" as an individual, like claiming to master being a great jazz ensemble.

Three critical dimensions:

1. Thinking insightfully about complex issues — many minds more intelligent than
   one (teams often default to *less* intelligent than their best member).
2. Innovative, coordinated action — operational trust where members act in
   complementary ways.
3. Propagating learning — senior-team practices spread to other teams that
   carry out the work.

Team learning requires mastering [dialogue and discussion](dialogue-versus-discussion.md)
and facing conflict through [productive conflict](productive-conflict-in-learning-teams.md)
without [defensive routines](defensive-routines.md).
[Alignment](alignment-in-teams.md) is the necessary precondition before
empowering individuals empowers the whole team.

Management teams rarely have the [practice fields](practice-fields-for-management-teams.md)
athletes and musicians use — one reason talented groups fail to learn together.
