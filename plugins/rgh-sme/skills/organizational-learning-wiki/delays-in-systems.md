type: concept
title: Delays in Systems
description: >
  Gaps between actions and consequences that cause overshoot, oscillation, and
  misattribution — making aggressive intervention worsen outcomes under delay.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 5"
---

**Delays** are interruptions between actions and their consequences, present in
virtually all feedback processes though often unrecognized. Ray Stata (Analog
Devices): "one of the highest leverage points for improving system performance
is the minimization of system delays."

Unrecognized delays cause **overshoot** — going further than needed:

- Eating past fullness because satiety signals lag.
- Real-estate overbuilding from construction-to-completion delays.
- The beer game's overordering from misjudged order-fulfillment delays.

**The sluggish shower** example: a balancing process (adjusting water temperature)
with significant delay before the faucet change is felt. Turning up the heat
produces no immediate response, so the person keeps turning it up; when hot water
finally arrives it's scalding, triggering overcorrection — oscillation. "The more
aggressive you are in your behavior... the longer it will take to reach the right
temperature."

Delays matter in [reinforcing feedback](reinforcing-feedback.md) too — in
escalation cycles, each side perceives temporary advantage from delay in the
other's response, sustaining the spiral.

Systems thinking is oriented to the long term because delays and feedback loops
"are inconsequential" in the short term but "come back to haunt you in the long
term." Connects to [fixation on events](fixation-on-events.md) and [delusion of learning from experience](delusion-of-learning-from-experience.md)
when consequences occur elsewhere in the system before circling back.
