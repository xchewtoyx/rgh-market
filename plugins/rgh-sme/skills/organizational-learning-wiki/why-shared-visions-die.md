---
type: concept
title: Why Shared Visions Die
description: >
  Limits-to-growth structures that arrest the reinforcing loop spreading a
  vision — and the levers that revive enrollment rather than compliance.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 10"
---

Many visions with genuine merit never take root because [limits to growth](limits-to-growth-archetype.md)
structures arrest the reinforcing process that would spread them. The base loop:
as people talk, the vision gets clearer; clarity builds enthusiasm; enthusiasm
drives more communication and commitment — spiraling upward, reinforced by early
successes. Several balancing processes typically intervene:

**Diversity and polarization.** As more people engage, differing views on the
ideal future surface. If the organization cannot allow this diversity — if people
conclude the vision is fixed and their views don't matter — enrollment stalls
and polarization reduces clarity. The visioning process is "a special type of
inquiry process ... an inquiry into the future we truly seek to create." Becoming
pure advocacy yields compliance, not commitment. [Balancing inquiry and advocacy](balancing-inquiry-and-advocacy.md)
and [mental models](mental-models-as-organizational-routine.md) skills are the
primary lever: advocates who inquire into others' visions let the vision evolve
and become larger than any individual's — the hologram principle.

**Organizational discouragement.** As the vision clarifies, so does awareness
of the gap between vision and current reality, which can produce cynicism. The
limiting factor is capacity to hold [creative tension](creative-tension.md) —
why personal mastery is bedrock for shared vision.

**Being overwhelmed by current reality.** People consumed by firefighting lose
focus on the vision; leverage lies in reducing firefighting or separating vision
pursuers from current-reality managers — "skunk works" — though this risks
polarized camps.

**Forgetting connection to one another.** Once people stop asking "What do we
really want to create?" and start proselytizing an "official" vision, relationships
erode, splitting insiders from outsiders. Urgency to get sign-ups leaves no time
to really talk and listen.

Shared vision lacks critical underpinning without [systems thinking as learning language](systems-thinking-as-learning-language.md):
vision paints what we want; systems thinking reveals how we created what we have.
Many "vision bandwagon" failures stem from a **reactive orientation toward current
reality** — managers not experiencing themselves as contributing to current reality,
so problems are attributed "out there." "Can do" optimism can be a thin veneer
over fundamentally reactive views. As people learn how existing policies create
current reality, "a new, more fertile soil for vision develops."
