title: Leader's Toolkit for Psychological Safety
description: >
  Three interrelated leadership practices — setting the stage, inviting
  participation, responding productively — that build and restore candor.

  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 7"
---

Edmondson organizes practices that build [psychological safety](psychological-safety.md)
into three categories, each accomplishing something distinct and requiring
ongoing repetition — not one-time events:

1. **[Setting the stage](setting-the-stage-for-candor.md)** — frame the work
   (expectations about failure, uncertainty, interdependence) and emphasize
   purpose (what is at stake, for whom). Accomplishes shared expectations
   and meaning.

2. **[Inviting participation](inviting-participation.md)** — demonstrate
   situational humility, practice proactive inquiry, and create structures for
   input. Accomplishes confidence that voice is welcome.

3. **[Responding productively](responding-productively-to-voice.md)** —
   express appreciation, destigmatize failure, and sanction clear violations.
   Accomplishes orientation toward continuous learning.

Framing in particular is ongoing: frequently calling attention to uncertainty
or interdependence helps people remember they must stay alert and candid to
perform well. See also [psychological safety as an ongoing process](psychological-safety-as-ongoing-process.md).
