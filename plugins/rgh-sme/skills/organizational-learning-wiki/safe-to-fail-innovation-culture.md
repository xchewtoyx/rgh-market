type: concept
title: Safe-to-Fail Innovation Culture
description: >
  Rewarding teams for killing unpromising ideas early — making fast, evidence-
  based failure the path of least resistance in exploratory work.
sources:
  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 5"
---

Google X ("Captain of Moonshots") argues you cannot yell at people to fail fast
— they resist, fearing ridicule or termination. "The only way to get people to
work on big, risky things is if you make that the path of least resistance."

When Project Foghorn (seawater-to-fuel) could not reach commercial costs, the
team terminated after two years — and every member received a bonus. X rewards
teams for killing their own ideas with peer applause, leadership recognition,
promotions, and bonuses. "Real failure is trying something, learning it doesn't
work, then continuing to do it anyway" — hence framing success as "failing to
fail."

**Rapid Evaluation** vets proposals with a [premortem technique](premortem-technique.md)
generating reasons an idea could fail — akin to Pixar's [institutionalized candor](institutionalized-candor.md).
Survivors build crude prototypes quickly; Foundry asks whether the solution
should exist and whether people would use it. Failed prototypes are showcased;
annual celebrations place failed prototypes on an altar and discuss emotional
investment released.

This operationalizes [uncoupling fear and failure](uncoupling-fear-and-failure.md)
and the [failure taxonomy's](failure-taxonomy.md) intelligent-failure category:
celebrate thoughtful experiments that terminate early when evidence warrants.

Connects to [strategy as hypothesis](strategy-as-hypothesis.md) — moonshots are
hypotheses tested until disconfirmed.
