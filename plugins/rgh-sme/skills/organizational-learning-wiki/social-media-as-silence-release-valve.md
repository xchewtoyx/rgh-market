title: Social Media as Silence Release Valve

  When internal psychological safety is absent, social media can make prolonged
  organizational silence unsustainable — with reputational and human costs.

Edmondson shifts from physical-safety accidents to reputational and cultural
harm from workplace silence — and to social media as a mechanism that makes
prolonged organizational silence **unsustainable**.

**Susan Fowler at Uber** (Feb 2017, nine months before #MeToo): a former site-
reliability engineer published a blog describing harassment reported to HR as a
manager's "first offense" despite prior reports; high performers were "promoted
and protected." Fowler and colleagues "fell silent — for a while." Core values
implicated: "super-pumpedness," "make bold bets" (forgiveness over permission),
"meritocracy and toe-stepping." Engineering org went from 25%+ women to 6% during
her tenure. The post triggered investigative reporting, lawsuits, departures,
valuation drop, and CEO resignation.

**#MeToo** (Oct 2017): Alyssa Milano's tweet ignited mass participation; people
previously afraid to tell even close relations spoke publicly — building on Tarana
Burke's decade-old movement.

Two problems with organizations that "actively advocate for a psychologically
unsafe culture" in the social-media era: (1) such advocacy is harder to sustain
when silence breaks externally; (2) people suffer real, unnecessary harm meanwhile.
New CEO Dara Khosrowshahi "came in listening" — the operative word bridging to
organizations where leaders make listening and speaking up the norm.

Extends [dangerous silence](dangerous-silence.md) and [epidemic of workplace silence](epidemic-of-workplace-silence.md)
beyond high-risk physical industries to cultural/reputational harm. Pairs with
[illusion of success from silence](illusion-of-success-from-silence.md) — until
the external release valve opens.
