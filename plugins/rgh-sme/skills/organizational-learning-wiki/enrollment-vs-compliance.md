---
type: concept
title: Enrollment Versus Compliance
description: >
  A taxonomy of attitudes toward organizational vision — from commitment and
  enrollment through grudging compliance to apathy — where 90% of "commitment" is compliance.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 10"
---

Senge distinguishes "selling" (getting someone to act without full possession
of facts) from **enrolling** (Kiefer: "the process of becoming part of something
by choice"). Real commitment is rare — "90 percent of the time, what passes for
commitment is compliance."

Taxonomy from strongest to weakest:

- **Commitment** — wants it, will make it happen, creates whatever structures
  ("laws") are needed.
- **Enrollment** — wants it, will do whatever can be done within the "spirit
  of the law."
- **Genuine compliance** — sees benefits, does everything expected and more
  ("good soldier").
- **Formal compliance** — sees benefits on the whole, does what's expected and
  no more.
- **Grudging compliance** — doesn't see benefits but won't lose their job;
  does enough while signaling they're not on board.
- **Noncompliance** — "I won't do it; you can't make me."
- **Apathy** — no interest, no energy ("Is it five o'clock yet?").

Genuine compliance is most easily mistaken for enrollment — but committed people
bring energy compliance cannot generate. "The committed person doesn't play by
the rules of the game. He is responsible for the game" and will change rules
that block the vision.

Guidelines for enrollment (free choice; you cannot force it):

- Be enrolled yourself — attempting to enroll others while not enrolled is
  "selling."
- Be on the level — describe honestly, don't inflate benefits or hide problems.
- Let the other person choose — persuasion attempts are seen as manipulative.

Where only compliance is needed, be explicit rather than pretending to seek
enrollment. Pairs with [shared vision as common caring](shared-vision-as-common-caring.md)
and [why shared visions die](why-shared-visions-die.md) when advocacy replaces inquiry.
