---
type: concept
title: The Enemy Is Out There
description: >
  A learning disability that locates blame externally when problems often
  straddle inside and outside the organization.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 2"
---

When things go wrong, organizations find someone or something external to
blame — marketing blames manufacturing, manufacturing blames engineering.

This is a by-product of [I am my position](i-am-my-position.md): focusing only
on one's position blinds people to how their actions extend beyond it. When
consequences return, new problems are misperceived as externally caused — "like
the person being chased by his own shadow."

People Express Airlines blamed aggressive competitors while declining service
quality left low fares as its only pull — none of the frantic responses
addressed the real problem.

"The enemy is out there" is almost always an incomplete story. "Out there" and
"in here" are usually part of a single system; this disability makes leverage
"in here" nearly impossible to detect. Senge's eleventh law: **there is no
blame** — the cure lies in your relationship with your apparent adversary, not
in defeating a separate "other."
