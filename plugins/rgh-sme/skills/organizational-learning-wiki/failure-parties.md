title: Failure Parties
description: >
  A ritual honoring intelligent, high-quality experiments that failed —
  building safety, freeing resources, and spreading lessons.

Eli Lilly's chief scientific officer introduced **failure parties** to honor
[intelligent failures](failure-taxonomy.md) — thoughtful experiments that did
not achieve desired results.

Edmondson defends the practice on three grounds: it builds a psychologically
safe climate for thoughtful risk; it helps people acknowledge failure promptly,
freeing scientists and materials for redeployment; and gathering people to
learn lowers the risk of repeating the same failure (a repeated intelligent
failure is no longer intelligent).

Failure parties exemplify [destigmatizing failure](responding-productively-to-voice.md)
as ongoing organizational practice, not incident-triggered postmortem mechanics
alone.
