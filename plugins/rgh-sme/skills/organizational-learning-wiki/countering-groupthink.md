---
type: concept
title: Countering Groupthink
description: >
  Structural and leadership interventions that license skepticism, assign
  dissent roles, and reduce hierarchy so teams test assumptions instead of
  treating unanimity as proof.
sources:
  - title: "Superforecasting: The Art and Science of Prediction"
    resource: "Superforecasting (Tetlock & Gardner), ch. 9"
---

After the Bay of Pigs, Kennedy's inquiry identified "cozy unanimity" as the
core problem and implemented reforms before the Cuban Missile Crisis deliberations.
The same advisers who bungled 1961 reached a negotiated peace in 1962 — not
because the team changed but because the process did.

Effective countermeasures include:

- **Skepticism as explicit norm** — participants licensed to question anything,
  not only within their own expertise.
- **Assigned dissent roles** — Theodore Sorensen and Robert Kennedy as
  "intellectual watchdogs" tasked to "pursue relentlessly every bone of
  contention"; Robert Kennedy as an outspoken devil's advocate.
- **Relaxed hierarchy and protocol** — freewheeling discussion; new advisers
  brought in for fresh perspectives.
- **Leader absence** — JFK sometimes left the room because his presence
  suppressed genuine give-and-take among advisers.
- **Withholding early anchors** — JFK kept his initial leaning (preemptive air
  strikes) private early so it would not anchor group discussion; by end of day
  one the committee had seriously discussed ten alternatives.

These practices connect to [inviting participation](inviting-participation.md)
(proactive inquiry, explicit invitations for pushback),
[setting the stage for candor](setting-the-stage-for-candor.md) (framing work
so questioning is expected), and [dialogue versus discussion](dialogue-versus-discussion.md)
(suspending assumptions rather than defending positions).

Forecasting teams in Tetlock's Good Judgment Project received a team primer
warning against [groupthink](groupthink.md): be cooperative but not deferential;
consensus is not always good and disagreement is not always bad; agreement alone
is never proof of being right. [Precision questioning](precision-questioning.md)
was taught as the technique for "constructive confrontation" — disagreeing without
being disagreeable. The [premortem technique](premortem-technique.md) offers a
similar pre-commitment license for dissent in planning settings.
