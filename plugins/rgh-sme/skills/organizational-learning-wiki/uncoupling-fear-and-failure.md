---
type: concept
title: Uncoupling Fear and Failure
description: >
  Separating the emotional terror of mistakes from the learning value of
  failure — so people innovate rather than repeat safe past successes.
sources:
  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 5"
---

Creative and innovative work requires freedom to fail early. Without it,
people "seek instead to repeat something safe that's been good enough in the
past" — derivative rather than innovative output.

Pixar co-founder Ed Catmull argues moviemaking resembles learning to ride a
bicycle: no one pedals gracefully without falling first. Leaders who try to
avoid failure "by outthinking it" doom themselves to fail. The goal is to
**uncouple fear and failure** — recognizing both the pain of mistakes and the
benefit of resulting growth.

Pixar manages failure's cost by front-loading it: directors may spend years in
development (salary cost, limited production cost) while [institutionalized candor](institutionalized-candor.md)
surfaces problems before expensive production commits. Termination follows only
when a director loses the team's confidence or repeatedly ignores constructive
Braintrust feedback.

Google X extends the pattern: teams receive bonuses and celebration when they
kill unpromising moonshots early — making fast, evidence-based termination the
path of least resistance. See [safe-to-fail innovation culture](safe-to-fail-innovation-culture.md).

This pairs with the [failure taxonomy](failure-taxonomy.md): intelligent
failures in new territory must be celebrated; leaders must [destigmatize failure](responding-productively-to-voice.md)
while maintaining [performance standards](psychological-safety-and-performance-standards.md).
