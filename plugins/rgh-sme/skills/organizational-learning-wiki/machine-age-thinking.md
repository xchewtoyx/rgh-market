---
type: concept
title: Machine-Age Thinking
description: >
  An implicit image of organizations as machines to be run and driven — language
  and design that block seeing companies as human communities capable of learning.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 12"
---

Arie de Geus (*The Living Company*) asks: "How do we see a business — as a
human community or as a machine for making money?" Management language reveals
an implicit machine image even among people who'd reject it explicitly: managers
"run" a company, there are "owners," leaders "drive change" — language suited
to machines but awkward applied to human communities (contrast trying to "drive"
a spouse or teenage child).

Shell's corporate-longevity study (de Geus directed) found Fortune 500 life
expectancy under 40 years, but ~20 companies worldwide survived two centuries.
Common feature across differences in history, culture, industry, and technology:
long-lived companies thought of themselves more as **human communities** than
financial institutions — they "had a sense of who they were that transcended what
they did," giving capabilities to evolve, adapt, and learn that contemporaries
lacked.

Gregory Bateson: "The source of all our problems today comes from the gap between
how we think and how nature works." Dominant institutions assume "all systems must
have someone in control," whereas healthy [living systems](organizations-as-living-systems.md)
distribute control — but people are so habituated to "closet Newtonian" control
mindsets that alternatives go unseen.

Etymology reinforces the shift: English "company" derives from French *compaigne*
— sharing of bread, same root as "companion." Oldest Swedish business term,
*narings liv*, means "nourishment for life"; oldest Chinese symbol for business
translates as "life meaning."

Pairs with [leader as designer](leader-as-designer.md) (participants in living
systems, not outside engineers) and [purpose beyond profit](purpose-beyond-profit.md).
