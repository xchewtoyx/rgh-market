---
type: concept
title: Shared Vision as Common Caring
description: >
  A shared vision is a force in people's hearts from common caring — not an
  imposed idea, and distinct from compliance-producing "vision statements."
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 10"
---

A shared vision is not an idea — "not even an important idea such as freedom."
It is "a force in people's hearts, a force of impressive power." At its simplest,
a shared vision answers "What do we want to create?" Personal visions derive
power from individual deep caring; shared visions derive power from **common
caring**. A vision is truly shared "when you and I have a similar picture and
are committed to one another having it, not just to each of us, individually,
having it."

Shared vision is vital because [adaptive versus generative learning](adaptive-vs-generative-learning.md)
requires striving for something that matters deeply — "generative learning
occurs only when people are striving to accomplish something that matters deeply
to them." Most corporate "visions" are one person's vision imposed on the
organization — producing compliance at best, not commitment.

The **hologram metaphor**: unlike a photograph, each fragment of a hologram
shows the whole image. Each person in a shared vision holds the whole picture
from a unique vantage point, sharing responsibility for the whole, not just
their piece. Adding more visions makes it "more intense, more lifelike" — people
become cocreators.

Building shared vision must be "a central element of the daily work of leaders,"
ongoing and never-ending — not a one-shot retreat. Visions can originate anywhere;
"the origin of the vision is less important than the process whereby it comes
to be shared." Leaders must share personal vision and ask "Will you follow me?"
— not treat position as automatic ownership of "the organization's vision."

Governing ideas answer three questions: **vision** (what), **purpose/mission**
(why), **core values** (how). Values need translation into concrete behaviors
(e.g., "openness" requiring [balancing inquiry and advocacy](balancing-inquiry-and-advocacy.md)).

Negative visions ("avoid bankruptcy") divert energy into prevention, imply
powerlessness, and evaporate when the threat passes. Fear produces fast change;
aspiration endures as a continuing source of learning.

Connects to [why shared visions die](why-shared-visions-die.md) and [creative tension](creative-tension.md).
