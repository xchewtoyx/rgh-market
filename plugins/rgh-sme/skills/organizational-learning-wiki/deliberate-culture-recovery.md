title: Deliberate Culture Recovery
description: >
  Organizations can shift from fearful silence toward psychological safety
  through sustained norm-setting, practice, and leadership reinforcement —
  a long journey, not an overnight fix.
sources:

---

Recovering from cultures of fear requires deliberate, sustained effort.
Edmondson's "driving fear out" framing treats culture shift as a realistic
long journey — not a single initiative.

**Nokia's deliberative turnaround** after the Symbian collapse illustrates
recovery at the board level. New discussion rules required every voice to be
heard and respected — but rules alone were insufficient. When a board member
made a hostile comment, the chairman required a public apology at the next
meeting, consciously reinforcing norms. Over years, "thinking out loud" and
throwing in ideas produced new strategies — paralleling Pixar's Braintrust in
rejecting unusable options until leadership felt confident in direction.

**NASA's "Sounds of Silence" workshop** followed decades without internal
engagement on silence after Challenger. New structures included a dissenting-
opinion mechanism, safety reporting, an ombudsman, and a "Lean Forward, Fail
Smart Award." Chief Knowledge Officer Ed Rogers stressed that a listening
culture matters as much as a speaking-up culture — "communication involves
transmitting and receiving."

**Uber's culture reset** under new leadership paired top-down value changes
("Do the Right Thing. Period." replacing "toe-stepping") with bottom-up skills
for responding to inappropriate "just kidding" moments, and simple structural
fixes like banning phones during meetings that had signaled whispered distrust.

These cases share a pattern: [psychological safety as an ongoing process](psychological-safety-as-ongoing-process.md),
not a pronouncement; leadership that models vulnerability and enforces norms;
and structures ([failure parties](failure-parties.md), dissent channels, dialogue
forums) that make candor repeatable.

Prior failures — Nokia's [illusion of success from silence](illusion-of-success-from-silence.md),
NASA's Columbia-era silence, Uber's toxic norms — show how fragile gains can be
and why continuous nurturing matters.
