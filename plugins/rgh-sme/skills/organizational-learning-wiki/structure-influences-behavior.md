type: concept
title: Structure Influences Behavior
description: >
  When people in the same system produce similar results regardless of who they
  are — because behavior arises from structure, not individual character alone.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 3"
---

The MIT **beer game** — a production/distribution simulation played thousands of
times across continents — produces the same qualitative crisis every time: demand
surge that can't be met, depleted inventories, backlogs, then mass arrival of
product just as orders collapse. Since the pattern recurs regardless of who plays
which role, "the causes of the behavior must lie in the structure of the game
itself" — the first principle of systems thinking: **structure influences behavior**.
When placed in the same system, people, however different, tend to produce similar
results.

Donella Meadows: "A truly profound and different insight is the way you begin to
see that the system causes its own behavior." Most players blame each other, then
consumers, then the system — each explanation falsified by data. When asked to
sketch consumer demand afterward, wholesaler/brewery players draw curves matching
*their own* orders, not actual flat demand — revealing [the enemy is out there](the-enemy-is-out-there.md)
externalization.

In human systems, "structure" includes operating policies — how people translate
perceptions, goals, rules, and norms into actions — not just org charts. Because
we are part of the structure, we often have latent power to alter it but don't
perceive that power. Zimbardo's prison experiment and escalation rationalizations
(e.g., Soviet Afghanistan, Vietnam) illustrate structural compulsion.

Real parallels: memory-chip cycles, auto overproduction, inventory-accelerator
business cycles. Used as [systems thinking as learning language](systems-thinking-as-learning-language.md)
to depersonalize conflict — "We have met the enemy and he is us" (Pogo).

Pairs with [three levels of explanation](three-levels-of-explanation.md) (structural
explanations as most generative) and [i am my position](i-am-my-position.md)
(becoming one's role in the game).
