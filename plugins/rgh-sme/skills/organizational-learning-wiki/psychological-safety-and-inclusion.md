---
type: concept
title: Psychological Safety and Inclusion
description: >
  Inclusion and belonging require psychological safety — the subjective goals
  cannot be achieved without broad input across groups.
sources:
  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 8"
---

A truly inclusive, belonging-centered workplace is a psychologically safe
workplace. Diversity can be achieved through hiring, but inclusion does not
automatically follow — new hires may be excluded from decisions. A diverse
workforce does not guarantee belonging when no one at the top looks like you.

The three goals range from relatively objective (workforce diversity) to
highly subjective (do I feel I belong?). As goals become more subjective,
[psychological safety](psychological-safety.md) becomes more valuable — there
is no way to know if inclusion is working without broad input.

Psychological safety acts as the
[extra ingredient](psychological-safety-as-moderator.md) that lets diversity
be leveraged: diverse perspectives are more likely heard, and minority
employees may especially need safety to engage.

A singular focus on psychological safety alone is not a diversity strategy —
the goals must go hand in hand.
