title: Peacekeeping Circles
description: >
  A collective reflective practice that builds transformative relationships
  through equal, supportive conversation rather than hierarchical intervention.

Roca ("rock" in Spanish), serving immigrant communities in Chelsea, Massachusetts,
builds "networks of transformative relationships." Its core method is
**peacekeeping circles** — a collective reflective practice based on Native
American learning and healing traditions.

Founder Molly Baldwin: "Our first job is simply to show up for them... consistent,
in a relationship of complete support for who they really are." In circle,
participants are equals: "we all have problems and we learn by helping one
another." A Navajo tribal-court saying captures the ethic: "You can't get to a
good place in a bad way."

Example: a pregnant teenager afraid to tell her mother was supported via a small
circle of trusted people rather than left to escalate into conflict — "the key
was to create a conversation that would let the mother listen to her daughter."

Circles exemplify [reflective openness](reflective-openness.md) and [dialogue versus discussion](dialogue-versus-discussion.md)
— listening that generates shared understanding, not talk-at-each-other
participative openness alone. They move [tacit knowledge through storytelling](tacit-knowledge-through-storytelling.md)
and relationship: trust and how people relate form the basis for improved
collective thinking.

Over 18 years Roca's approach correlated with dramatic decreases in crime and
increased graduation rates in Chelsea. The method scales through relationship
quality, not capture-and-disseminate knowledge management.

Pairs with [inviting participation](inviting-participation.md) structures that
make silence more awkward than speaking — here, the circle itself is the structure.
