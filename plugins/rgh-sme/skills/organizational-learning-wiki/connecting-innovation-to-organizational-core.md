  Anchoring new learning practices in an organization's core identity and natural
  way of creating value — so radical ideas find fertile soil rather than being pushed in.

Deeper than [becoming bicultural as innovator](becoming-bicultural-innovator.md):
connecting new ideas to an organization's **core identity** and its most natural
way of creating value. Requires believing such an identity exists beyond current
products or profit, and a spirit of discovery.

Two subtle barriers change leaders miss:

1. Not going deep enough into themselves to find what truly calls them (pursuing
   "good ideas" that don't tap real passion).
2. Not going deep enough into what the organization stands for (ending up trying
   to "push" ideas in rather than connect them).

**Nike / Darcy Winslow** case: after environmental designers assessed a shoe's
toxicity, Winslow asked "there must be more to this business than the next cool
gadget." A new role — general manager of sustainable business strategies — was
created because existing sustainability work was compliance-focused rather than
embedded in product creation. She had a big mandate but no formal authority over
product teams.

Reading *The Tipping Point* (~20% of a population moving together) shaped her
strategy of hosting a two-day meeting for ~200 key managers. Breakthrough:
reconnecting with "who Nike is" — a company built by runners "who innovate"
(Nike's first maxim: "It is in our nature to innovate") reframed sustainability
as an innovation opportunity rather than a compliance mandate.

Having found the core identity, the leader must connect it to how the organization
actually generates value — for Nike, its ~300 designers and product managers.
Winslow went "knocking on doors" one-on-one rather than issuing top-down mandates;
contrast a Corporate Responsibility "PVC-free" mandate that closed doors versus
her framing that opened them ("untold opportunities for creating completely new
products"). Over five years: zero-waste goals, disassemblable products, organic-
cotton line, sustainable-materials group, take-back initiatives; "deliver
sustainable products and innovation" became a top-three company management goal.

Connects to [creative tension](creative-tension.md) (vision grounded in what the
organization truly stands for) and [leader as steward](leader-as-steward.md)
(caring for the organization's deeper purpose).
