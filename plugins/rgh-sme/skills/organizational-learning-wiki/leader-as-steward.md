---
type: concept
title: Leader as Steward
description: >
  Leadership in service of a larger purpose — balancing commitment with
  uncertainty, conservation with change, and sustainable results over ambition.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 15"
---

**Stewardship** is service first — to people and to a larger purpose. Robert
Greenleaf: the servant leader is servant first; the conscious choice to lead
follows wanting to serve. In combat, people reliably follow officers they trust
to have their well-being at heart.

Bill O'Brien: "All genuine commitment is to something larger than oneself."
Managers without a larger story may hold authority but not stewardship.

**Two paradoxes:**

1. **Certainty and commitment** — false certainty breeds fanaticism; genuine
   commitment coexists with questioning. Whoever acts with certainty of having
   the answer acts as a fanatic.

2. **Conservation and change** — deep purpose anchors while pursuing what's new.
   Humberto Maturana: evolution is "transformation through conservation." Change
   leaders should ask "What do we seek to conserve?" — often identity and
   relationships — to release fear of the unknown.

**Power and ambition:** networks of collaboration at business-unit level often
give way to networks of ambition at headquarters. Personal ambition spent on
turf compromises results and creates dependency on the person in power.

**Sustainable results** intervene "in a way that enables people to know better
how to help themselves in the future" — so problems don't return when attention
shifts.

Stewards hold vision without possessiveness: "We become a steward of the vision.
We are 'its' as much as it is ours."

Development rests on **[creative tension](creative-tension.md)** — holding
vision while looking honestly at current reality. Effective leaders instill
confidence that "we can learn whatever we need to learn," not that they have
all answers.
