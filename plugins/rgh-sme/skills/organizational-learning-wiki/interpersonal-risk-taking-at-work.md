type: concept
title: Interpersonal Risk-Taking at Work
description: >
  The social hazards people weigh when deciding whether to speak up — looking
  ignorant, incompetent, or disruptive.
sources:
  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 1"
---

At work people constantly manage others' impressions, consciously and
automatically — a pattern Erving Goffman described as everyday impression
management. The risks are interpersonal: looking ignorant, incompetent, or
disruptive.

Folk rules follow quickly: don't want to look ignorant → don't ask questions;
don't want to look incompetent → don't admit mistakes; don't want to look
disruptive → don't make suggestions. People learn these rules early and
practice them into adulthood.

Unlike a social event, privileging looking good over making a difference at
work can thwart innovation, harm customers, or cost lives. A NICU nurse who
hesitates to question a physician's order after overhearing a public berating
is performing a [micro-assessment](micro-assessment-of-interpersonal-risk.md):
an unconscious trade of delayed patient welfare against immediate interpersonal
risk.

[Psychological safety](psychological-safety.md) exists when people believe
neither formal nor informal consequences of these risks will be punitive —
that candor is both allowed and expected.
