When personal aspiration connects with organizational essence, **community**
forms as both strategy and outcome — not limited to businesses.

Les Omotani (superintendent, West Des Moines public schools): the discovery that
mattered most was getting people to ask "What is good for the kids?" Regular
community dialogues (teachers, administrators, parents, business people, town
officials) surfaced questions about "immovable walls" — the 180-day school year,
half-day kindergarten, underinvestment in older parts of town — organically, not
as Omotani's pushed agenda. Parallel tracks of dialogue and implementation-team
work fed each other. Outcomes: arts-integrated approach at a Title I school;
newest elementary built in the town's oldest area; extended ~210-day school year;
early-childhood center the community chose to keep funding after Omotani left.
Achievement gaps narrowed. Guiding phrase: "As the community, so the schools;
as the schools, so the community."

Juanita Brown (World Café method): "Communities grow from people pursuing
questions that have heart and meaning to them." Creating social space for deeper
conversation must be done consciously, but resulting community formation is a
natural process that resists being controlled or manipulated — attempts to
control it can backfire.

Learning communities depend on [dialogue versus discussion](dialogue-versus-discussion.md)
(divergent inquiry that does not aim at immediate decision) and complement
[working across difference](working-across-difference.md) — community's shadow
side is clique or cult that excludes others.
