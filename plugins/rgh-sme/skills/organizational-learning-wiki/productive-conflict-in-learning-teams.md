---
type: concept
title: Productive Conflict in Learning Teams
description: >
  Visible conflict of ideas — not absence of conflict — signals a team that
  is continually learning when handled without defensive routines.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 11"
---

Contrary to popular myth, great teams are not characterized by absence of
conflict. One reliable indicator of a continually learning team is **visible
conflict of ideas**.

Teams sharing a common vision may diverge sharply on how to achieve it — "the
loftier the vision, the more uncertain we are how it is to be achieved." Free
flow of conflicting ideas is critical for solutions no individual would reach
alone; conflict becomes part of ongoing [dialogue](dialogue-versus-discussion.md).

Mediocre teams show either a smooth surface (suppressed conflict, fear the
team would tear apart) or rigid polarization (everyone speaks but positions
don't move).

[Psychological safety as moderator](psychological-safety-as-moderator.md):
conflict improves performance under high safety and harms it otherwise. The
difference is how teams face conflict and the [defensive routines](defensive-routines.md)
surrounding it.
