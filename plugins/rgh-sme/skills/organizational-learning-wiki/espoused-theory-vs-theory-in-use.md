type: concept
title: Espoused Theory versus Theory-in-Use
description: >
  The gap between what people say they believe and the mental models implied
  by their actions — a reflective entry point for organizational learning.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 9"
---

Chris Argyris distinguished **espoused theory** (what we say) from **theory-in-use**
(the implied theory in what we do). People behave congruently with theories-in-use,
not always with espoused theories — though gaps are not necessarily hypocrisy.

A basic reflective skill uses the gap as awareness: professing that people are
trustworthy while never lending money and jealously guarding possessions. If
trusting people is genuinely part of one's vision, the gap holds potential for
[creative tension](creative-tension.md). "The problem lies not in the gap but in
failing to tell the truth about the gap."

First question when facing a gap: do I really value the espoused theory? If not,
tension is between reality and a view advanced for appearance's sake, not reality
and vision. Because theories-in-use are hard to see for oneself, a "ruthlessly
compassionate" partner helps — "the eye cannot see the eye."

Part of [mental models as organizational routine](mental-models-as-organizational-routine.md);
pairs with [leaps of abstraction](leaps-of-abstraction.md) when untested
generalizations drive behavior inconsistent with stated values.
