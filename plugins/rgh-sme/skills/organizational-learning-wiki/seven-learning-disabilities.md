title: Seven Learning Disabilities
description: >
  Recurring organizational patterns that block learning despite bright,
  committed people — identified by Peter Senge as antidotes to the five disciplines.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"

---

Peter Senge argues poor organizational learning is produced by how organizations
are designed and managed — and by how people are taught to think and interact.
These **learning disabilities** operate despite the best efforts of bright,
committed people; often the harder people try, the worse results get.

The seven disabilities:

1. **[I am my position](i-am-my-position.md)** — confusing job with identity;
   limiting responsibility to position boundaries.
2. **[The enemy is out there](the-enemy-is-out-there.md)** — blaming external
   forces when consequences of one's own actions return.
3. **[Illusion of taking charge](illusion-of-taking-charge.md)** — aggressive
   "proactiveness" that is reactiveness in disguise.
4. **[Fixation on events](fixation-on-events.md)** — short-term event focus
   that caps learning at reaction.
5. **[Boiled frog](boiled-frog.md)** — failing to sense slow, gradual threats.
6. **[Delusion of learning from experience](delusion-of-learning-from-experience.md)**
   — consequences of important decisions fall outside the learning horizon.
7. **[Myth of the management team](myth-of-the-management-team.md)** — teams
   that avoid embarrassment produce [skilled incompetence](skilled-incompetence.md).

The five disciplines of the learning organization act as antidotes — but first
the disabilities must be seen clearly amid daily events.
