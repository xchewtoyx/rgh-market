title: Leader as Designer
description: >
  Leadership focused on shaping learning infrastructures, guiding ideas, and
  organizational artifacts that enable collective learning — not heroic command.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 15"
---

In a learning organization, leaders are **designers** — a role more important
than captain, navigator, or helmsman on an ocean liner. What good is ordering
"turn starboard" when the rudder only turns port?

Leaders are participants in living systems, not outside engineers. They create
organizational artifacts — metrics, roles, meeting formats, intranets — but what
matters is what happens when people use them.

Learning infrastructures develop through **open, iterative design**: recognize
unmet communication needs; have courage to break molds; assess results and
adjust; let others evolve infrastructures for their contexts. Saudi Aramco's
evolving "world café" sequence illustrates years of iteration.

New IT infrastructure typically reinforces existing culture rather than changing
it — unless design addresses how technology enables people to find and help each
other.

**Guiding ideas** (purpose, vision, values) are design objects: worry less about
wording, more about engagement; take longer; judge by how ideas influence daily
decisions — "How did our vision and values influence decisions I made today?"

Good design's hallmark is the absence of crisis — often invisible in heroic-leader
cultures. Lao Tzu: "The great leader is he of whom the people say, 'We did it
ourselves.'"
