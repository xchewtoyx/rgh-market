title: Institutionalized Candor
description: >
  Organizations that embed structured, rule-governed forums for frank feedback
  — like Pixar's Braintrust — make candor a practice rather than a personality.
sources:
  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 5"
---

Candor — forthrightness about what works and what does not — can be
institutionalized rather than left to individual temperament. Pixar's
**Braintrust** exemplifies the pattern: a small group of directors and
storytellers meets every few months to watch an early cut, eat together, and
give the director candid feedback on the project.

Explicit rules govern the exchange:

- Feedback is constructive and about the project, not the person; the filmmaker
  must be ready to hear truth without defensiveness.
- Comments are suggestions, not prescriptions — the director retains
  responsibility and can accept or reject solutions.
- Candid feedback comes from empathy, not "gotcha"; the Braintrust is
  benevolent with no selfish agenda.

The Braintrust functions as a neutral "it" rather than a fearsome "them."
When people feel safe contributing, observations build on each other and
collective knowledge in the room increases exponentially compared with
gathering individual feedback separately.

Braintrusts are fragile — personality and chemistry matter — and require
active management over time. They resemble academic peer review and art-school
crits but, at their best, lack the competitive edge those processes can carry.

Institutionalized candor connects to [psychological safety](psychological-safety.md)
as the climate that lets such forums work, and to [uncoupling fear and failure](uncoupling-fear-and-failure.md)
when creative work requires early honest critique of weak drafts.
