title: Responding Productively to Voice
description: >
  How leaders react when people speak up — appreciation, destigmatizing
  failure, and fair sanctions — determines whether safety persists.

**Responding productively** is the third category in the
[leader's toolkit](leaders-toolkit-for-psychological-safety.md). How leaders
respond determines whether [psychological safety](psychological-safety.md)
persists or evaporates after someone speaks.

**Express appreciation** — thank people for raising concerns regardless of
whether the input seems good or bad; educate afterward. Praising effort (not
just outcomes) keeps people willing to take risks under uncertainty.

**Destigmatize failure** — reframe failure as a natural byproduct of
experimentation; promote fast learning over hiding. Use
[focused event analysis](focused-event-analysis.md) for complex failures and
[failure parties](failure-parties.md) for intelligent ones.

**Sanction clear violations** — fair, thoughtful sanctioning of repeated
rule-breaking or blameworthy acts can *reinforce* safety: most people
recognize that reckless shortcuts endanger colleagues. The key is clarity
about what violates stated policies versus honest mistakes worth learning from.

Under volatile conditions, good process can yield bad outcomes and bad process
can temporarily look successful — so productive response to bad news matters
especially when simple cause-effect reasoning fails.
