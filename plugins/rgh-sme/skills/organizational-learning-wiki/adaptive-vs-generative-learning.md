---
type: concept
title: Adaptive Versus Generative Learning
description: >
  Survival learning adapts to change; generative learning enhances the capacity
  to create — both are needed, but survival alone is not enough.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 1"
---

A learning organization is "an organization that is continually expanding its
capacity to create its future." Survival is not enough.

**Adaptive learning** (a.k.a. survival learning) is necessary — responding to
change, coping with threats. **Generative learning** enhances the capacity to
create — new patterns of thinking, collective aspiration set free, learning how
to learn together.

Senge distinguishes invention (proven in the lab) from innovation (reliably
replicated at meaningful scale and practical cost). Learning organizations have
been invented but not yet innovated — like powered flight before the DC-3
combined five component technologies into a working ensemble.

Organizations where people "continually expand their capacity to create the
results they truly desire" need generative learning, not only training that
takes in information. Connects to [metanoia](metanoia.md) (real learning that
re-creates us) and [deep learning cycle](deep-learning-cycle.md) (developing
capacity, not one-off results).

[Fixation on events](fixation-on-events.md) blocks generative learning: "Generative
learning cannot be sustained in an organization if people's primary focus is on
events rather than on longer-term patterns."
