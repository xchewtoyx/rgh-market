---
type: concept
title: Error-Reporting Paradox
description: >
  Better teams often report more errors not because they make more mistakes
  but because their climate makes reporting safe.
sources:
  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 1"
---

Edmondson's accidental discovery launched the psychological-safety research
program. Studying medication errors across hospital units, she expected more
effective teams would have fewer errors. Instead, better teams (per survey
ratings) had *more* recorded errors — a ten-fold spread between best and worst
units, statistically significant in the wrong direction.

The eureka: better teams don't make more mistakes; they *report* more because
of a climate of openness. A blind follow-up study through observation and
interviews found willingness to talk openly about mistakes correlated almost
perfectly with detected error rates.

This [error-reporting paradox](psychological-safety.md) is why silence can
mask problems: low reported errors may signal fear, not quality. Leaders who
punish messengers learn less about what is actually going wrong.
