---
type: concept
title: Speaking Up for Physical Safety
description: >
  Worker physical safety depends on psychological safety first — people must
  feel able to report hazards before technical fixes can work.
sources:
  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 6"
---

Increasing physical safety in hazardous work is "as much about transforming
old attitudes and changing the culture to make it safe to speak up as it was
about technical or process improvements." Culture must be co-created and
continuously nurtured by multiple stakeholders — not driven top-down by one
leader alone.

**Anglo American** under CEO Cynthia Carroll illustrates [setting the stage](setting-the-stage-for-candor.md)
for safety speak-up. After appalling fatality rates, Carroll shut down the
Rustenburg platinum mine — unprecedented — and insisted on hearing from every
worker before restart. Executives addressed thousands at a time; a theater group
role-played supervisor-worker safety interactions; workers in small groups voiced
concerns only after the question reframed from "give your opinion on safety"
to "what do we need to create a work environment of care and respect?" Union-
supported **lekgotla** circle assemblies (speak without interruption until
consensus) let reluctant workers raise issues like missing hot water at the
site. Signed safety contracts co-signed by workers and executives followed;
employees gained explicit right to stop unsafe work.

**Fukushima Daini** superintendent Naohiro Masuda built [psychological safety](psychological-safety.md)
through honesty and vulnerability under extreme danger: sharing calculations on
a whiteboard rather than issuing blind orders, letting workers choose whether
to join reconnaissance teams, admitting when his cabling plan was wrong, and
listening when engineers noticed Unit 1's pressure rising faster than Unit 2's.

**DaVita Kidney Care** links belonging and voice to clinical outcomes: technicians
who smile and relate compassionately help patients comply with rigorous dialysis;
frequent cross-role communication supports holistic care.

Worker safety starts with encouraging and reinforcing employees' speaking up
about hazards — a precondition for effective protocols, as
[psychological safety and emergency protocols](psychological-safety-and-emergency-protocols.md)
shows in aviation.
