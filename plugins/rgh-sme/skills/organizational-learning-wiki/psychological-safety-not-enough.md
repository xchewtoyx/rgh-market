type: concept
title: Psychological Safety Is Not Enough
description: >
  Safety removes brakes on learning but does not replace high standards,
  purpose, or the motivation to reach them.
sources:
  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 1"
---

"Psychological safety takes off the brakes that keep people from achieving
what's possible. But it's not the fuel that powers the car."

Leaders have two vital, distinct tasks: build
[psychological safety](psychological-safety.md) to spur learning and avoid
preventable failures; and set
[high performance standards](psychological-safety-and-performance-standards.md)
while inspiring people toward a worthy purpose.

In knowledge work, excellence cannot be verified step-by-step like inspecting
widgets. Results of uncertain programs take time to surface. Leaders must
motivate through inspiration, coaching, and feedback rather than fear and
inspection — yet open communication remains comparatively neglected as a
21st-century leadership responsibility.

You cannot have "too much" psychological safety in the sense of excess candor —
most people set their speaking-up threshold too high, erring toward silence.
Where someone speaks too often or unhelpfully, the fix is feedback on impact,
not reducing safety. Interpersonal fear is never particularly helpful at work;
unlike fear of missing a deadline, fear of one's boss actively risks the
organization when [silence](epidemic-of-workplace-silence.md) hides problems.
