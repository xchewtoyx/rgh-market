  Moving fluently between an open learning-oriented team culture and the
  traditional mainstream organization — respecting each world's ground rules.

Local learning success does not guarantee organizational spread — sometimes it
causes trouble for innovators. AutoCo's "Epsilon team" cut a five-year product-
development cycle by a year, returned $50M+ in unspent costs, and produced the
smoothest launch in company history, yet senior team leaders were pushed into
early retirement during a subsequent reorganization. Art Kleiner's *The Age of
Heretics* documents 1960s pioneers driven out despite — or because of — their
success. The "build a better mousetrap" theory fails against organizational
politics.

Root cause: the **true believers syndrome** — innovators' passion (necessary for
risk-taking and perseverance) can blind them to how outsiders perceive them,
make them dismissive of others' concerns, and lead them into jargon that reads
as cult-like ("ladders of inference," "systems thinking" struck one VP as
"incomprehensible"; another noted the team "were having too much fun"). Enthusiastic
advocates are prejudiced assessors of their own performance.

The remedy: leaders must become **bicultural**, moving fluently between the
open/learning-oriented team culture and the traditional mainstream organization,
respecting each world's ground rules.

Two tactics:

- **Stealth transformation** (Intel's Dave Marsing): keep innovations below
  senior-management radar without hiding them — avoid jargon, don't make a big
  deal, explain plainly if asked; align innovation metrics with what the culture
  already tracks (e.g., ROI, technological agility).
- **Working in the language of the incumbent** (Roger Saillant): translate
  deep-values language into the numbers language management understands, and
  vice versa for the workforce; "under-promise and over-deliver" — craft goals
  in the hierarchy's language, then help the team make sense of them internally.

Pairs with [grassroots learning initiatives](grassroots-learning-initiatives.md)
(local wins that need translation to spread) and [leader as teacher](leader-as-teacher.md)
(helping others see new perspectives without cult-like advocacy).
