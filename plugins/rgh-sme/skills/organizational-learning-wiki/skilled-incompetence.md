Chris Argyris observed that most managers find collective inquiry threatening.
School trains people never to admit not knowing; corporations reward advocating
one's views over inquiry. People learn to protect themselves from the pain of
appearing uncertain or ignorant.

The resulting condition is **skilled incompetence** — teams full of capable
people who are incredibly proficient at keeping themselves from learning.

This connects to [defensive routines](defensive-routines.md): entrenched habits
that protect from embarrassment while blocking learning about its causes.
Breaking skilled incompetence requires [dialogue](dialogue-versus-discussion.md)
and mutual inquiry rather than confronting others as "defensive."

Named in the context of the [myth of the management team](myth-of-the-management-team.md)
learning disability.
