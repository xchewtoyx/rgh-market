type: concept
title: Premortem Technique
description: >
  A structured team exercise that imagines a planned decision has already failed,
  surfacing suppressed doubts before commitment and countering post-decision groupthink.
sources:
  - title: "Thinking, Fast and Slow"
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 25"
---

Gary Klein proposed the **premortem** — a procedure organizations can use when
almost but not formally committed to an important decision. Gather knowledgeable
individuals and give this premise: "Imagine that we are a year into the future.
We implemented the plan as it now exists. The outcome was a disaster. Please take
5 to 10 minutes to write a brief history of that disaster."

**Two main advantages:**

1. Overcomes [groupthink](groupthink.md) that affects teams once a decision
   appears made.
2. Unleashes knowledgeable individuals' imagination in a much-needed direction.

**Mechanism:** as a team converges on a decision — especially once the leader
tips her hand — public doubts are gradually suppressed and eventually treated
as evidence of disloyalty. Suppression of doubt contributes to overconfidence
when only supporters have a voice. The premortem licenses dissent before
commitment — complementing [countering groupthink](countering-groupthink.md)
structures like devil's advocates and [precision questioning](precision-questioning.md).

Organizations may tame optimism better than individuals: subjective confidence
"is determined by the coherence of the story one has constructed, not by the
quality and amount of the information that supports it." The premortem is not a
panacea and does not fully protect against nasty surprises, but reduces damage
from plans subject to uncritical optimism.

Used in innovation vetting — Google X's Rapid Evaluation runs pre-mortems on
proposals — see [safe-to-fail innovation culture](safe-to-fail-innovation-culture.md).
Distinct from incident-triggered postmortems (incident-management domain); this
is a standing practice for candid planning before commitment, supporting
[psychological safety](psychological-safety.md) for surfacing threats the group
has neglected.
