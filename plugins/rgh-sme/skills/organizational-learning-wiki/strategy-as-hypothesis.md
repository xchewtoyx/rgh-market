---
type: concept
title: Strategy as Hypothesis
description: >
  Treating strategy as a hypothesis to be tested continuously — using
  front-line data to adapt rather than punishing disconfirming signals.
sources:
  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 3"
---

In volatile, uncertain, complex, and ambiguous conditions, an organization's
strategy works best as a **hypothesis to be continually tested** — not a fixed
plan assumed to hold until proven otherwise.

Like any hypothesis, strategy begins with situation assessment but must then
be tested through action. Customer encounters, field observations, and
engineering findings generate data of direct interest to senior executives —
not merely execution-tracking against a plan frozen in the past.

When Wells Fargo's cross-sell targets collided with customers' actual spending
capacity, repeated missed targets and fabricated accounts were strategic
signals. Treating them as employee underperformance — firing thousands while
keeping the "Going for Gr-Eight" goal — cemented failure. Had leadership read
the data as testing the cross-selling hypothesis, it should have triggered
strategic adaptation.

Sometimes a performance gap genuinely is underperformance requiring better
motivation. But in a VUCA world that is not even the most likely explanation.
Gaps between results and plans should first trigger analysis, not automatic
proof of execution failure. "Cheating and covering up are natural by-products
of a top-down culture that does not accept 'no' or 'it can't be done' for an
answer."

Success requires leaders to engage thoughtfully and frequently with operations
across levels, because front-line staff hold the most important strategic data:
what customers want, what competitors do, what technology allows. Organizational
learning must be championed by leaders but enacted by everyone — actively
seeking deviations that challenge current assumptions and welcoming them for
their informative value.

This framing depends on [psychological safety](psychological-safety.md): without
it, disconfirming signals never reach decision-makers and the
[illusion of success from silence](illusion-of-success-from-silence.md)
persists. Pushing harder on execution when the underlying problem is flawed
market intelligence can worsen outcomes.
