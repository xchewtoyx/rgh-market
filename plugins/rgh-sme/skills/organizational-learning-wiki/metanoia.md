title: Metanoia
description: >
  A fundamental shift of mind — from seeing ourselves as separate from problems
  to seeing how our own actions create the reality we experience.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 1"
---

Senge uses **metanoia** for the deepest meaning of what happens in a learning
organization: "a shift of mind." Etymology: Greek *meta* (above/beyond) +
*noia*/*nous* (mind). In early Gnostic Christian tradition it meant awakening
shared intuition and direct knowing of the highest; in the Catholic corpus it
was eventually translated as "repent."

Senge distinguishes real learning from the degraded sense of "taking in
information" (e.g., attending training). Real learning "gets to the heart of
what it means to be human": through it we re-create ourselves, become able to
do what we couldn't before, reperceive the world and our relationship to it,
and extend our capacity to create. Edward Hall: "Humans are the learning
organism par excellence."

The shift is concrete: from seeing ourselves as separate from the world to
connected to it; from seeing problems as caused by someone/something "out
there" to seeing how our own actions create the problems we experience. "A
learning organization is a place where people are continually discovering how
they create their reality. And how they can change it."

[Systems thinking as learning language](systems-thinking-as-learning-language.md)
makes this shift understandable; without [personal mastery](team-learning.md)'s
motivation to see how one's actions affect the world, people remain in a
reactive mindset — "someone/something else is creating my problems."
