type: concept
title: Dangerous Silence
description: >
  When fear of speaking up about safety or wrongdoing leads to preventable
  physical harm — illustrated by high-risk industry accidents.
sources:
  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 4"
---

Low [psychological safety](psychological-safety.md) is not only a
performance problem. When people see something physically unsafe or wrong and
fear reporting it, silence can cause widespread frustration, anxiety, and
physical harm.

**Columbia (2003):** An engineer suspected foam damage but felt he "couldn't"
speak in a meeting with senior leadership present — not "chose not to," but
was literally unable. Hierarchy made salient silences those higher up often
cannot see.

**Tenerife (1977):** A co-pilot noted missing takeoff clearance but did not
push back against an irritated captain; a flight engineer asked about runway
clearance but did not challenge the captain's reassurance. Edmondson cautions
against exhorting "more backbone" — that puts the onus on individuals without
creating conditions where speaking up is expected. Post-Tenerife, aviation
institutionalized crew resource management: co-pilots assert, captains listen.

**Healthcare:** A patient died from a four-day chemotherapy overdose that
multiple staff noticed but no one persisted in questioning — deference to
authority and informal communication left no routine checks.

The lesson: a climate favoring silence over raising concerns is a serious risk
factor. It is better to ask and be wrong than to hold back.
