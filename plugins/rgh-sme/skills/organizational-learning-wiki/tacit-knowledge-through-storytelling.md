type: concept
title: Tacit Knowledge Through Storytelling
description: >
  Moving organizational know-how through narratives, experience-sharing, and
  review rituals rather than capture-and-disseminate databases alone.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 13"
---

Organizational learning depends on **knowledge flow as a social phenomenon** —
networks of collaboration and shared experience, not only formal knowledge-
management repositories.

The Singapore Police Force connected officers to tacit knowledge by capturing
critical-incident accounts as narratives, running experience-sharing sessions,
implementing After Action Reviews, and maintaining an unmoderated electronic
bulletin board where "what you read is what our police officers actually feel
and think." Standard operating procedures were rewritten to stress principles
rather than prescriptive instructions — enabling discretion grounded in shared
stories of practice.

Commissioner Khoo's theory: "it is the people who are the drivers of any
organizational transformation. Trust and focusing on how people relate to one
another form the basis... As the quality of relationships strengthens, the
quality of thinking improves."

Storytelling complements [practice fields for management teams](practice-fields-for-management-teams.md)
and [team learning](team-learning.md): tacit knowledge moves when people recount
what happened under pressure, not when documents alone are filed.

Contrast with capture-and-disseminate knowledge management that ignores the
social mechanics of who trusts whom enough to share what they know.

Pairs with [psychological safety](psychological-safety.md) as the climate that
makes honest narratives possible.
