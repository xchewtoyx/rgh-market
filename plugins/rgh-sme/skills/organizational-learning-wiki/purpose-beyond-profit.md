---
type: concept
title: Purpose Beyond Profit
description: >
  Profitability is a performance requirement for staying in the game, not an
  organization's purpose — treating it as purpose tends toward financial mediocrity.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 12"
---

Peter Drucker: "making money for a company is like oxygen for a person; if you
don't have enough of it you're out of the game." Profitability is a **performance
requirement**, not a purpose. Companies that treat profit as purpose are "like
people who think life is about breathing" — and studies of long-term business
performance suggest equating purpose with the bottom line tends to doom the
enterprise to financial mediocrity.

Goran Carstedt (Volvo, IKEA): creating an environment for growth starts with
"having a purpose worthy of people's commitment." The real question is not whether
people are committed to organizational goals, but "what is the organization
committed to, and is that worth my time?" Despite abundant vision/values
statements, cynicism persists about where a company's real commitment lies.

This connects to [shared vision as common caring](shared-vision-as-common-caring.md)
and [enrollment versus compliance](enrollment-vs-compliance.md): people enroll
in visions they care about, not slogans. [Personal mastery as organizational commitment](personal-mastery-as-organizational-commitment.md)
requires purposes worth pursuing — Tantawy-Monsou aligned her career with
Unilever's sustainability agenda when the company's commitment to sustainable
fishing made the purpose genuine.

Leaders who [set the stage for candor](setting-the-stage-for-candor.md) emphasize
purpose so people understand why learning and voice matter beyond quarterly targets.
