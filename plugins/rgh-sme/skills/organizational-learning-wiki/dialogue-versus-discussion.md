---
type: concept
title: Dialogue Versus Discussion
description: >
  Two distinct team conversation modes — dialogue diverges to access collective
  meaning; discussion converges to decide among alternative views.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 11"
---

[Team learning](team-learning.md) requires mastering two ways teams converse,
from David Bohm's theory:

**Discussion** shares a root with percussion and concussion — a ping-pong of
views where the implicit goal is to win, to have one's view prevail. Different
views are presented and defended to support a decision that must be made now.
Discussions **converge** on a conclusion.

**Dialogue** (Greek *dialogos*, "meaning flowing through") accesses a larger
pool of common meaning not available individually — "the whole organizes the
parts." Purpose is to go beyond any one person's understanding; "we all win if
we are doing it right." Dialogues **diverge**; they seek richer grasp of
complex issues, not agreement.

Bohm's purpose for dialogue: reveal incoherence in thought. Three conditions:

1. **[Suspend assumptions](suspending-assumptions.md)** — hold them accessible
   to questioning, not defended or suppressed.
2. **Regard one another as colleagues** — even when views differ; hierarchy is
   antithetical to dialogue unless seniors surrender privilege of prevailing.
3. **A facilitator holds context** — catching drift into discussion until skill
   dissolves the role into the group.

A learning team masters movement back and forth. Failing to distinguish them,
teams usually have neither dialogue nor productive discussion. Regular dialogue
builds trust that carries into discussions and teaches holding views "gently."

Distinct from convergent, decision-producing conversation in decision-alignment
domains — dialogue deliberately does not aim at a decision.
