title: Limits to Growth Archetype
description: >
  A reinforcing success process eventually hits a balancing limit — leverage
  lies in removing the constraint, not pushing harder on growth.
sources:

In the **limits to growth** archetype, a reinforcing process produces desired
results — then creates secondary effects that form a balancing loop slowing
or reversing success.

**Management principle:** don't push growth; remove factors limiting growth.

Examples span personal habit change, organizational growth plateaus, and change
efforts where initial implementation gains trigger resistance from control-
oriented managers, delayed metrics, or culture-conservers. Advocates who push
harder in response often worsen resistance — the natural but wrong response.

**Leverage lies in the balancing loop**, not the reinforcing loop: identify
and change the limiting factor. A high-tech firm's new-product growth can stall
when senior engineers absorb management burden and have less engineering time,
lengthening development cycles. A professional firm's morale spiral reverses
when promotion opportunities shrink after growth slows.

There is always another limiting factor waiting; skillful leaders continually
look for the next constraint. In some domains, removing limits to extend growth
only forestalls a reckoning that reinforcing dynamics bring sooner than expected.

language; pairs with [boiled frog](boiled-frog.md) when gradual limits go
unnoticed.
