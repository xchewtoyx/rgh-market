---
type: concept
title: Psychological Safety and Learning Behavior
description: >
  Research links psychological safety to interdependent learning — reporting
  errors, sharing knowledge, and fixing root problems rather than workarounds.
sources:
  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 2"
---

Across 100+ studies in companies, hospitals, schools, and government
agencies, [psychological safety](psychological-safety.md) predicts learning
behaviors that require interpersonal risk.

**Error reporting.** In Belgian hospital departments, higher psychological
safety correlated with more reported errors — echoing the
[error-reporting paradox](error-reporting-paradox.md). When nurses also
believed patient safety was a high priority, fewer errors were actually made;
low safety produced more errors despite professed commitment to safety.

**Learn-how versus learn-what.** In NICU quality-improvement teams,
psychological safety predicted team-based "learn-how" activities (sharing
knowledge, brainstorming) but not solo "learn-what" activities (reading
literature). Safety helps people overcome interpersonal risk for
interdependent learning, not for independent study.

**Workarounds.** Cancer care teams with low psychological safety relied more
on shortcuts that accomplished immediate goals but silenced signals that
systems needed fixing. High-safety teams diagnosed and addressed root problems.

**Low-confidence ideas.** Psychological safety helped people speak up when
they were uncertain about their knowledge — surfacing good-but-uncertain
ideas. Communication frequency also fed back into building safety.
