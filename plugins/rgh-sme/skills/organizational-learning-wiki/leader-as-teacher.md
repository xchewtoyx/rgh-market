title: Leader as Teacher
description: >
  Leadership that creates space for learning by helping people see reality and
  build capacity — starting from the leader's own learning.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 15"
---

"A great teacher is someone around whom others learn" — creating space for
learning and inviting people into it, rather than focusing only on content
delivered.

Teaching work often starts from recognizing a **gap in organizational capacity**.
Vivienne Cox's BP "salons" — agenda-free conversations with diverse participants
— seeded similar practices elsewhere without her direct control. Her lesson on
scale: focus on few, carefully considered interventions; control construction
of space and intention, not outcomes.

The **teacher's dilemma**: helping people see problems as systemic structures
takes time, while pressure demands quick fixes. Quietly launching work streams
from systems diagrams can hit targets without building broader capacity —
creating dependence on experts (a shifting-the-burden trap). Strategy: respond
to short-term opportunities in ways that also build longer-term capacity.

**To be a true teacher, you must be a learner first.** Effective leaders were
"inevitably real learners" whose past accomplishments figured little in their
self-image. Even highly committed advocates can be blind to their own need to
learn — as when a peacemaking training "blew up" and the leader recognized her
own divisiveness.

[designer](leader-as-designer.md) and [steward](leader-as-steward.md) roles.
