---
type: concept
title: Epidemic of Workplace Silence
description: >
  Widespread withholding of questions, ideas, and concerns even when people
  believe speaking up could matter for the organization or themselves.
sources:
  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 2"
---

Multiple studies document how common workplace silence is: people not asking a
question they wanted to ask, or not offering an idea. They often hold back
even when they believe what they have to say could matter for the
organization, the customer, or themselves — and later report regret or pain
over their silence.

Milliken, Morrison, and Hewlin interviewed employees across industries and
found every interviewee reported failing to speak up on at least one occasion,
usually about something they were very concerned about. Top reasons: fear of
being viewed negatively, fear of damaging relationships, futility, and fear
of retaliation. Issues withheld ranged from harassment and supervisor
competence to process-improvement suggestions.

[Psychological safety](psychological-safety.md) is not a perk like free lunch.
In knowledge-dependent organizations — especially under volatility and
interdependence — it is a requirement for people to use the talent they were
hired for. A CEO's greatest fear may be not knowing what is really going on.
