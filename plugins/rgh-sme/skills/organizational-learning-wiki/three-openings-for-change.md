description: >
  Otto Scharmer's thresholds of head, heart, and will — inner orientations that
  determine whether learning interventions produce profound results.

  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"

---

Over decades of organizational learning work, some interventions attain remarkable
results and some accomplish very little — and the difference is not intelligence,
commitment, position, or power. Bill O'Brien: "The primary determinant of the
outcome of an intervention is the inner state of the intervenor."

Otto Scharmer describes three **thresholds** for leading profound change:

1. **Opening the head (suspension)** — seeing what is in front of us but have
   not yet been able to see; [suspending taken-for-granted assumptions](suspending-assumptions.md).
   Signal: people treat opinions less as facts, develop humor, lighten up — "a
   sense of real inquiry is developing" (BP's Vivienne Cox).

2. **Opening the heart** — seeing connection to pain, suffering, and joy around
   us; moving beyond comfortable stories that blame outside forces; seeing "we
   too are part of the problem." Requires vulnerability few managers traditionally
   cultivate (Ford's Roger Saillant: bosses never asked how he achieved turnaround
   results, sensing it required vulnerability they weren't prepared to feel).

3. **Opening the will** — letting go of the small self; allowing intentions and
   strategies to be molded by forces larger than individual will; willingness
   to let go of predetermined agendas.

Roger Saillant on passing through: "If you can achieve real innocence... gifts
arrive... It's the place where miracles come from." Marianne Knuth: "In the
beginning was the meeting... How we meet people determines all else."

Connects to [metanoia](metanoia.md), [dialogue versus discussion](dialogue-versus-discussion.md),
and [leader as teacher](leader-as-teacher.md) — the inner state enabling others
to learn.
