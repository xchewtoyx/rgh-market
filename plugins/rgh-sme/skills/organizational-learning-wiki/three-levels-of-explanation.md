title: Three Levels of Explanation
description: >
  Event, pattern-of-behavior, and structural explanations — equally true but
  vastly different in usefulness for generative organizational learning.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 3"
---

Senge distinguishes three levels of explanation for any complex situation, all
"equally true" but with very different usefulness:

1. **Event explanations** ("who did what to whom") — most common in contemporary
   culture; reason reactive management prevails. E.g., "I ordered forty at Week
   11 because my retailers ordered thirty-six." Holding only event explanations
   dooms reactiveness — see [fixation on events](fixation-on-events.md).

2. **Pattern-of-behavior explanations** — focus on longer-term trends, e.g.,
   "production/distribution systems are inherently prone to cycles." These break
   the grip of short-term reactiveness.

3. **Structural explanations** — rarest and most powerful; answer "what causes
   the patterns of behavior?" Show how orders, shipments, and inventory interact
   given built-in [delays](delays-in-systems.md) to generate instability.
   Structural explanations are "inherently generative": since structure produces
   behavior, and structure in human systems includes decision makers' operating
   policies, redesigning our own decision making redesigns the system's structure.

Franklin Roosevelt's 1933 banking-holiday radio address exemplifies a leader
delivering structural explanation under pressure — explaining how banks invest
deposits, why reserves fail against mass withdrawals, and why a four-day closure
was necessary — generating public support for radical action.

The beer game shows most players do worse than a "no strategy" baseline (order
exactly what you received) despite trying hard — improvement requires a shift
in [mental models](mental-models-as-organizational-routine.md), not more effort.
Guidelines: "Take two aspirin and wait" (account for pipeline delay); don't panic
and order more when backlogs build.

[Generative learning](adaptive-vs-generative-learning.md) cannot be sustained
where event thinking predominates. Connects to [structure influences behavior](structure-influences-behavior.md).
