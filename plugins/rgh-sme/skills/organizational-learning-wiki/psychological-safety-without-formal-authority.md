type: concept
title: Psychological Safety Without Formal Authority
description: >
  Anyone can contribute to psychological safety through questions, listening,
  framing, and vulnerability — not only people with positional power.
sources:
  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 8"
---

Bosses play an outsized role in shaping [psychological safety](psychological-safety.md),
but anyone can contribute:

- **Ask a good question** motivated by genuine curiosity — creating a small
  safe zone by conveying interest in what someone has to say.
- **Listen actively** and build on ideas — conveying respect without requiring
  agreement.
- **Frame the challenge** — remind people work is uncertain or interdependent,
  signaling no one is supposed to have all the answers.
- **Use vulnerability phrases** — "I don't know," "I need help," "I made a
  mistake," "I'm sorry" — giving others permission to do likewise.
- **Offer availability** — "What can I do to help?" "What are you up against?"

The personal challenge is remembering, in the moment, to be vulnerable and
interested. People frustrated that "those above me don't do this" can still
create a pocket of excellence in their own group — and it may spread.

"You don't have to be the boss to be a leader."
