---
type: concept
title: Implicit Theories of Voice
description: >
  Taken-for-granted beliefs about when and how it is appropriate to speak up at
  work — often learned and rarely examined.
sources:
  - title: The Fearless Organization
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Edmondson), ch. 2"
---

Detert and Edmondson identified common, rarely-examined beliefs about speaking
up — **implicit theories of voice** — from interviews across a large
multinational company. Illustrative rules:

1. Don't criticize something the boss may have helped create.
2. Don't speak unless you have solid data.
3. Don't speak up if the boss's boss is present.
4. Don't raise anything negative in a group — brief the boss one-on-one instead.
5. Speaking up brings career consequences.

A follow-up vignette study found managers were significantly less likely to
share an important correction when the boss's boss was present — even when the
correction was warranted.

Related folk wisdom: suggesting a change risks implying the boss's creation
("the boss's baby") is flawed — "calling the boss's baby ugly." Silence feels
safer even at the cost of depriving the organization of ideas.

Leaders who want candor must surface and challenge these inherited rules rather
than assume people will speak when they "should."
