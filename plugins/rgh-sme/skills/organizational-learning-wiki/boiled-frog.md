---
type: concept
title: Boiled Frog
description: >
  A learning disability of failing to sense slow, gradual threats because
  threat-detection is geared to sudden change.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 2"
---

A frog dropped into boiling water scrambles out immediately. A frog in water
heated gradually will not sense the mounting threat and will sit until it boils
— threat apparatus is geared to sudden changes, not slow gradual ones.

The American auto industry's response to Japanese market share illustrates
the pattern: share rose slowly for decades before Detroit critically examined
its own practices.

Learning to see slow processes requires slowing down and attending to the
subtle as well as the dramatic — "our minds are so locked in one frequency,
it's as if we can only see at 78 rpm; we can't see anything at 33-1/3."

One of seven [learning disabilities](seven-learning-disabilities.md);
complements [fixation on events](fixation-on-events.md).
