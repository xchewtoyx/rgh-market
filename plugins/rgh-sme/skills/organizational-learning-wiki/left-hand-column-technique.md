  reveal how mental models drive unproductive exchanges.

The **left-hand column** technique from Chris Argyris's action science makes
visible how [mental models](mental-models-as-organizational-routine.md) operate
in a specific situation.

Pick an unproductive exchange. Write the actual dialogue on the right-hand
side. On the left, write what you were thinking but not saying at each point.

A surface exchange about a colleague's failed presentation may reveal hidden
assumptions: Bill lacks confidence (so the author avoids direct feedback) and
Bill lacks initiative (so the author plans to "manufacture pressure" rather
than state a view). Neither assumption may be true, but both drove behavior.
Both parties talk around the problem; no clear course of action emerges.

Seeing one's own contribution is the necessary start. Moving forward requires
sharing one's view and its data while staying open that the other person may
not share the view or the data — either may be wrong. Reframing the exchange
so both can learn is what Argyris calls
[balancing inquiry and advocacy](balancing-inquiry-and-advocacy.md).

Connects to [defensive routines](defensive-routines.md): the left-hand column
often exposes how we protect ourselves from learning situations rather than
address issues directly.
