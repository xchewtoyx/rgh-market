---
type: concept
title: Networks of Collaboration
description: >
  Knowledge flows through social collaboration networks — not capture-and-
  disseminate databases — and strengthens when people reflect on who they work with.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 12"
---

Anne Murray Allen (HP Ink Supply Organization) moved away from "lessons learned"
databases and codify-and-capture knowledge-management approaches that produced
little payoff industry-wide: "The problem starts with not understanding knowledge...
because **knowledge is social**. Knowledge is what we know how to do, and we do
things with one another. That is how the work gets done. **Collaboration is the
flip side of knowledge management.**"

Her group shifted to **networks of collaboration** — an organic process that can
nonetheless be understood and supported. With social-network researcher Dennis
Sandow, engineers mapped social networks tied to different technical capabilities;
Sandow "taught the engineers how to become their own network analysts" rather
than doing analysis for them — giving "a whole new language for communicating
with managers" about reorganization consequences.

"Networks of knowledge expand and become stronger through reflection... When we
think about who we collaborate with, and together reflect on our process of
collaboration, we legitimize one another." Example: mapping the HP–vendor system
for a materials-chemistry problem built trust and mutual visibility ("people felt
seen"), reducing a new ink-jet cartridge's development time by 16 weeks after
weeks of collaboration.

Contrasts with capture-and-disseminate knowledge management in
[tacit knowledge through storytelling](tacit-knowledge-through-storytelling.md).
Part of [learning infrastructure beyond training](learning-infrastructure-beyond-training.md)
and [organizations as living systems](organizations-as-living-systems.md) —
knowledge flow as a social phenomenon. Requires [legitimacy of the other](legitimacy-of-the-other.md)
for network members to accept each other as participants.
