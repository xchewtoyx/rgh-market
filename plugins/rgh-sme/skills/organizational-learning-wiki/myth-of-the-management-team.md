---
type: concept
title: Myth of the Management Team
description: >
  A learning disability where management teams avoid embarrassment, producing
  watered-down compromises instead of collective inquiry.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 2"
---

Business teams often spend time fighting for turf, avoiding anything that makes
them look bad, and pretending everyone backs the collective strategy. Disagreement
is squelched; joint decisions become compromises or one person's view imposed.

Chris Argyris: "Most management teams break down under pressure... The team may
function quite well with routine issues. But when they confront complex issues
that may be embarrassing or threatening, the 'teamness' seems to go to pot."

School and corporations reward advocacy over inquiry into complex issues. People
protect themselves from appearing uncertain — blocking understanding that might
threaten them. The result is [skilled incompetence](skilled-incompetence.md):
teams "full of people who are incredibly proficient at keeping themselves from
learning."

One of seven [learning disabilities](seven-learning-disabilities.md).
