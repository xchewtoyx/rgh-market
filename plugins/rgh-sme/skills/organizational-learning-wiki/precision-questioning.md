**Precision questioning** (Dennis Matthies and Monica Worline, via Tetlock)
is how teams practice constructive confrontation — Andy Grove's term for
disagreeing without being disagreeable. It targets claims, not people.

Given the claim "the popularity of soccer as a pastime is starting to decline,"
bad responses attack the person ("you're silly"), flatly dismiss ("I don't think
so"), or stay too vague ("what do you mean?"). Good responses zero in: "What do
you mean by 'pastime'?" or "What evidence is there that soccer's popularity is
declining? Over what time frame?" Answers need not settle the matter; they
expose reasoning so the group can test it.

Applied retroactively to the Bay of Pigs contingency plan, precision questioning
("How far is it from the landing site to the Escambray Mountains?" "Eighty miles."
"And what's the terrain?" "Mostly swamp and jungle.") would likely have surfaced
the plan's fatal flaw before launch.

Precision questioning pairs with [balancing inquiry and advocacy](balancing-inquiry-and-advocacy.md)
and [countering groupthink](countering-groupthink.md). It is one way teams achieve
[productive conflict in learning teams](productive-conflict-in-learning-teams.md)
without [defensive routines](defensive-routines.md) — the opposite failure mode
of rancor and flame-war dynamics that [team collective open-mindedness](team-collective-open-mindedness.md)
must also avoid.
