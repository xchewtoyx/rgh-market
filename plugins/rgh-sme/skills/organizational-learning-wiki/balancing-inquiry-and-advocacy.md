---
type: concept
title: Balancing Inquiry and Advocacy
description: >
  Collaborative learning skill of making one's reasoning explicit while
  genuinely exploring others' views — neither pure advocacy nor pure inquiry.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Senge), ch. 9"
---

Most managers are trained purely as **advocates** — competence means figuring
out what needs doing and enlisting support. Inquiry skills go unrecognized.
As issues exceed personal experience, pure advocacy becomes counterproductive.

Two advocates in "open, candid" exchange rarely learn: positions harden into
an escalation archetype structurally like an arms race. Simple inquiry questions
("What leads you to that position?") can interrupt escalation — but **pure
inquiry is also limited**. Endless questioning while hiding one's own view
avoids learning because we almost always do have a view.

**Reciprocal inquiry** makes thinking explicit and open to examination:
"Here is my view and how I arrived at it. How does it sound to you?" The goal
shifts from winning the argument to finding the best argument. Under pure
advocacy, data is used selectively; under balanced inquiry/advocacy, disconfirming
data and weaknesses in one's reasoning are actively sought.

Guidelines include: when advocating, make reasoning explicit and invite others
to explore gaps; when inquiring, state assumptions clearly and ask only questions
you genuinely want answered; at impasse, ask what data might change views or
propose joint experiments; when exchange is blocked, name what makes openness
difficult.

These are "training wheels" — useful on rough terrain, discardable as skill
grows. They require genuine curiosity and **willingness to be wrong** — nothing
less makes it safe for others to do likewise.

The discipline's goal is not agreement. Many mental models can coexist and be
tested against situations. Hanover's Bill O'Brien: "We don't have any anointed
mental models, we have a philosophy of mental modeling." Imposing a favored
model on others usually backfires.
