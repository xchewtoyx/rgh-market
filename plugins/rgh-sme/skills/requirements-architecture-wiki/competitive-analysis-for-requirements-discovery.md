type: concept
title: Competitive Analysis as a Requirements Discovery Technique
description: >
  Comparing a product against competitors and adjacent-category tools
  along a shared set of dimensions surfaces feature gaps and
  opportunities that interviews with your own stakeholders won't reveal.
sources:
  - title: "Agile Software Requirements: Lean Requirements Practices for Teams, Programs, and the Enterprise"
    resource: "Agile Software Requirements: Lean Requirements Practices for Teams, Programs, and the Enterprise (Dean Leffingwell), ch. 12"
---

Competitive analysis is a discovery technique distinct from
[elicitation from stakeholders](requirements-elicitation-techniques.md):
instead of asking your own users and experts what they need, build a
comparison matrix scoring a candidate product against direct competitors
*and* against tools from adjacent categories, along a shared set of
dimensions relevant to the product's domain. The adjacent-category
comparison matters as much as the direct-competitor one — a tool solving a
related problem in an unrelated category can surface a dimension your
domain's direct competitors all happen to share a blind spot on, which
interviewing only your own stakeholders (who are anchored to what they
already know your product's category can do) is unlikely to surface on
its own.

The output is a candidate list of feature gaps and opportunities to feed
into the rest of discovery — it identifies *where to look* for missing
requirements, not a finished, validated requirement in itself; a gap found
this way still needs the same scrutiny (rationale, [fit
criterion](fit-criterion.md), stakeholder confirmation) as one surfaced
through any other technique before it's treated as settled.
