title: Interview Convergence as a Discovery Stopping Signal
description: >
  Recording each interviewee's top few needs and watching them start to
  repeat across separate interviews is an early, concrete signal that
  discovery is converging, rather than having to guess when to stop.
sources:
  - title: "Agile Software Requirements: Lean Requirements Practices for Teams, Programs, and the Enterprise"

A practical discipline for [interviewing](requirements-elicitation-techniques.md):
close every interview by asking the stakeholder to name their three most
important needs or problems, and record that short list separately from
the rest of the interview notes. The value shows up across interviews, not
within one: after only a handful of interviews, the same handful of
high-priority needs typically start recurring across different
stakeholders' top-three lists.

That repetition is itself useful information — a concrete, observable
signal that discovery is converging on the real set of important needs,
rather than a subjective judgment call about whether "enough" interviews
have happened. It also gives the emerging feature backlog an early seed:
the needs that keep reappearing across independent stakeholders are the
ones with the strongest claim to being genuine (not one person's
idiosyncratic preference), well before formal
[prioritization](requirements-prioritization.md) begins.
