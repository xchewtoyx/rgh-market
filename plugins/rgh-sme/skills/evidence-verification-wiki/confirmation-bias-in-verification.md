# Confirmation Bias in Verification

**Confirmation bias** in a verification context is the tendency to accept, share, or under-scrutinize a claim because it matches the reviewer's pre-existing beliefs, while applying disproportionately harsh scrutiny to claims that conflict with those beliefs. "At any given time, the chief source of bullshit with which you have to contend is yourself."

This is distinct from [knowledge bias in verification](knowledge-bias-in-verification.md): knowledge bias is skipping a check because the claim seems *familiar*, while confirmation bias is skipping a check because the claim seems *agreeable* — the two often co-occur but fail for different reasons, and a reviewer can be vulnerable to confirmation bias on an unfamiliar claim in a domain they hold strong priors about.

## Worked Example
A viral chart appearing to show stark, systematic gender-biased language in academic recommendation letters was shared widely, including by reviewers who correctly believed such bias exists generally. What triggered scrutiny in one case was not doubt about the underlying belief, but that the pattern looked "too clean" for real human-behavior data (real patterns are typically noisier) — investigation traced the chart back to the original paper and found it depicted the study's *hypothesized* categories, not its actual *results*; the real results showed letters for different groups were more similar than different. The claim was shared thousands of times before this was caught, precisely because it matched what many sharers already believed.

## Unfalsifiable Authority: Every Outcome Confirms the Belief
Galen, dominant medical authority for over a millennium, treated every patient outcome as confirming his theories: recoveries proved the treatment worked; deaths proved the case was incurable — "obvious, therefore, that it fails only in incurable cases." That pattern makes a claim immune to disconfirmation and is indistinguishable from [cargo cult science](cargo-cult-science.md) dressed in expert credentials. A reviewer should treat any account where success and failure both get reinterpreted to support the same conclusion as structurally unverified, regardless of the author's status.

## Verification Action
- Apply the same verification rigor to claims that confirm your prior beliefs as to claims that challenge them — a claim's directional agreement with what you already think is true is not evidence of its accuracy.
- Treat "too clean," suspiciously tidy results as a specific trigger for extra scrutiny regardless of whether the conclusion is one you'd like to be true.
- When a chart or figure appears to summarize a study's results, trace it back to the primary source to confirm it actually reports results, and not a hypothesis, framework, or illustrative diagram.

## Expertise Does Not Protect Against This — It Can Sharpen It
Being knowledgeable or numerically sophisticated is not a defense against confirmation bias on emotionally or politically charged claims, and can make it worse. A controlled study gave subjects identical fabricated numeric data, framed once as a neutral skin-cream effectiveness trial and once as a politically charged gun-control statistic. On the neutral framing, subjects with higher numeracy interpreted the data more accurately than less numerate subjects, as expected. On the charged framing, higher numeracy instead *increased* the rate of politically-motivated misreading of the identical numbers — more skilled subjects were better at constructing a self-serving interpretation, not more resistant to bias. A reviewer's own subject-matter expertise or quantitative skill should not be treated as a safeguard against motivated reasoning on a claim they have a stake in — if anything, a reviewer with more analytical firepower needs the structural countermeasures below more, not less, on exactly the claims they feel most equipped to judge.

## A Structural Countermeasure: Record Disconfirming Evidence Immediately
Because confirmation bias operates largely unconsciously, deliberately trying to "be more objective" in the moment is a weak defense — disconfirming material is disproportionately likely to be noticed and then quietly forgotten before it gets used. A stronger, structural countermeasure (Darwin's own lifelong practice): make it a standing rule to immediately write down any fact, source, or observation that runs counter to the conclusion you're building toward, at the moment you encounter it — before it has a chance to be rationalized away or simply fade. This converts a one-time willpower demand ("stay objective") into a habitual, low-effort recording action, which is much more reliably sustained. A related reframe that helps during source-gathering specifically: stop sorting new evidence on a confirm/disconfirm axis relative to a conclusion you already favor, and sort it instead on a relevant/irrelevant axis — evidence that disconfirms a working conclusion is exactly as relevant, and exactly as worth capturing, as evidence that supports it, which removes the incentive to under-collect the inconvenient half.
