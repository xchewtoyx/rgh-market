# Heterogeneous Benchmarking

A **heterogeneous benchmark** gives an estimator a known reference quantity, even one only loosely related to what's being estimated, to establish a sense of scale for an otherwise ungrounded judgment. Comparisons are elicited in relative terms ("about the same as," "half as bad as," "ten times worse than") rather than asking for an absolute number cold.

## Worked Example: Reputational "Brand Damage" from a Data Breach
A claim like "the reputational damage from a data breach can't be measured" was addressed by pairing hypothetical breach scenarios against known, real-cost benchmark events the estimators already had intuitions about ("how bad is losing email access for an hour?") and asking for relative-magnitude comparisons. Cross-checked against a larger evidence base — real forensic investigations of past breaches, public/executive perception surveys, and observed stock-price reactions to real incidents — this converted a claim previously treated as having no measurable scale at all into one with at least a defensible relative ranking (e.g., breach-by-hacking ranked as reliably worse than a misplaced backup tape), sufficient to prioritize security spending by expected relative loss.

## Why This Works
A quantity that feels totally ungrounded in isolation (there's no intuitive unit for "brand damage") becomes tractable once compared against something the estimator already has calibrated intuition for. This is a specific application of the broader finding that people spontaneously update estimates given even loosely related reference points — a comparable finding from a small-sample-estimation exercise showed telling participants unrelated reference weights (a paper clip, a dime) sharply tightened their estimate of an unfamiliar object's weight, evidence that people do this kind of relative-anchoring naturally once given something to anchor to.

## Verification Action
When a document or source declares a quantity impossible to estimate because "there's no unit for it" or "nothing to compare it to," check whether a heterogeneous benchmark exists or could be constructed — any known, better-measured event or quantity that a knowledgeable estimator could plausibly rank the claim against in relative terms. Treat "there's nothing to compare this to" as a claim to test, not accept, before concluding a quantity truly resists even rough measurement.
