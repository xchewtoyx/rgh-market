# Reductio ad Absurdum Refutation

**Reductio ad absurdum** accepts a claim's underlying assumptions or model at face value and pushes them further than the original claim did, to a conclusion that is self-evidently absurd. Because it works entirely inside the claim's own logic, it can discredit a flawed model without requiring the reviewer to bring in independent outside evidence.

## Worked Example
A published extrapolation of straight-line trends in sprint times predicted that women would out-sprint men by a specific future year. A rebuttal used the *exact same* linear model, extrapolated further into the future, to show it also predicted *negative* sprint times a few centuries later — a physically impossible result that discredits the naive linear-extrapolation model without needing to argue the underlying biology directly. The real flaw (that the model ignores physiological limits on running speed) is exposed by the model's own logic taken to its natural conclusion.

## Verification Action
When a draft relies on extrapolating a trend, formula, or model beyond the range it was fit to, check what the same extrapolation implies at more extreme values. If that extension produces an absurd or physically impossible result, the model's applicability at the originally-claimed range is also suspect, even if that specific claimed value looks individually plausible.
