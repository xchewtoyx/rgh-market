# Verification Markup Conventions

Using standardized markup conventions ensures that the verification status of every claim in a document is clear to the author, the reviewer, and editors. These markers help manage unresolved facts, flag uncertainties, and preserve an audit trail of decisions.

## Common Shorthand Markers
- **TK (To Come)**: Used as a placeholder in a draft when a fact, statistic, or citation is not yet available. "TK" is a deliberate non-word that stands out during text searches, preventing the placeholder from accidentally being published. 
  - *Verification Action*: A reviewer must treat every "TK" as an unverified gap. If a writer uses "TK" because a fact is hard to find, the reviewer must determine if the fact exists at all; if not, the claim must be removed.
- **CK (Check)**: Inserted in parentheses after a fact (e.g., `the system responded in 10ms (CK)`) to indicate that the writer is unsure of the claim and it is based on memory.
  - *Verification Action*: Unlike standard text which the author presents as true, "CK" flags high-priority targets that the reviewer must target immediately during [claim verification triage](claim-verification-triage.md).
- **Stet (Let it stand)**: A Latin-derived proofreading term used when a proposed correction is rejected, indicating that the original text should remain.
  - *Verification Action*: In a verification workflow, every "stet" represents a point where a reviewer's correction was overruled by an author or editor. The reviewer must maintain a written record of all stet decisions and the names of the individuals who made the call. This record functions as an audit trail if the published document contains an error that the reviewer had flagged — it establishes that the error was identified and the decision to publish anyway was made elsewhere, protecting the reviewer from being blamed for a defect they surfaced. Retain this record at least as long as the document could plausibly be revisited or disputed, not just until publication.
