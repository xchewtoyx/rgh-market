# Quote Verification Techniques

Verifying a quote is not just confirming the words are accurate — a verbatim quote can still mislead if it is clipped out of context, mistranscribed, or embeds a factual claim outside the speaker's expertise.

## Checking Against Recordings and Transcripts
- **Audio recordings**: always listen before and after the quoted moment to confirm the surrounding context matches the way the document uses the quote.
- **Transcripts**: only as reliable as the transcriber (human or AI) — both err on jargon, poor audio, accents, or background noise. A transcription error can silently substitute a plausible-sounding wrong word for the real one, so a quote drawn only from a transcript should be checked against the underlying audio wherever possible.

## Checking Directly with the Source
Most publications forbid reading a quote back to a source verbatim (the source may recant it or try to "improve" it after the fact). Alternative techniques, in increasing order of directness:
1. Ask several related questions and see if the source's spontaneous answer matches the original quote in content *and* tone (sarcasm vs. earnestness).
2. Paraphrase the quote's content and ask if it's accurate, without revealing that it's a direct quote.
3. Tell the source you're paraphrasing a direct quote to check its accuracy, while clarifying that only factual matters (not phrasing) can be changed.
4. Read the quote back verbatim and disclose the outlet's editorial policy — used only where the publication permits it.

## Embedded Factual Claims
Check factual claims that appear *inside* a quote but fall outside the speaker's own expertise or firsthand knowledge (e.g., a speaker referencing an anecdote about someone they couldn't plausibly have met). If such an embedded claim is false, the quote may need to be cut or replaced even though the words themselves were accurately transcribed — see [source authority scope](source-authority-scope.md) for the general principle that a speaker's authority is scoped to their specific expertise, not a blanket property of who they are.
