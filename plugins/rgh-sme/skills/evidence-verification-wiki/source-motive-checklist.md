# Source Motive Checklist

A fast first-pass filter for any claim, before deeper verification work begins, is three questions:
1. **Who is telling me this?**
2. **How do they know it?**
3. **What are they trying to sell me?**

## "Selling" Is Broader Than Commerce
The third question applies even when no product is involved — a source can be "selling" an ideology, a political framing, or a reputation, not just a physical good. A claim with an obvious ideological or reputational stake behind it warrants the same scrutiny as one with a commercial one.

## Applying the Checklist
- If the answer to "how do they know it" reveals no plausible mechanism for the source to actually have that knowledge (no credentials, no access, no evidence trail), treat the claim as unverified regardless of how confidently it's stated.
- If a claim originates from an unaccountable source with no plausible corroboration path (e.g., an anonymous social account rather than an outlet with editorial or institutional accountability), and no independent outlet would plausibly have missed reporting it if true, that absence of corroboration is itself a red flag.
- Combine this with an explicit check for what the source stands to gain if the claim is believed — this overlaps with, but is broader than, financial [conflict of interest](evidence-weighting-vs-false-balance.md).

A source's revenue model is itself a "what are they trying to sell me" signal — see [engagement incentive distortion](engagement-incentive-distortion.md) for how click-driven economics produces detectable headline and framing patterns.
