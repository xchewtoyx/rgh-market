# Evidence Strength vs. Effect Magnitude

A claim can conflate two independent axes: how strong/robust the *evidence* is that a relationship exists, and how large the relationship's *real-world effect* actually is. Classification systems that rank by evidence strength are especially prone to being misread as ranking by risk magnitude.

## Named Case: IARC Classification Confusion
The WHO's International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) ranks substances by the *strength of evidence* that they cause cancer, not by the *degree of risk* they pose. In 2015, news coverage wrongly implied that bacon (placed in Group 1, the same evidence tier as tobacco) was "as likely to cause cancer as tobacco." Both are in Group 1 because the evidence for a causal link is equally robust for each — not because the magnitude of risk is equal; bacon consumption is far less risky in absolute terms.

## Verification Action
When a draft cites a classification, rating, or tier system, confirm what axis the system actually measures (confidence in a link vs. size of its effect) before letting a claim about one stand in for the other.
