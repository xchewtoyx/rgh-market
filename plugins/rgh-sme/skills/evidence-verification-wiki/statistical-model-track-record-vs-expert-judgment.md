# Statistical Model Track Record vs. Expert Judgment

This is an empirically tested question, not a matter of opinion: does a simple statistical/historical-data model produce more reliable predictions than an unaided human expert's judgment, in the same domain, given the same information? The accumulated answer across many studies favors the model far more often than intuition suggests it should.

## Documented Findings
- Paul Meehl, starting in the 1950s, compiled comparisons across domains (business-failure prediction, sports outcomes, clinical diagnosis) finding simple statistical models beat subjective expert judgment in nearly every case studied.
- A later Grove & Meehl meta-analysis of 136 studies found roughly half were statistical ties, but among the studies with a clear winner, about 40% favored the statistical model versus about 10% favoring the human expert.
- Philip Tetlock's *Expert Political Judgment* tracked 284 experts across 20 years and over 82,000 forecasts on elections, wars, and economic outcomes, concluding it was not possible to find a domain where humans clearly outperformed crude extrapolation algorithms, let alone more sophisticated statistical ones.
- Historical-data models drew on 100+ past space missions and outperformed engineer/scientist judgment on cost overrun, schedule overrun, and mission-failure-risk predictions for new, ostensibly unique missions — see [fallacy of close analogy](fallacy-of-close-analogy.md) for why "this case is unique" does not, by itself, undercut this kind of comparison.

## Why This Matters for Verification
This is not a license to reject expert testimony outright — expert judgment remains a legitimate and often necessary input, especially where no comparable historical data exists at all. But when a draft frames a confident expert prediction or judgment call as self-evidently more trustworthy than an available statistical or historical-base-rate estimate, that framing is itself an unverified assumption running against a well-documented pattern in the opposite direction. The burden should fall on the claim that unaided expert judgment beats the numbers, not the reverse.

## Verification Action
When a document sets an expert's confident prediction or judgment against a competing statistical/historical estimate and simply defers to the expert, flag this as an unexamined assumption. Ask whether a comparable track record exists for predictions of that type, and if the statistical estimate is dismissed, require a specific reason tied to the case at hand rather than a general preference for expert intuition.
