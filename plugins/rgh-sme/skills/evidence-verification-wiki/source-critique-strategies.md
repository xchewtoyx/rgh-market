# Source Critique Strategies

Reading a source critically is a distinct step from reading it generously to understand it — a reviewer should be able to summarize a source's argument fairly, to their own satisfaction, before disagreeing with it (see [charitable interpretation in review](charitable-interpretation-in-review.md)). Once that understanding is in place, several distinct angles can surface genuine problems, beyond checking whether the cited facts are individually accurate:

- **Classification**: does the source's argument depend on a category boundary that's actually contested or arbitrary? Reclassifying a case can undermine a conclusion that depended on which category it was placed in.
- **Parts and wholes**: does a claim about the whole actually follow from evidence about its parts, or vice versa — does the source generalize from a part to a whole (or a whole to a part) without justification?
- **History**: does the source's claim hold across the actual historical record, or only for the specific period/instance it examined? Check whether a claim presented as general was actually only ever tested against one moment in time.
- **Causation versus correlation**: see [correlation vs. causation](correlation-vs-causation.md).
- **Perspective**: whose viewpoint does the source's framing implicitly adopt, and does the argument still hold from a different, equally legitimate perspective?

## Reading Competing Arguments
Deliberately reading arguments that reach different conclusions on the same question serves two purposes for a reviewer: it surfaces objections the source under review may not have anticipated or addressed, and it exposes different reasoning models that can be borrowed — reuse a competing argument's *method* (how it structures and supports its reasoning) even when its specific conclusions are rejected.

## Verification Action
When reviewing an argument that survives a plain fact-check, run it through these five angles before accepting its conclusion — many arguments that are accurate in every individual claim still rest on an unexamined classification choice, an unjustified part-to-whole leap, an unstated historical scope limit, or a specific unstated perspective.
