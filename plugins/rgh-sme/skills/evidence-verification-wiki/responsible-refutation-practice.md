# Responsible Refutation Practice

Publicly correcting a claim carries more weight, and more risk, than privately noticing an error — it demands more rigor than the original spotting did, because it is a public commitment rather than idle skepticism.

## Be Correct
Verify a refutation as thoroughly as the claim it's refuting, including the finding's own sourcing and reasoning — "check and double-check and then check one more time." A strong practice is **adversarial pre-publication review**: having a colleague deliberately try to poke holes in a new finding before it's made public, specifically to catch errors that the original author's own review missed. This is a peer-level complement to individual [author self-verification](author-self-verification.md).

## Admit Fault
When a refutation itself turns out to be wrong, own the mistake swiftly and openly. Doubling down on an error to avoid the appearance of having been wrong is a bad default — sustained credibility across many corrections is worth more than winning any single dispute.

## Be Clear
A clear, jargon-free, well-organized refutation is often *more* effortful to produce than the original bullshit was to state — strong refutations frequently combine several techniques (a [null model](null-model-testing.md), a [redrawn chart](chart-framing-verification.md), a synthetic dataset) and get tested on an outside reader before being published, rather than being published as a first draft.
