# Denominator Manipulation

A fraction or percentage can fall even while the underlying absolute number it's built from rises, if the denominator (the total it's a share of) is growing faster still. Reporting only the percentage can create a false impression of improvement or decline.

## Worked Example
One group's share of a total jailed population fell over a several-year period — a share that, taken alone, sounds like real progress. But the *absolute number* of people from that group who were jailed actually rose over the same period; the share only fell because a different group's absolute numbers rose even faster, inflating the total (denominator) the first group's share was measured against.

## Verification Action
When a draft reports a share, percentage, or rate falling (or rising) over time, check the absolute numerator alongside it, not just the ratio. If the denominator is also changing over the same period, the ratio's direction can point the opposite way from the underlying absolute trend — both figures are needed to know what's actually happening.
