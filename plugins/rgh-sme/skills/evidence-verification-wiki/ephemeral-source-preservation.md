# Ephemeral Source Preservation

Online sources are not stable evidence — pages get redesigned or taken down, links break, and social media posts get deleted, sometimes within hours of being cited. A source that was valid when a claim was verified can become impossible to re-check later unless a durable copy was made at the time.

## Preservation Practice
Proactively screenshot, download, or save a PDF/print copy of every online source at the moment it's used to support a claim — not after, and not only if the source looks unusually likely to disappear. This is the online-evidence equivalent of citing a locator precisely enough that a reader could re-find the exact page (see [primary vs. secondary sources](primary-vs-secondary-sources.md)).

## Recovery When Preservation Was Missed
If a source disappears before it was preserved, recovery options include: web archival services (which periodically snapshot pages), a search engine's cached version of the page, and, for vanished social media content specifically, screenshots taken and preserved by other people. A recovered screenshot from a third party must itself be authenticated (checked against other available evidence that it's genuine and unaltered — see [image authenticity verification](image-authenticity-verification.md)) before being relied upon as if it were the original source, since a found screenshot is itself just another unverified claim until confirmed.

## Verification Action
When citing a live web source in a claim that will need to survive independent review later, preserve a durable copy at the point of citation. When a cited source has already vanished and no local copy exists, attempt archival/cache recovery before concluding the claim is unverifiable, but treat any recovered copy as needing its own authenticity check before being trusted.
