# SIFT Framework

The **SIFT Framework**, developed by Michael Caulfield, is a highly effective, four-step information-literacy process designed to help researchers and reviewers quickly evaluate the credibility of digital claims and trace them back to their original context.

## The Four Steps
1. **S - Stop**: 
   - When first encountering a claim or source, stop before sharing, citing, or accepting it.
   - Ask yourself: Do I know the reputation of this source or website? If not, do not use its claims as evidence until you investigate it.
2. **I - Investigate the source**:
   - Look up who is publishing the information. Check their credentials, institutional backing, funding model, and track record.
   - The goal is to determine the source's expertise, authority, and potential bias before reading their specific claims.
3. **F - Find better coverage**:
   - Look around to see if other, more authoritative sources have covered the same topic.
   - Search for established consensus or meta-analyses. If the claim is only reported by one obscure site, it is highly likely to be unverified or false.
4. **T - Trace claims, quotes, and media to their original context**:
   - Tracing is the act of chasing the citation chain back to the primary data or original statement.
   - Review the context surrounding a quote or image to ensure it has not been clipped, edited, or manipulated to support a false narrative.
