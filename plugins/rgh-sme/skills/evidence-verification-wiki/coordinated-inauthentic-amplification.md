# Coordinated Inauthentic Amplification

**Coordinated inauthentic amplification** is the use of bots, fake accounts, or sockpuppets to manufacture the appearance that a claim is independently corroborated, widely believed, or organically popular, when the apparent volume of support in fact traces back to a single coordinated source. A substantial share of internet traffic and social-media accounts are automated or fabricated: platforms have deleted fake accounts numbering in the billions, and public comment processes have received millions of duplicate or stolen-identity submissions in a single period — including hundreds of thousands of identical comments submitted within the same second. Fabricated personas can accumulate large, seemingly organic followings and go on to be treated as genuine independent voices by otherwise-careful outlets.

## Why It Defeats Ordinary Triangulation
[Evidence triangulation](evidence-triangulation.md) treats independent corroboration by multiple sources as evidence of a claim's validity, and [illusory truth effect](illusory-truth-effect.md) already warns that repeated exposure alone isn't corroboration. Coordinated inauthentic amplification is a more adversarial version of the same failure mode: it doesn't just repeat one honest source many times, it actively disguises a single (often bad-faith) source as many independent ones, defeating a naive count of "how many places say this" as a reliability signal. Apparent grassroots consensus — many seemingly unconnected accounts converging on the same claim, phrasing, or framing in a short window — should itself be treated as a red flag warranting investigation rather than as stronger evidence.

## Verification Action
- Treat sudden, large-scale, near-identical repetition of a claim across many accounts or comments — especially within a narrow time window — as a signal to check for coordination, not as independent triangulation.
- When corroborating a claim via social proof (many people saying the same thing), check whether the accounts involved have an independently verifiable identity, track record, or history predating the claim in question.
- Recognize that a persona's or account's popularity (follower count, apparent influence) is not itself evidence of its authenticity or reliability — assess it under [source reliability indicators](source-reliability-indicators.md) rather than deferring to its apparent reach.
