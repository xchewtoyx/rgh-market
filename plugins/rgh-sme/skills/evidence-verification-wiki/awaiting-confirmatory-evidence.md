# Awaiting Confirmatory Evidence

A credible verification workflow often includes a **preliminary judgment** and a **confirmatory check** — pathology after surgery, lab results after a clinical impression, an independent audit after an internal review. The failure mode is treating the preliminary step as settled fact while the confirmatory step is still outstanding.

## Worked Example: Diagnosis Before Pathology
Archie Cochrane — a physician who later championed evidence-based medicine — accepted a surgeon's off-the-cuff terminal-cancer verdict and began planning accordingly, even though the pathologist had not yet reported and Cochrane knew the report was pending. The pathologist later found no cancer. Cochrane noted he "never doubted the surgeon's words" — neither he nor the specialist treated the diagnosis as provisional pending confirmation.

The verification lesson is structural, not personal: when a document's workflow explicitly includes a later confirming step, any conclusion drawn *before* that step completes should be labeled provisional and should not drive irreversible commitments. This applies even when the preliminary source is a credentialed expert — see [source authority scope](source-authority-scope.md) for why expertise in one moment does not substitute for the designated verification artifact.

## Verification Action

When reviewing a claim backed by "the expert said" or an interim finding, check whether a defined confirmatory process was still in flight. If so, flag any downstream reasoning that treated the interim step as final. Where stakes are high (medical, legal, financial, safety), require explicit separation between **preliminary assessment** and **verified conclusion**, with the audit trail showing which status each statement had when decisions were made — see [verification accounting](verification-accounting.md).
