# Garden Path Fallacy

Explaining away a *single* piece of mildly discrepant evidence is often perfectly reasonable on its own — real evidence is noisy, and not every anomaly signals a problem with the working theory. The **garden path fallacy** is what happens when this reasonable move is repeated: a series of individually-sensible dismissals accumulates until the working explanation has drifted far from what the evidence actually supports, without any single step in the sequence feeling like the moment to stop and reconsider. Each explaining-away is locally defensible; the cumulative path is not.

## The "Snap-Back" Recovery Mechanism
People do eventually self-correct, but only past a threshold: once the pile of explained-away anomalies grows large enough, the working theory becomes so convoluted that confidence in it collapses all at once, prompting a full re-examination of the previously dismissed evidence for a better-fitting alternative. The catch is that the threshold for this "snap-back" is unpredictable and is reached only after a potentially long period of accumulating small, individually-rationalized errors — by the time it triggers, whatever early warning signs existed have long since been explained away rather than acted on.

## Worked Illustrations
- Two ships approaching each other radio a passing arrangement, but one radio message is never received; each captain, observing the other's unexpected maneuvering, explains it away as a temporary error the other will soon correct — rather than a sign their own model of the situation is wrong — and the compounding "corrections" lead directly to a collision.
- A person deliberately trying to induce paranoia in themselves by interpreting every incidental event (a glance, a shying horse) as evidence of a plot found the accumulating explanations becoming so convincing that they became genuinely frightened for their own state of mind, and had to deliberately stop the exercise.
- Two people who realize only very late that they've been hiking in the wrong direction each individually rationalize successive terrain mismatches (an unfamiliar creek "must be new," a path that should be straight is "sort of straight for a bit") rather than questioning their basic orientation, sometimes for hours before the accumulated implausibility forces a full reassessment.

## Why This Matters for Verification
A reviewer checking a single claim or a single piece of evidence in isolation can reasonably conclude each individual objection has a plausible answer — that's not itself a verification failure. The failure mode specifically to watch for is a document or a source's *pattern* of always having a ready explanation for every piece of contrary evidence raised, each one individually plausible, with the explanations never resolving into anything that could be checked independently. This is a close cousin of the recognition that a legitimately updating account occasionally revises its own working theory; an account that only ever adds compensating explanations without ever revising course is exhibiting the garden path pattern.

## Verification Action
Rather than evaluating each explaining-away move in isolation, track the *cumulative* pattern across a document or a source's responses to objections: how many separate ad hoc explanations has the account required to remain intact? A high and rising count, even if each individual explanation seems reasonable, is itself evidence the underlying theory should be re-examined from scratch rather than patched again — treat repeated patching as a trigger for the kind of full reassessment a "snap-back" would eventually force naturally, rather than waiting for that threshold to be reached.
