# Source Proximity Heuristic

During a fast-moving event, secondhand accounts amplify quickly, and distant outlets or observers relaying an unconfirmed rumor can outpace and drown out the accounts of parties who were actually close to the event. Several major breaking-news failures shared the same root cause: national or distant outlets repeated an unverified rumor (a claimed casualty count, a claimed identity, a claimed status) that outlets or parties closer to the event either hadn't reported or had already reported correctly.

## Two-Part Technique
- **Prefer proximity**: when accounts conflict during a fast-developing situation, weight the account from the source geographically or institutionally closest to the event more heavily than a distant secondhand repost or aggregation.
- **Confirm with the authority directly**: treat any claim circulating through informal channels (social media, chat, secondhand relay) as unconfirmed until verified by direct contact with the actual responsible institution (the organization, agency, or system owner with firsthand knowledge) — not by finding more secondary sources repeating the same unconfirmed claim.

## Verification Action
When a claim about a live, fast-developing situation is sourced only to social media, informal channels, or distant secondary coverage, do not treat repetition across multiple such sources as corroboration (see [illusory truth effect](illusory-truth-effect.md)) — trace it to the nearest authoritative party and confirm directly before treating it as established.
