# Equivalent Bet Calibration Test

To test whether a stated confidence level (e.g., a 90% confidence interval) reflects genuine belief rather than a reflexive verbal habit, offer a choice between two bets that pay the identical prize under different conditions:

- **(A)** Win a fixed prize if the true value falls inside your stated range.
- **(B)** Win the same fixed prize if a fair random device (e.g., a dial with a marked 90% wedge) lands on the marked outcome.

If you'd rather take the dial (B), your real confidence in your own range is below the stated 90% — you're overconfident in your original estimate. If you'd rather bet on your own range (A), you're underconfident — you actually believe your range more strongly than 90%. The calibration point is wherever you're genuinely indifferent between the two. About 80% of people, tested cold, prefer the dial — direct behavioral evidence of pervasive overconfidence, distinct from and more convincing than simply asking someone to introspect on their confidence.

## Getting the Phrasing Right
The comparison only works if both options pay the same prize under different randomizing conditions, with no mention of "confidence" in the framing itself. Invalid phrasings that defeat the test's purpose: "Would you rather be 90% confident or spin for $1,000?" and "Would you rather be right 90% of the time or have $1,000?" — both smuggle a confidence judgment into the choice itself rather than forcing a genuine revealed-preference bet.

## Why a Fake Bet Still Works
Merely *pretending* to place the bet (no real money changing hands) measurably improves calibration almost as much as betting for real — the mechanism is forcing a genuine trade-off comparison, not the stakes themselves.

## Verification Action
When assessing how much weight to give someone's stated confidence in an estimate, consider posing (or imagining posing) the equivalent-bet question rather than taking the stated percentage at face value: would this person actually prefer their own range to a fair random device calibrated to their stated odds? A source who visibly hedges when the equivalent bet is made concrete has just revealed their true, lower confidence — treat the originally stated number as overconfident and downgrade the claim's certainty accordingly.
