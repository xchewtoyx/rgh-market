# Hedge Words for Uncertain Claims

When a claim cannot be fully verified or is genuinely subjective/extrapolated, the reviewer's remediation is not always to cut it — it can instead be to calibrate its confidence language to match what the evidence actually supports. As one fact-checker put it: "Hedge words are the error bars of writing."

## Technique
Swap an assertive verb or framing for a qualified one — "will" becomes "may," and words like *about*, *appear*, *likely*, *mainly*, *more or less*, *partially/in part*, *perhaps*, *presumably*, *quite*, *supposedly* signal to the reader that the claim carries residual uncertainty rather than being independently confirmed fact.

For a claim that cannot be verified at all under deadline (e.g., an unverifiable historical anecdote), hedge its sourcing directly rather than asserting it: "often cited as..." or "usually attributed to..." rather than stating it as settled.

## Calibration Guidance
- Reserve hedges for genuinely sensitive or inconclusive claims (e.g., an inconclusive medical-study finding) — not for trivial claims that don't need one, since overuse dilutes their signal and authors/editors will resist hedging that weakens prose unnecessarily.
- Higher-stakes claims warrant hedging more readily; hedging is also a legitimate fallback under tight deadlines when full verification isn't possible in the time available.
- If hedging a claim would undermine the entire point of including it, that is itself a signal the claim should be cut rather than hedged.
