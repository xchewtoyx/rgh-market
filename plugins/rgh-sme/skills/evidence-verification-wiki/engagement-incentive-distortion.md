# Engagement Incentive Distortion

A publisher's revenue model shapes the accuracy of what it produces. Under **subscription economics**, a publisher is rewarded for long-term reader trust, so accuracy and relevance are the asset being sold. Under **click economics**, a publisher is rewarded for the single click regardless of what happens after, so headlines and framing optimize for immediate emotional pull ("sparkle") rather than for correctly conveying the underlying information. This is a source-level instance of [incentive bias in verification](incentive-bias-in-verification.md)'s general pattern — but the incentive here belongs to the source producing the claim, not the reviewer evaluating it, so it's a [source motive checklist](source-motive-checklist.md) concern to check before deeper verification begins.

## Detectable Patterns of Click-Optimized Content
- **Forward-reference headlines**: a headline is engineered to withhold its own payoff, forcing a click to learn what it's actually about (e.g., naming a category without saying which member of it, or describing an effect without naming its cause).
- **Emotional-experience headlines**: headlines that promise a feeling ("will make you...") rather than stating a fact, in contrast to older factual headline conventions that stated the news directly.
- **Audience-reaction-as-story**: headlines that make the story about how other people reacted to an event, rather than about the event itself, one further step removed from checkable substance.

None of these patterns alone proves a claim false, but each is a signal that the piece was optimized for engagement rather than for accurate transmission of information, which should lower prior confidence and raise the bar for independent verification before relying on it.

## Verification Action
- When triaging a source, ask what its revenue model rewards — subscription/reputation-based models have a structural incentive toward accuracy that ad-driven, click-based models lack.
- Treat forward-reference or emotion-first headlines as a cue to check the underlying article's substance against independent sources before trusting the framing, not as disqualifying on their own.
- Note that a satirical, self-referential test of this pattern (a fake headline about headline-skimming, with no real article body, that was itself shared widely without being read) demonstrates how effectively engagement-optimized framing can spread independent of substance — treat high share counts as evidence of engagement, not evidence of accuracy.
