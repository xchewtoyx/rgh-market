# Decontextualization Traps in Investigation

When reconstructing a timeline of events (an incident investigation, an audit of a sequence of decisions), evidence can be technically accurate at the level of each individual data point while still being assembled in a way that badly misrepresents what actually happened. Three specific, named failure modes:

- **Micro-matching**: comparing isolated fragments of an actor's performance directly against a written procedure or against cues that are only clearly significant in hindsight, rather than against what the actor could actually perceive as their situation unfolded. This measures behavior against a hindsight reality the actor didn't have access to, not against their actual moment-to-moment awareness.
- **Cherry-picking across a timeline**: extracting isolated quotes or data points from different points in a timeline to support a predetermined label or conclusion, while ignoring how the situation actually evolved between those points. This is the timeline-reconstruction version of [cherry-picking](cherry-picking.md) — instead of omitting whole sources, it treats non-contemporaneous fragments as if they were equally representative evidence for a single characterization.
- **Shopping-bagging**: bundling scattered, weak, and sometimes contradictory cues that actually arrived gradually and ambiguously over time into a single "bag," then treating that bag as though it had been presented to the actor all at once as an obvious, coherent warning. This overstates how clear a warning signal actually was by collapsing its real timeline into a single moment.

## Why These Are Distinct from Ordinary Cherry-Picking
Ordinary [cherry-picking](cherry-picking.md) omits contradicting sources or data points. These three traps are specifically about how a *timeline* gets distorted during reconstruction — they can occur even when every individual data point cited is accurate and even when contradicting information isn't omitted, purely through how fragments are selected, compared, and recombined across time. A reviewer checking a timeline-based account for cherry-picking in the ordinary sense (are contradicting sources omitted?) can still miss these, because the distortion is in the *temporal assembly* of accurate fragments, not in which fragments were included.

## Verification Action
- When reviewing a reconstructed timeline (an incident report, an investigation narrative), check whether each cited data point is being compared against what was actually perceivable to the actor at that moment, not against a procedure or a cue whose significance only became clear afterward (micro-matching).
- Check whether quotes or observations drawn from different points in the timeline are being used to support one characterization of the actor's state throughout — if the underlying situation evolved between those points, using them together may misrepresent it (cherry-picking across a timeline).
- Check whether a set of "warning signs" being presented together in the write-up actually arrived together in real time, or whether the write-up has compressed a gradual, ambiguous trickle of weak signals into what reads like a single clear alarm (shopping-bagging).
