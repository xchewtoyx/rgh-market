# Illusory Truth Effect

The **illusory truth effect** is the tendency for a claim to feel more credible the more times it has been encountered, regardless of whether any of those encounters constitute independent evidence. A claim seen or cited repeatedly can feel well-established even when every citation traces back to the same single unverified origin.

## Why It Matters for Verification
This effect is the psychological mechanism that makes circular sourcing dangerous: a reviewer who has simply seen a number or claim many times, across many documents, is at risk of treating that repeated exposure itself as corroborating evidence — see [evidence triangulation](evidence-triangulation.md) on tracing a claim's pedigree back to confirm sources are truly independent rather than all restating the same origin. A number's familiarity is not evidence of its accuracy.

## Verification Action
When a claim feels obviously true because "everyone cites it," treat that feeling itself as a prompt to trace the claim's pedigree, not as a substitute for doing so. A claim repeated by many secondary sources that all trace back to one unverified original should be treated as having exactly one source of evidence, no matter how many times it has been repeated.
