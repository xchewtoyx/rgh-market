# Counterexample Refutation

If a claim asserts "A implies B" as a general rule, a single verified case of A without B fully refutes the general claim — and finding one is often far cheaper and more decisive than constructing a complete technical rebuttal of the reasoning behind the claim.

## Worked Example
A model argued that any long-lived multicellular organism necessarily needs certain adaptive-immune features (antibody diversity via random generation and selection) to survive. A single question — "what about trees?" — demolished the general claim: trees are long-lived multicellular organisms that entirely lack these immune features. No detailed rebuttal of the model's internal logic was needed once a real counterexample was in hand.

This asymmetry generalizes beyond biology: in mathematics, proving a general conjecture true can take years of intricate work, while disproving a false conjecture can require nothing more than a single computer-found numeric counterexample.

## Verification Action
When a draft asserts a general rule ("X always leads to Y," "every case of A has property B"), the fastest verification check is to actively search for a known exception before evaluating the supporting argument's internal logic. If a genuine counterexample exists, it settles the claim's falsity regardless of how sound the surrounding argument otherwise appears — a reviewer does not need to out-argue the reasoning to invalidate the conclusion.
