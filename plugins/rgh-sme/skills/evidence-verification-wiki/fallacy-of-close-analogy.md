# Fallacy of Close Analogy

Also called the "Uniqueness Fallacy." A claim that a case is too unique for data from other, similar cases to be informative — treating apparent uniqueness as if it erases the relevance of a comparable reference class entirely, rather than merely requiring the reference class to be applied with appropriate adjustment.

## Worked Examples From the Source
- An insurance actuary is not actually "stumped" by having no death data on one specific individual — mortality-table data from demographically similar people is exactly the relevant reference class, despite that individual being, in the trivial sense, unique.
- Historical-data models drawing on 100+ past space missions consistently outperformed engineer/scientist judgment on cost overrun, schedule overrun, and failure-risk predictions for *new* missions — even though each mission was, in the naive sense, "unique" in its specific design and goals. The claim "this project is unlike anything done before" did not, in practice, make outside base-rate data on similar-structure projects irrelevant.
- Statistical interpolation from comparable historical cases outperformed expert judgment even in domains explicitly framed as requiring individualized assessment (e.g., parole-violation risk, admissions-candidate potential).

## Why This Matters for Verification
A claim's author dismissing a relevant comparison ("our situation is different," "that data doesn't apply to us") should not be accepted at face value — check whether the dismissal is backed by an actual argument about which specific structural feature breaks the analogy, or whether it's simply an assertion of uniqueness used to avoid an inconvenient reference class. Genuine uniqueness in surface details rarely means zero transferable structure; the relevant question is whether the mechanism driving the reference-class data still applies, not whether the case is identical in every particular.

## Verification Action
When a draft rejects a comparison to similar past cases, precedents, or reference-class data by asserting the current situation is "too different," require the author to name the specific structural difference that breaks the analogy. Treat an unsupported claim of uniqueness as itself an unverified assertion, not a reason to drop the comparison.
