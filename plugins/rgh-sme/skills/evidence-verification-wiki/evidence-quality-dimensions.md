# Evidence Quality Dimensions

Evidence being true is not sufficient for it to actually support a claim. Check each piece of cited evidence against five separate dimensions:
- **Accurate**: the underlying facts or data are correct.
- **Precise**: the evidence is specific enough to bear on the exact claim, not just a loosely related generality.
- **Sufficient**: enough evidence is present to support the claim's scope — a single data point rarely supports a general claim.
- **Representative**: the evidence reflects the actual population or phenomenon the claim is about, not an unrepresentative slice of it (see [sampling and measurement bias](sampling-and-measurement-bias.md)).
- **Appropriate**: the evidence is the right *kind* for the claim being made — a correlational study can't by itself support a causal claim (see [correlation vs. causation](correlation-vs-causation.md)); an anecdote can't by itself support a general statistical claim.

## Evidence Doesn't Interpret Itself
A document that presents accurate evidence but never explains *how* it supports the specific reason or claim leaves that connection for the reader to construct — and a reader who constructs it differently than the author intended may reasonably reject the argument even though the evidence itself was sound. The relevance of evidence to a reason must be argued, not assumed to be self-evident.

This applies just as directly to a chart, table, or diagram as to a cited statistic or quotation: a figure dropped into a document without being introduced (what it shows), oriented (what to notice in it), and connected (why it bears on the claim) leaves the same interpretive gap a reader can fill in unintended ways. A visual doesn't interpret itself just because it's accurate. Separately, check that a figure is actually equipped to be checked at all — an informative title, labeled axes with units, a legend where needed, and a cited data source — and be wary of a level of displayed precision (e.g., decimal places) the underlying data doesn't actually support, which manufactures the same false rigor as [quantitative authority illusion](quantitative-authority-illusion.md) does in prose.

## Verification Action
For each piece of evidence in a draft, check it against all five dimensions independently — evidence can be perfectly accurate and still fail to support its claim because it's imprecise, insufficient, unrepresentative, or the wrong kind of evidence for what's being asserted.
