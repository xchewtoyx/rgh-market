title: Documentation Research Artifacts
description: >
  Four reusable artifacts — persona, user story, journey map, and
  friction log — condense audience research into a form later drafting
  and revision work can actually be checked against.
sources:
  - title: "Docs for Developers: An Engineer's Field Guide to Technical Writing"
    resource: "Docs for Developers (Bhatti, Corleissen, Lambourne, Nunez, Waterhouse), ch. 1"
---

Raw research findings — interview notes, support-ticket themes, survey results — need to be condensed into a small set of artifacts that a writer (or a whole team) can keep referring back to while drafting, rather than being re-derived from scratch for every document:

- A **persona** is a semi-fictional target reader built from the research, not an exhaustive demographic profile — its job is to make "who is this for" answerable in one sentence at the point of writing, not to model every real reader precisely.
- A **user story** captures a specific need in the fixed form "As a [user], I want [goal], so that [benefit]," which forces the goal and its underlying motivation to be stated together rather than the goal alone.
- A **journey map** lays out the stages, actions, questions, emotions, touchpoints, and points of friction a reader moves through around one goal, giving a writer a picture of the whole path rather than just the page currently being drafted.
- A **friction log** is produced by having a representative newcomer actually attempt the task while every stall, workaround, confusion, and question they hit is recorded in real time. This is the most direct of the four artifacts: it exposes missing instructions and specific points of confusion that no amount of expert review will catch, because the expert no longer experiences the task as confusing.

These artifacts feed forward into later stages of the documentation lifecycle: a friction log is also the tool used to find good places for callouts during drafting (see [formatting for scanning](formatting-for-scanning.md)) and to check, after publishing, whether a revision actually reduced the friction it targeted (see [closing the feedback loop](closing-the-feedback-loop.md)).
