---
type: concept
title: The Funnel Technique
description: >
  An introduction should narrow from a broad, engaging opening to a
  precise question one paragraph at a time, with each paragraph building
  on the one before rather than stacking background facts.
sources:
  - title: "Writing Science: How to Write Papers That Get Cited and Proposals That Get Funded"
    resource: "Writing Science (Joshua Schimel), ch. 5-6"
---

The opening of a document is a power position: it has to frame the driving problem, introduce the relevant characters, and signal who the piece is for, and it can foreshadow the challenge and conclusion to come. What breadth and language it uses depends on the audience — a specialist reader can be met inside the field's own shared schemas, while a general reader needs recognizably concrete stakes, surprise, or curiosity to get engaged at all, and the same underlying work may need a genuinely different opening for a basic-science funder than for a management-focused one.

Two failure modes are common. A **misdirection** opening launches one story and then pivots to an apparently unrelated mechanism, leaving readers unsure why the pivot happened. A **no-direction** opening recites general, schema-level background without ever showing where the piece is actually headed. Both are usually fixed the same way in revision: cut whatever precedes the point where the real story starts, rather than trying to patch the transition.

The **funnel** is the bridge between that broad opening and the precise challenge or question the document will answer, the point where [OCAR story structure](ocar-story-structure.md) moves from Opening to Challenge: each paragraph should narrow the story by building on the paragraph before it, establishing what's already known, identifying the specific gap or limitation, and showing why closing that gap matters for the problem raised in the opening. A reader working through a well-built funnel should be able to infer why the final question follows inevitably from what came before it — not be surprised by it. Two failures mirror the opening's: an abrupt jump from broad context straight to a narrow technical question with no connective tissue, and an exhaustive literature dump that recites everything relevant without ever narrowing. Background material earns its place in a funnel only if it visibly advances the narrowing; anything else should be cut, however much work went into gathering it.

A concrete revision check: trace each paragraph's own opening and closing point. A paragraph's ending should supply the premise or the vocabulary the next paragraph needs to open, and the final paragraph of the funnel should set up the challenge directly, so that stating the challenge feels like the funnel's natural conclusion rather than an abrupt new topic.
