type: concept
title: Translating Technical Material for General Readers
description: >
  Explaining specialist work to a non-specialist reader means starting from
  the human question the work answers, separating essential content from
  technical debris, and explaining process and uncertainty, not just results.
sources:
  - title: On Writing Well
    resource: "On Writing Well, 30th Anniversary Edition (William Zinsser), ch. 15-16"
---

Writing about a technical field for readers outside it starts with a different question than writing for peers: not "what did we do and find" but "what problem, mystery, consequence, or person makes this worth a reader's attention." Establishing that human stake first is what earns the reader's effort to follow unfamiliar material — this is the same transaction that makes any nonfiction readable, applied to technical subject matter specifically.

Doing this well requires the writer to understand the field well enough to tell essential content apart from technical debris that specialists need but general readers don't. Jargon is not inherently wrong — it is necessary shorthand among specialists — but a general-reader account needs the *meaning* behind a technical term, not the term itself used as a display of expertise. The writer should also resist false simplification: explaining only the result and skipping the process and its uncertainty flattens work that is actually inquiry, revision, and imaginative effort, and readers can sense when a genuinely uncertain or difficult finding has been oversimplified into something falsely tidy.

The same translation discipline applies inside organizations, not just to public science writing: business and technical prose aimed at colleagues or customers should state its purpose early, name who is doing what, use short familiar words, and cut bureaucratic padding and passive concealment — precisely because clarity there also builds trust that the writer is telling the truth plainly rather than managing an impression. See [cutting clutter](cutting-clutter.md) for the sentence-level technique this depends on.
