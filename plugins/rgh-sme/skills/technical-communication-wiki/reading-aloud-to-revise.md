---
type: concept
title: Reading Prose Aloud to Revise
description: >
  Reading a draft aloud surfaces clutter, monotony, and rhythm problems
  that a silent read of the same text does not catch.
sources:
  - title: On Writing Well
    resource: "On Writing Well, 30th Anniversary Edition (William Zinsser), ch. 2, 6, 20"
---

Prose is heard inwardly even by a silent reader — sentence length, word order, rhythm, and the sound of the final word all affect how easily meaning lands. A writer proofreading silently tends to read what they meant to write rather than what is actually on the page, which is why clutter, awkward phrasing, and monotonous sentence rhythm survive silent passes but announce themselves the moment the text is read aloud.

Reading aloud is also a check on voice: writing should sound like a real, alert, humane person talking to another person, not like an institution or a borrowed authoritative register. If a sentence would feel strange to say to the reader's face, that is a signal to revise it, not a stylistic preference to override.

Use this as a deliberate revision step, distinct from drafting — see [separating drafting from revision](separating-drafting-from-revision.md) — and as one of the checks applied when [cutting clutter](cutting-clutter.md), since clutter is often what a read-aloud pass exposes first.
