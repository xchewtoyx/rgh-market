title: Matching Document Structure to Reader Patience
description: >
  The same underlying story needs a different structural shape depending
  on how much patience and prior context the intended reader brings to it.
sources:
  - title: "Writing Science: How to Write Papers That Get Cited and Proposals That Get Funded"
    resource: "Writing Science (Joshua Schimel), ch. 4"
---

[OCAR story structure](ocar-story-structure.md) — Opening, Challenge, Action, Resolution, in that order — fits a specialist reader who has the patience to follow background and method before reaching a conclusion. Other readers don't have that patience, and the same underlying story needs a different shape to reach them:

- **ABDCE** (Action, Background, Development, Climax, Ending) — common in proposals, which often need to lead with the proposed action itself before justifying it, because a reviewer's first question is what will actually be done.
- **LDR** (Lead, Development, Resolution) — common in magazine-style writing, where a concrete lead earns attention before the supporting development and resolution follow.
- **LD** (Lead, Development) — common in news writing, which often has no dedicated resolution at all because readers may stop after the lead and still have the essential point.

The underlying content and evidence don't change between these forms — what changes is how much is front-loaded versus earned through development, calibrated to how much the reader already wants to be there and how much context they'll tolerate before reaching a point. Choosing the wrong shape for an audience — OCAR's patient build-up for a reviewer who wants the action first, or a bare LD lead for a specialist who needs the full evidentiary case — costs the same document its effectiveness even though nothing about the underlying research changed. This is the document-structure analog of adapting the same material for an executive summary versus a deep reference versus a quickstart: the content is fixed, the shape that delivers it to a given reader is not.
