---
type: concept
title: The Vertical Question-Answer Relationship
description: >
  Every point in a document should raise a specific question in the
  reader's mind that the points immediately beneath it answer, and a
  writer should never answer a question the reader hasn't yet been led
  to ask.
sources:
  - title: "The Minto Pyramid Principle: Logic in Writing, Thinking and Problem Solving"
    resource: "The Minto Pyramid Principle (Barbara Minto), ch. 2"
---

Within a [pyramid structure](pyramid-structure.md), the relationship between one level and the level directly beneath it is a continuing dialogue: a point raises a question in the reader's mind, and the points grouped immediately below it exist to answer that specific question — nothing else belongs there. Each of those supporting points, in turn, should raise its own further question that its own supporting detail answers, recursively, all the way down to the document's concrete evidence.

The discipline this imposes is sequencing, not just content selection: a writer must not answer a question before the reader has actually been led to ask it. Material that is true and relevant but arrives before its triggering question has been raised reads as a non sequitur, even though the same material placed after the right lead-in would read as the obvious next step. Checking a draft against this relationship — for every point, what question does it raise, and does the very next material actually answer that question and nothing else — is a direct test of whether a document's structure is sound, independent of whether any individual sentence is well written.
