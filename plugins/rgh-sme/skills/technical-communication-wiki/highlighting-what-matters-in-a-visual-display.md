type: concept
title: Highlighting What Matters in a Visual Display
description: >
  What deserves attention permanently should be marked by a fixed
  position a reader learns to expect; what deserves attention only
  right now should be marked by a visual attribute that stands out
  from its neighbors — and either kind of highlighting fails once
  overused.
sources:
  - title: "Information Dashboard Design"
    resource: "Information Dashboard Design (Stephen Few), ch. 5"
---

Not everything in a display is equally important, and a display that gives everything the same visual weight leaves the reader with no cue about where to look first. There are two distinct kinds of "important," and each needs a different highlighting mechanism.

Content that's **always important** — a headline figure, a primary metric — should be highlighted through **static** means, chiefly position: a fixed, predictable location that doesn't move once a reader has learned to expect it there. The most emphasized regions of a page or screen are the top-left (from left-to-right, top-to-bottom reading order) and the visual center (a more basic perceptual pull, though content only reads as emphasized there if it's set apart from its surroundings, e.g. by white space). Squandering that prime position on a logo or on navigation controls — content that isn't actually the most important thing on the page — throws away the strongest highlighting tool the layout has.

Content that's **important only right now** — a value that's fallen outside a normal range, a fleeting condition needing a response — needs **dynamic** highlighting instead, built on the same [preattentive attributes](preattentive-attributes-for-rapid-visual-perception.md) that make some visual differences register instantly. Two flavors: an attribute pushed further along its own scale than normal (darker or more saturated color, bigger size, thicker line — all read as "more important" by degree), or an attribute that simply contrasts with the surrounding norm (a distinct hue, an added mark, an enclosure where none is typical — these read as "different" without implying more or less). Varying the intensity of a single hue to show degree of urgency, rather than switching between different hues for different urgency levels, has the added benefit of staying legible to colorblind readers, where a hue-only encoding does not.

Both mechanisms fail the same way if overused: highlighting only works by contrast with an unhighlighted baseline, so highlighting too much of a display leaves nothing standing out and defeats the purpose entirely. The discipline is the same one that makes any emphasis technique work — in prose or in a graphic, an emphasis applied everywhere is an emphasis applied nowhere; see [stress position and emphasis](stress-position-and-emphasis.md) for the prose-level version of the same principle.
