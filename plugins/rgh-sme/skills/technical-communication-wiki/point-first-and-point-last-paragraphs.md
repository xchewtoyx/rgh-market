---
type: concept
title: Point-First and Point-Last Paragraphs
description: >
  A paragraph is a complete mini-story, and a writer should deliberately
  choose whether it states its point first and then develops it, or
  develops evidence first and resolves with the point at the end.
sources:
  - title: "Writing Science: How to Write Papers That Get Cited and Proposals That Get Funded"
    resource: "Writing Science (Joshua Schimel), ch. 11"
---

Treat the paragraph as a genuine unit of composition — a complete mini-story contributing to the larger document, not just text accumulated until a visual break feels due. Two deliberate shapes are available, and the choice should be made on purpose rather than defaulted into.

**Point-first paragraphs** open with a topic sentence and then develop it — the paragraph-level version of [point sentences and document-level coherence](point-sentences-and-document-coherence.md). This shape dominates technical writing because it lets a reader skim the argument by reading only opening sentences; a longer lead can still build toward the point before developing it further, as long as the point arrives early enough to frame what follows.

**Point-last paragraphs** develop evidence, a question, or a line of reasoning first and only resolve with the conclusion at the end. These are useful at openings, transitions, resolutions, and in longer paragraphs where the payoff benefits from being earned rather than announced — but a point-last paragraph has to actually land its point; a paragraph that develops material and never states what it adds up to is a "point-nowhere" paragraph, and that failure mode is common enough to check for explicitly.

To repair a paragraph that isn't working, identify what its real underlying story is, deliberately choose point-first or point-last for it, separate any tangled threads it's trying to carry at once, make sure its characters and their relationships to each other are established, and then make its final point explicit rather than leaving it implied.
