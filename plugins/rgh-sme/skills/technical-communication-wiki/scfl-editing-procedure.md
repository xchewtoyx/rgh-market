---
type: concept
title: SCFL Editing Procedure
description: >
  A complex draft has faults that interact, so revise it in repeated,
  top-down passes — Structure, Clarity, Flow, Language — rather than
  trying to fix everything in one read.
sources:
  - title: "Writing Science: How to Write Papers That Get Cited and Proposals That Get Funded"
    resource: "Writing Science (Joshua Schimel), ch. 17"
---

A complex draft's problems are rarely independent — a wording fix in one paragraph can expose a structural gap, and fixing the structure can demand a fresh pass on flow and word choice that follow from the new arrangement. **SCFL** names four passes to run, in order, from the largest scale of the document down to the smallest: **Structure** (does the piece deliver a genuine story and [OCAR arc](ocar-story-structure.md)?), **Clarity** (are the ideas concrete and comprehensible sentence by sentence — see [characters as subjects](characters-as-subjects.md) and [diagnosing nominalizations](diagnosing-nominalizations.md)), **Flow** (are the thoughts linked from one to the next — see [flow by linking stress to topic](flow-linking-stress-to-topic.md)), and **Language** (does the prose have grace and sound, the level worked on last because it's the level most likely to be disturbed by upstream structural changes).

The order matters, and the passes are not meant to be four one-shot mechanical checklists run once each: work from the argument down to the sentence, never purely bottom-up, because polishing individual sentences before the structure is settled means re-doing that polish once the structure changes anyway.

A worked instance of this process on a 42-word opening sentence about plant light shows what a real pass looks like: settle what the actual story is, activate and concretize the verbs, link topic and stress across sentences, remove redundancy, and then — as a final step — reconsider whether an entire preliminary sentence is still needed at all once the rest is tightened; the example ends at 19 purposeful words. As a last check after the SCFL passes, read the clean draft aloud — the ear catches awkward rhythm, missed transitions, and clunky phrasing the eye skips over (see [reading prose aloud to revise](reading-aloud-to-revise.md)). It's normal to re-edit passages that were already edited once, since a change earlier in the document changes the context a later sentence has to work within.
