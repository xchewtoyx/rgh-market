---
type: concept
title: Flow by Linking Stress to Topic
description: >
  Prose flows when the new information a sentence delivers at its end
  becomes the familiar topic the next sentence opens with, since chaining
  topic to topic produces a list rather than a story.
sources:
  - title: "Writing Science: How to Write Papers That Get Cited and Proposals That Get Funded"
    resource: "Writing Science (Joshua Schimel), ch. 13"
---

Every sentence and paragraph faces two directions at once, backward and forward: its [topic](topic-position-and-old-before-new-information.md) supplies familiar context connecting it to what came before, and its [stress](stress-position-and-emphasis.md) delivers the new idea that should launch whatever comes next. Flow depends on deliberately linking those two: the prior sentence's stress — its new, end-of-sentence information — becomes the next sentence's topic, whether by repeating a word, restating a related idea, or explicitly naming the relationship between them.

This produces a useful diagnostic distinction. **Topic-to-topic** linking, where each sentence's opening connects back to the previous sentence's opening rather than its ending, produces a list of related facts rather than a developing story — technically connected, but flat. **Stress-to-topic** linking, where each sentence's new information becomes the next sentence's starting point, produces an actual chain of development, because each sentence is visibly built out of what the last one just delivered.

A local fix at the sentence-to-sentence level can't rescue a globally wrong arc — if the transitions in a passage keep failing no matter how they're worded, the more likely diagnosis is an extraneous sentence or a misordered sequence of ideas, which needs restructuring or relocating rather than a better connective word. Paragraph breaks are allowed to shift the flow's arc, but the new paragraph should still open by reaching back to the resolution or theme the previous paragraph ended on, saving genuinely new material for its own stress position rather than opening with it cold.
