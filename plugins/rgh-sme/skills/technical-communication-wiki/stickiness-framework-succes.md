---
type: concept
title: The SUCCES Stickiness Framework
description: >
  An idea is more likely to be remembered and acted on when it is Simple,
  Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, and delivered as a Story —
  used as a diagnostic checklist, not a requirement to force every
  element into every piece.
sources:
  - title: "Writing Science: How to Write Papers That Get Cited and Proposals That Get Funded"
    resource: "Writing Science (Joshua Schimel), ch. 3"
---

Adapted from the Heath brothers' work on why ideas stick, SUCCES names six properties that make a message memorable rather than merely accurate: **Simple** (a compact core claim, not simplistic content — the goal is finding the one thing that matters most, not dumbing anything down); **Unexpected** (a genuine gap between what the reader assumed and what's true creates curiosity that carries them through the explanation); **Concrete** (specific examples make an abstraction imaginable in a way the abstraction alone can't); **Credible** (specifics and real data let readers trust a claim instead of just accepting it on authority); **Emotional** (this includes intellectual emotions — curiosity, surprise, concern, wonder — not just sentiment, and it's what makes a reader care enough to keep engaging); and **Story** (the element that integrates the rest, by showing a problem, an action taken in response, and a changed understanding as a result).

These are communication functions to check a piece against, not decorations to add or a license to exaggerate — forcing all six into every document produces contrived, padded writing, and most effective pieces lean on only two or three of them deliberately chosen for the audience and material at hand. Used diagnostically, the checklist is: what is this piece's actual core message, and which of these six properties would most help this particular audience grasp and remember it? See [motivating the reader with a problem](motivating-the-reader-with-a-problem.md) for the closely related technique of using an explicit problem-and-cost to earn a reader's attention, which SUCCES's "Story" element formalizes into a checklist item.
