---
type: concept
title: Action Titles and On-Chart Annotation
description: >
  Charts need words as much as marks: action titles state the finding, axis
  and chart titles remove decoding work, and direct annotations explain the
  points that matter so viewers do not have to invent the conclusion.
sources:
  - title: Storytelling with Data
    resource: "Storytelling with Data (Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic), ch. 5"
---

Text labels, introduces, explains, reinforces, highlights, recommends, and
tells the story a graphic alone rarely finishes. Near-mandatory elements —
chart title, axis titles (exceptions extremely rare) — stop the audience from
burning [working memory](short-term-memory-chunk-limit.md) on "what am I
looking at?" Omit them and even a clear context still produces a pause;
include them so brainpower goes to the content. Prefer
[direct labels](direct-labeling-over-legends.md) over distant keys. This is
the explanatory companion to
[a standalone graphic must be self-sufficient](self-sufficient-graphic-elements.md).

**Action titles** replace redundant descriptive titles ("2015 Budget") with
the recommendation or finding ("Estimated 2015 spending is above budget").
On a slide or dashboard tile, that title bar is precious real estate — use it
to state what the audience must not miss and to set expectations for the rest
of the view.

**Annotation** labels important or interesting points on the graphic itself —
external events, outliers, "so what" callouts — so nuances are accessible
with minimal cognitive effort. Words that carry the conclusion should also
receive [preattentive emphasis](visual-hierarchy-with-preattentive-attributes.md);
do not assume two viewers will draw the same conclusion from marks alone. A
rare exception to "always title axes" is when the story is only relative
peaks and valleys and naming the metric would invite unproductive debate —
omit only consciously, and only when relative shape is truly the sole point.

Without titles and annotation, a decluttered, highlighted chart can still
"mean little"; with them, the same marks become an actionable display.
