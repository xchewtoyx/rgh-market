---
type: concept
title: Arrange Dashboard Content by Importance and Use, Not by Org Chart
description: >
  Placement should follow which data is most important, most urgent, or most
  needs to be compared to something else — not a default grouping by
  department or data source.
sources:
  - title: Information Dashboard Design
    resource: "Information Dashboard Design (Stephen Few), ch. 3 §3.9, ch. 7 §7.1"
---

Poor spatial arrangement — placement not driven by importance or intended
viewing sequence, groups that fragment related data into a "confusing
labyrinth" — produces clutter even with a modest amount of data. Arrangement
is not a cosmetic afterthought; it must fit how the data is actually used.

Practical rules, in order:

1. **Organize groups by business function, entity, or actual use** — the
   natural first-pass grouping follows functions (order entry, shipping),
   entities (departments, projects), or intended use (e.g., a need to compare
   revenue and expenses directly). But someone whose role spans many units
   (Few's example: a CEO) may need an integrated, cross-cutting grouping —
   e.g., ordered by relative importance to the bottom line rather than by
   org-chart structure. Learn precisely how the information will be *used*
   before deciding how to group it; don't default to organizational
   structure.
2. **Co-locate items that belong to the same group** — proximity is a
   preattentively perceived grouping cue; see
   [Gestalt proximity](gestalt-proximity.md).
3. **Delineate groups with the least visible means** — see
   [delineate groups with the least visible means](delineate-groups-with-least-visible-means.md).
4. Put the most important data in the highest-emphasis screen position — see
   [screen position emphasis](screen-position-emphasis.md).
5. Position data that needs to be compared so the comparison is easy to make —
   see [support meaningful comparisons](support-meaningful-comparisons.md).

A concrete anti-pattern: reserving the most prominent screen position for a
vendor logo or navigation controls while an average-order-size gauge (perhaps
not the most important measure) sits prominently, and two line graphs that
invite direct comparison are placed apart from each other rather than
adjacent or stacked.
