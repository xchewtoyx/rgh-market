title: "Gestalt Principle: Connection"
description: >
  Objects joined by a visible line are grouped together more strongly than
  proximity or similarity can achieve, making connection the right tool for
  showing relationships between entities.
sources:
  - title: Information Dashboard Design
    resource: "Information Dashboard Design (Stephen Few), ch. 4 §4.3"
  - title: Storytelling with Data
    resource: "Storytelling with Data (Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic), ch. 3"

One of six Gestalt grouping principles, and the second-strongest: objects
joined by a visible line or similar connector are perceived as grouped —
a stronger cue than [proximity](gestalt-proximity.md) or
[similarity](gestalt-similarity.md), weaker only than
[enclosure](gestalt-enclosure.md). Ordered strength hierarchy: enclosure >
connection > proximity/similarity. Line thickness and darkness can tune how
strongly connection competes with other cues — part of
[visual hierarchy](visual-hierarchy-with-preattentive-attributes.md).

Connection is especially useful for tying together non-quantitative data
where the relationship itself is the point — representing links between
process steps, or between people in an organization chart. Line graphs
leverage the same principle so the eye reads order and sequence across
points. See
[drawing objects for relationships](drawing-objects-for-relationships.md) for
how this plays out concretely (entities as shapes, relationships as
connecting lines, arrows added for directionality).
