---
type: concept
title: Delineate Groups with the Least Visible Means
description: >
  Separate dashboard sections using the least visually intrusive technique
  that works — white space first, subtle borders only when density leaves no
  room for white space.
sources:
  - title: Information Dashboard Design
    resource: "Information Dashboard Design (Stephen Few), ch. 7 §7.1"
---

Grid lines, borders, and fill colors used to separate groups are all
non-data pixels (see [data-ink ratio](data-ink-ratio.md)) and should be no
more visible than necessary to do the job. **White space is the least
visible, and preferred, means of delineation** — it relies purely on
[Gestalt proximity](gestalt-proximity.md) and costs nothing in visual weight.

Where data is packed too tightly for white space alone to read as a gap, use
a subtle, light border instead of removing the separation altogether — a
border can be surprisingly faint and still function as a group boundary,
because [Gestalt enclosure](gestalt-enclosure.md) works even with very low
contrast. Both approaches — four tables separated by white space, or four
tables placed close together and separated by light borders — are shown to
work equally well; the choice is about which fits the available density, not
which is "correct" in isolation.
