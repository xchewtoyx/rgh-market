---
type: concept
title: Color Logic on Dark Backgrounds
description: >
  Prefer light backgrounds so attention stays on the data, but when a dark
  brand template forces a dark ground, invert emphasis logic — white becomes
  the standout, yellow becomes highly attention-grabbing, and grey still
  recedes.
sources:
  - title: Storytelling with Data
    resource: "Storytelling with Data (Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic), ch. 9"
---

Default to a **white (or near-white) background**: dark grounds feel heavy
and pull the eye to the background; light-on-dark contrast can be strong but
is generally harder to read for dense quantitative marks. See
[aesthetics serve communication](aesthetics-serve-communication.md) and
[vivid color restraint](vivid-color-restraint.md).

When a brand template forces a dark, saturated frame, a white-background
makeover can look meek. Rework by **reversing normal color logic**:

- On white, distance from white = emphasis (grey barely pops; black pops a
  lot).
- On black, black is the baseline — grey still recedes, but **white** becomes
  the strong standout; colors normally avoided on white (e.g., yellow) become
  highly attention-grabbing.

Validate every accent
[in the actual surrounding context](color-perception-context-dependence.md),
not on a light swatch card. Content can be identical across light and dark
treatments while tone changes completely — choose the ground and accents so
they serve the communication, not just the template.
