---
type: concept
title: Usability-Test a Single Best-Effort Prototype
description: >
  Test one well-designed prototype with real users and real data rather than
  presenting multiple design alternatives for them to choose between.
sources:
  - title: Information Dashboard Design
    resource: "Information Dashboard Design (Stephen Few), ch. 7 §7.5"
---

Present intended users with a **single, best-effort prototype**, not several
alternative designs. The rationale: users know what they need to accomplish
but not how a dashboard *should* be visually designed to achieve it —
offering a menu of mediocre options just outsources design judgment back to
people who aren't equipped to exercise it. This is the same reasoning as
Alan Cooper's diagnosis in [dashboard definition](dashboard-definition.md)'s
framing of feature-list-driven, "disaster by committee" design.

Test the single prototype with real data and the actual intended users,
observing them use it rather than just asking their opinion of it. If a
display medium is unfamiliar to that audience, give simple instruction plus
the rationale for the choice — see
[customize numeric presentation to the audience](customize-numeric-presentation-to-audience.md).
A well-prepared design — one that already applied the book's principles —
usually yields only minor tweaks during usability testing, not major
revisions, though exceptions always occur "when dealing with the foibles of
human beings."
