---
type: concept
title: "Gestalt Principle: Proximity"
description: >
  Objects located near each other are perceived as belonging to the same
  group — often the simplest and least visually intrusive way to link data.
sources:
  - title: Information Dashboard Design
    resource: "Information Dashboard Design (Stephen Few), ch. 4 §4.3, ch. 7 §7.1"
  - title: Storytelling with Data
    resource: "Storytelling with Data (Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic), ch. 3"
---

One of six Gestalt grouping principles (Gestalt School of Psychology, 1912)
directly applicable to tying data together, separating it, or making it
stand out — and a toolkit for spotting [clutter](minimize-extraneous-cognitive-load.md)
versus signal. Proximity: objects placed close together are read as one group,
with no border or color needed to say so — plain white space is often the
simplest and least visually intrusive way to link related data (see
[delineate groups with the least visible means](delineate-groups-with-least-visible-means.md)).
In tables, varying spacing between rows or columns (rather than adding lines)
draws the eye down columns or across rows.

Proximity is also directional: placing sections closer together horizontally
encourages left-to-right scanning between them; placing sections closer
together vertically encourages top-to-bottom scanning. This makes proximity a
practical tool for shaping the order in which a viewer's eye moves across a
[dashboard](dashboard-definition.md), not just for showing that two things
are related — see
[arrange dashboard content by importance and use](spatial-arrangement-by-importance-and-use.md)
and [support meaningful comparisons](support-meaningful-comparisons.md).

Proximity is the weakest of the ordered grouping cues: enclosure is stronger,
and connection is stronger than proximity or similarity — see
[Gestalt connection](gestalt-connection.md).
