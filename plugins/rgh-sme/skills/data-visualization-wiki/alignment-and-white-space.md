---
type: concept
title: Alignment and White Space
description: >
  Clean horizontal and vertical alignment (prefer upper-left justification for
  how-to-read chrome) and deliberate white space make a layout comfortable;
  center-aligned floating titles and stuffed margins make even good content
  feel sloppy.
sources:
  - title: Storytelling with Data
    resource: "Storytelling with Data (Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic), ch. 3, ch. 8"
---

Thoughtful arrangement fades into the background; poor placement burdens the
audience even when takeaway, graph, and labels are each fine. Two levers:

**Alignment.** Prefer left-justified over center-aligned text — center
alignment rarely creates clean edges and reads as sloppy. Create continuous
horizontal and vertical lines among titles, legends, axes, and annotations.
Because audiences start top-left and scan in a "Z,"
**upper-left-justify** graph title, axis titles, and legend so "how to read
this" is encountered before the data ([screen position](screen-position-emphasis.md)).
Use rulers/grids (or a temporary invisible table) for precise placement.
Avoid diagonal lines and rotated labels — rotated text is substantially
slower to read (Wigdor & Balakrishnan, 2005: ~52% slower at 45°, ~205% at
90°); abbreviate category names so they stay horizontal instead.

**White space.** Blank ground is not wasted: like pauses in speech, it makes
the page breathable and draws attention to whatever is *not* blank. Keep
margins clear; size visuals to their content rather than stretching them to
fill the slide. Do not add data merely to fill emptiness — only add with a
specific purpose. White space is also the preferred
[group delineator](delineate-groups-with-least-visible-means.md) via
[Gestalt proximity](gestalt-proximity.md).

Together with [strategic contrast](vivid-color-restraint.md) and
[decluttering chrome](declutter-chart-chrome.md), alignment and white space
cut [extraneous cognitive load](minimize-extraneous-cognitive-load.md)
without changing the underlying message.
