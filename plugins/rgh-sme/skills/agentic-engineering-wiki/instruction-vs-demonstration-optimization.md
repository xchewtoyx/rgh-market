title: Instruction vs Demonstration Optimization
description: >
  Instruction-only optimizers rewrite system prompts; demonstration bootstrappers
  add few-shot traces — complementary levers that can be combined.
sources:
  - title: "TextGrad: Automatic Differentiation via Text"
    resource: "TextGrad (Yuksekgonul et al.), ch. 3 §3.3"
---

Two common [prompt optimization tooling](prompt-optimization-tooling.md)
strategies attack different parts of the prompt:

- **Instruction optimization** (e.g. TextGrad on a system prompt with
  `requires_grad`) rewrites natural-language guidance while keeping zero
  demonstrations — inference stays short.
- **Demonstration bootstrapping** (e.g. DSPy BootstrappedFewShot) mines
  few-shot traces that pass a metric and packs them into the prompt —
  often stronger format teaching via [in-context learning](in-context-learning.md),
  at higher token cost per call. This is one instance of
  [DSPy module parameterization](dspy-module-parameterization.md), where
  demonstrations are an explicit, searchable parameter rather than a
  hand-picked constant, generated by the
  [candidate-generation stage](dspy-compiler-three-stages.md) of DSPy's
  compiler.

On Big-Bench Hard Object Counting / Word Sorting and GSM8k, TextGrad’s
instruction-only prompts matched or beat DSPy with up to eight demos; combining
DSPy demos with a TextGrad-optimized instruction on GSM8k edged higher still.
Treat them as complementary: optimize the instruction for reasoning habits
([chain of thought](chain-of-thought-prompting.md), verify/re-check cues), then
decide whether demos are worth the inference budget. Prefer
[strong-to-weak prompt optimization](strong-to-weak-prompt-optimization.md)
when the instruction search can use a stronger feedback model than the
deployed reasoner.
