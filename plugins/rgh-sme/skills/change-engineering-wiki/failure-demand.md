# Failure Demand

Failure demand (John Seddon) is demand for work generated by failing to do
something correctly the first time: rework, incident response, and
hotfixing, as opposed to "value demand" — the actual feature or capability
work a team intends to spend its time on.

A high [change failure rate](change-failure-rate.md) is a failure-demand
generator: every failed change consumes engineering capacity on diagnosis,
rollback or hotfix, and follow-up, capacity that would otherwise go to
value work. Continuous Delivery technical practices ([build quality in](build-quality-in.md),
[working in small batches](working-in-small-batches.md)) reduce failure
demand by lowering the rate at which changes fail in the first place, which
is a stronger lever than getting faster at handling failures after they
happen.
