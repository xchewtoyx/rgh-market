---
type: concept
title: Obligation to Voice Critical Views
description: >
  In collective decision work, holding a critical opinion without
  speaking it withholds information the group needs — silence is
  treated as a failure of duty, not neutral assent.
sources:
  - title: "The Fearless Organization"
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Amy Edmondson), ch. 5"
---

Some high-stakes decision cultures treat unspoken criticism as
unethical, not merely cautious: if you think something critical,
you must say it, because unspoken ideas belong to the collective
enterprise trying to decide. The analogy is medical: if you have a
terminal illness, you need the truth to act — the same logic applies
to a plan heading toward failure that only you can see.

This is a stronger norm than
[poll-for-named-support](poll-for-named-support.md)'s procedural
check ("is there any disagreement?"). Polls treat silence as
unverified assent; an speak-up obligation treats silence as
withholding. It pairs with tools that make criticism legible and
recoverable — issue logs of mistakes and strengths, recorded
reactions to specific feedback, and norms against discussing someone
who is not present to hear it (talking behind backs undermines the
same transparency the obligation demands).

The norm only works when dissent can land without personal attack —
see [dissent-without-confrontation](dissent-without-confrontation.md)
and [precision-questioning](precision-questioning.md). It also
requires distinguishing opinions backed by experience from mere
conjecture; see [earned-opinion-right](earned-opinion-right.md).
Without those guardrails, "you must speak" becomes performance
conflict rather than [productive-idea-conflict](productive-idea-conflict.md).

Extreme versions of this culture are not for everyone — attrition
among newcomers is often high — but the decision-alignment lesson
is portable: design processes so critical views are expected inputs,
not heroic exceptions, and so the person who stays quiet while
knowing better bears responsibility alongside the person who
proposed the flawed plan.
