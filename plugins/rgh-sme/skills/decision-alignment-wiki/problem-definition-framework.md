---
type: concept
title: Problem Definition Framework
description: >
  A scaffold that defines a decision problem as opening scene,
  disturbing event, undesired result, desired result, and the
  resulting question before analyzing options.
sources:
  - title: "The Minto Pyramid Principle: Logic in Writing, Thinking and Problem Solving"
    resource: "The Minto Pyramid Principle (Barbara Minto), ch. 8"
---

Define the problem before analyzing solutions. This framework
names the gap that a recommendation must close:

1. **Opening scene** — the existing structure or process.
2. **Disturbing event** — external, internal, or newly recognized
   change that breaks the status quo (if unknown in a proposal,
   move directly to the undesired result).
3. **R1 (undesired result)** — what is going wrong now.
4. **R2 (desired result)** — what success looks like,
   stated specifically enough that options can be judged
   against it ([desired-result-as-decision-criterion](desired-result-as-decision-criterion.md)).
5. **Question** — the gap between R1 and R2 that the
   decision document must answer
   ([problem-stage-question](problem-stage-question.md)).

Visualize the opening scene as a simple process or structure.
A clear R1/R2 plus explicit constraints converts long history
and many options into one answerable question and a defensible
recommendation. This framing feeds
[strategic-diagnosis](strategic-diagnosis.md) and complements
[working-backward-problem-framing](working-backward-problem-framing.md),
which starts from a proposed action instead of from the gap.
