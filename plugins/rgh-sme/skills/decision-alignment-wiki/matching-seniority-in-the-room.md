---
type: concept
title: Matching Seniority in the Room
description: >
  Compose a working session from participants at roughly the
  same organizational level so one senior voice does not
  silently set the answer for everyone else.
sources:
  - title: "Designing Connected Content: Plan and Model Digital Products for Today and Tomorrow"
    resource: "Designing Connected Content (Mike Atherton, Carrie Hane), ch. 5"
---

In hierarchical organizations, a working session (a modeling
workshop, a requirements session, an options review) composed
of people at very different levels of seniority tends to
collapse into deference: junior or mid-level participants stop
offering genuine input once a senior figure has staked out a
position, whether or not that position is actually correct.
The room stops doing the analytical work it was convened for
and starts doing the social work of agreeing with the most
senior person present.

The fix is compositional, not behavioral: invite participants
at roughly the same organizational level to the same session,
rather than relying on facilitation technique alone to keep a
mixed-level room honest. Counterintuitively, some of the most
useful sessions come from participants who mildly disagree
with each other — a room with no visible disagreement is more
likely evidence of suppressed input than of genuine consensus.

This is a room-composition companion to
[individual-input-before-group-synthesis](individual-input-before-group-synthesis.md)
(which counters groupthink through procedure) and to
[group-risk-decision-biases](group-risk-decision-biases.md)
(which names specific ways a group's stated position diverges
from its members' real views) — all three exist because a
group's visible output does not automatically reflect what its
individual members actually think. See
[decision-influence-concentration](decision-influence-concentration.md)
for a way to check whether mismatched seniority actually produced
this collapse in a given session, rather than assuming it from the
composition alone.
