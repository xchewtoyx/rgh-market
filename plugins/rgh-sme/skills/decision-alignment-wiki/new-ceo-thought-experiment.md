The **New CEO Thought Experiment** is a cognitive technique used to overcome emotional attachment and status quo bias during critical business choices. 

When faced with a painful pivot or divestment, leaders ask themselves:
> *"If we got kicked out and the board brought in a new CEO, what would they do?"*

By shifting to an outsider perspective, decision-makers strip away historical commitments, sunk costs, and internal political pressures, making the logical recommendation clear.

For example, in 1985, Intel's leadership (Andy Grove and Gordon Moore) was struggling with whether to exit their core memory chip (DRAM) business. By asking what a new CEO would do, they realized the obvious choice was to exit DRAM and focus on microprocessors. The clarity gained from this question allowed them to execute the trade-off themselves rather than leaving it to a successor.

This technique is a valuable tool in options analysis, allowing teams to evaluate choices objectively and reinforce the [necessity-of-strategic-choice](necessity-of-strategic-choice.md).
