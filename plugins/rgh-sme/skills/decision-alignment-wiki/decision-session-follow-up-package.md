---
type: concept
title: Decision-Session Follow-Up Package
description: >
  Close a stakeholder alignment session with a fixed set of
  follow-up artifacts, sent promptly, so the decision and its
  next steps survive past the room where they were made.
sources:
  - title: "Deciphering Data Architectures"
    resource: "Deciphering Data Architectures (James Serra), ch. 3"
---

A session that ends in agreement but produces no written follow-up
loses most of its value within days — attendees' memories of what
was decided and who owns what diverge quickly once they scatter back
to other work. Send a fixed package shortly after the session, while
the discussion is still fresh:

- **Summary document** of what was discussed and decided, pitched at
  two depths if the audience needs it: a short version for
  stakeholders who weren't in the room, and a more detailed version
  for those who were.
- **The session's working artifact**, exported as-is — the diagram,
  option comparison, or whiteboard the group actually built together,
  not a redrawn or prettified version. Attendees recognize what they
  produced; a slicker restatement invites re-litigation of choices
  that were already settled.
- **Action items**: each with an owner and a next step, per
  [action-item-closure-tracking](action-item-closure-tracking.md) —
  not a bare list of what was agreed.
- **Parking-lot items** ([parking-lot-technique](parking-lot-technique.md)),
  written out in more detail than the session record captured, each
  with an owner, so deferred-but-real points don't quietly vanish
  once the list itself is no longer on screen.
- **A short feedback survey** on the session itself, timed so it
  actually gets completed — handed out near the end of an in-person
  session (e.g. during the last break) or posted in chat shortly
  before a remote session ends, with the link also included in the
  follow-up email as a backstop. Keep it brief; a long survey after a
  long session gets skipped.

Sending this promptly, rather than "getting to it later," is itself
part of the follow-up discipline: the gap between a session's
apparent agreement and its actually being written down is where
[unfinished-alignment-business](unfinished-alignment-business.md)
tends to resurface as later disagreement about what was actually
decided.
