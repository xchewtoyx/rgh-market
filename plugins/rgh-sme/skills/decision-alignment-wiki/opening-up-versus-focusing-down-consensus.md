---
type: concept
title: Opening-Up Versus Focusing-Down Consensus
description: >
  "Consensus" can mean finding common ground across existing
  views or opening each view as a window on a larger reality —
  decision teams need both modes and must not conflate them.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Peter Senge), ch. 11"
---

Experienced decision facilitators intuitively separate two kinds of
**consensus** that the same word covers:

- **Focusing-down consensus** — find the common denominator or
  shared content across individual views. This supports
  [dialogue-versus-discussion](dialogue-versus-discussion.md)'s
  **discussion** mode: converge on a choice among defended
  alternatives when a decision must be made now.
- **Opening-up consensus** — treat each person's view as a unique
  perspective on a larger reality; looking through one another's
  views reveals what neither could see alone. This supports
  **dialogue**: diverge to deepen understanding before converging.

Using focusing-down moves during an opening-up phase produces
premature compromise — the team settles on the lowest common
denominator before seeing the full problem. Using opening-up moves
during a focusing-down phase produces endless exploration when the
group needed a call — see
[consensus-driven-paralysis](consensus-driven-paralysis.md).

Regular [dialogue-session-ground-rules](dialogue-session-ground-rules.md)
build the opening-up kind; explicit decision closure in
[managing-alignment-meetings](managing-alignment-meetings.md)
supplies the focusing-down kind. Name which consensus the room is
pursuing before asking "do we agree?"
