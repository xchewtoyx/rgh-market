---
type: concept
title: Bad Strategic Objectives
description: >
  Objectives go bad as uncoordinated laundry lists or as
  blue-sky end-states that skip how to get there.
sources:
  - title: "Good Strategy/Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters"
    resource: "Good Strategy/Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters (Richard P. Rumelt), ch. 3"
---

A strategic objective is a means to an end. Objectives
become defective in two common forms:

**Dog's dinner objectives** — a chaotic list of dozens of
uncoordinated initiatives from planning retreats that
satisfy political factions and produce zero focus. This is
[consensus-driven-paralysis](consensus-driven-paralysis.md)
written as a portfolio, and the opposite of
[coherent-actions](coherent-actions.md).

**Blue-sky objectives** — restating the desired end-state
while skipping *how* to get there, often as hard as the
original problem ("transformational leadership teams,"
"empowered communities") without diagnosing real obstacles
([failure-to-face-the-challenge](failure-to-face-the-challenge.md)).

Good objectives concentrate effort behind a
[guiding-policy](guiding-policy.md). Prefer
[proximate-objectives](proximate-objectives.md) over laundry
lists and blue-sky restatements — those signal
[mistaking-goals-for-strategy](mistaking-goals-for-strategy.md).
