**Working-Backward Problem Framing** is a technique that shifts the analytical focus from "what we should do" to "what problem does this address?"

When a team proposes a specific action, they often suffer from [avoiding-quick-closure](avoiding-quick-closure.md). By working backward from the proposed action to the underlying challenge, decision-makers can:
1. **Identify Substitutes**: Surface alternative solutions that address the same root cause.
2. **Expose Second-Order Effects**: Understand the wider consequences of the proposed action.
3. **Reveal Constraints**: Uncover structural or political barriers that would limit the proposal's efficacy.

### Case Example
When TiVo originally framed its challenge as marketing a "time-shift television" device, its solutions focused on consumer advertising. Reframing the problem to *"the cable/satellite operator's control of the set-top box"* revealed that the core constraint was monopoly bundling. This shifted the strategic recommendation from marketing to legal and antitrust partnerships.

This reframing technique helps refine the
[strategic-diagnosis](strategic-diagnosis.md) and ensures
options analysis is based on the correct problem definition.
