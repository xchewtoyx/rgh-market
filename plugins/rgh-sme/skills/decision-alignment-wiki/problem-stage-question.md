---
type: concept
title: Problem-Stage Question
description: >
  The reader's decision question depends on how far the
  problem is already understood, and that question shapes
  the recommendation form.
sources:
  - title: "The Minto Pyramid Principle: Logic in Writing, Thinking and Problem Solving"
    resource: "The Minto Pyramid Principle (Barbara Minto), ch. 8"
---

After the
[problem-definition-framework](problem-definition-framework.md)
lays out opening scene, R1, and R2, the decision document's
job is set by *how much is already known*. Typical stages:

- How to move from R1 to R2 (solution unknown).
- Whether a proposed solution is right.
- How to implement an agreed solution.
- What to do after a failed solution.
- Which known alternative to choose.
- What strategy or objective to pursue when R2 is unclear.
- Whether R1 exists at all (e.g. benchmarking).

Matching the document to the real question prevents answering
the wrong one — for example arguing implementation steps when
stakeholders still disagree on R2
([desired-result-as-decision-criterion](desired-result-as-decision-criterion.md)).
The last known fact becomes the complication; the unanswered
gap becomes the question the recommendation must settle.

Once the stage question is clear, match the document to its
genre's expected shape:
