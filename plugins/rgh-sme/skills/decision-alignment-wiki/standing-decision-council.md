---
type: concept
title: Standing Decision Council
description: >
  A recurring, cross-functional review body that decides
  contested funding or priority calls, giving each hard
  decision institutional backing instead of one person's word.
sources:
  - title: "Agile Software Requirements: Lean Requirements Practices for Teams, Programs, and the Enterprise"
    resource: "Agile Software Requirements (Dean Leffingwell), ch. 12"
---

Form a standing group of the key stakeholders whose areas
compete for the same funding or priority, and have it meet on a
recurring cadence to review the backlog of candidate initiatives
and decide investment and resourcing. Membership should be set
deliberately — using
[stakeholder-analysis-attributes](stakeholder-analysis-attributes.md)
if the right members are not obvious — rather than defaulting to
whoever is easiest to convene.

The council's practical value is political cover for whoever has
to act on a hard call: "there were multiple opinions on this, but
this is what we agreed together" carries more weight, and is
harder to quietly reverse, than the same call issued by a single
owner. This directly counters
[single-champion-fragility](single-champion-fragility.md) — a
decision that rests on one advocate's continued presence decays
when they leave, but a decision the council owns collectively
survives any one member's departure. It also gives
[sponsorship-versus-mandate](sponsorship-versus-mandate.md) a
concrete mechanism: a council decision, minuted and revisited on
a cadence, is closer to mandate than any individual member's
private sponsorship could be on its own.
