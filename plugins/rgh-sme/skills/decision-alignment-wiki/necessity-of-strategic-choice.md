---
type: concept
title: Necessity of Strategic Choice
description: Strategy requires making difficult, mutually exclusive choices and explicitly rejecting alternative paths.
sources:
  - title: "Good Strategy/Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters"
    resource: "Good Strategy/Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters (Richard P. Rumelt), ch. 4"
---

Having a strategy requires choosing one path and explicitly eschewing others. When organizations fail to make these hard choices, they actively avoid the hard work of problem-solving. This often leads to strategic paralysis or fluffy, non-committal compromise statements that do not direct coordinated action.

A key challenge in decision-making is when leaders attempt to achieve consensus among team members with competing, mutually exclusive preferences. This dynamic can lead to [consensus-driven-paralysis](consensus-driven-paralysis.md), where group preferences cycle and prevent a stable majority from forming.

To overcome the emotional attachments, sunk costs, and internal biases that prevent decisive choice, decision-makers can employ the [new-ceo-thought-experiment](new-ceo-thought-experiment.md).
