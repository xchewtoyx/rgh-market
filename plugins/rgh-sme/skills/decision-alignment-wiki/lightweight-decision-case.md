---
type: concept
title: Lightweight Decision Case
description: >
  Cap a funding or go/no-go case at a page or two of essential
  fields so the decision stays fast, cheap to reverse, and easy
  to cancel if early progress disappoints.
sources:
  - title: "Agile Software Requirements: Lean Requirements Practices for Teams, Programs, and the Enterprise"
    resource: "Agile Software Requirements (Dean Leffingwell), ch. 21, 22, 23"
---

A long, exhaustively detailed business case creates the opposite
of what it seems to promise: false precision about a future that
is not actually knowable in that much detail, heavy overhead to
keep updating it, and — because so much has been invested in
producing it — real resistance to changing course once it is
approved, even when new information says the plan should change.
The heavier the document, the more a "no" or a "stop" later feels
like wasting everyone's prior effort, which quietly narrows the
set of outcomes the case can actually lead to.

The fix is a hard cap: a lightweight case of a page or two,
carrying only the fields a decision-maker actually needs —
recommendation, the essential rationale, who sponsors it, what it
affects, and the go/no-go call itself — rather than a
comprehensive specification of the solution. Cases built this way
are funded via short exploratory work that either earns
continued investment or gets canceled early, cheaply, without the
sunk-cost drag a heavier document would create.

This is a specific member of the family
[recurrent-decision-document-patterns](recurrent-decision-document-patterns.md)
catalogs — the spending/investment-request genre — but pushed
toward brevity as a deliberate choice, not an omission: a case
this short only works when it is paired with an
[opportunity-cost-decision-gate](opportunity-cost-decision-gate.md)
that can actually say no, and with cheap-to-reverse funding (fund
in small increments, cancel early) that makes the case's
inevitable gaps and later corrections affordable rather than a
sign the case itself was inadequate.
