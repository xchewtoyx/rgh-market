title: Precision Questioning
description: >
  Dissect a vague or overconfident claim with tactful, specific
  questions about definitions, evidence, and time frame — so
  reasoning is exposed for testing instead of attacked or waved
  away.
sources:
  - title: "Superforecasting: The Art and Science of Prediction"
    resource: "Superforecasting (Philip E. Tetlock, Dan Gardner), ch. 9"
---

**Precision questioning** is constructive confrontation: tactfully
dissecting vague claims rather than attacking the person, flatly
dismissing the view, or disagreeing too vaguely to be useful.

Given the claim "the popularity of soccer as a pastime is starting
to decline," poor responses are personal ("you're silly"),
dismissive ("I don't think so"), or too vague ("what do you mean?").
Useful responses zero in: "What do you mean by 'pastime'?" "What
evidence is there that soccer's popularity is declining?" "Over what
time frame?" The answers need not settle the matter; they expose
reasoning so the group can test it.

Applied retroactively to the Bay of Pigs contingency plan, the
sequence — "How far is it from the landing site to the Escambray
Mountains?" "Eighty miles." "And what's the terrain?" "Mostly swamp
and jungle." — would likely have surfaced a fatal flaw before
launch. That is the practical payoff: precision questions turn
[groupthink](groupthink.md)'s cozy assumptions into checkable facts.

This is the technique side of "disagree without being
disagreeable" (Andy Grove's constructive confrontation). It
complements [dissent-without-confrontation](dissent-without-confrontation.md)
(phrasing that lowers defensiveness) and
[fair-treatment-of-objections](fair-treatment-of-objections.md)
(testing objections seriously once raised). It differs from a
standing devil's-advocate role, which manufactures opposition to a
whole position; precision questioning drills into the weakest link
in whatever claim is on the table.

Team norms that support it: be cooperative but not deferential;
consensus is not always good and disagreement is not always bad;
agreement alone is never proof of being right; pointed questions
are as essential to a decision team as vitamins are to a body.
