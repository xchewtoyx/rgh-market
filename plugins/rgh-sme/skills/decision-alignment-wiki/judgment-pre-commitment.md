**Judgment Pre-Commitment** is a structured protocol designed to combat hindsight bias and improve decision-making capacity over time.

Without pre-commitment, once a decision outcome is known, hindsight bias causes individuals to believe they "knew it all along," preventing real learning.

### The Protocol
Before attending a critical alignment meeting or board discussion, participants must privately record:
1. Which issues will arise.
2. Which positions different stakeholders will take.
3. Their own best [strategic-diagnosis](strategic-diagnosis.md) and recommended action.

By comparing these pre-committed notes with the actual meeting outcomes and subsequent results, decision-makers can calibrate their judgment, reduce overconfidence, and build alignment based on objective track records.

This protocol ensures that a decision-making group remains accountable and that their decisions are systematically documented and defensible over time.

A narrower sibling technique, [pre-committing-success-criteria](pre-committing-success-criteria.md), applies the same before-you-look discipline to a single test or experiment: lock in the exact threshold that counts as a pass before gathering evidence, rather than pre-recording each participant's predicted position on a whole decision.

Pre-commitment defends against hindsight bias from the decider's own side; [unfalsifiable-bias-framing](unfalsifiable-bias-framing.md) defends the same territory from the reviewer's side — guarding against a critique that only ever identifies "bias" once the outcome is known, and would have called the opposite outcome biased just as readily.
