---
type: concept
title: Backcasting Technique
description: >
  Imagine the goal already achieved and work backward to the
  necessary path, the positive-space complement to a pre-mortem's
  negative space.
sources:
  - title: "Thinking in Bets: Making Smarter Decisions When You Don't Have All the Facts"
    resource: "Thinking in Bets (Annie Duke), ch. 6"
---

**Backcasting** asks a team to imagine a specific, concrete image
of future success — a headline announcing the goal was met — and
work backward from it to identify the path that must have been
taken to get there. Forcing the imagined outcome to be already
achieved, rather than merely hoped for, produces the same kind of
gain that a [pre-mortem-technique](pre-mortem-technique.md) gets
from imagining failure as already certain: treating an outcome as
settled, rather than speculative, measurably improves a person's
ability to correctly identify the causal factors that led to it —
one study found roughly a 30 percent gain in correctly identified
causal factors from this "prospective hindsight" framing, in either
direction.

Backcasting and a pre-mortem are complements, not alternatives, and
running only one is a mistake in the same direction each time:
vividly imagining a positive outcome without also confronting
obstacles reliably produces *worse* real-world performance than
imagining the obstacles too — across weight loss, job seeking, and
other goal pursuits, people who rehearsed only the success story
did worse than people who also rehearsed what stood in the way,
because success and failure probabilities are the two sides of one
total and only examining the positive side leaves the plan blind to
whichever branch it didn't rehearse. Run backcasting to identify
the path, then a pre-mortem to stress-test it — doing only the
former inherits the same optimism bias
[pre-mortem-technique](pre-mortem-technique.md) exists to correct.

Reframing failure-hunting as itself a "team player" activity —
casting the pre-mortem's obstacle-finder as the most productive
contributor to planning, not the least — also makes it easier to
get genuine engagement in the negative-space half of this pairing;
see [dissent-without-confrontation](dissent-without-confrontation.md)
for the broader technique of designating dissent as a legitimate,
expected role rather than something to be tolerated reluctantly.
