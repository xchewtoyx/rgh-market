---
type: concept
title: Escalating Unresolved Disagreements
description: >
  A structured path for taking a disagreement that working-level
  discussion cannot resolve to the designated decision-makers,
  instead of letting it stall or turn confrontational.
sources:
  - title: "Building Secure and Reliable Systems"
    resource: "Building Secure and Reliable Systems (Heather Adkins, Betsy Beyer, Paul Blankinship, Piotr Lewandowski, Ana Oprea, Adam Stubblefield), ch. 21"
---

When two parties hold genuinely different views on the best course
of action and ordinary discussion is not converging, the fix is not
to keep circling the same conversation — it is a deliberate
escalation to whoever actually holds decision authority:

1. **Get an outside read first.** Walk the situation past a
   colleague, mentor, or manager who has no stake in either side
   before deciding to escalate at all — an unbiased sounding board
   catches cases where the disagreement is smaller, or the framing
   is more one-sided, than it feels from inside it.
2. **Summarize the situation and the decision options, briefly and
   factually.** State each option and its likely impact as plainly
   as possible, linked to the underlying data, conversations, and
   designs rather than restated from memory. This summary is what
   the eventual decision-makers will actually read, so its clarity
   determines the quality of the decision they can make.
3. **Align your own leadership first.** Share the summary with your
   own chain before the joint session, both so they are not
   surprised and so related escalations can be merged or
   sequenced instead of arriving at the deciders piecemeal.
4. **Bring the affected chains together with named deciders.**
   Schedule a session where the designated decision-maker(s) from
   each affected side hear the same summary at the same time, then
   let them make the call — either in that session or in a
   follow-up they own.

Treating this as a routine, expected path — rather than a sign that
something has gone wrong — keeps disagreement from calcifying into
confrontation: two parties who can't agree on the best option is a
normal outcome of [necessity-of-strategic-choice](necessity-of-strategic-choice.md),
not a failure either side needs to hide from
[managing-alignment-meetings](managing-alignment-meetings.md).

This handles disagreement that has already been voiced. See
[surfacing-preconscious-team-knowledge](surfacing-preconscious-team-knowledge.md)
for the earlier, quieter failure — correct information a team member
holds but never mentions, so there is no disagreement to escalate
because no one realizes a conflicting view exists at all.
