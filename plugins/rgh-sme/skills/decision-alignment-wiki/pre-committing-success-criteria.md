---
type: concept
title: Pre-Committing Success Criteria
description: >
  Agree the exact threshold that will count as a pass before
  gathering evidence, so the team cannot silently disagree
  on what a result means or rationalize it after seeing it.
sources:
  - title: "Continuous Discovery Habits"
    resource: "Continuous Discovery Habits (Teresa Torres), ch. 10"
---

Vague success criteria ("some people should do X") let a
decision-making group discover only after the fact that they
never agreed on what the result would mean — one member reads
a mixed result as a pass, another reads the same numbers as a
fail, and the exercise that was supposed to inform the
decision instead becomes a fresh site of disagreement. Fix
this by committing, before gathering any evidence, to a
specific number: how many instances will be observed, and
exactly how many favorable outcomes count as a pass (e.g. "at
least 3 of 10," not "some people").

This upfront commitment serves two purposes at once: it
aligns the team on interpretation ahead of time, when nobody
yet has a stake in a particular reading, and it guards against
confirmation bias — once a result exists, people reliably find
reasons to read it as support for what they already wanted,
so the threshold has to be locked in before that motivation
appears. Setting the threshold is itself a judgment call, not
a formula: weigh how fast a signal the team needs against how
convincing a result would need to be before the team acts on
it, and adjust for known weaknesses in how the evidence will
be gathered (raise the bar if the method might be biased in
favor of the outcome someone hopes for).

This is a narrower sibling of
[judgment-pre-commitment](judgment-pre-commitment.md): that
technique pre-records each participant's predicted *position*
before a meeting to combat hindsight bias; this one
pre-records the group's shared *threshold* for interpreting
evidence before it arrives, to combat confirmation bias and
prevent the group discovering its disagreement only after
the fact.
