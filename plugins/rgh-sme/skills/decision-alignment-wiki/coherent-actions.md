**Coherent Actions** represent the final, action-oriented
component of [the-strategic-kernel](strategic-kernel.md).
They translate a high-level
[guiding-policy](guiding-policy.md) into concrete,
coordinated steps and resource allocations.

Feasible immediate actions are not downstream
"implementation details" — they are the punch of the
decision itself. Omitting them produces the
[execution-fallacy](execution-fallacy.md): goal lists
misread as strategy, then blamed as execution failure.

For actions to be coherent, they must satisfy two criteria:
1. **Functional Alignment**: Steps across different
   departments, teams, or time horizons must reinforce each
   other rather than work at cross-purposes.
2. **Design Coordination**: The power of strategy comes from
   concentrating effort. Uncoordinated lists of unrelated
   tasks (e.g., mixing HR changes with marketing spend
   without a unifying thread) represent basic management,
   not strategic coherence.

### The Trade-off of Coordination vs. Specialization
Centralized coordination is costly; it interrupts
specialized efficiency and can lead to bureaucracy. To
avoid stasis, decision-makers must **isolate and enforce
only essential coordination** where strategic leverage is
highest, rather than trying to connect everything to
everything.

Coherent actions ensure that a decision document leads
directly to coordinated execution with clear ownership.
