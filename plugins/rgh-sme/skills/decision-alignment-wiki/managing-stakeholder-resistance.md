During stakeholder alignment, resistance is a predictable emotional response to receiving help, dependency, exposure, loss of control, or the prospect of action. 

### Key Characteristics
- **Not an Analytical Deficit**: Resistance is not irrational opposition to data. Consequently, introducing more logic, evidence, or force generally intensifies it rather than resolving it.
- **Common Forms**: Silence, intellectualizing, requesting excessive detail, attacking credentials or data validity, denial, compliance without energy, and shifting responsibility to absent people.
- **Diagnostic Information**: The appearance of resistance often indicates that the alignment process has reached a critical, high-stakes issue.

### Approach
Decision-makers should view resistance as valuable information about the system's readiness to commit. Rather than fighting it head-on or defending recommendations, leaders should accept the stakeholders' right to disagree, which is necessary for authentic commitment. 

To resolve resistance, teams can use the [neutralizing-resistance-protocol](neutralizing-resistance-protocol.md) during [managing-alignment-meetings](managing-alignment-meetings.md).
