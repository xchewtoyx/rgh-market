---
type: concept
title: Surface Ambivalence Early
description: >
  Treat mixed motivation—wanting help while resisting
  exposure or loss of control—as expected contracting
  content, not as irrational delay.
sources:
  - title: "Flawless Consulting"
    resource: "Flawless Consulting (Peter Block), ch. 5"
---

A request for help always carries mixed motivation:
stakeholders want improvement and resist what help may
expose, change, or take from their control. Questions about
credentials, cost, and timing often express these deeper
concerns.

Surface ambivalence during
[stakeholder-contracting](stakeholder-contracting.md)
instead of waiting for it to appear as
[managing-stakeholder-resistance](managing-stakeholder-resistance.md)
after recommendations land. Naming the mixed motives early
prevents
[unfinished-alignment-business](unfinished-alignment-business.md)
and keeps the
[necessity-of-strategic-choice](necessity-of-strategic-choice.md)
honest: proceed only with real choice, not coerced
compliance.

Low motivation is the central case worth diagnosing directly:
ask whether the stakeholder has an urgent problem they want
solved, or whether they instead want a consultant, sponsor, or
facilitator to carry the responsibility for them. Asking
directly about desire, willingness to invest, and what they
want to be different distinguishes the two — and if
[naming-the-contracting-impasse](naming-the-contracting-impasse.md)
becomes necessary, this is usually the concern underneath it.
