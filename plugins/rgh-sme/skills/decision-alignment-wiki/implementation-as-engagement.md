---
type: concept
title: Implementation as Engagement
description: >
  Translating a decision into changed behavior depends on
  ownership and continued engagement more than on a
  technically perfect plan.
sources:
  - title: "Flawless Consulting"
    resource: "Flawless Consulting (Peter Block), ch. 19"
---

Knowing what to do and finding a way to do it are different
worlds. Organizations overinvest in analysis and
recommendations and underestimate the complexity of turning
decisions into changed behavior.

Implementation succeeds less through a technically right
plan than through ownership, repeated engagement, visible
choices, supportive structures, and attention to the
concerns action evokes. Treat it as a continuing phase after
the decision — not mechanical execution that ends when the
memo ships.

This is distinct from the
[execution-fallacy](execution-fallacy.md): Rumelt's point is
that coherent actions must be in the strategy; Block's
point is that even a clear decision still needs
[line-ownership-of-implementation](line-ownership-of-implementation.md)
and
[small-observable-commitments](small-observable-commitments.md).
Invite affected people into shaping the work, surface
doubts early
([managing-stakeholder-resistance](managing-stakeholder-resistance.md)),
and make each implementation event an example of the change
sought.
