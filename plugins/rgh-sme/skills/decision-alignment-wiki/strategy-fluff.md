type: concept
title: Strategy Fluff
description: >
  Superficial restatement of the obvious dressed in buzzwords
  that creates the illusion of a decision without selecting
  a path.
sources:
  - title: "Good Strategy/Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters"
    resource: "Good Strategy/Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters (Richard P. Rumelt), ch. 3"
---

**Strategy fluff** is a hallmark of bad strategy: restating
the obvious in inflated "Sunday words" and esoteric jargon
so a memo looks intellectual while saying nothing decidable.

Example: *"Our fundamental strategy is customer-centric
intermediation"* strips to "we take deposits and make
loans" — i.e., "our strategy is being a bank." Fluff
blocks the
[necessity-of-strategic-choice](necessity-of-strategic-choice.md)
and pairs with
[mistaking-goals-for-strategy](mistaking-goals-for-strategy.md)
and
[failure-to-face-the-challenge](failure-to-face-the-challenge.md).

In decision documents, strip jargon and ask whether a
reader could reject an alternative against a concrete
[desired-result-as-decision-criterion](desired-result-as-decision-criterion.md).
If not, the text is fluff, not a recommendation.
