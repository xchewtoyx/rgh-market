title: Absorb Strategic Ambiguity
description: >
  Leadership takes on high-level ambiguity and converts it
  into a concrete proximate objective for operational teams.
sources:
  - title: "Good Strategy/Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters"
    resource: "Good Strategy/Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters (Richard P. Rumelt), ch. 7"
---

A primary responsibility of strategic leadership is
**absorbing ambiguity**: taking unresolved high-level
uncertainty and translating it into a concrete, achievable
[proximate objective](proximate-objectives.md) for the
people who must act.

Without that translation, teams face
[bad-strategic-objectives](bad-strategic-objectives.md) or
invent local goals that do not cohere
([coherent-actions](coherent-actions.md)). Absorbing
ambiguity is not
[avoiding-quick-closure](avoiding-quick-closure.md) —
diagnosis and option testing still happen upstream — it is
refusing to push unresolved strategic fog onto operators as
their "strategy."

In decision memos, name what has been resolved for the next
phase and what remains open for later recontracting under
[implementation-as-engagement](implementation-as-engagement.md).
Check
[objective-capability-prerequisites](objective-capability-prerequisites.md)
so the proximate target matches routines the organization
already has.
