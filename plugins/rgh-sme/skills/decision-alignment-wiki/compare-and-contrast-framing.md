---
type: concept
title: Compare-and-Contrast Framing
description: >
  Pose decisions as a choice among several named options
  rather than a yes/no on one, to counter narrow framing.
sources:
  - title: "Continuous Discovery Habits"
    resource: "Continuous Discovery Habits (Teresa Torres), ch. 2"
---

"Whether or not" framing ("should we build this feature?")
presents a single option and invites a binary yes/no —
narrowing the decision to the one alternative someone
happened to propose first. "Compare and contrast" framing
("which of these opportunities is most important to address
now?") forces at least two named alternatives onto the table
before a choice is made.

This is one of Chip and Dan Heath's four antidotes to their
"villains of decision-making" (narrow framing, confirmation
bias, short-term emotion, overconfidence — from *Decisive*).
Narrow framing is the default failure mode: a team fixates on
its first idea and evaluates only whether to do it, not
whether it beats the alternatives.

Widening a decision from "whether or not" to "compare and
contrast" is a lightweight framing discipline applicable
wherever options are being decided among — it complements
[funneling-to-decision-themes](funneling-to-decision-themes.md)
by ensuring the candidate set reaching that funnel already
spans real alternatives, not one proposal plus its rejection.
[Dot-voting-to-narrow-options](dot-voting-to-narrow-options.md)
and
[multi-lens-comparison-without-false-precision](multi-lens-comparison-without-false-precision.md)
are two ways to actually run the comparison once framed this
way. Once options are on the table, give each a
[fair-treatment-of-objections](fair-treatment-of-objections.md)
rather than dismissing the ones not chosen. Make sure the options
that reach the table are actually different from each other, not
near-twins — see
[radically-different-alternatives](radically-different-alternatives.md)
— and don't assume the comparison must end in picking one of them
unchanged; see
[synthesize-across-compared-alternatives](synthesize-across-compared-alternatives.md).
