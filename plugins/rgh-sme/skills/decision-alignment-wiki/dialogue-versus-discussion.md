---
type: concept
title: Dialogue Versus Discussion
description: >
  Dialogue diverges to access a larger pool of shared meaning;
  discussion converges to choose among defended views — a
  decision team must master both and not confuse them.
sources:
  - title: "The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization"
    resource: "The Fifth Discipline (Peter Senge), ch. 11"
---

Team learning requires mastering two distinct ways teams converse —
**dialogue** and **discussion** — with different ground rules and
goals. Failing to distinguish them, teams usually get neither
productive dialogue nor productive discussion.

**Discussion** (root: percussion, concussion) is a ping-pong of
presenting and defending views, with an implicit goal of winning —
having one's view prevail. Different views are weighed to support a
decision that must be made now. Discussions **converge** on a
conclusion or course of action.

**Dialogue** (Greek *dialogos*, "meaning flowing through") accesses
a larger pool of common meaning not reachable alone — "the whole
organizes the parts." Purpose is to go beyond any one person's
understanding; in dialogue "we all win if we are doing it right."
Dialogues **diverge**; they deepen grasp of complex issues, not
immediate agreement. New actions may emerge as a byproduct, not as
the focus.

Dialogue reveals incoherence in collective thought — for example,
thought denying that it is participative, thought drifting from
reality like a program, or thought using its own broken frame as
the standard for fixing problems it helped create. Participants
become observers of thinking itself, distinguishing the ongoing
stream of "thinking" from individual "thoughts" that surface.

A learning team moves back and forth between the two modes.
Regular dialogue builds trust that carries into discussions and
teaches members to hold views gently rather than being held by
positions. When a decision is due, alternatives surfaced in
dialogue must still be weighed in discussion — see
[managing-alignment-meetings](managing-alignment-meetings.md).

Procedural support for dialogue includes
[dialogue-session-ground-rules](dialogue-session-ground-rules.md).
Bridgewater's [discussion-debate-teaching-modes](discussion-debate-teaching-modes.md)
label a related but separate taxonomy oriented to hierarchy and
skill gaps. Both frameworks share the rule: do not run a
convergence meeting with divergence norms, or vice versa.
