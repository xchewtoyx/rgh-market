---
type: concept
title: Empirical Test Over Authority
description: >
  Settle a live disagreement between technical alternatives by
  building and observing both, rather than deferring to the
  most senior voice in the room.
sources:
  - title: "Agile Software Requirements: Lean Requirements Practices for Teams, Programs, and the Enterprise"
    resource: "Agile Software Requirements (Dean Leffingwell), ch. 20"
---

When a disagreement is genuinely about which of two technical
alternatives will work better, and testing both is actually
feasible, resolve it by building and observing rather than by
deferring to whoever is most senior in the room — a pattern
nicknamed the **HiPPO** problem (Highest Paid Person's Opinion).
The instruction is concrete: "if you have a question about what
you should do, code it up, let people use it, and see which
alternative gives you the results you want." The decision
authority shifts from a person's rank to the observed outcome.

This only applies where
[reversibility-as-decision-criterion](reversibility-as-decision-criterion.md)
already says quick, cheap experimentation is warranted — a
two-way-door choice where building both options costs less than
the meetings it would take to argue one into existence. It does
not extend to one-way-door choices, where the cost of building
and discarding a wrong alternative is exactly the kind of
expense that criterion says to avoid.

Distinguish this from
[multi-lens-comparison-without-false-precision](multi-lens-comparison-without-false-precision.md):
that technique is for options that are genuinely subjective and
not resolvable by observation (strategic fit, positioning).
Empirical testing over authority applies instead when the
disagreement really is an empirical question with a knowable
answer, and the only thing standing between the group and that
answer is deference to seniority.
