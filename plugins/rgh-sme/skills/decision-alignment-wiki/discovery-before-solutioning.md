---
type: concept
title: Discovery Before Solutioning
description: >
  Withhold specific solution options until discovery questions are
  fully asked, so the recommendation is narrowed from what the
  stakeholder actually needs rather than pitched from what the
  facilitator already has on hand.
sources:
  - title: "Deciphering Data Architectures"
    resource: "Deciphering Data Architectures (James Serra), ch. 3"
---

A facilitator who knows a technology or product well is tempted to
show it early — but presenting solution options before discovery is
complete risks solving a problem the stakeholder does not actually
have. Stay with discovery questions (current pain points, current
architecture, prior decisions already made, business context, desired
outcomes) until they are exhausted, even when a plausible answer
seems obvious partway through. Jumping to a demo or product pitch
before discovery is finished can waste real effort on something the
stakeholder didn't need — the fix costs one uncomfortable moment of
patience up front, the alternative costs a redone engagement.

The discipline is: ask questions first, then narrow to a workable
set of options — rather than opening with the full range of what's
available and letting the stakeholder pick blind. Showing everything
at once shifts the cognitive load of narrowing onto someone who does
not yet have enough context to do it well; asking first and narrowing
second keeps that work with whoever best understands the problem
space.

This sharpens the general instruction in step 2 of
[contracting-meeting-structure](contracting-meeting-structure.md) —
"do not jump to solutions; pursue why the issue matters" — into a
concrete failure mode and its cost, and applies equally inside a
working/discovery session with stakeholders, not only at initial
contracting. It also motivates why
[managing-alignment-meetings](managing-alignment-meetings.md) treats
the stakeholder's own talking, not the facilitator's presenting, as
the bulk of a session's early time.
