---
type: concept
title: Team-Then-Stakeholder Review Sequence
description: >
  Run an honest internal delivery-team review in a safe space
  before widening to a public stakeholder review that connects
  results back to the larger product vision.
sources:
  - title: "User Story Mapping"
    resource: "User Story Mapping (Jeff Patton), ch. 18"
---

After a development cycle, review work in **two deliberate
audiences**, not one combined session:

**Team product review and reflection** — restricted to people who
understood and planned the work together: product owner, builders,
QA, and active delivery roles. Business stakeholders are excluded
on purpose: the team needs a safe place to grade quality honestly,
discuss overhang (work started but not finished), compare discovery
time budgeted versus used, and try process changes without
performing for outsiders.

**Stakeholder product review** — once the team has fairly evaluated
its own product, widen to anyone in the organization interested.
These people were not part of deferral and scoping decisions, so
the team must connect what was delivered to the **bigger product
vision** — individual stories are "little rocks chipped off a
larger vision," and outsiders will notice what is missing. Bring
the whole delivery team so members see that their work matters to
the organization.

The sequence matters for alignment: internal candor first, then
external narrative. Skipping the team-only pass produces either
dishonest public reviews or no space to fix quality and planning
before stakeholders see the work. Skipping the stakeholder pass
leaves decision-makers who must fund and prioritize next cycles
without the context to agree or redirect.

Pair internal review with
[decision-session-follow-up-package](decision-session-follow-up-package.md)
discipline (what was decided, what is next) and stakeholder review
with [walk-stakeholders-through-the-reasoning](walk-stakeholders-through-the-reasoning.md)
— outsiders need the *why*, not only a demo.
