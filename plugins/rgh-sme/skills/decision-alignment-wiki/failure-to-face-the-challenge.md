---
type: concept
title: Failure to Face the Challenge
description: >
  A decision that skips naming and diagnosing the real
  obstacle cannot be evaluated, improved, or executed.
sources:
  - title: "Good Strategy/Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters"
    resource: "Good Strategy/Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters (Richard P. Rumelt), ch. 3"
---

Bad strategy often fails to recognize, define, or diagnose
the underlying problem. If you cannot define the obstacle,
you cannot evaluate or improve the approach.

Listing goals ("improve margins," "defuse regional
conflicts") without saying *why* the problem persists is not
[strategic-diagnosis](strategic-diagnosis.md).
Underperformance is a **result**, not a diagnosis — a
defensible decision needs a plausible theory of cause and a
lever that would change the dynamic
([problem-definition-framework](problem-definition-framework.md)).

When documents skip the challenge, they invite
[bad-strategic-objectives](bad-strategic-objectives.md) and
the [execution-fallacy](execution-fallacy.md). Face the
obstacle first; then choose a
[guiding-policy](guiding-policy.md).
