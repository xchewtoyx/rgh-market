type: concept
title: Naming the Contracting Impasse
description: >
  When agreement stalls, name the stuckness directly instead
  of treating it as routine clarification, then offer real
  alternatives rather than conceding to reach a deal.
sources:
  - title: "Flawless Consulting"
    resource: "Flawless Consulting (Peter Block), ch. 8"
---

Agreement in
[contracting-meeting-structure](contracting-meeting-structure.md)
sometimes stalls: wants and offers do not match, the
discussion circles without progress, commitments stay vague,
or a party keeps deferring the decision. The first mistake is
treating this as normal back-and-forth clarification instead
of recognizing it as an impasse.

To work through it:
1. **Name it in the here and now** — state plainly that the
   conversation is stuck, rather than continuing to circle.
2. **Restate the different wants and offers** so both sides
   hear the actual gap, not a blurred version of it.
3. **Ask what makes agreement difficult** and pursue the
   concern beneath the stated position — this is where
   [surface-ambivalence-early](surface-ambivalence-early.md)
   material often surfaces.
4. **Do not resolve the other party's discomfort by quietly
   dropping your own requirements** — a contract reached that
   way is not a real agreement and reappears later as
   [unfinished-alignment-business](unfinished-alignment-business.md).

If no mutual contract emerges even after naming the impasse,
offer explicit alternatives instead of forcing a deal: revise
the scope, bring in a currently missing party
([multi-party-decision-contracts](multi-party-decision-contracts.md)),
postpone, or decline the work entirely. Leaving a no-win
negotiation without blaming the other party keeps the
relationship usable for a future attempt, and a clear no is a
better outcome than a project built on hidden reluctance.
