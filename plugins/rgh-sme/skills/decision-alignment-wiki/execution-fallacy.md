The **execution fallacy** treats failure as an implementation
gap when the real gap is an incomplete decision. Performance
targets, market-share ambitions, and vision statements feel
like strategy but do not select a path — they do not specify
*how*, *why*, and *where* energy and resources apply. That
content defect is
[mistaking-goals-for-strategy](mistaking-goals-for-strategy.md).

When a decision document stops at goals, stakeholders later
report "execution problems." A true decision specifies
feasible immediate
[coherent-actions](coherent-actions.md) as part of the
argument, not as a separate afterthought. Those actions are
the punch of the
[strategic-kernel](strategic-kernel.md), carrying out a
[guiding-policy](guiding-policy.md) grounded in
[strategic-diagnosis](strategic-diagnosis.md).

Symptoms of the fallacy:
- Strategy equated with ambition, determination, or
  financial targets.
- High-sounding ends ("growth," "excellence") without a
  coordinating approach to obstacles
  ([failure-to-face-the-challenge](failure-to-face-the-challenge.md)).
- Refusal to diagnose hard obstacles or make the
  [necessity-of-strategic-choice](necessity-of-strategic-choice.md)
  that would offend internal interests.
- [Strategy-fluff](strategy-fluff.md) or
  [bad-strategic-objectives](bad-strategic-objectives.md)
  standing in for a kernel.
