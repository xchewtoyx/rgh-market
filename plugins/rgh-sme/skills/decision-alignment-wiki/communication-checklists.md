In highly complicated or complex environments, top-down command-and-control structures fail due to information bottlenecks. To achieve reliability, organizations can implement **Communication Checklists** (or submittal schedules).

Unlike standard procedural checklists that enforce sequential tasks, communication checklists:
- Dictate **who must talk to whom and when** rather than prescribing the exact solution.
- Force multi-disciplinary experts to collaborate on complex trade-offs and unexpected site conditions.
- Facilitate [decentralized-decision-making-under-complexity](decentralized-decision-making-under-complexity.md) by ensuring the right people are aligned before actions are taken.

For example, modern construction projects use communication schedules to force structural engineers, contractor teams, and trade leads to align on conflicts (such as design clashes or cost-saving material substitutions) within strict resolution windows, ensuring near-zero catastrophic structural failures.
