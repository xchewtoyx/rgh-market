---
type: concept
title: Cumulative Voting Budget
description: >
  Give each participant a fixed budget to spend across
  options however they like, so a vote can express intensity
  of preference instead of just a single pick.
sources:
  - title: "Agile Software Requirements: Lean Requirements Practices for Teams, Programs, and the Enterprise"
    resource: "Agile Software Requirements (Dean Leffingwell), ch. 12"
---

Give each participant a fixed amount of "virtual money" (a
common figure is $100) to allocate across the candidate options
in whatever proportion they choose — all of it on one option,
or split thinly across several. Unlike a fixed number of votes
spent one-per-option
([dot-voting-to-narrow-options](dot-voting-to-narrow-options.md)),
a spendable budget lets a participant express *how much* they
prefer one option over another, not just *that* they prefer it —
someone who feels strongly about a single option can put all
$100 behind it, while someone genuinely torn between three can
spread their budget evenly.

Use this instead of plain dot-voting when the group's felt
intensity of preference is itself decision-relevant information
— for example when a handful of options are each backed by a
different stakeholder faction and the strength of each faction's
conviction matters for sequencing, not just which options clear
a threshold. It shares dot-voting's core discipline: score only
the criterion the decision is actually about, and treat the
result as an input to a real group conversation rather than an
automatic, un-discussed cutoff.
