---
type: concept
title: Diagnostic Versus Decision Structures
description: >
  Use diagnostic frameworks to find what causes the
  undesired result; use decision trees for action
  dependencies once the choice path is clear.
sources:
  - title: "The Minto Pyramid Principle: Logic in Writing, Thinking and Problem Solving"
    resource: "The Minto Pyramid Principle (Barbara Minto), ch. 9"
---

Decision documents mix two different analytical jobs. Keep
them distinct:

**Diagnostic frameworks** generate yes/no questions about
what is causing R1. Build them by mapping the relevant
system (structure, cause-and-effect trees, MECE cause
classes, or sequential choice frames). Yes/no answers
isolate causes and mark when diagnosis is done — the path
into [strategic-diagnosis](strategic-diagnosis.md) and
[yes-no-issue-analysis](yes-no-issue-analysis.md).

**Decision trees / dependency diagrams** reveal action
order and prerequisites once you are choosing or
implementing a solution. They organize
[coherent-actions](coherent-actions.md), not cause-finding.

Logic trees also help generate solution options, expose
gaps in grouped ideas, and organize issue analysis. Using a
decision tree when you still need diagnosis — or dumping
diagnostic detail into an implementation plan — confuses the
[problem-stage-question](problem-stage-question.md) and
delays a clear recommendation.
