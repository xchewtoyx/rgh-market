---
type: concept
title: Line Ownership of Implementation
description: >
  The line organization owns implementation results;
  facilitators design process and make commitments explicit
  without repossessing responsibility.
sources:
  - title: "Flawless Consulting"
    resource: "Flawless Consulting (Peter Block), ch. 19"
---

The client or line organization owns implementation. External
or staff helpers can design processes, provide expertise,
convene learning, and help make commitments explicit — but
must not repossess responsibility for results.

Repossession looks like writing the plan for them, chasing
every deliverable, or becoming the single owner named in the
decision document. That produces compliance without energy
and collapses when the helper leaves.

Results cannot be guaranteed: stakeholders control whether
to act. Evaluate the process on authentic phase completion
and developing capacity
([decision-engagement-phases](decision-engagement-phases.md)),
even if a project stops or recommendations go unused. The
right to decline or fail must not provoke takeover.

Decision documents and
[managing-alignment-meetings](managing-alignment-meetings.md)
should name line owners for
[coherent-actions](coherent-actions.md) and keep
follow-through under
[implementation-as-engagement](implementation-as-engagement.md)
with
[small-observable-commitments](small-observable-commitments.md)
held by those owners.

An owner cannot actually own the outcome, though, if they only
received the plan and not the reasoning behind it — see
[communicating-intent-for-coordinated-action](communicating-intent-for-coordinated-action.md)
for what a decision handoff needs to include so the owner can adapt
when field conditions depart from what the plan anticipated, instead
of either freelancing or stalling.

Correct line ownership is not by itself sufficient insurance:
see
[single-champion-fragility](single-champion-fragility.md)
for the related risk that even a properly line-owned decision
can still collapse if it depends on one internal advocate's
continued tenure rather than institutionalized process.
