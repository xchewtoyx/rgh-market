---
type: concept
title: Objective Capability Prerequisites
description: >
  High-level objectives are only executable when lower-level
  routines and skills are already operational.
sources:
  - title: "Good Strategy/Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters"
    resource: "Good Strategy/Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters (Richard P. Rumelt), ch. 7"
---

Complex objectives sit on a hierarchy of competences.
Higher rungs require lower rungs to be automatic — as
formation flying requires night flying, which requires basic
autorotation control.

Organizations cannot execute ambitious strategies (or even
demanding
[proximate-objectives](proximate-objectives.md)) if
foundational cross-functional routines are not already
working. Setting a target that skips missing capabilities
recreates
[bad-strategic-objectives](bad-strategic-objectives.md) and
feeds the
[execution-fallacy](execution-fallacy.md).

When writing
[coherent-actions](coherent-actions.md), ask which
prerequisite skills must already exist — and if they do not,
make building them the proximate objective first
([absorb-strategic-ambiguity](absorb-strategic-ambiguity.md)).
