---
type: concept
title: ROAM Risk Disposition
description: >
  Force every risk or impediment surfaced during a planning
  event into one of a fixed set of dispositions — Resolved,
  Owned, Accepted, or Mitigated — so none are left vague.
sources:
  - title: "Agile Software Requirements: Lean Requirements Practices for Teams, Programs, and the Enterprise"
    resource: "Agile Software Requirements (Dean Leffingwell), ch. 16"
---

A planning or decision event that surfaces risks and impediments
without a mechanism to dispose of them produces a list that
looks thorough but changes nothing: items get named, nodded at,
and then forgotten because nobody was forced to say what happens
to each one next. **ROAM** fixes this by requiring every surfaced
item to land in exactly one of four categories before the
session ends:

- **Resolved** — no longer a concern; move it off the active
  list entirely.
- **Owned** — cannot be resolved in the room, but a named
  person or team takes it on, committing to whatever action
  keeps it from jeopardizing the plan.
- **Accepted** — a real risk, but one the group deliberately
  decides to understand and live with as-is, rather than act on.
- **Mitigated** — reduced, though not eliminated, by a specific
  action taken or agreed.

Discuss each item until the group actually agrees on its
category — the categorization itself is where the group's real
judgment about the risk gets made, not a bureaucratic label
applied afterward. This is a stronger discipline than merely
listing risks: it rules out the default failure mode where an
unresolved item just sits, unowned and unacknowledged, until it
resurfaces as a surprise later.

ROAM's "Owned" category is what
[action-item-closure-tracking](action-item-closure-tracking.md)
then tracks to actual completion — ROAM decides *what to do* with
each open item at the moment it surfaces; closure tracking
verifies, after the fact, that an Owned item's owner actually
followed through.
