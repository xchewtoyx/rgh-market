**Funneling** means cutting abundant findings down to a
small number of significant, controllable themes. Collect
enough information to reveal patterns — not every fact —
including political, relational, and management-style data
that determine whether action is possible.

A long comprehensive report can be a way of avoiding the
writer’s judgment and the reader’s choice. That habit fights
[yes-no-issue-analysis](yes-no-issue-analysis.md) and
[necessity-of-strategic-choice](necessity-of-strategic-choice.md).

Prepare each theme with
[observation-interpretation-implication](observation-interpretation-implication.md)
logic so the picture is understandable, accurate enough,
nonpunitive, and connected to a decision. Distinguish data
from interpretation; leave room for stakeholders to test and
add without abandoning an independent view
([recommendations-begin-conversation](recommendations-begin-conversation.md)).
Use the
[three-layer-system-picture](three-layer-system-picture.md)
as the funnel’s depth check.

When the funnel is run live with the stakeholders who contributed
the raw findings, rather than by a solo analyst afterward, see
[consolidation-requires-proposer-consent](consolidation-requires-proposer-consent.md)
for the safeguard that keeps merging near-duplicates from silently
dropping part of what someone actually meant.

**Map each theme back to the specific commitment it threatens**, not
just to a category of concern. A pile of individually technical-
sounding findings ("scattered documentation gaps," "inconsistent
failure handling") reads as background noise to a manager until each
resulting theme is explicitly tied to the particular business goal
or public commitment it puts at risk — the same finding, reframed
this way, becomes a threat to something the audience already
committed to on record, not a fresh ask to care about a new topic.
This is the concrete mechanism behind
[building-a-case-for-change](building-a-case-for-change.md)'s
"align incentives" step when the case is built from a funneled set
of findings rather than a single proposal.
