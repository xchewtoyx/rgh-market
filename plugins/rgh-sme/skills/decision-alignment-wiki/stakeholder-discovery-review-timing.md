title: Stakeholder Discovery Review Timing
description: >
  Review discovery work with stakeholders before heavy build
  investment — that is when their feedback can still change
  direction cheaply.
sources:
  - title: "User Story Mapping"
    resource: "User Story Mapping (Jeff Patton), ch. 18"
---

The best time to get stakeholder feedback on a product direction
is **before** heavy investment in building — when discovery work
can still be shown, debated, and redirected.

In a stakeholder product review, treat **discovery work** as its
own category alongside delivery:

- Who the opportunity is for and why you are pursuing it.
- Expected outcomes if it succeeds.
- Work done to understand problem and solution.
- Prototypes, experiments, and what customers or users actually said.

"The only thing that trumps an executive opinion is a cold, hard
fact" — user evidence and experiment results belong in the review
package before executives' preferences anchor the room. This is the
decision-alignment mirror of
[discovery-before-solutioning](discovery-before-solutioning.md):
stakeholders who were not in the room during scoping will otherwise
judge delivery against a vision they never agreed to.

Delivery work is reviewed at **solution** level (the minimum viable
solution as the "big rock"), including target customers, success
criteria, holistic progress toward releasability, and why the
software may look incomplete if you are using a breadth-first
strategy (sketch of the whole canvas, not a finished square inch).

Be ready to write new stories from feedback surfaced in the review.
Some suggestions will miss the current target outcome — handle those
with [anchor-stakeholder-suggestions-to-outcomes](anchor-stakeholder-suggestions-to-outcomes.md)
rather than dismissive rejection.
