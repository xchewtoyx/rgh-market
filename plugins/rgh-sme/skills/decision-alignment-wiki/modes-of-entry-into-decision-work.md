---
type: concept
title: Modes of Entry into Decision Work
description: >
  Whether decision-support work arrives invited, self-initiated,
  or assigned by leadership changes what must be established
  before contracting can proceed honestly.
sources:
  - title: "Flawless Consulting"
    resource: "Flawless Consulting (Peter Block), ch. 7"
---

Work arrives in one of three ways, and each demands a
different opening move before
[stakeholder-contracting](stakeholder-contracting.md) can
begin on solid ground:

- **Invited** — the client asks directly. Resist accepting
  the request at face value too quickly; ask about the
  client's real concern, motivation, and expectations before
  agreeing to a scope.
- **Want in** — the consultant or facilitator initiates,
  seeking entry to work they believe is needed. Disclose the
  agenda openly rather than disguising advocacy as neutral
  help; a hidden agenda here becomes
  [unfinished-alignment-business](unfinished-alignment-business.md)
  later.
- **Sent in** — leadership assigns the work to an operating
  client who did not request it. The sponsor's authority does
  not substitute for that client's own commitment. Acknowledge
  the mandate openly, give the client room to express
  resistance, and contract directly with them about what each
  party can actually choose — treating the sponsor's say-so as
  equivalent to the client's agreement is a common source of
  later
  [managing-stakeholder-resistance](managing-stakeholder-resistance.md).

In every mode, the goal is the same explicit mutual agreement
about the work and the working relationship — including the
freedom to agree not to work together — established through
[contracting-meeting-structure](contracting-meeting-structure.md).
