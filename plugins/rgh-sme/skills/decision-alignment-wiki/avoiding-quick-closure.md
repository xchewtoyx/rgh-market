**Quick Closure** is a cognitive bias where decision-makers jump to the first salient problem area and generate a single recommendation, failing to re-evaluate their initial problem framing or generate alternative options.

### Root Cause
Complex, ill-structured situations produce significant psychological discomfort. The first internally coherent idea provides immediate relief and cognitive orientation. Because returning to open-ended ambiguity is uncomfortable, teams tend to spend their energy justifying their initial hunch rather than critically analyzing it or exploring alternatives.

### Countermeasures
To produce defensible decisions, teams must combat quick closure through:
- **Reframing**: Flipping the discussion using [working-backward-problem-framing](working-backward-problem-framing.md).
- **Alternative Generation**: Forcing the team to develop and compare at least two distinct, mutually exclusive proposals.
- **Rigor Testing**: Applying the [create-destroy-technique](create-destroy-technique.md) to challenge the primary recommendation.
- **Gating on Past Mistakes**: Running a
  [decision-checklist-as-bias-gate](decision-checklist-as-bias-gate.md)
  built from catalogued prior errors before committing.
- **Imagined Failure**: Running a
  [pre-mortem-technique](pre-mortem-technique.md) before
  committing, to surface risks the team would otherwise only
  see in hindsight.

Resisting quick closure ensures that options analysis is rigorous and that the final path is chosen based on merit rather than convenience, supporting the [necessity-of-strategic-choice](necessity-of-strategic-choice.md).

Push the countermeasures too far, though, and they invert into the opposite failure: refusing to settle on anything short of an exhaustive, fully-decomposed justification. See [hyperrationality](hyperrationality.md) for that mirror-image trap.
