---
type: concept
title: Earned Opinion Right
description: >
  Before treating a strong view as decision input, ask whether
  the holder has earned the right to that opinion through track
  record and demonstrated responsibility in the domain at stake.
sources:
  - title: "The Fearless Organization"
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Amy Edmondson), ch. 5"
---

Candor cultures that invite everyone to speak can still fail if
strong opinions carry equal weight regardless of preparation.
**Earned opinion right** is the check: ask whether you have earned
the right to hold an opinion before offering it — earned through
track record and proven responsibility in the relevant domain.
The analogy: do not tell others how to ski a difficult slope if you
cannot ski it yourself.

For decision meetings this separates two kinds of contribution:

- **Opinions backed by experience** — worth weighing heavily in
  options analysis and recommendation review.
- **Conjecture** — worth airing as a hypothesis to test, not as
  a veto or anchor.

Managers enforcing "the logic of conversations" must make this
distinction explicitly when emotions run hot, so
[obligation-to-voice-critical-views](obligation-to-voice-critical-views.md)
does not devolve into loudest-voice wins. It complements
[conditions-for-exploratory-thought](conditions-for-exploratory-thought.md)
(a well-informed audience can tell superficial answers from deep
ones) and [empirical-test-over-authority](empirical-test-over-authority.md)
(when the question is factual, test rather than defer to rank).

A culture that prefers opinionated people still needs this filter
to avoid arrogance — and to preserve hierarchy where it helps:
open debate can coexist with a lead decision-maker whose job is
to choose after hearing alternatives, not to pretend consensus
decided the call.
