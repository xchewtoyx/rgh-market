---
type: concept
title: Predefined Abandonment Triggers for a Plan in Flight
description: >
  Decide in advance what evidence would mean the current plan isn't
  working, so abandoning it is a pre-agreed observation instead of a
  contested argument made under pressure while it's failing.
sources:
  - title: "Incident Management for Operations"
    resource: "Incident Management for Operations (Rob Schnepp, Ron Vidal, Chris Hawley), ch. 3"
---

A plan that is underway is emotionally harder to abandon than one
still on the drawing board — the people executing it are invested,
and the moment things start going sideways is also the worst possible
moment to have a calm, evidence-based argument about whether to
switch course. The fix is to settle the abandonment question before
that moment arrives: while the current plan (call it Plan A) is still
being approved, or right as it starts executing, ask and record the
answers to four standing questions:

- What are the key indicators that the plan is working, or not
  working?
- What is the specific trigger point at which the plan gets
  abandoned?
- Would an alternative plan need different people or different
  resources than are already engaged?
- How and to whom will an abandonment or pivot actually get
  communicated, if it happens?

None of this requires the alternative plan to be fully designed yet —
only the conditions under which Plan A gets declared not-working need
to be pre-agreed. This converts a live pivot decision from "should we
change course" (a fresh, contestable argument, made worse by sunk-cost
attachment to the current plan) into "have we hit the condition we
already agreed on" (a factual check). It also means the group revisits
this question routinely, not once — the moment a plan is approved is
the same moment the group should already be asking what would prove
it wrong, and that question stays open for as long as the plan runs.

This is the in-flight complement to
[pre-mortem-technique](pre-mortem-technique.md): a pre-mortem surfaces
failure modes before a plan is chosen at all, while this technique
keeps watching for those failure modes' real-world signatures after
the plan is already running, so noticing one doesn't require
reopening the whole decision from scratch. It also gives concrete
content to
[decision-quality-independent-of-outcome](decision-quality-independent-of-outcome.md)'s
requirement for a plan to say "what would change the decision" —
here, stated in advance, as a specific observable trigger rather than
a vague willingness to reconsider.
