type: concept
title: Proximate Objectives
description: >
  Near-term objectives close enough to be feasible so the
  organization can focus energy on a target it can hit.
sources:
  - title: "Good Strategy/Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters"
    resource: "Good Strategy/Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters (Richard P. Rumelt), ch. 7"
---

A **proximate objective** is close enough at hand to be
feasible — a target the organization can reasonably hit or
overwhelm — rather than a blue-sky restatement of the end
state ([bad-strategic-objectives](bad-strategic-objectives.md)).

Proximate objectives translate
[guiding-policy](guiding-policy.md) into
[coherent-actions](coherent-actions.md) teams can own. They
are the opposite of
[mistaking-goals-for-strategy](mistaking-goals-for-strategy.md):
ambition is absorbed into a concrete next win that advances
the diagnosis, not a slogan that skips *how*.

Examples of the shift:
- Vague "top-tier regional school" → identify ten non-
  recruiting employers and tailor curriculum to their specs.
- Unresolved lunar-surface debate → land a probe that
  measures bearing strength before designing landing gear.

Prefer proximate targets in decision documents and
[small-observable-commitments](small-observable-commitments.md).
Leaders must still
[absorb-strategic-ambiguity](absorb-strategic-ambiguity.md)
upstream so operators are not left to invent the target.
