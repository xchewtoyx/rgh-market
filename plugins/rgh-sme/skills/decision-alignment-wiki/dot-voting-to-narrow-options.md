---
type: concept
title: Dot-Voting to Narrow Options
description: >
  Narrow a large option set to a short list by giving each
  participant a fixed number of votes to spend as they see
  fit, then let top vote-getters pitch before a final round.
sources:
  - title: "Continuous Discovery Habits"
    resource: "Continuous Discovery Habits (Teresa Torres), ch. 8"
---

Once a group has generated many candidate options (see
[individual-input-before-group-synthesis](individual-input-before-group-synthesis.md)
for why generation should start solo, not in a group), narrow
them with **dot-voting**: give each participant a small fixed
number of votes (e.g. three), which they can spend however
they like — all on one option or spread across several — and
score only on the criterion the decision is actually about
(how well the option addresses the target problem), not on
unrelated qualities like novelty or ease of implementation,
which belong to a later evaluation step.

Groups tend to be better at *evaluating* a generated option
set than at generating it in the first place — this is the
reverse of the finding that solo generation beats group
brainstorming — which is why the narrowing step, unlike the
generation step, benefits from being done together rather
than solo-then-merged.

Run more than one round if needed: after an initial vote, let
the top vote-getters briefly make the case for their option
before revoting. Stop narrowing at a small number (e.g.
three) rather than one — this feeds a genuine
[compare-and-contrast-framing](compare-and-contrast-framing.md)
decision downstream instead of prematurely collapsing to a
single option before it has been tested against alternatives.
Before finalizing the short list, poll for authentic
enthusiasm: if nobody on the team is genuinely excited about
any of the surviving options, that is a signal to reopen
generation rather than force a decision on a weak set.

Two variants suit different situations: a
[cumulative-voting-budget](cumulative-voting-budget.md) lets
voters express intensity of preference, not just a pick, when
that intensity is itself decision-relevant; a
[weighted-category-vote](weighted-category-vote.md) suits a
much larger group prioritizing many options at once, where the
goal is surfacing every stakeholder's non-negotiable needs
rather than narrowing a short list for further debate.
