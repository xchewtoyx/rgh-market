title: Discussion Debate and Teaching Modes
description: >
  Label alignment conversations as open exploration, peer
  contest, or knowledge transfer so participants know whether
  to ask questions, argue alternatives, or receive instruction.
sources:
  - title: "The Fearless Organization"
    resource: "The Fearless Organization (Amy Edmondson), ch. 5"
---

Not every alignment conversation should run the same way. Three
modes, often fluid in practice, serve different decision needs:

- **Discussion** — open exploration among people of varying
  experience and authority. Everyone asks questions and offers
  opinions; all views are considered. Use when the problem is
  ill-defined and the group is still framing options.
- **Debate** — structured contest between approximate equals,
  aimed at surfacing alternatives for a lead decision-maker.
  Use when options exist and the task is to stress-test them,
  not to teach basics.
- **Teaching** — transfer between people at different levels of
  understanding. Use when one party lacks context needed to
  participate in a real choice.

Naming the mode prevents category errors — treating a teaching
moment as a debate (where the junior party feels attacked) or a
debate as a discussion (where no one is licensed to argue a
position hard). Explicit hierarchy and psychological safety are
not mutually exclusive: speaking up can coexist with track-record-
based rank, and decisions need not be made by consensus if open
debate supplies the lead decision-maker with perspectives before
the call.

Conflict in these modes should serve "what is true and what to do
about it" — task-based exchange of alternate viewpoints — not
winning the argument. Finding out you are wrong is more valuable
than being right, because you are learning. That orientation links
to [precision-questioning](precision-questioning.md),
[fair-treatment-of-objections](fair-treatment-of-objections.md),
and [managing-alignment-meetings](managing-alignment-meetings.md)
(process control without owning the outcome).

Managers should stay calm and analytical when emotions run hot —
"enforce the logic of conversations" rather than the volume of
voices. See [earned-opinion-right](earned-opinion-right.md) for
weighting contributions by demonstrated competence, not by
assertiveness alone.
