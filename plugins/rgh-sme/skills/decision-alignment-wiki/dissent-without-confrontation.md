Skepticism is asking why something might not be true, not declaring
that it isn't — and the difference is mostly in the phrasing. "You're
wrong" makes a claim about the other person; "I'm not sure about
that" or "have you considered this other way of looking at it"
makes a claim about your own belief state and leaves room for either
party to be persuaded. The second framing gets the same substantive
challenge onto the table with a fraction of the defensive reaction,
  because it never asks anyone to accept they were wrong in front of
others — only to consider a different view.

A complementary phrasing from organizational-learning work pairs
**self-disclosure with inquiry**: name your own unease first, then
invite the other person to explore it together — "I notice that I
am feeling threatened by this new proposal. You may be also. Could
you help me see where this uneasiness is coming from?" That framing
targets [defensive routines](defensive-routines.md) directly:
it lowers the threat that makes people hide the thinking behind
their views, and it takes shared responsibility for the dynamic
instead of labeling someone else as defensive.

Two structural techniques make dissent a routine, expected part of
a review rather than an interpersonal risk each time it happens:

- **A standing dissenting role**, filled by a designated person or
  body whose job is explicitly to argue against the prevailing
  view — a lineage running from the Catholic Church's historical
  "devil's advocate" in canonization proceedings through modern
  organizational red teams. Naming the role in advance means a
  challenge is understood as the role being performed, not a
  personal attack, which is what makes people willing to voice it.
  European royal courts used an older variant of the same
  mechanism: a jester licensed to voice dissent as a joke, which
  protected the message-bearer from the punishment a straight
  objection to the sovereign would have invited, while still
  landing memorably enough to change the sovereign's mind. The
  underlying move is the same one a modern devil's advocate role
  performs — give the dissenting content a protected, expected
  form so raising it carries no personal cost — indirection and
  humor are just an older technology for buying that protection
  than an assigned title is.
- **Debate-swap**: when two people already disagree, have each
  argue the *other's* position while a third person referees. This
  changes the incentive from defending one's own view to
  understanding the opposing one well enough to argue it, and
  reliably moves both parties toward the middle — occasionally
  surfacing a consideration neither side had actually weighed on
  their own side of the table.

Both techniques only function inside a group that has met
[conditions-for-exploratory-thought](conditions-for-exploratory-thought.md);
assigning a devil's-advocate role to a group with no real interest
in accuracy just produces a ritual objection nobody takes seriously.
They give
[fair-treatment-of-objections](fair-treatment-of-objections.md) a
concrete way to generate the strongest form of an objection or
alternative even when no one on the team happens to hold it
personally — the role or the debate-swap manufactures the dissent
the review needs, rather than waiting for it to occur naturally.
