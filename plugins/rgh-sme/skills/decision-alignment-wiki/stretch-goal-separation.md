---
type: concept
title: Stretch Goal Separation
description: >
  Commit publicly to a core set of objectives sized to land
  most of the time, and track further upside as a separate,
  explicitly uncommitted stretch list, instead of demanding
  100% completion.
sources:
  - title: "Agile Software Requirements: Lean Requirements Practices for Teams, Programs, and the Enterprise"
    resource: "Agile Software Requirements (Dean Leffingwell), ch. 15, 16"
---

Demanding 100% completion of committed objectives produces bad
economics, not good discipline. To reliably hit 100%, a team
must either strip out enough ambition and risk-taking that the
plan is padded, or quietly under-commit ("sandbag") to guarantee
a comfortable margin — neither actually optimizes what gets
delivered. A team measured this way stops accepting real
stretch, because a miss reads as failure regardless of how much
value still shipped.

The fix has two parts that work together:

1. **Track a process-control band, not a 100% target.** Measure
   the fraction of committed objectives actually met over time
   and expect it to land in an acceptable range — comfortably
   above sandbagging, comfortably below the point where more
   ambition would help. A worked example: hitting 79% of release
   objectives is neither an automatic failure nor an automatic
   pass; it needs to be evaluated against the band the
   organization has agreed is healthy (a yield anywhere above
   roughly 80% is a commonly cited threshold), not against a
   literal 100% bar.
2. **Keep a separate, explicitly-uncommitted stretch list**,
   ranked by the same value criteria as the committed set. The
   organization commits to and depends on the core list; the
   team pursues the stretch list as capacity allows, without its
   completion (or non-completion) counting against the team's
   track record.

This gives committed objectives their bite as a
[pre-committing-success-criteria](pre-committing-success-criteria.md)-style
threshold decided in advance, while keeping the incentive to
push past it intact — a team punished identically whether it
hits 80% or 100% of a single undifferentiated list has no reason
to reach further once the safe number is in sight.
