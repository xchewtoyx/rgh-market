---
type: concept
title: Confidence Vote for Commitment
description: >
  Replace a flat public commitment ask with a graduated vote
  on confidence, because no one will openly declare
  non-commitment in front of their own managers.
sources:
  - title: "Agile Software Requirements: Lean Requirements Practices for Teams, Programs, and the Enterprise"
    resource: "Agile Software Requirements (Dean Leffingwell), ch. 16"
---

Asking a team to flatly "commit" to a plan in front of their
management produces a distorted signal: no rational participant
will publicly declare they are not committed, regardless of
their real confidence, so a yes/no commitment ask always returns
yes and tells the room nothing true about actual risk.

The fix is to ask a different, graduated question instead: "how
confident are you that you and your team will meet these
objectives?" — answered with a public show-of-hands vote on a
numeric scale (a five-finger vote is a common format, from "no
confidence, will not happen" up through full confidence). A
graduated scale gives people a face-saving way to register real
doubt without being the one person who broke the room's apparent
consensus, and it surfaces exactly which objectives or teams
carry the most risk before the plan is finalized, not after it
has already failed.

This is a variant of
[dot-voting-to-narrow-options](dot-voting-to-narrow-options.md)'s
principle — a numeric vote surfaces a distribution of honest
opinion that a single yes/no question would suppress — applied
to gauging confidence in an already-chosen plan rather than
narrowing a set of options. Low or split results are themselves
the useful output: they identify which
[escalating-unresolved-disagreements](escalating-unresolved-disagreements.md)
or scope adjustments still need to happen before the group can
treat the plan as genuinely settled, rather than a number to be
smoothed over.

[Poll-for-named-support](poll-for-named-support.md) is the same
mechanism compressed for a live, time-pressured decision rather than
a planning meeting: naming each respondent instead of using a
show-of-hands scale, and asking specifically for disagreement to get
a faster, equally honest answer.
