---
type: concept
title: Describe Before Explain in Post-Decision Review
description: >
  Open a review of a past decision with how/what/when questions
  that elicit description, and hold off on "why" — asking why too
  early produces a defensive explanation instead of an account of
  what actually happened.
sources:
  - title: "Incident Management for Operations"
    resource: "Incident Management for Operations (Rob Schnepp, Ron Vidal, Chris Hawley), ch. 6"
---

The first question asked in a review of a past decision sets the tone
for everything that follows. "Why did you do that?" invites the
person being asked to justify themselves — it produces a defense, not
a description, because it frames the conversation as an accounting
for a choice rather than a reconstruction of what happened. "What did
you see at the time?", "how did that unfold?", and "when did that
information arrive?" ask for facts a person can simply report,
without first deciding whether to defend or concede anything.

The distinction matters because there is no single, objective account
of a contested decision to recover — only each participant's own
partial view of it, and a review's job is to collect and reconcile
enough of those views to reconstruct what actually happened, not to
adjudicate whose account is the official one. A review that opens
with "why" gets a narrower, filtered version of one person's story;
a review that opens with "what/how/when" and only turns to "why"
later, once the facts are on the table, gets the material actually
needed to understand the decision.

This is a question-ordering discipline that supports
[outcome-blind-decision-review](outcome-blind-decision-review.md) and
[self-serving-outcome-attribution](self-serving-outcome-attribution.md):
both describe the distortions a post-decision review needs to guard
against; this note is the concrete first move that keeps the
conversation from triggering those distortions before the facts are
even established. It also depends on
[consensual-truthseeking-critique](consensual-truthseeking-critique.md)'s
precondition — a "why" question, asked once trust and consent for
scrutiny are established, is a different and legitimate move; the
caution here is specifically about sequencing it after description,
not about avoiding it forever.
