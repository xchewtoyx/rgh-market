title: Situational Bias Recognition
description: >
  Biases resist direct correction of intuitive thinking; the main achievable
  progress is recognizing situations where errors are likely and slowing down.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), Conclusions"
---

Little can be achieved about cognitive biases without a considerable investment
of effort. Kahneman reports from personal experience that [System 1](system-1-and-system-2.md)
is **"not readily educable"**: apart from some effects he attributes to age, his
own intuitive thinking remains just as prone to overconfidence, extreme predictions,
and the [planning fallacy](planning-fallacy.md) as before he studied these issues.

The progress that **is** achievable:

- **Situational recognition** — learning to notice contexts where specific biases
  are likely ("This number will be an anchor…," "The decision could change if the
  problem is reframed…"). See [anchoring effect](anchoring-effect.md),
  [framing effects](framing-effects.md).
- **Recognizing others' errors** more readily than one's own — a persistent asymmetry
  that makes external review and [independent judgments before discussion](independent-judgments-before-discussion.md)
  valuable.

Continuous vigilance over every intuition is impractical; the workable compromise
is high-stakes error-prone situations (already noted for [cognitive illusions](system-1-and-system-2.md))
plus a growing catalog of situational triggers. [Habituated System 2](habituated-system-2.md)
techniques can automate some checks, but practitioners remain "always just a System 2
slipup away" from failure.

Debiasing is therefore less about eliminating biased intuitions and more about
**building recognition-and-response habits** — premortems, checklists, outside views,
and calibration feedback loops that engage System 2 when the situation warrants it.

See [premortem technique](premortem-technique.md), [forecast postmortem](forecast-postmortem.md).
