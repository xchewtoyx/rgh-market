Tetlock's **optimistic skeptic** stance rejects both nihilism ("the future is
unpredictable") and credulous expert worship. Neither debunkers nor defenders of
expert forecasting are fully right.

**Bill Flack vs. Tom Friedman**: media defer to celebrity pundits whose accuracy
is never rigorously scored — only partisan post-hoc debate about whether they
"nailed" or "screwed up" calls. Flack, a retired USDA employee among ~20,000 GJP
volunteers, had every forecast dated, recorded, and independently scored — and
ranked among the ~**2%** "superforecasters." Ordinary people with no special
access can outperform presumed experts when measured like baseball stats.

**Dart-throwing chimp distortion**: Tetlock's 1984–2004 expert-political-judgment
study found average experts did about as well as chance on **many** questions —
not all — with accuracy falling sharply as horizons stretched to **three-to-five
years**. Telephone-game retelling inflated this into "experts know no more than
chimpanzees," co-opted by nihilists and know-nothing populists alike.

**Chaos and clouds**: the Arab Spring cascade from Mohamed Bouazizi's self-
immolation (Dec 2010) illustrates sensitive dependence — even regionally
knowledgeable experts could only say Tunisia/Egypt were long-standing "powder
kegs," unfalsifiable until ignition. Lorenz's butterfly-wing chaos undercuts
Laplace's omniscient demon; ironically, more science and computing power today
coincide with **less** confidence in ultimate predictability. The world mixes
**clocks and clouds** — not simply predictable or not.

**Clocklike pockets**: ordinary life runs on predictable routines (commutes, store
hours, actuarial mortality tables, tides, short-horizon weather). Predictability
is **graded** — depending on what, how far out, and under what circumstances.

**Forecast, measure, revise**: meteorology improves by measuring accuracy at each
horizon and iterating models — nonlinearity caps gains but drives steady progress.
Geopolitics, punditry, and much finance **never close the loop** because consumers
rarely demand accuracy evidence (demand-side failure). Without agreed rules and
independent scoring, improvement is unknowable — like running with no timers.
Measurement against clear goals drives progress (Gates, paraphrased) but is
"surprisingly rarely done properly." Forecasting also serves entertainment,
mobilization, prestige, and partisan comfort — unacknowledged mixed goals block
rigorous measurement.
