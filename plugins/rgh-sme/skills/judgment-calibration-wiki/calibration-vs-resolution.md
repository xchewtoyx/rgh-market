---
type: concept
title: Calibration vs Resolution
description: >
  Calibration asks whether stated probabilities match frequencies; resolution
  asks whether forecasts sort outcomes decisively away from 50%.
sources:
  - title: Superforecasting
    resource: "Superforecasting (Tetlock, Gardner), ch. 3"
---

**Calibration**: if a forecaster says "70% chance" across many events, do those
events happen ~70% of the time? Perfect calibration plots on the diagonal.
Above the diagonal = underconfident (20% forecasts happen 50% of the time);
below = overconfident (80% forecasts happen 50% of the time).

**Resolution**: ability to assign probabilities well away from 50% and be right.
A forecaster calibrated but always hovering near 40–60% has poor resolution
("cowardly"); one calibrated *and* confidently extreme when warranted has good
resolution ("brave").

Calibration alone is insufficient — mindless 50/50 calls can be well-calibrated
but useless. Good forecasting needs both, scored together via the
[Brier score](brier-score.md).

Contrast with the [wrong-side-of-maybe fallacy](wrong-side-of-maybe-fallacy.md):
public judgment often treats any >50% forecast that fails as wrong, ignoring
calibration entirely.
