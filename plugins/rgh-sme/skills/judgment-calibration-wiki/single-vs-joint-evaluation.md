---
type: concept
title: Single Versus Joint Evaluation
description: >
  Judgments made in isolation often diverge from comparative evaluation because
  System 1 responds to salient emotions and anchors invisible in side-by-side view.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 33"
---

We live mostly in **single evaluation** (between-subjects) — one scenario at a
time, [WYSIATI](wysiati.md) active, no contrasting alternative present.
**Joint evaluation** compares options together, typically invoking slower
[System 2](system-1-and-system-2.md) assessment. Moral principles endorsed in
joint mode often fail to govern emotional dollar judgments in single mode.

**Burglary compensation**: shot during a robbery at your regular store vs. at an
alternate store used only because the regular one closed for a funeral. Joint
evaluation: almost universal agreement compensation should be identical — same
injury. Single evaluation (Miller & McFarland): far higher awards when shot at the
rarely visited store — poignancy/regret counterfactual ("if only he'd shopped at
his regular store...") drives System 1 intensity matching into dollar figures.

**Preference reversals (Lichtenstein & Slovic)**: Bet A (11/36 win $160, 25/36 lose
$15) vs. Bet B (35/36 win $40, 1/36 lose $10) — most **choose** B (safer). Asked
to set minimum selling prices **separately**, people price A above B — large prize
anchors valuation when each bet is alone. Genuine reversal: choose B, price A
higher. Within-subject replication: subjects cycle, paying to trade the bet just
chosen for the one just rejected. Joint choice makes A's risk salient; single
pricing anchors on prize size suppressed in joint comparison.

Grether & Plott (*AER*): economists tested thirteen alternatives to "discredit
psychologists"; only surviving hypothesis — **psychologists were right** — context
determines choice, violating standard coherence. Theory changed little; dialogue
between economics and psychology grew.

**Category norms**: "How tall is John?" depends on implicit age norm (5' tall at 6
vs. 16). Single evaluations within a shared category cohere with joint ones
(apples vs. peaches). Cross-category items (apples vs. steak) lack a common norm —
no stable preference.

**Evaluability (Hsee)**: secondhand music dictionaries — A: 10,000 entries, like new;
B: 20,000 entries, torn cover. Single: A valued higher (entry count not evaluable
alone; cover dominates). Joint: B wins — entry advantage becomes obvious. An
attribute affects judgment only in proportion to how **evaluable** it is in the
current mode.

**Dolphins vs. farmworkers**: single evaluation ranks dolphins higher (species
GOOD–BAD ranking); joint evaluation activates "farmworkers are human" — larger
contributions to farmworkers. Decisive features invisible in single mode become
salient only comparatively.

**Mock jury punitive damages**: child burned in defective pajamas vs. bank defrauded
$10M. Single: bank award higher ($10M anchor). Joint: child awards more than double
single-evaluation child awards; bank stays anchored on loss magnitude.

**Takeaway**: broader frames (joint evaluation) usually serve rationality better
than single emotional intensity — but beware joint evaluation when someone
controlling context has vested interest (sales manipulation).
