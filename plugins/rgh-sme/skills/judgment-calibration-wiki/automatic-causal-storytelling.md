---
type: concept
title: Automatic Causal Storytelling
description: >
  System 1 automatically links events into causal narratives for coherence,
  often post-hoc and regardless of actual causation.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 6"
---

"Fred's parents arrived late. The caterers were expected soon. Fred was angry."
Readers instantly know Fred was angry about lateness, not caterers — associative
links connect anger to punctuality. Finding causal connections is automatic in
story comprehension; [System 2](system-1-and-system-2.md) is offered the interpretation
and accepts it.

**Taleb's bond-market example**: on Saddam Hussein's capture day, bond prices rose
then fell — Bloomberg headlines attributed both moves to the capture ("may not curb
terrorism" / "boosts allure of risky assets"). One event explaining contradictory
outcomes explains nothing; salience made capture the default cause regardless of
market mechanics. See [narrative fallacy](narrative-fallacy.md).

**Pickpocket study**: "lost wallet" + "New York" + "crowds" evokes pickpocket on
recall more strongly than "sights" — though pickpocket never appeared and other
causes fit equally.

Albert **Michotte**: physical causality is **perceived directly** (like color), not
inferred from correlation — moving squares "launched" on contact; six-month-olds
show surprise at violated sequences.

**Heider and Simmel**: moving triangles and circles evoke intentional drama (bully,
victim, alliance) — irresistible except in autism; infants identify pursuers and
expected paths.

Physical and intentional causality are separate systems; freely willed action feels
disembodied — basis for soul/intention distinctions Paul Bloom links to religious
intuition.

Forecast postmortems must separate coherent stories from valid causal attribution.

[outcome bias and hindsight](outcome-bias-and-hindsight.md).
