---
type: concept
title: Prototype vs Sum Representation
description: >
  System 1 represents categories by prototypes or typical exemplars, so it
  handles averages well but neglects quantity when judging sum-like variables.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 8"
---

Because [System 1](system-1-and-system-2.md) represents categories via a
**prototype** or set of typical exemplars, it handles **averages** well but
**sums** ("sum-like variables") poorly — category size tends to be neglected.

Block-tower displays: a glance gives immediate impression of relative height and
similarity, but not exact counts — confirming counts requires System 2. Line arrays:
System 1 answers "What is the average length?" almost instantly (even under
cognitive load) by matching another line's length, but "What is the total length?"
stumps System 1 entirely — only System 2 can estimate average, count lines, and
multiply.

**Exxon Valdez willingness-to-pay**: groups told nets would save 2,000, 20,000, or
200,000 migratory birds gave average contributions of **$80, $78, and $88** — essentially
flat. Participants responded to a single prototype image (an oiled, drowning bird),
not to quantity. This "almost complete neglect of quantity in emotional contexts"
has replicated many times.

Forecasting implication: vivid single-case stories activate prototypes; scope and
magnitude require explicit System 2 work. See [scope sensitivity](scope-sensitivity.md),
[vivid outcomes reduce probability sensitivity](vivid-outcomes-reduce-probability-sensitivity.md),
[affect heuristic](affect-heuristic.md).
