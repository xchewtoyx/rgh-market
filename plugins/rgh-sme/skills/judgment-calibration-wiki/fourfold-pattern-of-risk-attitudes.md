Combining reference-point evaluation with non-linear [decision weights](prospect-theory-decision-weights.md)
yields the **fourfold pattern** — a core prospect-theory achievement:

| | High probability | Low probability |
|---|---|---|
| **Gains** | Fear of disappointment → **risk averse** (accept less than EV to lock gain) | Hope → **risk seeking** (lotteries) |
| **Losses** | Fear → **risk seeking** (gamble to avoid sure loss) | Hope of avoiding disaster → **risk averse** (insurance) |

**Top-right cell** (high-probability losses) was new to Kahneman & Tversky:
**diminishing sensitivity** makes a large sure loss especially painful while the
[certainty effect](possibility-and-certainty-effects.md) underweights the 90%
gamble's aversiveness — both push toward gambling. "Many unfortunate human situations
unfold in the top right cell" — businesses losing to superior technology burn assets
in futile catch-up; losing sides in wars fight past decided outcomes because accepting
the sure loss is too painful and complete relief hope too enticing.

**Bottom cells**: diminishing sensitivity and probability overweighting **oppose**
each other — small-probability dominance produces lottery buying and insurance
premiums above expected value ("peace of mind").

**Chris Guthrie (law)**: plaintiff with strong case (95% win) sits top-left — risk
averse, attracted to sure substantial gain, fears regret if case lost after rejecting
settlement ~90% of claim. Defendant with weak case (95% lose) sits top-right — risk
seeking; settlement nearly as painful as worst outcome; hope of trial victory remains
attractive. Settlement negotiations often pit risk-averse plaintiff against risk-
seeking defendant.

## Frivolous litigation

For "frivolous litigation" — flimsy claim both sides know unlikely to succeed —
the negotiation sits in the bottom row: plaintiff has small chance to win large
amount (lottery ticket); defendant faces small risk of very bad outcome. [Overweighting
small probabilities](possibility-and-certainty-effects.md) makes plaintiff bold;
overweighting small chance of large loss makes defendant risk-averse and willing to
settle generously (buying insurance against unlikely verdict). Plaintiffs with
frivolous claims tend to obtain more generous settlements than statistics justify.

Worked example: city facing 200 frivolous suits/year, each 5% chance of costing
$1M, could settle each for $100,000. Litigating all 200 costs $10M (10 losses ×
$1M); settling all 200 costs $20M. Systematic deviation from expected value — risk
aversion or risk seeking — is costly across many similar decisions, even though each
individual decision is not "obviously unreasonable."

Stock phrases: overweighting small probabilities when settling to avoid a "freak
loss"; paying premium for certainty (vacation bookings); risk-seeking in losses
(not cutting losses while break-even chance remains); [possibility effect](possibility-and-certainty-effects.md)
driving demand to mitigate minuscule risk for "peace of mind."
