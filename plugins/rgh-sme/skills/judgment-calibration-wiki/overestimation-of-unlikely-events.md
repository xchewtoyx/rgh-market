  Probability judgments for rare focal events inflate when alternatives are diffuse,
  driven by confirmatory retrieval and cognitive ease of plausible scenarios.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow

**Overestimation** (inflated probability judgments) and **overweighting** (inflated
[decision weights](prospect-theory-decision-weights.md) in choices) are distinct but
share mechanisms: focused attention, [confirmation bias](confirmation-bias-and-system-1-gullibility.md),
and cognitive ease.

When judging the probability of an unlikely event (e.g., a third-party presidential
win), [System 1 and System 2](system-1-and-system-2.md)'s associative machinery
works in confirmatory mode, selectively retrieving evidence and images consistent
with the statement — biased but constrained by plausibility. The probability
judgment is determined by the **cognitive ease/fluency** with which a plausible
scenario comes to mind.

Attention can shift to the alternative when the target event is very likely: asked
the probability a newborn is released within 3 days, people focus on the abnormal
alternative (medical complications) because the mind spontaneously focuses on
"whatever is odd, different, or unusual." The [availability heuristic](availability-heuristic.md)
then operates on scenarios of problems. **The probability of a rare event is most
likely to be overestimated when the alternative is not fully specified.**

## Denominator diffuseness (Fox NBA study)

Craig Fox asked basketball fans to estimate each of 8 NBA playoff teams' win
probability — each team's victory was focal, with a diffuse alternative ("one of
the other seven"). Judgments summed to **240%** (should be 100%). When asked
whether the winner would come from the Eastern or Western conference — equally
specific alternatives — judgments summed correctly to 100%.

Fans also stated cash equivalents for $160 bets on each team; summed equivalents
totaled **$287** — guaranteed loss of $127 for betting on all eight, despite
knowing only eight teams existed. Fans both overestimated focal probabilities and
overweighted them in betting.

Connects to the [planning fallacy](planning-fallacy.md): successful execution is a
single, specific, easy-to-imagine outcome; failure is diffuse (innumerable ways to
go wrong) — entrepreneurs and investors overestimate success and overweight those
estimates.

Contrast [overweighting rare events in decisions](possibility-and-certainty-effects.md)
and [choice from experience vs description](choice-from-experience-vs-description.md),
where rare draws may be underweighted when never sampled.

Prospect theory originally said "highly unlikely events are either ignored or
overweighted" without specifying conditions; emotion and vividness (via fluency
and availability) explain excessive response to rare events we don't ignore.
