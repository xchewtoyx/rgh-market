---
type: concept
title: Sunk-Cost Fallacy
description: >
  Continuing to invest in a losing course because prior costs feel unredeemed,
  ignoring better forward-looking options.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 32"
---

A rational decision maker cares only about **future** consequences of current
investments, not justifying earlier mistakes. The **sunk-cost fallacy**: pouring
more resources into a losing account when better alternatives exist.

Driving into a blizzard because you paid for the ticket; funding $60M more into a
$50M failed project instead of a superior new project. Often sits in the choice
between sure loss and unfavorable gamble — unwisely preferred.

**Agency problem**: canceling a project stains the executive's record; personal
interest may favor gambling with firm resources or postponing reckoning. Boards
sometimes replace CEOs encumbered by prior mental accounts so a fresh leader can
ignore sunk costs.

Keeps people in poor jobs, unhappy marriages, unpromising research. Economics
training helps graduate students walk away from failing projects.

Requires System 2 counterfactual: ticket price is irrelevant to whether the trip
is worth the blizzard risk today. See [mental accounting](mental-accounting.md).
