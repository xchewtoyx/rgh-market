One of [System 1 and System 2](system-1-and-system-2.md)'s main functions is
monitoring and controlling System 1's suggestions — endorsing some, suppressing
others. Failures reflect **insufficient motivation, not incapacity** — the same
students who miss minitests can solve harder problems when not tempted by a
superficially plausible fast answer. Kahneman labels this pattern **lazy**; its
opposite **engaged** — more alert, skeptical of intuitions, less willing to settle.
Keith Stanovich would call the engaged group "more rational."

## Minitests

- **Bat-and-ball**: "$1.10 total, bat costs $1 more than ball" → intuitive 10¢
  wrong (implies $1.20 total); correct 5¢. >50% at Harvard/MIT/Princeton failed;
  >80% at less selective universities. Anyone answering 10¢ did not check.

- **Syllogism**: "All roses are flowers. Some flowers fade quickly. Therefore some
  roses fade quickly" — flawed, yet most endorse it. "If System 1 is involved, the
  conclusion comes first and the arguments follow."

- **Michigan/Detroit murders**: average guesses for "Michigan" lower than for
  "Detroit" despite Detroit being in Michigan — implicates both System 1 retrieval
  (whether Detroit comes to mind) and System 2's option to search memory deliberately.

## Intelligence vs. rationality

Mischel marshmallow: resisters at age 4 had higher executive control and intelligence
scores 10–15 years later. Attention-training games improved both control and
nonverbal IQ in young children.

Frederick's **Cognitive Reflection Test (CRT)**: bat-and-ball plus two similar
items. Low scorers accept first ideas, are impulsive — 63% preferred $3,400 this
month over $3,800 next month vs. 37% of perfect scorers; paid roughly twice as much
for overnight book delivery. "System 1 is impulsive and intuitive; System 2 is
capable of reasoning, and it is cautious, but at least for some people it is also
lazy."

Stanovich distinguishes **algorithmic mind** (slow computation, what IQ tests measure)
from **rationality** (reflective monitoring). High intelligence does not confer
immunity to cognitive biases; CRT items predict susceptibility somewhat better than
conventional IQ. Distinction attractive but not yet fully settled.

Cognitive strain mobilizes System 2: CRT in washed-out gray font cut error rate
from 90% to 35% among Princeton students — strain triggers rejection of intuitive
wrong answers. See [cognitive ease](cognitive-ease.md) for the ease/strain dial.
