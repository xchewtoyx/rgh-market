System 1 operates automatically and quickly, with little voluntary control —
"a machine for jumping to conclusions." System 2 allocates attention to effortful
activities but is optional, effortful, and often lazy; System 1 runs first. Fast
thinking encompasses expert [intuition as recognition](intuition-as-recognition.md),
heuristic-driven intuition, and automatic perception/memory; System 1 "is more
influential than your experience tells you, and it is the secret author of many of
the choices and judgments you make."

**Division of labor**: System 1 runs automatically; System 2 normally rests in low-
effort mode. System 1 continuously generates impressions, intuitions, feelings;
System 2 endorses them with little modification unless difficulty, surprise, or
impending error mobilizes effort. System 2 monitors behavior (politeness when
angry) and takes the last word when engaged — but "most of what you think and do
originates in System 1."

**Attention budget**: you dispose of limited attention; exceeding it fails.
Effortful tasks interfere (can't compute 17×24 while turning into dense traffic);
easy tasks combine (talk while driving empty highway). Under peak load, attention
narrows selectively — see [effort narrows attention](effort-narrows-attention.md)
and [cognitive pupillometry and mental effort](cognitive-pupillometry-and-mental-effort.md).
**Invisible gorilla**
(Chabris & Simons): counting basketball passes, ~half miss a gorilla visible 9
seconds — "blind to the obvious, blind to our blindness."

**System 1 examples** (increasing complexity): distance detection, orienting to
sound, "bread and…", disgust face, hostility in voice, 2+2, billboard reading,
empty-road driving, chess master's strong move, simple sentences, stereotype match.

**System 2 examples**: brace for starter gun, focus on clowns, one voice in noise,
find white-haired woman, memory search, faster walking, social monitoring, count
letter "a," recite phone number, narrow parking, compare washing machines, tax form,
validate logic. All disrupt when attention is pulled away. System 2 can reprogram
System 1 attention (look for white-haired woman; capitals starting with N).

**Conflict**: Stroop-like tasks — overcoming automatic reading response strains
System 2. Everyday: resist staring at odd couple; focus boring book; "steer into
skid, don't touch brakes"; not telling someone to go to hell. "System 2 is in
charge of self-control."

**Cognitive illusions** (Müller-Lyer): measuring proves lines equal but perception
persists — learn to recognize pattern and mistrust impression; illusion never
vanishes. Psychotherapy anecdote: instant rapport with patient who failed multiple
therapists claiming "you are different" — specific danger sign like Müller-Lyer
fins, not general distrust of feelings. Continuous vigilance impractical; compromise:
recognize high-stakes error-prone situations and try harder there.

**Useful fictions**: personifying systems aids comprehension — not brain regions,
but shorthand agents readers already understand. Shorter labels ("System 1") reduce
working-memory load.

People apply causal thinking where statistical reasoning is needed; System 1 lacks
statistical capability — System 2 can learn it but few are trained.

See [confirmation bias and System 1 gullibility](confirmation-bias-and-system-1-gullibility.md),
[substitution heuristic](substitution-heuristic.md), [halo effect](halo-effect.md).

The Cognitive Reflection Test's bat-and-ball problem illustrates the split:
System 1 instantly supplies "ten cents" (wrong; correct answer five cents);
most people, including very smart people, answer without engaging System 2.

Faulty intuitive judgments implicate both systems: System 1 proposes the
intuition; System 2 endorses it without checking — either from a belief that
base rates are irrelevant given individual information, or from inattention. See
[lazy System 2 and rationality](lazy-system-2-and-rationality.md).

## Book roadmap (five parts)

- **Part 1**: two-systems approach, associative memory, judgment-heuristic vocabulary.
- **Part 2**: why statistical thinking is difficult — we think associatively,
  metaphorically, causally; statistics requires many things at once.
- **Part 3**: overconfidence, hindsight illusion; Taleb's black swan influence.
- **Part 4**: prospect theory, isolated problems, [framing effects](framing-effects.md)
  — challenge to rational-agent economics.
- **Part 5**: experiencing vs. remembering self (well-being measurement).
- **Conclusion**: revisits three distinctions; "educating gossip" for organizations.

Humans are creative confabulators: Michael Gazzaniga's split-brain patients
invent plausible but false explanations for actions driven by the silent
hemisphere. Financial journalists attribute stock moves to specific news even
when the news broke after the market moved.

Magnus Carlsen: "Often, I cannot explain a certain move, only know that it
feels right... I usually know what I am going to do after 10 seconds; the rest
is double-checking." Prescription: respect intuition but verify when time allows;
hold current certainty loosely.

Facial-feedback experiments on the Tom W problem: frowning increased
[base-rate](base-rate-neglect.md) sensitivity; cheek-puffing replicated pure
[representativeness](representativeness-heuristic.md) reliance — base-rate neglect
partly reflects laziness, not pure ignorance.

## Final recap: balance, limits, and endorsement

The page count devoted to System 1's errors is a poor indicator of the true
balance: System 1 is the origin of much that goes wrong, but also of most of
what we do right — "which is most of what we do." Its marvels include a rich
associative model of the world that distinguishes surprising from normal events
in a fraction of a second, generates what was expected instead, and automatically
searches for causal interpretations as events unfold. It also holds the vast
repertory of practiced skills (walking around obstacles, defusing angry customers)
built when a [regular environment and adequate feedback](two-conditions-for-trustworthy-expert-intuition.md)
allow learning.

System 2 is who we think we are — it articulates judgments and makes choices —
but often just endorses or rationalizes what System 1 generated. You may not
know you are optimistic about a project because its leader reminds you of your
sister, or dislike someone who looks like your dentist. Asked for an explanation,
System 2 searches memory for presentable reasons, finds some, and believes the
resulting story. System 2 is not merely an apologist: it suppresses foolish
thoughts and inappropriate impulses, and attention investment improves
performance on comparison, choice, and ordered reasoning. Still, System 2 is
"not a paragon of rationality" — its abilities and available knowledge are
limited, and many reasoning errors happen because System 2 does not know any
better, not because an intrusive intuition overrode it.

A marker of skilled performance is handling large amounts of information swiftly;
when a skilled response exists, it is evoked. Only rare, specific-answer problems
(17 × 24) make it obvious System 2 must step in — System 1 is rarely "dumbfounded."
It is not capacity-limited and computes profligately: while searching for one
answer it simultaneously generates answers to related questions, and may
[substitute](substitution-heuristic.md) the response that comes to mind more
easily for the one actually requested. A heuristic answer is not necessarily
simpler than the original question — only more accessible, computed more quickly.
Heuristic answers are often approximately correct but sometimes quite wrong; see
[intuition without a reliability signal](intuition-without-reliability-signal.md).

Operative features such as [WYSIATI](wysiati.md), intensity matching, and
associative coherence give rise to predictable biases: anchoring, nonregressive
predictions, overconfidence, and more. [Biases resist direct self-correction](situational-bias-recognition.md);
recognizing error-prone situations is the main practical progress available.
