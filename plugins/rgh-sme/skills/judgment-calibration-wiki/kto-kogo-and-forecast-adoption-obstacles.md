Lenin's **kto, kogo?** ("who, whom" — who does what to whom): politics as pure
power struggle where arguments and evidence are adornments; forecasting advances
tribal interests; accuracy matters only when aligned. Examples:

- **Nate Silver**: praised as prophet when 2012 forecasts favored Democrats; demoted
  to "stumblebum" when 2014 Senate forecasts favored Republicans — same methodology.
- **Banco Santander Brasil analyst**: fired after warning markets would decline if
  left-wing candidate won — accuracy irrelevant to bank–president relations.
- **Jonathan Schell's 1982 holocaust warning**: inaccurate but mobilized disarmament
  — "worked" in Lenin's terms.
- **Dick Morris**: admitted 2012 Romney landslide forecast was morale duty, not accuracy.

Tetlock's rebuttal: "Lenin was a tad dogmatic. People want power, yes. But they value
other things too."

## Counterexamples: status quos can change

**Ernest Codman's End Result System** (~1915): hospitals publish treatment outcomes;
physicians hated reputational risk; Codman expelled from Massachusetts General, satirized
Harvard dignitaries, lost posts — yet publicity spread outcome tracking nationwide.
Lesson with Archie Cochrane: change "not by storming ramparts" but relentless focus
on making the sick well.

Broader **evidence-based movement**: policy analysis, Gates-style philanthropy
evaluations, sports "Moneyball" analytics — shift from gut and authority toward
quantification. Evidence-based forecasting would be the next domain to catch up.

## Intelligence community tensions

IC most needed better forecasting yet seemed least likely to adopt it:

- [Wrong-side-of-maybe fallacy](wrong-side-of-maybe-fallacy.md) makes numeric forecasts
  politically dangerous — vague language safer than falsifiable numbers.
- Whipsawed between blame for missing 9/11 dots and over-connecting Iraq WMD dots —
  overcorrection blocks long-term quality investment.
- Scorekeeping strips political cover; hypothetical DNI hearings show exposure when
  big calls miss.

Despite kto-kogo forces, IC funded IARPA tournament; DNI interest in scorekeeping
reported — "could all be undone in a heartbeat."

Genuine learning requires unambiguous scorable forecasts (Daniel Drezner after Scottish
referendum: "make a clear prediction... I want to keep score"). See
[forecast accuracy accountability](forecast-accuracy-accountability.md),
[cargo cult science and doubt](cargo-cult-science-and-doubt.md).
