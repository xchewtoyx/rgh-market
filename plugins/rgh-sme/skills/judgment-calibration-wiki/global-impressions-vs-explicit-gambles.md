title: Global Impressions vs Explicit Gambles
description: >
  System 1 pools experiences into character impressions unless a rare event is
  vivid enough to be recalled separately — explaining when rare events are neglected.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 31"

Choosing between colleagues Adele (consistently helpful) vs. Brian (less consistent
but occasionally extremely generous):

- **As gambles**: Brian offers slightly inferior likely outcome with low-probability
  very good one — [possibility effect](possibility-and-certainty-effects.md)
  overweighting favors Brian.

- **As global impressions**: good and bad experiences pool into normal-behavior
  representation; unless rare event is extreme enough to be recalled separately
  (Brian verbally abused a colleague), norm biases toward typical/recent instances —
  favors Adele.

The second interpretation is more plausible in a two-system mind: System 1 generates
global representations with emotional attitude and approach/avoid tendency; comparing
these suffices. Unless the rare event comes to mind explicitly, it will not be
overweighted. Same logic applies to [choice from experience vs description](choice-from-experience-vs-description.md):
options develop integrated "personalities" with attached emotional responses rather
than explicit probabilities.

## Conditions for overweighting vs neglect

- Rare events often **overestimated** in probability judgment due to confirmatory
  bias of memory — thinking about an event, you try to make it true in mind. See
  [overestimation of unlikely events](overestimation-of-unlikely-events.md).

- **Overweighted in decisions** when they specifically attract attention: explicit
  description ("99% chance $1,000, 1% chance nothing"), obsessive concerns (bus in
  Jerusalem), vivid images, concrete representations (1 of 1,000), explicit reminders.

- "When there is no overweighting, there will be neglect." The mind is not designed
  to get rare probabilities right — bad news for events no one has yet experienced.

Stock phrases: vividness drives tsunami overestimation in Japan; "disaster cycle" of
exaggeration then neglect; avoid single-scenario framing — set alternatives summing
to 100%; [denominator neglect](denominator-neglect.md) used rhetorically ("1 death
per 1,000").
