---
type: concept
title: Negativity Dominance
description: >
  Bad news, threats, and losses receive priority processing over good news —
  the broader psychology behind loss aversion.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 28"
---

Loss aversion embeds in a view where "negativity and escape dominate positivity
and approach." Threat information travels via fast channels to the amygdala —
subconscious threat detection before conscious "seeing." Angry faces pop out of
happy crowds; happy faces do not pop from angry crowds.

Negative words (war, crime) capture attention faster than positive (peace, love).
Threat in opinion sentences registers in under a quarter-second when clashing with
one's view.

Paul Rozin: one cockroach wrecks a bowl of cherries; a cherry does nothing for a
bowl of cockroaches. "Bad Is Stronger Than Good" — bad emotions, feedback, and
stereotypes have more impact; bad impressions form faster and resist disconfirmation.
John Gottman: stable relationships need good interactions to outnumber bad ~5:1.

Some good/bad boundaries are hardwired; often the boundary is a shifting reference
point — relief behind a rock from cold rain feels intensely good as signal of
improvement.

Manifestations include transport-loss compensation focusing on out-of-pocket
losses, reform failure rates, and golfers putting better for par than birdie
([goals as reference points](goals-as-reference-points.md)).
