title: Product Build Outcome Base Rates
description: >
  Most shipped software features and product bets land in a mediocre middle —
  reference-class statistics for calibrating discovery and build optimism.
sources:
  - title: User Story Mapping

Discovery conversations and story maps produce a **guess** at a minimum viable
solution — viability is unknown until you ship and observe what customers actually
do. The biggest mistake is **believing the minimal viable solution will succeed.**

Patton's informal base-rate split from observation (not formal research):

- ~**20%** genuinely successful / impactful
- ~**20%** genuine failure (negative impact — e.g., redesign drops sales, customers
  reject and demand revert)
- ~**60%** neither success nor failure — the costly middle: development spend on
  work you eventually wish you had not built

Supporting reference-class citations Patton cites:

- Standish Group "Chaos" reports: **64–75%** of features rarely or never used
- **75–90%** of software startups fail (Wall Street Journal venture-capital survey)

These statistics anchor the [outside view](inside-view-vs-outside-view.md) for
product and feature forecasts — before treating discovery output as a plan likely
to succeed. Connect [optimistic bias](optimistic-bias.md), [planning fallacy](planning-fallacy.md),
[reference class forecasting](reference-class-forecasting.md). Design thinking's
empathize–test loop is a debiasing workflow, not a guarantee — see
[process-skill decoupling](process-skill-decoupling.md).
