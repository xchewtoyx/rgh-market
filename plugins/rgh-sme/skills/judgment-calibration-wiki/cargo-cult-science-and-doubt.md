---
type: concept
title: Cargo Cult Science and Doubt
description: >
  Practices with science's outward form but without systematic doubt and testing
  produce confident error — as in pre-RCT medicine and untested forecasting methods.
sources:
  - title: Superforecasting
    resource: "Superforecasting (Tetlock, Gardner), ch. 2"
---

Richard Feynman's **cargo cult science** (after Pacific islanders building fake
runways to lure back WWII cargo planes) describes practices with the form of science
but lacking what makes science real. Medicine before rigorous randomized controlled
trials had impressive titles, Latin lectures, and case studies — but **lacked doubt**.

Feynman: "Doubt is not a fearful thing, but a thing of very great value... it is
of paramount importance, in order to make progress, that we recognize this ignorance
and this doubt... The rate of the development of science is not the rate at which
you make observations alone but, much more important, the rate at which you create
new things to test."

Historian Druin Burch on medicine's history: most treatments were useless or harmful;
Galen treated every outcome as confirming his theories ("All who drink of this
treatment recover... except those whom it does not help, who all die"). Surgeon-historian
Ira Rutkow: physicians "like blind men arguing over the colors of the rainbow."

Archie Cochrane's personal cancer scare illustrates the pattern: told terminal cancer
before pathology returned, he never doubted the surgeon's words — despite devoting
his career to insisting intuition and authority are insufficient without controlled
evidence. Tetlock's closing line: "Happily, physicians now know the cure for all this.
It is a tablespoon of doubt."

Forecasting in the twenty-first century often resembles nineteenth-century medicine:
theories, assertions, famous confident figures — but little experimentation. Bad
forecasting harms are less visible than bad medicine's but still real. See
[forecast accuracy accountability](forecast-accuracy-accountability.md),
[simple models beating expert judgment](simple-models-beating-expert-judgment.md).
