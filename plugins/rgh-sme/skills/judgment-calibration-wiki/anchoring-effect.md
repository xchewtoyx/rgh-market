type: concept
title: Anchoring Effect
description: >
  Estimates stay near a previously considered number even when that number is
  arbitrary or uninformative.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 11"
---

The **anchoring effect**: considering a particular value before estimating an
unknown quantity pulls the estimate toward that number — one of the most reliable
results in experimental psychology.

Wheel-of-fortune experiment: after spinning to 10 or 65, UN African-nations
estimates averaged 25% vs 45% despite the spin carrying no information. Listing
price shifts house perceived value even when buyers resolve to resist.

Two distinct mechanisms:

1. **[Adjust-and-anchor](adjust-and-anchor-heuristic.md)** (System 2): start at
   the anchor, adjust until uncertain whether further movement is warranted —
   adjustment stops prematurely (lazy System 2).

2. **[Anchoring as priming](anchoring-as-priming.md)** (System 1): the anchor
   evokes compatible associative evidence; System 1 "tries to make sentences true."

Anchoring index = (difference in mean estimates) ÷ (difference in anchors). Typical
~55%; judges sentencing shoplifters after rolling loaded dice (3 vs 9): 5 vs 8
months (50% index). Random SSN digits as anchors work too — anchors need not be
believed as informative.

Defenses: deliberately search memory for arguments *against* the anchor ("thinking
the opposite"); refuse to negotiate with an outrageous opening number on the
table. Policy caps (e.g. $1M damage awards) can anchor *up* smaller awards.

[System 2](system-1-and-system-2.md) completes anchoring tasks but has no
awareness of System 1's selective retrieval — you cannot know how you would have
judged absent the anchor.
