---
type: concept
title: Goals as Reference Points
description: >
  Short-term targets become reference points where falling short feels like loss
  and exceeding feels like gain — distorting effort and economic logic.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 28"
---

[Loss aversion](loss-aversion-and-reference-points.md) compares outcomes to a
reference point — status quo or a **future goal**. Not reaching a goal is a loss;
exceeding it a gain. Per [negativity dominance](negativity-dominance.md),
aversion to falling short exceeds desire to exceed.

People adopt short-term goals, strive to hit but not exceed them, then reduce
effort once met — sometimes violating economic logic.

**NYC cabdrivers**: economic logic → work more on rainy high-fare days, rest on
slow days. Observed pattern (loss-aversion logic): hit daily earnings target fast
on rainy days and stop; chase target long hours on pleasant slow days.

**Professional golf** (Pope & Schweitzer): par = reference point; bogey = loss,
birdie = foregone gain. ~2.5M putts: players succeed ~3.6% more often on par
putts than birdie putts at every distance — nonconscious extra concentration to
avoid bogey. Tiger Woods in peak years: one stroke per tournament from equal
birdie/par putting ≈ $1M/season.
