---
type: concept
title: Process-Skill Decoupling
description: >
  Following a rigorous discovery or design process does not guarantee good
  outcomes — skill, focus, and calibration checks remain separate.
sources:
  - title: User Story Mapping
    resource: "User Story Mapping (Patton), ch. 15"
---

Patton: "Process is not the same as skill." Even well-run design thinking can fail
predictably — and he admits guilt on almost every failure mode while remaining an
advocate for design processes.

Common **judgment and calibration failures** in product discovery:

- Starting without framing business needs and target customer — no basis to judge
  solution validity or prioritize.
- Unbounded research ("You'll never run out of things to learn — so why stop?") —
  missing [forecastability triage](forecastability-triage.md) and time-boxing.
- Skipping user contact, relying on existing data and confidence in solution ideas —
  [inside view](inside-view-vs-outside-view.md) without empathy checks.
- Solving many problems for many people — conflicting needs, solutions nobody likes.
- **First viable idea wins** instead of generating multiple solutions — obvious first
  ideas get built while better options never surface; pair with
  [independent judgments before discussion](independent-judgments-before-discussion.md).
- Beautiful prototype that **looks** professional but cannot be used to accomplish
  a real task — coherence and polish mistaken for validity; see
  [illusion of validity and skill](illusion-of-validity-and-skill.md).
- Convincing yourself a rigorous, well-researched solution will work **because the
  process was followed** — process substitution for outcome evidence.
- Ignoring build cost because "it's the right solution."
- When outcomes disappoint, blaming process breakdown or a person rather than
  re-examining the work — [outcome bias and hindsight](outcome-bias-and-hindsight.md)
  applied to methodology.

Patton's prior pattern: great idea → flesh out → build (always ~2× expected time)
→ ship → celebrate → treat as "done" → complaints or silence (no usage) → pretend
success. Agile velocity without impact calibration: "the faster you deliver crap, the
more crap you get" — quantity of software built correlates weakly with outcome.

Mitigation aligns with judgment-calibration toolkit: [reference class](reference-class-forecasting.md)
base rates ([product build outcome base rates](product-build-outcome-base-rates.md)),
[premortem technique](premortem-technique.md), prototype **test** as learning (not
bug-checking or selling), iterate rather than expect first-try success.
