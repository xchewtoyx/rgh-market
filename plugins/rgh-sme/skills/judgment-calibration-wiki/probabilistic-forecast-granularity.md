type: concept
title: Probabilistic Forecast Granularity
description: >
  Finer numeric probability distinctions improve forecast accuracy — rounding
  superforecaster estimates measurably hurts performance.
sources:
  - title: Superforecasting
    resource: "Superforecasting (Tetlock, Gardner), ch. 6"
---

Superforecasters default to probabilistic thinking — an 80% estimate implies 20%
for the opposite. They distinguish **epistemic uncertainty** (knowable in principle,
e.g. reverse-engineering a clocklike machine) from **aleatory uncertainty**
(genuinely unknowable, e.g. exact rainfall a year out). On cloudlike questions
(currency markets), they keep initial estimates in a cautious **35–65% shades-of-
maybe zone**, moving out only tentatively.

**Granularity matters**: ordinary forecasters mostly rounded to nearest **10%**;
superforecasters used **single-percentage-point** resolution on roughly one-third
of forecasts — debating 5% vs. 1% or whether a probability rounds to effectively
zero (Ebola, "next Google" questions). Forecasters using finer granularity score
more accurately; Barbara Mellers's experiment: rounding superforecasts to nearest
**0.05** hurt accuracy; rounding regular forecasters to **0.2** barely mattered.

Defaulting to **50%** as a stand-in for "maybe" correlates with lower accuracy.
Superforecaster Brian Labatte spontaneously offered "65/35" on reading preferences.
Careful thinkers use 50% no more often than any other value.

National Intelligence Council scales (five or seven degrees) beat binary/ternary
[dial thinking](three-setting-probability-dial.md) but remain coarser than committed
superforecasters — NIC could match granularity "if they valued and encouraged it."

Numeracy is common among superforecasters (most aced basic tests; many have math/
science backgrounds; some build Monte Carlo models) but explicit math models are
**rare** — "the great majority of forecasts are simply the product of careful
thought and nuanced judgment." Lionel Levine deliberately forecast well "without
using any" math as a point of pride.

**Fate vs. probability tension**: meaning-making ("everything happens for a reason")
correlates with psychological wellbeing but **negatively** with Brier accuracy —
superforecasters score lowest on "fate" scales. Tetlock leaves the existential tradeoff
unresolved.
