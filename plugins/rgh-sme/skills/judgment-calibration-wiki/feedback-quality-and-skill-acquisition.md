Within a regular environment, learning speed depends on **feedback quality and
speed** plus adequate practice opportunity.

Braking through curves while driving gives immediate, unambiguous feedback,
making the skill easy to acquire. Harbor pilots face an equally regular
environment but learn more slowly because of long delays between action and
outcome.

Expertise is a bundle of miniskills, not one skill. A professional can be
expert in some tasks and a novice in others:

- Psychotherapists get rich immediate feedback on in-session reactions (real
  short-term skill) but sparse, delayed feedback on long-term outcomes — confidence
  in short-term reading does not justify long-term forecasting confidence.
- Anesthesiologists get fast, clear feedback — a stated "feeling something is
  wrong" should be taken seriously.
- Radiologists get little feedback on diagnostic accuracy or missed pathologies.

Professionals commonly fail to recognize the boundary of their own skill — a
driver of [overconfidence](illusion-of-validity-and-skill.md). Evaluate whether
there was adequate opportunity to learn the specific cues before trusting a
claimed intuition; see [two conditions for trustworthy expert intuition](two-conditions-for-trustworthy-expert-intuition.md).

## Forecasting-specific feedback barriers

Police lie-detection: officers grow confident faster than accurate because feedback
is delayed and muddied (trial/plea months later). **Meteorologists** and **seasoned
bridge players** are calibration exceptions — clear, prompt feedback (rain falls or
not; tricks resolved each hand). Calibration skill does **not** transfer across
domains.

Two barriers to good forecast feedback:

1. **Ambiguous language** ("probably," "likely") — [Forer effect](wrong-side-of-maybe-fallacy.md):
   vague profiles rated 4.2/5 accurate. Use [Sherman Kent probability language](sherman-kent-probability-language.md).

2. **Time lag and hindsight bias**: 1988 USSR collapse estimates recalled 31 points
   higher on average in 1992–93 than actually given (one expert: 20% → recalled 70%).
   Forecasters using vague language and memory are like shooting free throws in a
   dark gym — ambiguous sounds, false confidence. [Brier score](brier-score.md) with
   explicit probabilities turns on the lights (Tim Minto: 0.07 excellent vs 1.46 miss
   on Yasukuni visit — unambiguous learning signal).
