title: Sherman Kent Probability Language
description: >
  Vague verbal probability terms hide disagreement; numeric ranges force
  metacognition and make forecasts testable.
sources:
  - title: Superforecasting

Sherman Kent (CIA Office of National Estimates) found vague probability language
creates dangerous ambiguity:

- NIE 29-51 called a Soviet attack on Yugoslavia "a serious possibility." Kent
  meant ~65:35 in favor; his team ranged from 80:20 to 20:80 — exact opposite.
- Before Bay of Pigs, "fair chance" of success was written by someone who meant
  3-to-1 *against* — Kennedy never saw the number.

Kent proposed mapping terms to numeric ranges: Certain=100%; Almost
certain=93%±6%; Probable=75%±12%; Chances about even=50%±10%; Probably
not=30%±10%; Almost certainly not=7%±5%; Impossible=0%. Never formally adopted
until post–Iraq WMD reforms.

Forcing forecasters to translate vague terms into numbers induces **metacognition**
— thinking about how you're thinking — and with practice they distinguish finer
gradations of uncertainty.

Everyday forecasts fail testing because implicit timelines and key terms fade
("significant market share," "risk of inflation," "may be days away"). See
[wrong-side-of-maybe fallacy](wrong-side-of-maybe-fallacy.md) for how vagueness
enables retroactive claiming of success.
