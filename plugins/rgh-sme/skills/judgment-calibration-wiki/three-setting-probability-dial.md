type: concept
title: Three-Setting Probability Dial
description: >
  Intuitive forecasting collapses uncertainty into gonna-happen, not-gonna-happen,
  or maybe — coarse settings that undermine calibration until subdivided numerically.
sources:
  - title: Superforecasting
    resource: "Superforecasting (Tetlock, Gardner), ch. 6"
---

Formal probability theory is recent (Bernoulli, 1713). Evolution favored a coarse
**three-setting dial** on System 1's tip-of-the-nose signal:

- Strong gut signal → binary **yes/no** ("it's a lion — run!")
- Weak signal → **maybe**
- Fine-grained distinctions (60% vs. 80% lion) cost cognition with little survival
  payoff — ignoring small probabilities and using worry-free thresholds was efficient.

Amos Tversky observed most people still have only three probability settings:
"gonna happen," "not gonna happen," "maybe." Harry Truman's "one-armed economist"
joke and media preference for confident [hedgehogs versus foxes](hedgehogs-vs-foxes.md)
reflect the same bias. Confidence and accuracy correlate positively, but people
**exaggerate** the link — trusting more-confident advisers with equal track records;
viewing hedged middle probabilities as "incompetent, ignorant… or lazy."

**Zero Dark Thirty vs. real Panetta**: fictional analysts give 60–80% then push to
"95%… a hundred percent!" — pure two-setting thinking frustrated by disagreement.
Real Panetta welcomed diverse 30–90% estimates as [dragonfly-eye perspective aggregation](dragonfly-eye-perspective-aggregation.md)
to synthesize, not suppress. Maya was **right but unreasonable** given evidence —
mirror of intelligence community's **wrong but reasonable** 100% WMD certainty.
[Process-outcome paradox](forecast-postmortem.md): judge process independently of
outcome.

Obama's "fifty-fifty" on Abbottabad despite ~70% crowd median: possibly non-literal
"maybe," possibly retreat to ignorance prior when **spread** unsettled him — a third
dial setting beyond yes/no.

Common misunderstandings: "70% chance of rain" misread as 70% of the day, area, or
forecasters agreeing — correct meaning is frequentist calibration over many 70%
forecasts. David Leonhardt treating a 75% forecast as "wrong" when the 25% outcome
hit exemplifies three-setting thinking. Policymakers treat 80% as near-certainty in
meetings yet call the error "obvious" in classrooms; 60/40 often easier to reason
about correctly than 80/20.

Scientists treat certainty as illusory — "Uncertainty is real… total certainty is
the illusion." The defensible setting after abandoning false certainty is **maybe**,
subdivided into numeric probabilities with granularity matching real calibration
distinctions — not vague "probably"/"unlikely." See [probabilistic forecast granularity](probabilistic-forecast-granularity.md)
and [Sherman Kent probability language](sherman-kent-probability-language.md).

Parents pay disproportionately more to cut disease risk 5%→0% than 10%→5% — buying
**certainty** that 0% and 100% overweight in minds vs. economic models.
