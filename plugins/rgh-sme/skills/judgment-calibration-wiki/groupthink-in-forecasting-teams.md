Irving Janis coined **groupthink**: members of small cohesive groups maintain esprit
de corps by unconsciously developing shared illusions and norms that interfere with
critical thinking and reality testing. Unanimity itself becomes evidence the group
is right ("we can't all be wrong, can we?").

## Bay of Pigs vs Cuban Missile Crisis

1961 Bay of Pigs: CIA-trained exiles, plan leaked to NYT, proceeded anyway. Failed
in three days — not execution failure but planning failure visible at the time
(historians agree). Contingency assumed retreat to Escambray Mountains after landing
site switched to Bay of Pigs — mountains 80 miles away across swamp; no one revisited.
Consequences included Cuban Missile Crisis within 18 months.

1962 Missile Crisis: essentially the *same* advisers, different process. Kennedy's
reforms after Bay of Pigs inquiry identified "cozy unanimity": skepticism licensed;
Robert Kennedy as devil's advocate; hierarchy set aside; JFK sometimes left the room;
kept initial lean private to avoid anchoring; ten alternatives discussed first day.
Resolved via negotiated blockade.

"Groups can be wise, or mad, or both. What makes the difference isn't just who is
in the group... The group is its own animal."

## GJP team design

Teams risk cognitive loafing and groupthink reinforcing each other — false agreement
produces self-righteous complacency. Benefits: sharing information for [dragonfly
eye perspective aggregation](dragonfly-eye-perspective-aggregation.md). But aggregation
cancels error only when judgments are **independent**; deliberation collapses
independence — loudmouths, bullies, credentialed figures pull toward shared error
(tulip mania, 2005 housing bubble).

GJP year-1: random assignment solo vs remote teams; individual scores pooled to
team score. **Team primer** warned against groupthink (consensus isn't always good;
disagreement isn't always bad) and dysfunction (disagree without being disagreeable —
Andy Grove's "constructive confrontation").

[Precision questioning](precision-questioning.md) applied to Bay of Pigs: "How far
to Escambray Mountains?" "Eighty miles." "Terrain?" "Swamp and jungle." — would
likely have surfaced fatal flaw.
