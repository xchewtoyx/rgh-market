---
type: concept
title: Dilution Effect
description: >
  Irrelevant information dilutes confidence in stereotype-consistent judgments
  even when it should be ignored — an overreaction failure mode.
sources:
  - title: Superforecasting
    resource: "Superforecasting (Tetlock, Gardner), ch. 7, Supernewsjunkies?"
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 14 (related)"
---

Tetlock's 1989 study (building on Nisbett): given a stereotype-fitting fact
("Robert studies 31 hours/week" → high GPA; "David is aroused by violent fantasies"
→ high abuse risk), subjects make confident stereotype-consistent judgments. Adding
clearly **irrelevant** facts (Robert plays tennis; David tells jokes) measurably
**dilutes** confidence — the subject is seen "more as a person than a stereotype,"
reducing perceived fit even though the noise should be ignored.

Named the **dilution effect**: sway in response to random irrelevant information.
Markets may partly reflect traders overreacting to noise; frequent household traders
underperformed buy-and-hold in a 1990s study — the mirror image of
[belief perseverance](belief-perseverance-and-identity-protective-cognition.md),
where low commitment invites shrugging positions off on noise.

Forecasting analogue: jumping from 55% to 95% on stale consensus in a fast-moving
domain ([granular Bayesian updating](granular-bayesian-updating.md) overreaction).
Discipline: update in proportion to **diagnostic** weight, not volume of news.

Contrast with formal [Bayesian updating with base rates](bayesian-updating-with-base-rates.md),
where irrelevant evidence should leave posteriors unchanged.
