type: concept
title: Intuition Without a Reliability Signal
description: >
  System 1 delivers fast, confident intuitive answers whether they come from
  skill or heuristics, with no built-in warning when they are unreliable.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), Conclusions"
---

[System 1](system-1-and-system-2.md) registers the [cognitive ease](cognitive-ease.md)
with which it processes information but generates **no warning signal** when that
processing becomes unreliable. Intuitive answers arrive quickly and confidently
whether they come from genuine skill or from [substitution heuristics](substitution-heuristic.md).

[System 2](system-1-and-system-2.md) has no simple way to tell the two apart
short of slowing down and constructing an independent answer — which its
indolence makes it reluctant to do. Many System 1 suggestions get only minimal
checking before being casually endorsed (as in the bat-and-ball problem). This
is how System 1 earns its reputation as the source of errors and biases despite
also being the source of most skilled performance.

The practical implication: **confidence and fluency are not diagnostic of validity.**
Evaluate [provenance](two-conditions-for-trustworthy-expert-intuition.md) — regular
environment, practice history, feedback quality — rather than how the answer feels.
When provenance is unclear, treat fast intuition as a hypothesis to verify, not a
conclusion to trust.

See [lazy System 2 and rationality](lazy-system-2-and-rationality.md),
[illusion of validity and skill](illusion-of-validity-and-skill.md).
