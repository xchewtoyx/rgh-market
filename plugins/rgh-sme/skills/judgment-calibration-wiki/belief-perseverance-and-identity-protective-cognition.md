  Tenacious resistance to revising beliefs tied to identity or public commitment,
  a major driver of underreaction in forecasting.

**Belief perseverance**: resistance to updating settled beliefs even against clear
disconfirming evidence. **Commitment** is the mechanism — the more publicly or
emotionally invested a belief, the harder to revise.

Jenga-tower metaphor: beliefs near the structural core (tied to self-perception or
identity) cannot move without risking collapse of surrounding beliefs; peripheral
beliefs discard easily.

Examples: WWII Japanese-American internment continued despite zero sabotage —
General DeWitt: "The very fact that no sabotage has taken place to date is a
disturbing and confirming indication that such action will be taken." Jean-Pierre
Beugoms updates slowly on military questions where his self-concept is invested:
"if I realize that I'm wrong, I might spend a few days in denial."

Dan Kahan's **identity-protective cognition**: views on gun control correlate with
views on climate change despite no logical link; people asked to imagine
conclusive disconfirming evidence on a strongly held risk belief often say they
would not change position.

Forecasting implication: career analysts and pundits with reputations on the line
underreact more than amateurs with little ego in any single forecast — though
"superforecaster" status can become its own identity. Pair with
[granular Bayesian updating](granular-bayesian-updating.md) and catching
[substitution](substitution-heuristic.md) when dismissing new signals.
