---
type: concept
title: Mental Accounting
description: >
  Informal accounts that organize money and outcomes into separate buckets,
  often distorting rational comprehensive evaluation.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 32"
---

Thaler's analogy: people use **mental accounts** like formal accounting —
spending money, general savings, earmarked education funds, with a hierarchy of
willingness to spend. Useful for self-control (budgets) but can be foolish
(simultaneous saving and credit-card debt).

Econs hold a comprehensive outcome view; Humans use [narrow framing](framing-effects.md)
to keep a finite mind manageable. Money often motivates via points on self-regard
and achievement — "rewards and punishments are all in our heads."

**Ticket/blizzard**: paid-ticket fan more likely to drive in a blizzard than
free-ticket fan — closing the paid account with negative balance (paid out and
deprived of game). An Econ treats ticket cost as sunk; counterfactual "Would I
drive if the ticket were free?" requires active System 2.

**Disposition effect**: sell winners (close account with gain), hold losers —
separate account per purchase. Rational agent sells whichever stock is least likely
to outperform; purchase price matters for taxes and short momentum, but emotional
"close with a gain" costs ~3.4% expected after-tax return in one comparison.

Golfers putt better avoiding bogey than achieving birdie — separate mental account
per hole ([goals as reference points](goals-as-reference-points.md)).

Pair with [sunk-cost fallacy](sunk-cost-fallacy.md) and [loss aversion](loss-aversion-and-reference-points.md).
