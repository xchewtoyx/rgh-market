type: concept
title: Effort Narrows Attention
description: >
  During peak mental effort, System 2 protects the priority task and can render
  people effectively blind to concurrent stimuli — selective overload, not blackout.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 2"
---

During a mental sprint, people can become **effectively blind** — an echo of the
[invisible gorilla](system-1-and-system-2.md) effect, but driven by transformation
load rather than pass counting.

Kahneman and Beatty: subjects performing Add-1 saw rapidly flashing letters and
reported afterward whether target letter K appeared. They almost never missed K at
task start or end but missed it nearly half the time at **peak mental effort** —
despite photographic evidence their eyes were open and pointed at it. Detection
failures followed the same inverted-V as pupil dilation, validating pupil size as
an effort index. See [cognitive pupillometry and mental effort](cognitive-pupillometry-and-mental-effort.md).

Unlike a home circuit breaker that trips everything at once, [System 2](system-1-and-system-2.md)'s
overload response is **selective and precise**: it protects the highest-priority
activity and allocates spare capacity second by second. When subjects prioritized
the digit task, timing of the visual target did not affect main-task performance —
a critical letter during high demand simply went undetected; detection improved when
transformation was less demanding.

Evolutionarily honed: fast orientation to gravest threats improved survival. Even
today, System 1 can take over in emergencies and assign total priority to
self-protection (reacting to a skidding car before full conscious threat appraisal).

Forecasting implication: demanding analytic work under time pressure may miss
obvious contradicting evidence — schedule [independent judgments before discussion](independent-judgments-before-discussion.md)
when possible; recognize [lazy System 2](lazy-system-2-and-rationality.md) may be
genuinely resource-saturated, not only unmotivated.
