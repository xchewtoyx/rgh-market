Commandment **(5)**: look for clashing causal forces. For every good policy
argument there is typically a counterargument worth acknowledging — doves should
list in advance what would nudge them hawkward; hawks should list what would nudge
them dovish.

Unlike classical dialectic (thesis + antithesis → synthesis), the **dragonfly
eye** image requires synthesizing *many* views into one — each eye has up to ~30,000
lenses with slightly different vantage points, synthesized into near-omnidirectional
sight. Foxes function like this, aggregating many perspectives into one judgment
(unlike [hedgehogs'](hedgehogs-vs-foxes.md) single green-tinted lens). An art with
no paint-by-number rules, reconciling irreducibly subjective judgments, ideally
transforming a cookie-cutter dove or hawk into a nuanced hybrid.

## Aggregating perspectives inside one head

*Financial Times* 1997 contest (Richard Thaler): guess a number 0–100 closest to
two-thirds of the average of all guesses. Naive game-theoretic reasoning iterates
to Nash equilibrium 0; actual average was 18.91, winning guess 13 — because real
contestants aren't uniformly hyper-rational; many stopped at 33 (assuming random
guesses, average 50, two-thirds = 33) or 22. The purely logical ("Spock") answer
failed because it ignored the psychological ("McCoy") perspective on how others
actually reason; the best answer synthesizes both — the "Captain Kirk perspective."
Returns to added perspectives taper in practice.

Annie Duke (elite poker player): most players assume an opponent who raises has a
strong hand, yet when asked to imagine holding a strong hand themselves, admit
they would *not* raise (they'd call, to avoid scaring the opponent off). Contradiction
shows how hard genuine perspective-taking is even among practiced players.
