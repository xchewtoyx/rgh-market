From Isaiah Berlin via Philip Tetlock's *Expert Political Judgment* (EPJ, 284
forecasters, ~28,000 predictions over ~20 years):

**Hedgehogs** "know one big thing" — organize thinking around one Big Idea
(environmental doomster, free-market fundamentalist, etc.; specific ideology
didn't matter). They force complex problems into a preferred cause-effect template,
treat misfits as irrelevant, pile up "furthermore/moreover" reasoning, are unusually
confident, readily declare things "impossible" or "certain," and resist changing
their minds even after clear predictive failure ("just wait"). Two hedgehogs debating
makes good TV.

**Foxes** draw on many analytical tools and information sources, shift mental gears
("however," "but," "although," "on the other hand"), talk in possibilities and
probabilities rather than certainties, and more readily admit error and update. Foxes
beat hedgehogs on both [calibration and resolution](calibration-vs-resolution.md)
— not merely by playing it safe; foxes had genuinely more foresight.

Headline punchline: average expert ≈ "dart-throwing chimp," but two groups emerged:
one failed to beat random guessing (on longer-range forecasts, worse than chimp);
another beat the chimp modestly — barely outperforming "always predict no change."
Differentiator was **not** credentials (PhDs, classified access) or ideology — it
was **how they thought**.

Within EPJ, hedgehogs' accuracy *declined* when forecasting within their own
specialty — the Big Idea distorted rather than sharpened view; more information
filtered through the same tinted lens without improving accuracy. Inverse correlation
between fame and accuracy: media favors hedgehogs because simple, confident Big-Idea
narratives make better TV — the most overconfident experts were most likely invited
onto news shows.

Illustrative case: Larry Kudlow declared "Bush boom" and "there is no recession"
through December 2007 and mid-2008, right up to Lehman collapse; career undamaged.

The model is a spectrum, not a dichotomy — "fox-hedgehogs" and "hedgehog-foxes"
exist; people think differently across contexts. George Box: "All models are wrong,
but some are useful."

Experts are led astray not by what they believe but **how they think**. More
knowledge can worsen reliability by inflating the [illusion of validity](illusion-of-validity-and-skill.md).
