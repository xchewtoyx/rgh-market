type: concept
title: Base Rate Neglect
description: >
  Overweighting case-specific information and underweighting population base
  rates when judging probabilities, even when base rates are known.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 16"
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 14"
---

Base rates are the proportion of cases in each category in the relevant
population. When no other information is given, using base rates is the obvious
move — rank graduate fields by how many students are enrolled in each.

With individual-case information, people often ignore base rates entirely and
rely on [representativeness](representativeness-heuristic.md) instead. In the
Tom W puzzle, respondents substituted similarity rankings for probability
rankings despite knowing the base rates.

People are not always blind to base rates: they use them spontaneously when no
description is provided, and weight them when made explicit as part of the
problem (though individual-case information is nearly always overweighted).
Norbert Schwarz found that "think like a statistician" instructions increased
base-rate use; "think like a clinician" instructions suppressed it.

A Harvard experiment combined the Tom W problem with facial feedback:
cheek-puffers (emotionally neutral) showed pure representativeness reliance;
frowners (increasing System 2 vigilance) showed measurable base-rate
sensitivity. This suggests base-rate neglect partly reflects *laziness*, not
pure ignorance — [System 2](system-1-and-system-2.md) knows base rates matter
but applies that knowledge only with extra effort.

The formal corrective is [Bayesian updating with base rates](bayesian-updating-with-base-rates.md):
prior beliefs combine with evidence diagnosticity. When evidence quality is
doubtful, stay close to the base rate.

See [causal versus statistical base rates](causal-vs-statistical-base-rates.md)
for why some base rates are used and others ignored despite identical math.
