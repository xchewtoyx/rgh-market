---
type: concept
title: Regression to the Mean
description: >
  Extreme observations tend toward the average on retest because luck and
  sampling variance do not persist — often mistaken for causation.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 17"
---

**Success = talent + luck**; **great success = a little more talent + a lot of
luck.**

Flight instructors observed: cadets praised after good maneuvers did worse next
try; cadets screamed at after bad ones improved — concluding punishment works.
Both groups were regressing toward their true average after lucky or unlucky
performance. Coin-throwing demos show the same pattern without any feedback.

In a two-day golf tournament, the day-1 leader is expected to do well day 2 but
less well (day-1 luck unlikely to recur); the laggard should improve. The gap
shrinks. "The best predicted performance on day 2 is more moderate, closer to
the average than the evidence on which it is based." More extreme original
scores imply more luck was involved.

Regression has an explanation but **not a cause** — it is mathematically
inevitable whenever correlation between two measures is less than perfect.
Correlation and regression are the same concept from different perspectives.

**Mauboussin's diagnostic** (*The Success Equation*): performance mixes skill and
luck; the **rate** at which extreme performers regress on retest reveals the mix
— slow regression = skill-dominated; fast = chance-dominated. Father-son height
correlation ~0.5: a 6-foot father predicts a ~5'10" son (halfway toward 5'8"
mean), not 6 feet. Tournament forecaster Frank (bottom 1% year 1) and Nancy (top
99%): pure luck → both to 50% year 2; half luck/half skill → Frank ~25%, Nancy
~75%; pure skill → no regression.

**Misapplied regression**: chronic back-pain sufferers seek treatment on worst
days; feeling better next day is often regression, not the homeopathy "working."

Common misreadings:

- **Sports Illustrated jinx**: cover athletes had exceptional (partly lucky)
  seasons; luck doesn't persist.
- **Intelligent women marry less intelligent men**: algebraically equivalent to
  imperfect spousal-intelligence correlation — not an interesting causal puzzle.

Treatment studies need **control groups**: depressed children given any
intervention will show improvement via regression alone if test-retest correlation
is imperfect — the question is whether treated groups improve *more* than
regression predicts.

For [skill vs luck diagnosis](skill-vs-luck-diagnosis.md): year-to-year ranking
persistence distinguishes skill from luck. System 1 automatically searches for
causal stories whenever an event draws attention — regression gets wrong causal
narratives instead of statistical ones.

Sales forecasting: naively applying uniform 10% growth to every store ignores
that stores with unusually high or low prior-year sales should regress toward
their long-run average.
