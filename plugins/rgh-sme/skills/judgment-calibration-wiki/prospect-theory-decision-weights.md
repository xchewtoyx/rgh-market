title: Prospect Theory Decision Weights
description: >
  People transform objective probabilities into decision weights that diverge
  from linear probability — the core of prospect theory's descriptive model.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 29"
---

Prospect theory (Kahneman and Tversky) describes choice under uncertainty when
people are not rational expected-utility maximizers. **Decision weights** differ
from probabilities:

- Endpoints (0%, 100%) match probabilities exactly.
- Near-zero probabilities overweighted ([possibility effect](possibility-and-certainty-effects.md)).
- Near-certain probabilities underweighted (certainty effect).
- Mid-range probabilities compressed — inadequate sensitivity.

Neuroscience: brain regions track these estimated decision weights when win
probability changes.

This distorts judgments under uncertainty: [framing effects](framing-effects.md),
[loss aversion and reference points](loss-aversion-and-reference-points.md),
mental accounting interact with nonlinear weighting. For forward-looking probability calibration, the lesson is
that stated or felt probabilities may not match the weights actually driving
decisions — separate calibration of beliefs from choice behavior.

Normative standard remains expected utility; prospect theory is descriptive.
[Allais paradox](allais-paradox.md) motivated the departure from linear weighting.
