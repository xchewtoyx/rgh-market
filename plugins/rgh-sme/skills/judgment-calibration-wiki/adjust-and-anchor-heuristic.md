---
type: concept
title: Adjust-and-Anchor Heuristic
description: >
  System 2 adjusts insufficiently away from an anchor, stopping at the edge of
  uncertainty nearest the starting point.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 11"
---

Tversky's account of [anchoring](anchoring-effect.md): start from the anchor,
judge too high or low, mentally move away — but adjustment stops prematurely
once no longer certain further movement is warranted.

Evidence: drawing a 2½-inch line from bottom-up yields shorter estimates than
top-down (Le Boeuf and Shafir). Driving too fast after leaving a highway;
teenagers turning music down from "too loud" but not to parent's "reasonable."
Questions like Washington's presidency (anchor 1776) or Everest boiling point
(anchor 100°C) trigger known-wrong anchors plus insufficient correction.

Epley and Gilovich: head-shaking (rejecting) moves estimates farther from anchor;
nodding enhances anchoring. Adjustment is effortful — depleted by digit memory
or mild intoxication. This is a System 2 failure under load.

Contrast [anchoring as priming](anchoring-as-priming.md) — both mechanisms
operate; debiasing activates System 2 search for counter-arguments.
