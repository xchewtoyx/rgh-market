---
type: concept
title: Law of Least Effort
description: >
  Given several ways to achieve the same goal, people gravitate to the least
  demanding — effort is a cost in the economy of action.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 2"
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 3"
---

If several ways achieve the same goal, people eventually choose the **least demanding**.
Effort is a cost in the **economy of action**; skill acquisition is driven by the
balance of benefits and costs. "Laziness is built deep into our nature."

As skill in a task increases, its energy demand diminishes — brain imaging shows
fewer regions active. **Talent** has a similar effect: more intelligent individuals
need less effort for the same problems (pupil size and brain activity). Connect
[cognitive pupillometry and mental effort](cognitive-pupillometry-and-mental-effort.md).

We normally avoid overload by breaking tasks into easy steps and offloading
intermediate results to long-term memory or paper rather than working memory — "we
conduct our mental lives by the law of least effort."

[System 2](system-1-and-system-2.md) believes itself the hero but is **reluctant to
invest more effort than strictly necessary**; thoughts and actions it believes chosen
are often guided by System 1. Some vital tasks still require System 2 effort and
self-control to override intuitions and impulses.

Same principle underlies [ego depletion and mental effort budget](ego-depletion-and-mental-effort-budget.md)
(shared pool), [lazy System 2 and rationality](lazy-system-2-and-rationality.md)
(insufficient monitoring motivation), and choosing [framing](framing-effects.md)
defaults over effortful reframing.
