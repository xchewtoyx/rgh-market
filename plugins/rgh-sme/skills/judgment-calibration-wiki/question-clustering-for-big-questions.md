title: Question Clustering for Big Questions
description: >
  Unscorable big-picture questions decompose into many small scorable forecasts
  whose Bayesian-style updates collectively answer what pundits leave vague.
sources:
  - title: Superforecasting
    resource: "Superforecasting (Tetlock, Gardner), ch. 12"
---

Big questions matter but resist scoring ("How does this all turn out?"); small
questions score but seem trivial alone. Tetlock's resolution: **the big question
is composed of many small questions** — pointillist painting; each dot adds little,
the pattern emerges from the collection.

2013 Korean peninsula crisis example: will North Korea test another rocket? rebuff
talks? fire artillery? fire on a South Korean ship? Each scorable "yes" shifts the
aggregate toward "this ends badly." GJP clusters arose incidentally from events;
future research should develop **Bayesian question clustering** deliberately as
diagnostic strategy.

Paul Saffo dismissed an IARPA rocket-launch question as trivial while answering
the big question fluently and unscorably — classic pundit-panel move Tetlock cannot
adjudicate years later.
