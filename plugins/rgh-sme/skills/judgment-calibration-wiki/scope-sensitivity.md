---
type: concept
title: Scope Sensitivity
description: >
  Probability estimates must vary with time horizon and question scope — a bias
  where people give the same answer regardless of scale.
sources:
  - title: Superforecasting
    resource: "Superforecasting (Tetlock, Gardner), ch. 11"
---

**Scope insensitivity** (Kahneman): Toronto residents asked willingness to pay
to clean up lakes in one region vs. all 250,000 Ontario lakes both said ~$10.
Migratory bird deaths at 2,000 / 20,000 / 200,000 birds/year all drew ~$80 —
people substitute "How bad does this make me feel?" for pricing the actual
quantity ([attribute substitution](substitution-heuristic.md)).

In geopolitical forecasting, probability estimates need a time frame often
silently dropped. Assad-regime-fall probabilities must differ for 3, 6, or 24
months.

Experiment: regular forecasters gave 40% (3-month) vs 41% (6-month) — essentially
scope-insensitive. Superforecasters: 15% vs 24% — real scope sensitivity.
Superforecasters mentally simulated other time frames, resisting bias. Training
guidelines urge thought experiments varying cutoff dates and targets.

Related: [frequency format framing](frequency-format-framing.md) makes subset
relations visually obvious; [WYSIATI](wysiati.md) drives tip-of-the-nose
generalizations (General Flynn calling current conflict "unprecedented" without
checking global violence trends).

The [focusing illusion](focusing-illusion.md) is a distinct scope failure: salient
factors (climate, disability) dominate global judgments even when they contribute
little to experienced well-being over time — duration and adaptation neglected.
