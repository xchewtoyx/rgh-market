type: concept
title: Skill vs Luck Diagnosis
description: >
  Persistence of individual differences in achievement distinguishes skill from
  luck in forecasting and trading domains.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 20"
  - title: Superforecasting
    resource: "Superforecasting (Tetlock, Gardner), ch. 4"
---

"The diagnostic for the existence of any skill is the consistency of individual
differences in achievement." Pure luck yields zero year-to-year ranking
correlation; skill produces stable rankings (golfers, car salespeople,
orthodontists).

**Langer's illusion of control**: Yale students told (falsely) they started a
coin-flip run with hits rated their predictive "skill" higher than students told
they started with misses — despite identical 15/30 records. Even elite students,
explicitly told the task is random, fell for it. Media profiles one dramatic
correct pundit call ("called the 2008 crash!") while ignoring the dart-throwing-
chimp base rate — anyone can "predict" a crash by repeating the warning forever.

**Survivorship / base-rate fallacy**: spotlighting one improbably successful
investor (six or seven years beating the market) while ignoring how many tried —
with enough attempts, repeated luck becomes near-certain (lottery analogy).
Business-success books built on this pattern rarely prove highlighted behaviors
caused outcomes.

**GJP superforecasters**: year-to-year individual performance correlation ~**0.65**
(higher than father-son height ~0.5). ~**30%** of individual superforecasters
drop out of the top 2% each year (~70% consistency). Under coin-flip correlation
0, odds of that consistency < 1 in 100,000,000; under observed 0.65, ~1 in 3.
Group-level lead **increased** in years 2–3 rather than regressing — suspicious
until you account for the **"super" treatment** (elite teams, recognition)
offsetting mean reversion. Genuine regression still appears individually: don't
treat any single year's top performer as infallible.

**Mutual funds**: stock selection for most managers is "more like rolling dice
than playing poker." At least two of three underperform in a given year;
year-to-year correlation "barely higher than zero" — successful funds are mostly
lucky. In efficient markets, educated guesses are no more accurate than blind
guesses.

**Terry Odean's research** (163,000 trades): stocks sold outperformed stocks
bought by 3.2 percentage points/year net of costs. Barber/Odean: "Trading Is
Hazardous to Your Wealth" — most active traders poorest results; "Boys Will Be
Boys" — men traded more on useless ideas. Investors sell winners and hold
losers, chase news stocks.

**Wealth advisers**: 28 pairwise year-to-year correlations averaged .01 — "zero."
Executives expected "not very high," not zero. "The firm was rewarding luck as
if it were skill" — finding quickly swept under the rug.

Stock-pickers exercise real skills (reading financials, evaluating management) but
skill at evaluating prospects ≠ skill at judging whether information is already
priced in. See [regression to the mean](regression-to-the-mean.md) and
[wicked and low-validity environments](wicked-and-low-validity-environments.md).
