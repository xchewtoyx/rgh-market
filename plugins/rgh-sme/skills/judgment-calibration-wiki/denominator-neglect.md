Denominator neglect is overweighting the numerator or vivid numerator story while
underweighting the population denominator — a cousin of [base rate neglect](base-rate-neglect.md).
Attention drawn to winning cases or numerators is not matched by equally careful
assessment of non-winning cases or the base population.

## Urn experiment

Participants choose between drawing a red (winning) marble from Urn A (10
marbles, 1 red — 10% chance) or Urn B (100 marbles, 8 red — 8% chance). Urn A
offers objectively better odds, yet **30%–40% of students choose Urn B** — the
urn with more winning marbles but worse probability. Seymour Epstein attributes
this to superficial processing by [System 1 and System 2](system-1-and-system-2.md)
(the "experiential system"). Vivid imagery amplifies the bias: a small urn evokes
one red marble against a vaguely defined white background; a large urn evokes
eight red marbles against an indistinct background, creating a more hopeful
feeling. Distinctive vividness of winning outcomes increases their [decision
weight](prospect-theory-decision-weights.md), enhancing the [possibility
effect](possibility-and-certainty-effects.md) — and by the same logic, the
[certainty effect](possibility-and-certainty-effects.md): a 90%-chance prize
feels less certain when framed as "10 of 100 losers" than "1 of 10 losers."

[Frequency format framing](frequency-format-framing.md) ("how many of 100")
makes denominators concrete and reduces errors that percentage-only framing hides.

Connects to [law of small numbers](law-of-small-numbers.md): ignoring sample size
when evaluating evidence reliability.
