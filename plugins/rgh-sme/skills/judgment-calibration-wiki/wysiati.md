WYSIATI — What You See Is All There Is — names the asymmetry between how the mind
treats available information and information it does not have (Amos Tversky's
"Primat of the Is"). The associative machine represents only activated ideas;
information not retrieved might as well not exist. System 1 constructs the best
story from activated ideas; success = **coherence**, not quantity or quality of
data. When information is scarce, System 1 jumps to conclusions.

Demonstration: "Will Mindik be a good leader? She is intelligent and strong…" —
answer comes immediately from adjectives; System 1 never asks what else is needed.
First impressions bias later adjectives ("corrupt and cruel" would revise, but
first-impression pull remains). Lazy System 2 often endorses ready answers.

**One-sided evidence study** (Thornton false-arrest lawsuit): participants hearing
only one lawyer's argument (adding no new facts beyond background) were **more
confident** than those hearing both sides — confidence tracks story coherence, not
completeness. "Knowing little makes it easier to fit everything you know into a
coherent pattern."

**Biases WYSIATI explains**:

- **[Overconfidence](illusion-of-validity-and-skill.md)**: neither quantity nor
  quality of evidence drives confidence much — story quality does.
- **[Framing effects](framing-effects.md)**: one formulation seen; equivalence
  invisible unless both shown.
- **[Base-rate neglect](base-rate-neglect.md)**: vivid description activates;
  statistical base rate absent from story.

In the Tom W study, participants were told the personality description's
psychological-test basis was "of uncertain validity," yet treated it as fully
informative. Unless you decide immediately to reject evidence, System 1 processes
it as valid — a driver of [insensitivity to evidence quality](representativeness-heuristic.md)
under the representativeness heuristic.

The prescribed discipline when evidence quality is doubtful: keep probability
judgments close to the [base rate](base-rate-neglect.md). This requires
effortful self-monitoring and self-control — a [System 2](system-1-and-system-2.md)
intervention.

Applied to under-informed decisions: acting on one good report without noticing
how little was actually gathered, or avoiding more information because it "might
spoil their story." WYSIATI makes a coherent partial story feel complete.
