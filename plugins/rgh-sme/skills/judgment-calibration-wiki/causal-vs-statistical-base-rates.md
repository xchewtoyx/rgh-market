---
type: concept
title: Causal Versus Statistical Base Rates
description: >
  Population statistics used only when they fit a causal story about the case;
  otherwise neglected in favor of case-specific narrative.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 16"
---

**Statistical base rates** are facts about a population — e.g., 85% of cabs are
Green. They are generally underweighted when case-specific information appears,
because [System 1](system-1-and-system-2.md) finds no causal link to chew on.

**Causal base rates** change how you view the individual case — e.g., Green cabs
cause 85% of accidents implies reckless Green drivers, a stereotype easily
combined with witness testimony. Mathematically identical cab problems yield
~80% answers in the statistical version and ~41% (Bayesian) in the causal version.

Stereotypes are how System 1 represents social categories (norms and prototypes).
In the cab problem, stereotyping improves accuracy; in hiring or profiling, social
norms correctly resist treating group statistics as presumptive facts about
individuals — with real costs to denying valid statistical information.

Icek Ajzen's exam study: "75% of this class passed" (causal — test difficulty)
heavily shapes individual pass predictions; "the sample was constructed with 75%
passers" (noncausal assembly fact) barely matters. System 1 handles causally
linked stories; it is weak at statistical reasoning.

Nisbett and Borgida's helping experiment: told 27% rushed to help, students still
predicted individuals in bland videos would rush — "Subjects' unwillingness to
deduce the particular from the general was matched only by their willingness to
infer the general from the particular." Surprising **individual** cases teach
statistics; surprising **statistical** facts do not transfer to individual
judgments.

Even compelling causal statistics may not shift beliefs rooted in personal
experience. Formal fix: [Bayesian updating with base rates](bayesian-updating-with-base-rates.md).

Pair with [representativeness heuristic](representativeness-heuristic.md) and
[affect heuristic](affect-heuristic.md) in politically charged debates.
