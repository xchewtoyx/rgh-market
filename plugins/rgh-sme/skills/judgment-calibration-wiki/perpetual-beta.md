Borrowing the software term for a product used, analyzed, and improved without a
"final release," Tetlock labels top forecasters **perpetual beta**: committed to
belief-updating and self-improvement over time.

In tournament data, this commitment is roughly **three times as powerful a
predictor** of rising into superforecaster ranks as the next-best predictor
(intelligence). Paraphrasing Edison: superforecasting is "roughly 75% perspiration,
25% inspiration" — no amount of raw IQ substitutes for growing one's synapses.

The forecasting try-fail-analyze-adjust cycle differs from bicycle-learning in
that it can take months or years and outcomes are more chance-dependent. Best
practices improve odds, but less reliably than in domains with fast, unambiguous
feedback. That slower, noisier loop demands **grit** — passionate perseverance
toward long-term goals despite frustration — combined with a **growth mindset**
(Carol Dweck): belief that abilities grow through effort, not fixed traits. Fixed-
mindset people treat setbacks as proof of immutable limits; growth-mindset people
treat failure as signal to think harder (Mary Simpson missed 2008 crisis, joined
GJP, became superforecaster). Brain scans: fixed-mindset volunteers attend only to
right/wrong; growth-mindset volunteers attend to learning information.

Keynes as "consistently inconsistent": admitted mistakes, reversed views, rebuilt
after 1920 currency losses and 1929 crash — leading toward value investing. Polanyi's
bicycle mechanics don't teach riding; forecasting requires trying, with **informed
practice** — GJP training booklet ~10% accuracy gain, interacting with practice
("Fortune favors the prepared mind").

Not every top forecaster has every trait; strengths can compensate for deficits.
But perpetual beta dominates the composite: see
[superforecaster composite profile](superforecaster-composite-profile.md) and
[forecast postmortem](forecast-postmortem.md) practice.
