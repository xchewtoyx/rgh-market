---
type: concept
title: Moses Illusion
description: >
  A question-acceptance error where an incongruous detail goes unnoticed because
  it is not abnormal within the activated context — norm theory in action.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 6"
---

"How many animals of each kind did **Moses** take into the ark?" — most people do
not notice it was Noah, not Moses. [Norm theory and surprise](norm-theory-and-surprise.md)
explains the error: "ark" activates a biblical context, and Moses is not abnormal
within that context — not actively expected, but not surprising either. "Moses" and
"Noah" share vowel sound and syllable count, producing [associative coherence](associative-coherence-and-activation.md)
accepted without scrutiny.

Substituting an incongruous name (e.g., "George W. Bush") breaks the illusion because
it does not fit the activated context — the violation registers as surprise.

Forecasting and review implication: fluent, coherent framing can smuggle wrong
premises past [System 2](system-1-and-system-2.md)'s lazy check. Pair with
[confirmation bias and System 1 gullibility](confirmation-bias-and-system-1-gullibility.md)
and [WYSIATI](wysiati.md).
