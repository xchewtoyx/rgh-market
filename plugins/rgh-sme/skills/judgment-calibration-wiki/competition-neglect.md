---
type: concept
title: Competition Neglect
description: >
  Forecasters focus on their own plan and capabilities while neglecting rivals'
  actions, producing excess entry and overestimated control.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 24"
---

Colin Camerer and Dan Lovallo coined **competition neglect**: entrepreneurs and
executives focus on what they know best — their own plans, actions, and immediate
threats like funding — and know less about competitors, so they imagine a future
where competition plays little part. [WYSIATI](wysiati.md) and the [substitution
heuristic](substitution-heuristic.md) jointly produce both competition neglect and
overly optimistic relative standing.

Informal survey of start-up founders — "To what extent will the outcome of your
effort depend on what you do in your firm?" — never got an answer below 80%, even
from founders unsure of overall success. Outcomes depend as much on competitors'
achievements and market shifts as on the firm's own effort.

Disney Studios' then-chairman explaining why big-budget films cluster on the same
release weekends: "Hubris... If you only think about your own business... you don't
think that everybody else is thinking the same way." The competition simply isn't
part of the decision; the hard question ("Considering what others will do, how many
people will see our film?") is replaced by an easy one ("Do we have a good film and
a good marketing organization?").

Consequence: **excess entry** — more competitors enter a market than it can
sustainably support, so the average entrant loses, even though the effect on the
economy overall can be positive. Giovanni Dosi and Dan Lovallo call failed
entrepreneurial firms that nonetheless signal new markets to stronger competitors
"optimistic martyrs" — good for the economy, bad for their investors.

Part of [optimistic bias](optimistic-bias.md) and [planning fallacy](planning-fallacy.md)
mechanisms: focusing on the goal and the plan while neglecting others' plans and
skills.
