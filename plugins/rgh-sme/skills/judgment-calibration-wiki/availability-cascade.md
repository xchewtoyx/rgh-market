---
type: concept
title: Availability Cascade
description: >
  Media-driven self-reinforcing cycles that make minor risks feel important and
  reset policy priorities through escalating public concern.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 13"
---

Sunstein and Kuran's **availability cascade**: "all heuristics are equal, but
availability is more equal than the others." Availability provides a heuristic not
just for frequency but for **importance** — judged by fluency and emotional charge.

Mechanism: a minor event catches attention and provokes worry; emotional reaction
becomes newsworthy, prompting more coverage and more concern — a self-sustaining
cycle. **Availability entrepreneurs** feed worrying news; media compete for alarming
headlines; scientists who dampen fear get hostile attention and cover-up suspicion.
The issue becomes politically important because it is on everyone's mind, pushing
other risks into the background.

**Love Canal (1979–80s)**: toxic waste exposure, foul smell, sustained resident
campaign — daily news at peak, scientists shouted down, empty baby coffins paraded.
Mass relocation and CERCLA/Superfund legislation — expensive; some argue same money
could have saved more lives elsewhere. Health-damage claims largely unsubstantiated;
Kuran and Sunstein treat it almost as pseudo-event.

**Alar scare (1989)**: apple-growth chemical at gigantic rodent doses → media fear
cascade; industry losses; FDA ban; subsequent research showed tiny possible risk —
"enormous overreaction," net health effect probably negative.

Combined with [probability neglect](probability-neglect.md) and [vivid outcomes reduce probability sensitivity](vivid-outcomes-reduce-probability-sensitivity.md),
cascades distort [risk perception](availability-heuristic.md). Terrorists are significant
practitioners — casualties usually small vs. traffic deaths, but gruesome repetition
"speaks directly to System 1."

Kahneman agrees with both Paul Slovic (public has richer risk conception; fear itself
matters) and Cass Sunstein (experts should resist populist excesses) — democratic
policy must weigh irrational but real pain of fear.
