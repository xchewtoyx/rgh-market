If a memory comes easily to mind, we judge the event as common or likely — the
**availability heuristic**: judging frequency by "the ease with which instances
come to mind." Both [System 1 and System 2](system-1-and-system-2.md) can be
involved; no instances need actually be retrieved — you can sense which letter
set yields more words, or which countries dominated the news, from an impression
of retrieval ease alone.

This is [attribute substitution](substitution-heuristic.md): an easier question
replaces a harder one. Combined with [WYSIATI](wysiati.md), System 1 treats
whatever evidence is at hand as reliable and sufficient for fast, confident
conclusions.

**Factors other than true frequency** that inflate availability (each a bias
source):

- **Salience**: attention-grabbing events (Hollywood divorces, political scandals)
  are overestimated.
- **Drama and recency**: a plane crash or burning car temporarily raises felt
  risk for the whole category.
- **Personal vividness**: own experiences and vivid images outweigh statistics or
  others' cases.

## Fluency vs. content (Schwarz experiments)

Norbert Schwarz: listing six vs. twelve instances of own assertive behavior before
self-rating — twelve instances retrieved more cases but with lower fluency. **Fluency
won**: twelve-instance group rated themselves *less* assertive; twelve non-assertive
instances made people feel quite assertive. Self-ratings dominated by ease of retrieval,
not amount retrieved.

Facial expression variant: frowners had more difficulty retrieving assertiveness
examples and rated themselves less assertive.

Paradoxical findings from same program:

- Believe bicycles used less after recalling many uses.
- Less confident in a choice after producing more supporting arguments.
- Less confident event was avoidable after listing more avoidance ways.
- Less impressed by a car after listing many advantages.
- UCLA course ratings: students listing *more* improvements rated course *higher*
  — difficulty generating many suggestions inferred "things can't be too bad."

**Unexplained unavailability**: effect reverses when difficulty is expected — judgment
turns on *unexpected* fluency drop. Giving external explanation (background music
said to help/hurt retrieval; curved text boxes) eliminates fluency cue — six vs.
twelve instances rated equally assertive.

Personal stakes change strategy: cardiac-health study — students with family history
of heart disease took task seriously and relied on *content* (number retrieved)
rather than fluency; those without history followed standard fluency heuristic.

System 1 sets expectations and registers surprise when violated; retrieves plausible
causes (warned-about oddity no longer surprises). See [norm theory and surprise](norm-theory-and-surprise.md),
[cognitive ease](cognitive-ease.md).

Resisting these biases is tiresome — ask whether a belief rests on a few recent
local instances or whether you skipped a flu shot because no acquaintance got
sick last year.

**When fluency beats content**: people go with retrieval ease over retrieved
content when simultaneously effortful on another task, in a good mood, low on
depression scales, knowledgeable **novices** (not true experts), high on faith in
intuition, or (intriguingly) **powerful** — merely recalling a time you had power
increases trust in your intuition.

**Slovic–Lichtenstein–Fischhoff causes-of-death study**: strokes kill almost twice
as many as all accidents combined, yet 80% judged accidental death more likely;
tornadoes seen as more frequent killers than asthma (20× more deaths from asthma);
lightning less likely than botulism (52× more frequent); disease vs accident judged
~equally likely though disease is 18× more common; accidents judged 300× more
likely than diabetes (true ratio 1:4). Estimates track media novelty and poignancy
— editors cover what interests the public; unusual events feel common. Treat as
[substitution](substitution-heuristic.md) from associative memory activation.

**Kunreuther (disaster memory)**: after earthquakes, Californians buy insurance
and mitigation; diligence fades as memory dims — recurring disaster–concern–
complacency cycles. Protective measures sized to worst **experienced** disaster,
not worst **possible** — pharaonic Nile high-water marks assumed floods would not
exceed recorded maxima because worse disasters don't come easily to mind.

**Speaking-of-availability examples**: overreacting to plane-crash coincidences;
underestimating indoor pollution (sparse media); CEO overconfidence after success
streaks.

**Credit allocation**: spouses estimating their share of household tasks summed
above 100% because each remembers their own efforts more vividly — not purely
self-serving (they also overestimated causing quarrels). Teams often feel
under-appreciated for the same reason. Knowing "there is usually more than 100%
credit to go around" can defuse conflict when roles are easy to identify.

After the 2011 Oslo bombing, early speculation converged on Islamist terrorism
from plausible-but-wrong cues; the perpetrator was Anders Breivik. Scientific
reasoning holds hypotheses provisionally and asks what would falsify them;
confirmation bias is the default — favoring supportive evidence and becoming
"motivated skeptics" toward disconfirming evidence.

## Terrorism and availability cascades

See dedicated notes on [availability cascade](availability-cascade.md) and
[probability neglect](probability-neglect.md).

Israel 2001–2004: 23 bus bombings caused 236 fatalities against ~1.3 million daily
bus riders — tiny per-traveler risk, yet the public avoided buses. Kahneman avoided
stopping next to buses at red lights despite knowing the risk was negligible (more
likely hurt in an ordinary driving accident). Terrorism induces an **availability
cascade**: vivid death images, reinforced by media and conversation, become highly
accessible when tied to a specific situation (a bus). The emotional response is
associative, automatic, and uncontrolled — impulse for protective action. System 2
may "know" the probability is low, but knowledge doesn't eliminate System 1's
self-generated discomfort — "System 1 cannot be turned off." The emotion is
disproportionate to probability *and* insensitive to its exact level (residents
told of two bombers vs. one don't feel proportionally safer).

Lottery ticket sales work in reverse: "thrilling possibility" of the big prize,
reinforced socially, is immediately rewarded by pleasant fantasies — as bus avoidance
is rewarded by relief from fear. "The actual probability is inconsequential; only
possibility matters." See [possibility and certainty effects](possibility-and-certainty-effects.md)
and [vivid outcomes reduce probability sensitivity](vivid-outcomes-reduce-probability-sensitivity.md).

Kahneman: "It is wise to take admissions of uncertainty seriously, but
declarations of high confidence mainly tell you that an individual has
constructed a coherent story in his mind, not necessarily that the story is
true."

Respect intuition where [two conditions for trustworthy expert intuition](two-conditions-for-trustworthy-expert-intuition.md)
hold; double-check with System 2 when time allows.
