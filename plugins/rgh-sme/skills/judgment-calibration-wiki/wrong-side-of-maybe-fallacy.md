type: concept
title: Wrong Side of Maybe Fallacy
description: >
  Judging probabilistic forecasts by which side of 50% they fell on rather than
  whether the stated probability was well-calibrated.
sources:
  - title: Superforecasting
    resource: "Superforecasting (Tetlock, Gardner), ch. 3"
---

The **wrong-side-of-maybe fallacy**: if a meteorologist says 70% chance of rain
and it doesn't rain, the public treats it as a miss — ignoring the implicit 30%
chance of no rain.

Example: prediction markets put 75% odds the Supreme Court would strike down
Obamacare; when it upheld the law, a *New York Times* reporter declared "the
wisdom of the crowds was wrong."

This creates perverse incentives: a stated 65% forecast that fails risks public
pillorying, pushing forecasters toward elastic language ("fair chance," "serious
possibility") retroactively stretchable to claim success. Niall Ferguson's
"Greek default may be a matter of days away" carries no probability and is
essentially unfalsifiable.

Proper evaluation requires [calibration vs resolution](calibration-vs-resolution.md)
over many forecasts with [Sherman Kent–style](sherman-kent-probability-language.md)
numeric probabilities and explicit timelines.
