---
type: concept
title: MPG Illusion
description: >
  Miles-per-gallon framing misleads fuel-saving comparisons because mpg is
  nonlinear in gallons consumed — gallons-per-mile is the rational frame.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 34"
---

Richard Larrick and Jack Soll (*Science*, 2008): traditional US "miles per gallon"
framing gives poor guidance for fuel-cost decisions because mpg is nonlinear with
respect to actual gallons consumed.

Adam switches 12→14 mpg; Beth switches 30→40 mpg (seen as more virtuous — bigger
mpg gain). Over 10,000 miles: Adam saves 119 gallons (833→714); Beth saves only
83 gallons (333→250). Adam's smaller mpg improvement saves more fuel.

The correct frame is gallons-per-mile (or liters-per-100km). Under President Obama,
Cass Sunstein helped push a partial fix: 2013 "fuel economy and environment" sticker
included gallons-per-mile in small print while mpg remained dominant — ~5 years from
paper to policy, "probably a speed record" for psychological science reaching policy.

Example of a **good frame** — demonstrably superior guide to rational decisions —
alongside [risk policies and broad framing](risk-policies-broad-framing.md) and
[sunk-cost fallacy](sunk-cost-fallacy.md) reframing.
