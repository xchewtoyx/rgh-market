title: Heuristics and Biases Vocabulary
description: >
  Diagnostic labels for predictable judgment errors — shared vocabulary lets
  agents locate, anticipate, and challenge unreliable intuitions.

  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow

Kahneman's aim: enrich the vocabulary agents use to discuss others' judgments and
choices — easier and more enjoyable to spot others' mistakes than one's own;
"intelligent gossip" from colleagues motivates self-criticism better than private
resolutions. Analogy to medical diagnosis: rich disease labels bind symptoms,
causes, and interventions; judgment work needs richer labels than everyday
language.

Systematic errors are **biases** — they "recur predictably in particular
circumstances." Example: [halo effect](halo-effect.md) — a confident, handsome
speaker gets comments judged more favorably; naming the bias makes it easier to
anticipate and recognize. Most impressions arise without knowing how; confidence
is not a reliable signal of correctness — "an objective observer is more likely
to detect our errors than we are."

**Kahneman–Tversky origins (1969–)**: studied whether people are good intuitive
statisticians — even statisticians exaggerated small-sample replication likelihood
("even statisticians were not good intuitive statisticians"). Method: continuous
conversation, inventing questions, examining their own wrong first intuitions;
shared intuitions likely generalize.

**Representativeness** ("Steve"): shy, tidy detail-lover — librarian or farmer?
Resemblance to stereotype dominates; [base rates](base-rate-neglect.md) ignored
(>20 male farmers per male librarian in the US).

**Availability**: divorce-rate question answered by ease of recalling examples;
letter-K position experiment; media-driven importance assessments.

**1974 *Science* paper**: ~20 biases as heuristic manifestations. Challenged 1970s
assumptions that (1) people are generally rational and (2) irrationality is mainly
emotion. Errors traced to "the design of the machinery of cognition," not emotional
corruption. Full demonstration questions in papers let readers trip themselves —
harder to dismiss as undergraduate artifacts. "Luck plays a large role in every
story of success."

**Prospect theory (1979)**: systematic violations of rational choice on simple
gambles — foundation of behavioral economics. Collaboration ended with geographic
separation; Tversky died 1996; Nobel 2002 (shared in spirit).

Original work noted heuristics "are quite useful, but sometimes lead to severe and
systematic errors" — focused on biases as evidence for heuristics. The book balances
skillful [intuition as recognition](intuition-as-recognition.md) with heuristic
failure modes across five parts (see [system 1 and system 2](system-1-and-system-2.md)
roadmap).
