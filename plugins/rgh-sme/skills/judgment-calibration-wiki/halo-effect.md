The **halo effect**: liking (or disliking) one trait spills to unobserved traits
— exaggerated emotional coherence. Meeting Joan as personable → guess she's
generous without evidence → liking increases further.

Solomon Asch: same six adjectives in different order ("intelligent…envious" vs.
reversed) radically changes impressions — early traits alter meaning of later ones
(suppressed ambiguity: "stubborn" coheres with admired vs. disliked context). First
impressions dominate; later information often wasted.

Kahneman's grading: booklet-by-booklet scores were spuriously homogeneous until
he graded question-by-question across students — revealed real inconsistency;
resisted harmonizing scores retroactively.

**CEO halo** (Rosenzweig): experts rating CEOs know firm performance — thriving
firm → "flexible, methodical, decisive"; same person when struggling → "confused,
rigid, authoritarian." Causal arrow reverses: "firm fails because CEO is rigid"
vs. "CEO appears rigid because firm is failing." Correlation CEO quality / firm
success ≈ **.30** → stronger-CEO firm wins ~**60%** of pairs (10 points above
chance) — significant but far from business-book determinism.

Business books (*Built to Last*, *In Search of Excellence*) compare lucky vs.
less-lucky firms; post-publication gaps shrink via [regression to the mean](regression-to-the-mean.md)
— "worst admired" firms sometimes outperform "most admired" afterward. Temptation
to narrate "complacency" is wrong; luck explains the pattern.

Subjective confidence reflects associative coherence, not accuracy. Structured
[trait-by-trait scoring](trait-by-trait-scoring.md) and
[independent judgments before discussion](independent-judgments-before-discussion.md)
reduce halo contamination.
