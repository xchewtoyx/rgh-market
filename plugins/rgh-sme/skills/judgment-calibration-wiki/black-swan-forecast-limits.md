---
type: concept
title: Black Swan Forecast Limits
description: >
  Radically improbable consequential events resist skill measurement and may
  dominate history — but many consequences and modest tail risks remain forecastable.
sources:
  - title: Superforecasting
    resource: "Superforecasting (Tetlock, Gardner), ch. 11"
---

Nassim Taleb argues black swans alone determine history's course — "What matters
can't be forecast and what can be forecast doesn't matter." Tetlock rejects the
false dichotomy of perfect forecasting vs. "forecasting is bunk" while taking Taleb
seriously as one of the strongest challenges to superforecasting.

**Definitions:** (1) Stringent — literally inconceivable before occurrence. Many
canonical "black swans" fail this test (9/11 war-gamed in 1998; WWI anxiety for
decades). (2) Modest — "highly improbable consequential event" — where Taleb's
real force lies.

**Measurement problem:** Short-horizon calibration is measurable quickly; 0.1% or
0.0001% events need centuries of data to judge tail-forecast skill. First-generation
IARPA tournaments "tell us nothing about how good superforecasters are at spotting
gray or black swans."

**History crawls too:** Life expectancy, ~1–2%/year compounded growth — incremental
edge forecasting (poker-style 68% vs others' 60%) differs from black-swan investing
(most bets fail, rare Google-scale wins) — neither superior, just different.

**Consequences are partly forecastable:** What makes a surprise a black swan is
consequences unfolding over time. Duncan Watts: broader you draw event boundaries,
more forecastable intermediate steps (post-9/11 ultimatum, Taliban response, invasion
timing). "To the extent that such forecasts can anticipate the consequences of events
like 9/11... we can forecast black swans."

Taleb, Kahneman, and Tetlock agree: no evidence geopolitical/economic forecasters
predict much beyond the obvious beyond ~10 years — EPJ accuracy declined toward
chance at 5 years. Yet institutions persist (Quadrennial Defense Review 20-year
forecasts). Linton Wells memo (2001): every decade 1900–2000 radically different
from ten years prior — "plan for surprise" (adaptability, antifragility). But
resilience still requires [probability judgments](sherman-kent-probability-language.md)
— Tokyo earthquake codes vs low-risk regions; two-war doctrine rested on implicit
probabilities often "swept under the rug."

**Fat tails:** Wealth is fat-tailed (500 US billionaires vs normal-distribution
prediction of virtually none). Taleb: historical possibility space is fat-tailed like
wealth, not bell-curved like height — 1914 official dismissing 10M war deaths as
vanishingly improbable under normal assumptions. Kahneman's 50/50 birth events for
Hitler/Stalin/Mao illustrate radical indeterminacy — profound humility warranted.

Bottom line: humility about deep uncertainty should not obscure that people can,
with effort, forecast some developments that matter on human scale. See [forecastability
triage](forecastability-triage.md), [outcome bias and hindsight](outcome-bias-and-hindsight.md).
