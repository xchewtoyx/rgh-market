title: Adversarial Collaboration for Forecasts
description: >
  Opposing experts jointly design scorable forecast questions under shared rules —
  many precise bets and clusters beat polemic that never updates on outcomes.
sources:
  - title: Superforecasting

Public policy debates often become "food fights" — Krugman vs. Ferguson cataloguing
each other's forecast failures without learning (2008–2009 Keynesian/Austerian fracas).
2010 Bernanke inflation-warning letter: four years later signatories said they'd
change nothing — inflation "in wrong place" (Grant) or hedged wording never
falsifiable (Ferguson). Tetlock is agnostic on economics; procedural point: **no one
budged** — unsatisfactory regardless of side.

**Remedy**: Kahneman–Klein **adversarial collaboration** — rivals commit to scientific
ground rules, identify precise disagreement, reconcile it. Apply to policy: opposing
figures, with trusted third parties, design forecast questions specifying magnitude,
benchmark, and time window — not bare "will cause inflation" but how much, measured
how, over what period.

"A single dot on a canvas is not a painting; a single bet cannot resolve a complex
theoretical dispute" — needs many questions and [question clusters](question-clustering-for-big-questions.md).
Split decisions (each side right on some, wrong on others) reflect mixed reality.

Requires **good faith** — wanting truth more than being right — rare among loudest
public voices. Hope rests on quieter actors willing to design clear tests; rationalizing
away disconfirming results carries reputational cost; "I was wrong" is honorable.
"All we have to do is get serious about keeping score."

Humanists' objection ("not everything that counts can be counted") has force — but
imperfect metrics beat judging forecasters by titles, confidence, book sales, CNN
appearances, and Davos time — analogous to credit scores vs. whimsical loan officers
relying on stereotypes.
