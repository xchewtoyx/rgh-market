Superforecasting commandment **(1) Triage**: focus on questions in the
"Goldilocks zone" — not "clocklike" questions solvable by simple rules of thumb,
and not "cloud-like" questions where even fancy models can't beat a dart-throwing
chimp.

**Clocks and clouds** is a false dichotomy — predictability is graded. Ordinary
life (commutes, store hours, actuarial tables, short-horizon weather) is
clocklike; Arab Spring–style cascades and long-horizon geopolitics are cloudlike.
Weather is reliable days out, unreliable beyond a week; dominant regimes can
persist then end abruptly.

Forecasting the 2028 US presidential winner today is as hopeless as forecasting
1952's winner (Eisenhower) in 1940 would have been — believing otherwise is
[hindsight bias](outcome-bias-and-hindsight.md). Some domains (oil prices,
currency markets) have well-earned unpredictability reputations, often discovered
only after wasted effort.

Two symmetric errors: failing to try predicting the potentially predictable vs.
wasting time on the unpredictable. Distinguishes **epistemic uncertainty**
(reducible with better models) from **aleatory uncertainty** (inherent randomness).

Meteorology's **forecast, measure, revise** loop drives incremental improvement;
most high-stakes domains never close the loop because consumers don't demand
accuracy — see [optimistic skeptic stance](optimistic-skeptic-stance.md).

[Black swan forecast limits](black-swan-forecast-limits.md): tail events resist
skill measurement; long horizons genuinely unforecastable — but consequence chains
and modest improbabilities remain in scope for triage.

Related: [Fermi decomposition](fermi-decomposition.md) for tractable
sub-problems within triaged questions.
