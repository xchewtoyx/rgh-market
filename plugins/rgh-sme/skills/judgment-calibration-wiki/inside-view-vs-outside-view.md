---
type: concept
title: Inside View vs Outside View
description: >
  Forecasting from case-specific plans versus anchoring on base rates from a
  reference class of similar cases.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 23"
---

The **inside view** focuses on the specific case: extrapolate from your plan,
chapters written, and margin of error. This is [WYSIATI](wysiati.md)-driven
forecasting. It fails because early work is easiest and done at peak commitment,
and because forecasters ignore "unknown unknowns" — unforeseeable divorces,
illnesses, and coordination crises.

The **outside view** ignores case specifics and asks the base rate in a
reference class of similar cases — the prediction you'd make knowing only the
category. That baseline anchors, then adjusts for case-specific information
(like predicting a NYC woman's height from the city average, then adjusting if
her son is a basketball center).

People who possess outside-view knowledge often never spontaneously use it —
Seymour Fox knew curriculum teams take 7–10 years but first estimated two years
for his own team. In competition with the inside view, the outside view doesn't
stand a chance. Pallid statistics lose to vivid personal impressions, as in
[base rate neglect](base-rate-neglect.md).

The preference for inside view can carry moral overtones ("every case is
unique"), tension with evidence-based medicine and checklists.

[Reference class forecasting](reference-class-forecasting.md) operationalizes
the outside view for project planning.
