When a difficult question is posed, System 1 often silently substitutes an
easier one and answers that instead — the core of the heuristics-and-biases
approach. The **target question** is what you intend to assess; the **heuristic
question** is the simpler substitute you actually answer.

A **heuristic** (Kahneman/Tversky): a simple procedure yielding adequate though
often imperfect answers to hard questions — not Pólya's deliberate System 2
strategy ("find an easier problem"), but an automatic consequence of the
**mental shotgun**: imprecise control over which question we are answering.

The substitution heuristic is the general mechanism behind many biases: the answer
feels like a direct response to the question asked, but it is actually an answer
to a different, easier one.

## Basic assessments and the mental shotgun

[System 1](system-1-and-system-2.md) continuously and effortlessly monitors the
internal and external situation, generating **basic assessments** without specific
intention — evolved to answer survival questions (threat? opportunity? normal?).
Good mood and [cognitive ease](cognitive-ease.md) are described as human equivalents
of assessments of safety and familiarity. These basic assessments matter because
they are easily substituted for harder questions — "the essential idea of the
heuristics and biases approach."

[System 2](system-1-and-system-2.md) can be asked unlimited questions and evaluate
unlimited attributes when directed; System 1's continuous basic assessments feed
substitution without being requested.

The **mental shotgun** names imprecise control over which question is answered:
System 1 tends to compute more than System 2 asked for. Rhyme-judgment experiments:
participants pressed a key when words rhymed (VOTE–NOTE vs. VOTE–GOAT); spelling
was irrelevant to the task, yet discrepant spelling slowed rhyming recognition —
System 1 computed spelling anyway. Literal-truth judgments showed similar excess
computation ("Some jobs are snakes" rejected faster than "Some jobs are jails"
despite all being literally false).

**Intensity matching** maps the heuristic answer onto the target scale — e.g.,
feelings about dolphins onto dollar contributions; perceived political skill
onto electoral success. System 1 matches intensities across entirely different
dimensions: crime severity to color depth or musical loudness; murder as deeper red
/ fortissimo vs. theft as lighter shade / pianissimo. Classic experiments adjusted
sound loudness to match crime severity and separately punishment severity; a mismatch
(one note per crime, one per punishment) produces a felt sense of injustice.

**Julie example**: told "Julie read fluently when she was four," people match her
reading precocity to height (~7 feet), income, crime severity, or Ivy League GPA.
Matches converge within a cultural milieu. This matching-by-translation is the
(statistically wrong) mechanism by which people predict Julie's GPA directly from
her reading age — natural to System 1 and acceptable even to System 2 for most
non-statisticians. See [regression-corrected intuitive prediction](regression-corrected-intuitive-prediction.md).

Example pairs:

| Target question | Heuristic question |
|---|---|
| How much to save an endangered species? | How much emotion do dying dolphins evoke? |
| How happy are you with life these days? | What is my mood right now? |
| How popular is the president now? | How popular will he be in six months? |
| How should financial predators be punished? | How angry do I feel about them? |
| How far will she go in politics? | Does she look like a political winner? |

The 3-D corridor illusion: perspective cues make identical 2-D figures look
different sizes — substitution of 3-D size for 2-D size, automatic and
unavoidable. Judgments based on substitution are predictably biased even when
you know the correct question.

**Mood heuristic**: asking German students about dates before general happiness
made dating dominate happiness reports; order reversed, correlation near zero.
Emotionally significant prior questions alter mood; [WYSIATI](wysiati.md) makes
present state of mind loom large.

A question about probability activates the mental shotgun, evoking answers to
related easier questions — one of which is an automatic
[representativeness](representativeness-heuristic.md) assessment. For laypeople,
"probability" is vague (uncertainty, plausibility, surprise). The Tom W study
showed perfect substitution: likelihood rankings did not differ from similarity
rankings, even among statistics-trained students who knew base rates.

In attitude contexts, System 2 acts more as apologist for System 1's emotions
than critic — searching for arguments consistent with existing beliefs. See
[affect heuristic](affect-heuristic.md).

The [focusing illusion](focusing-illusion.md) is substitution plus [WYSIATI](wysiati.md):
global evaluations overweight whatever aspect is currently focal — "How much pleasure
from your car?" answered as pleasure **when thinking about the car**, neglecting
how rarely the car is thought about while driving.
