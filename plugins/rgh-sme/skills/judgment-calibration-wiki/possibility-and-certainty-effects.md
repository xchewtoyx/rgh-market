---
type: concept
title: Possibility and Certainty Effects
description: >
  Decision weights overweight improbable outcomes and underweight near-certain
  ones relative to their true probabilities.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 29"
---

The **expectation principle** weights outcomes exactly by probability. Actual
judgment violates this:

- **Possibility effect**: 0%→5% chance of $1M feels far more impressive than
  5%→10% — hope that did not exist before. Lottery buyers pay far above expected
  value for tiny win chances.

- **Certainty effect**: 95%→100% eliminates residual doubt — a qualitative jump.
  Many would accept $910,000 for a 95% chance at $1M (EV $950,000). Structured
  settlements monetize this premium. Same for losses: 95% amputation risk vs
  certain amputation feels vastly worse than the 5-point gap suggests.

Improbable outcomes are overweighted; near-certain outcomes underweighted.
Between 5% and 95%, decision weights compress to ~13–79 — **insensitivity to
intermediate probabilities**. A 2% probability gets weight ~8.1 (~4× overweight).

Extreme tail probabilities: very rare events sometimes ignored until attended,
then markededly overweighted; almost no sensitivity among small probabilities
(0.001% vs 0.00001% cancer risk barely distinguished). Worry tracks decision
weight, not true probability — only driving probability to zero eliminates worry.

Parents paid $8.09 vs $2.38 to eliminate insecticide risk entirely vs reducing
15-per-10,000 to 5-per-10,000 — compatible with worry psychology, not rational
expected utility.

See [Allais paradox](allais-paradox.md) and [prospect theory decision weights](prospect-theory-decision-weights.md).
