type: concept
title: Wisdom of Crowds in Forecasting
description: >
  Averaging many independent estimates cancels random error and accumulates
  dispersed signal — but quality depends on diversity and knowledge.
sources:
  - title: Superforecasting
    resource: "Superforecasting (Tetlock, Gardner), ch. 3"
---

Sir Francis Galton's 1906 country-fair experiment: hundreds guessed the dressed
weight of an ox; the average (1,197 lbs) was one pound off the true weight (1,198
lbs). Mechanism: information relevant to the answer is dispersed across many people;
errors point in different directions and cancel, while valid signal accumulates.
Individual bull's-eye guessers who beat the group in one instance typically owe it
to luck — repeat the exercise and different individuals win each time.

Aggregation quality depends on what's aggregated: many knowledgeable-but-diverse
judgments beat aggregating uninformed ones. Aggregations of aggregations also work —
"poll of polls" (Nate Silver, Sam Wang on 2012 election), or PollyVote combining
election polls, expert panel, and quantitative models with strong track record of
sticking with eventual winner even when individual polls waver.

See [forecast aggregation hierarchy](forecast-aggregation-hierarchy.md) for how
crowd averages compare to teams, markets, and superteams; [extremizing algorithm](extremizing-algorithm.md)
when crowd forecasts are systematically underconfident.

Contrast [hedgehogs vs foxes](hedgehogs-vs-foxes.md): fame inversely correlates
with accuracy partly because media selects for confident single-narrative experts.
