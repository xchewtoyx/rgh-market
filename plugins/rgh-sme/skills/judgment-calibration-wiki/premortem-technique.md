title: Premortem Technique
description: >
  Before committing to a plan, imagine it failed and write the disaster history —
  a debiasing ritual against groupthink and uncritical optimism.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 25"
---

Gary Klein's **premortem** (Kahneman's adversarial collaborator): when an
organization has almost committed to an important decision, gather knowledgeable
people and ask: "Imagine we are a year into the future. We implemented the plan as
it now exists. The outcome was a disaster. Take 5–10 minutes to write a brief
history of that disaster."

**Advantages:**

1. Overcomes groupthink once a decision appears made — public doubts get suppressed
   and treated as disloyalty, feeding overconfidence.
2. Unleashes imagination in the direction of threats neglected by [WYSIATI](wysiati.md)
   and uncritical optimism.

Not a panacea — does not fully prevent nasty surprises — but reduces damage from
plans built on coherence without evidence quality. Subjective confidence "is
determined by the coherence of the story one has constructed, not by the quality
and amount of the information that supports it." Organizations may tame optimism
better than individuals.

Pair with [inside view vs outside view](inside-view-vs-outside-view.md),
[planning fallacy](planning-fallacy.md), and independent judgments before
discussion. Use as a debiasing technique here; alignment ritual belongs to
decision-alignment.
