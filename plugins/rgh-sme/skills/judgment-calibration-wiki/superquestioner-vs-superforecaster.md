title: Superquestioner Versus Superforecaster
description: >
  Asking the right unscorable question and answering scorable ones well are
  complementary skills — often carried by different psychological profiles.
sources:
  - title: Superforecasting
    resource: "Superforecasting (Tetlock, Gardner), ch. 12"
---

"Good Judgment Project" naming risks equating foresight with all of good judgment —
moral judgment and other facets resist quantification. A distinct skill is **asking
good questions**: no farsighted forecast until someone poses the right question.

**Smack-the-forehead test**: reading the question afterward, you think "if only I
had thought of that before." Example: Tom Friedman (Sept 2002) asking whether Iraq
was totalitarian because Saddam was cruel or Saddam was cruel because Iraq was an
"Arab Yugoslavia" — prescient framing of sectarian risk despite later strong
invasion support and divergent expectations. Superquestioning and superforecasting
**come apart in the same person**.

Friedman (Dec 2014, oil prices): vague "surprises" in Venezuela, Iran, Russia —
unfalsifiable as forecast, valuable as **question prompt** flagging domains to examine.

Tetlock's hypothesis (explicit guess, not established): superforecasters and
superquestioners are often **not** the same people. Superb question generation tends
toward [hedgehog](hedgehogs-vs-foxes.md)-like incisiveness and Big-Idea confidence;
superb forecasting toward foxy eclecticism and uncertainty sensitivity. Opening
"Tom vs. Bill" framing is a false dichotomy — questioners sharpen forecasters'
foresight; forecasters supply calibrated answers that let questioners revise models.
Holy Grail: tournaments that **depolarize** policy debate and make it collectively
smarter.
