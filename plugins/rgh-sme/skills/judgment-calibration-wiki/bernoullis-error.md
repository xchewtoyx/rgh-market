---
type: concept
title: Bernoulli's Error
description: >
  Utility-of-wealth models fail because happiness depends on changes relative to
  reference points, not absolute wealth states alone.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 25"
---

Daniel Bernoulli's utility theory (1738) explained risk aversion via **diminishing
marginal utility of wealth** — psychological response inversely proportional to
initial wealth, utility logarithmic in wealth. Choices use probability-weighted
**utilities**, not dollar expected value. The framework stood almost 300 years and
underpins expected utility theory / rational-agent economics.

Kahneman: "The errors of a theory are rarely found in what it asserts explicitly;
they hide in what it ignores or tacitly assumes." Bernoulli assumes utility depends
only on **wealth states** — no **reference points**.

**Jack and Jill**: both have 5 million today; yesterday Jack had 1 million, Jill
9 million. Bernoulli says equal happiness; obviously Jack is elated, Jill despondent.
Happiness depends on **recent change relative to reference points**.

**Reference dependence is ubiquitous**: same sound loud or faint after whisper or
roar; gray patch dark or light by background. Predicting subjective experience
requires reference, not absolute value alone.

**Anthony and Betty**: current wealth 1M and 4M; both choose gamble (even chance
1M or 4M) vs. sure 2M. Expected wealth favors gamble (2.5M vs. 2M) — Bernoulli
predicts identical choices. Anthony frames sure 2M as doubling wealth (attractive);
Betty as losing half (awful). Same dollar outcome, opposite valence → different
choices — divergence Bernoulli cannot capture.

This gap motivated [prospect theory](loss-aversion-and-reference-points.md): value
of gains and losses relative to reference point, loss aversion, diminishing
sensitivity. Richard Thaler's **Econs vs. Humans**: Humans have [System 1](system-1-and-system-2.md)
limited by [WYSIATI](wysiati.md) — not fully rational, selfish, or stable in tastes.

Kahneman and Tversky studied choices by trusting their own immediate temptations
when confident others would agree — building prospect theory as descriptive psychophysics
(paralleling Fechner's logarithmic stimulus–sensation relation). See [framing effects](framing-effects.md),
[goals as reference points](goals-as-reference-points.md).
