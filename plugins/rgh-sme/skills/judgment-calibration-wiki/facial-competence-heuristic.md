type: concept
title: Facial Competence Heuristic
description: >
  Voters substitute fast face-based competence impressions for the harder
  question of actual job performance — with larger effect for uninformed viewers.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 8"
---

Alex Todorov's face-reading research: from a single glance people evaluate
**dominance** (potential threat — e.g., strong square chin) and **trustworthiness**
(facial expression — smile vs. frown). Accuracy is imperfect but confers survival
advantage even when imperfect.

Campaign photos rated for competence (exposures as short as one-tenth of a second,
no political context) predicted election outcomes in about **70%** of senator,
congressman, and governor races in the U.S.; replications in Finland, England,
Australia, Germany, and Mexico. Competence ratings predicted outcomes far better
than likability. Competence judgments combine strength (chin) and trustworthiness
(smile) — a strong chin plus slight confident smile reads as competent. **No evidence**
these facial features predict actual job performance.

This is a **judgment heuristic**: voters [substitute](substitution-heuristic.md) a
fast automatic assessment for the harder question of how good a candidate will
actually be. Follow-up research found the effect of facial competence on voting is
**about three times larger for politically uninformed, TV-heavy voters** than for
better-informed, lower-TV voters — the relative weight of System 1 in a judgment
varies by individual and population.

Analogous misfire: substituting "does he look like a strong leader?" for "will he
be a good president?" (President Harding). See [intuition as recognition](intuition-as-recognition.md),
[representativeness heuristic](representativeness-heuristic.md).
