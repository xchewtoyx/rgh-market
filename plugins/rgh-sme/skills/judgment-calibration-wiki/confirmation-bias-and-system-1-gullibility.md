---
type: concept
title: Confirmation Bias and System 1 Gullibility
description: >
  System 1 tends to believe by default; System 2 must actively doubt — and is
  often busy, lazy, or confirmatory in hypothesis testing.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 7"
---

[System 1](system-1-and-system-2.md) jumps to conclusions — efficient when likely
correct and mistakes are cheap; risky when unfamiliar, high-stakes, or time-pressed.

**Neglect of ambiguity**: ambiguous "bank" or middle character disambiguated by
context without tracking rejected alternatives — "a definite choice was made, but
you did not know it." Doubt requires holding incompatible interpretations; belongs
to System 2.

**Bias to believe** (Gilbert/Spinoza): understanding requires first attempting to
believe — construct the best interpretation that would make a statement true, then
unbelieve if needed. Disrupting System 2 (digit memory) impairs unbelieving —
"when System 2 is otherwise engaged, we will believe almost anything."

**Confirmation bias**: "Is Sam friendly?" retrieves different instances than "Is
Sam unfriendly?" System 2's default hypothesis test is positive-test strategy —
seek compatible data, not refutation (contrary to Popperian ideal). Picturing a
tsunami hitting California makes it feel more probable.

For forecasting: hold hypotheses provisionally; ask what would falsify them;
become motivated skeptics toward confirming evidence. Connect
[availability heuristic](availability-heuristic.md) and [WYSIATI](wysiati.md).

Pair with [independent judgments before discussion](independent-judgments-before-discussion.md).
