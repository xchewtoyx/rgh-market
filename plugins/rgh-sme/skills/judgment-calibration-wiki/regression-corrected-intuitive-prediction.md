type: concept
title: Regression-Corrected Intuitive Prediction
description: >
  Shrink nonregressive System 1 forecasts toward the category mean in proportion
  to evidence-outcome correlation.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman), ch. 18"
---

[System 1](system-1-and-system-2.md) intuitive predictions are **nonregressive**:
as extreme as the evidence, via [substitution](substitution-heuristic.md) and
intensity matching. Julie read at four → GPA predicted at the same percentile as
reading precocity, ignoring that prediction should be far more uncertain than
evaluation of current evidence.

Experimental confirmation: percentile judgments for "how impressive is this
description?" matched percentile predictions for GPA — even though prediction
depends on an entire freshman year and counselor accuracy. IDF officer selectors'
predicted school grades mirrored their evaluation grades one-for-one.

**Four-step correction** (when stakes are high):

1. Estimate the average outcome for the reference category (baseline).
2. Match your intuitive impression of the evidence.
3. Estimate correlation between evidence and outcome.
4. Move from baseline toward the intuitive match by that correlation fraction
   (e.g., 30% of the distance if *r* = .30).

Corrected predictions eliminate systematic optimism for strong initial signals
and pessimism for weak ones — [regression to the mean](regression-to-the-mean.md)
applied prospectively. Parallel to categorical prediction: combine
[base rates](base-rate-neglect.md) with case evidence via
[Bayesian updating](bayesian-updating-with-base-rates.md).

**When to stay extreme**: venture capitalists fear missing "the next Google" more
than overestimating failures; a rational actor sizes bets without inflating belief.
Many people need distorted extreme estimates to act — but should know the
self-indulgence.

**Kim vs. Jane hiring**: spectacular-but-thin Kim vs. steady Jane — the
[law of small numbers](law-of-small-numbers.md) means Kim's sample allows more
extreme luck, so regress Kim further toward the mean; corrected analysis may
favor Jane despite weaker gut impression.

Skilled intuition ([two conditions](two-conditions-for-trustworthy-expert-intuition.md))
differs from heuristic substitution; many real judgments blend both.
