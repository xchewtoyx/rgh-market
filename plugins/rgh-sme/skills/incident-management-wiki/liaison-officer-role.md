title: Liaison Officer Role
description: A dedicated communication relay between an incident commander and a separate group or bridge, keeping the primary technical channel free of cross-group traffic.

A **Liaison Officer (LNO)** acts as an aide to the [incident
commander](incident-command-system.md), carrying communications between the
technical response channel and another group or bridge — most commonly
between the IC and [Unified Command](unified-command.md), but any group of
responders can appoint one for intergroup communication. The framing that
captures the role: "think of the LNO as a deputy incident commander — the
LNO speaks on behalf of the IC but doesn't make decisions for the IC."

The mechanics are a repeating shuttle: the LNO stays fully current on the
incident (understanding it the way the IC does, not secondhand) so they can
deliver a [CAN report](can-report.md) on demand; they carry the IC's status
to the other group, carry that group's follow-up questions back to the
technical bridge, and relay the answer back up — freeing the IC to stay
focused on the primary problem-solving channel instead of splitting
attention across two conversations at once. An effective LNO needs their own
version of [command presence](command-presence.md): the confidence that
there is no question they can't answer or quickly get answered.

The role exists specifically to protect [span of
control](span-of-control.md) — without an LNO, either the IC has to
personally manage two channels at once, or the two channels merge and the
technical bridge gets flooded with strategic business discussion it has no
bandwidth to also process.
