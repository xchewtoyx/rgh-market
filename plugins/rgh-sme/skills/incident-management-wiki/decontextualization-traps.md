type: concept
title: Decontextualization Traps
description: Investigative errors that remove data from its chronological and operational context, leading to distorted assessments of operator performance.
sources:
  - title: "The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error'"
    resource: "The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error' (Sidney Dekker), ch. 3"
---

When analyzing incidents, investigators often strip data from its surrounding context, falling into three common decontextualization traps:
1. **Micro-matching**: Comparing fragments of operator performance line-by-line against written procedures or hindsight-obvious cues. This ignores the fact that procedures are often incomplete or conflict with real-time operational demands.
2. **Cherry-picking**: Extracting isolated quotes or actions across a timeline to support an a-priori label (such as "complacency" or "lack of SA") while ignoring the surrounding context.
3. **Shopping-bagging**: Collecting all the weak, ambiguous cues scattered across time and placing them in a single "shopping bag," treating them as if they were presented simultaneously as a clear, obvious warning.

To avoid these traps, investigators must ground their analysis in the chronological flow of events, reconstructing the [local rationality principle](local-rationality-principle.md) of the operators, and document their findings in structured, context-rich [learning reviews](learning-reviews.md).
