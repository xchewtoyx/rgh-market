title: Survivorship Bias in Safety
description: The bias of studying only reported incidents (survivable failures) while remaining blind to the unrecorded adaptations of normal work where catastrophic risk incubates.
sources:
  - title: "The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error'"
    resource: "The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error' (Sidney Dekker), ch. 7"
---

**Survivorship Bias in Safety** is illustrated by Abraham Wald's bomber paradox. Wald analyzed returning aircraft during WWII and showed that commanders were adding armor to the wings and fuselage where bullet holes were most frequent. Wald pointed out that returning planes had holes in areas where damage was *survivable*. Armor needed to be added to areas with *no* bullet holes (e.g., the cockpit/engines), because planes hit in those spots did not return.

In safety:
* Formally reported incidents are like the wings of returning planes: they represent survivable failures that the system absorbed.
* Catastrophic disasters reside in the unrecorded, unreported daily adaptations of **normal work** (the "engines" of the aircraft).

Hyper-focusing on reported near misses or minor incident metrics creates a decoy phenomenon. Organizations must look beyond reported data to study normal work, verify safety margins, cultivate [chronic unease](chronic-unease.md), and understand the true scope of the [incident-fatality-paradox](incident-fatality-paradox.md).
