title: Peacetime/Wartime Mode Shift
description: Framing incident response as a deliberate switch from consensus-seeking steady-state operation to time-optimized command, and the mindset shift that switch requires of responders.
sources:
  - title: "Incident Management for Operations"
    resource: "Incident Management for Operations (Rob Schnepp, Ron Vidal, Chris Hawley), ch. 2"
---

**Peacetime** is the steady-state, non-incident mode of operation — simply,
uptime. **Wartime** is the urgent, degraded mode triggered by an incident —
downtime. The framing matters because the two modes call for genuinely
different decision-making postures: peacetime culture is typically
consensus-based, optimizing for the best decision; wartime culture optimizes
for the best decision *in the shortest time*. Mature organizations maintain
two distinct mental (and sometimes literal) org charts — a peacetime
structure of business-unit heads, and a wartime structure with the
[incident commander](incident-command-system.md) at the top and responders
listed by function rather than rank.

Adopting this switch surfaces real friction: the talent an incident
commander needs usually sits in business units outside their normal
authority; outside vendors balance their own SLAs against the incident's
urgency; and executives who join a wartime bridge may bring peacetime rank
with them, which either intimidates lower-ranked responders or tempts the
executive to act as if seniority alone qualifies them to lead — command is a
function, not a rank, and peacetime seniority does not carry over into who
should run the incident.

The corresponding shift in individual mindset is between two responder
postures: **responders** are calm, trained, and treat the incident as an
expected part of the job — the fire-service framing is "fire is not an
emergency to the fire department, it's what we do" — while **reactors** are
emotional, unfocused, and treat the same event as a personal emergency with
no coordination or delegation. The goal of training and of an [incident
response process framework](incident-response-process-framework.md) that
scores well on "Optimized" is to make the responder posture the default,
not something people have to discover for the first time mid-incident.
