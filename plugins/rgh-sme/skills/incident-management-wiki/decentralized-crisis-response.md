title: Decentralized Crisis Response
description: The tendency of institutions to centralize decision-making under crisis, and why pushing authority to frontline responders (backed by central logistics) outperforms it when conditions are complex and fast-changing.
sources:
  - title: "The Checklist Manifesto"
    resource: "The Checklist Manifesto: How to Get Things Right (Atul Gawande), ch. 4"
---

Institutions instinctively centralize decision-making authority when a
crisis hits — it works for simple, predictable problems, but breaks down
under complex, nonroutine conditions because a central authority becomes an
information bottleneck and cannot react at the speed the situation demands.
The alternative, the **peripheral power paradigm**, pushes decision
authority out to frontline responders who have direct, current information,
while the center's job shifts to supplying logistics, resources, and
cross-team coordination rather than approving every decision.

Hurricane Katrina (2005) is the contrasting case study. FEMA's centralized
command structure became a bottleneck: field reports of levee breaches and
stranded refugees sat unread by a director relying on the formal hierarchy,
and bureaucratic approval requirements blocked willing state and private
aid shipments for days. Walmart's response, by contrast, was explicitly
decentralized — leadership told store managers to "make the best decision
you can with the information available... and do the right thing" — and
store managers acted autonomously (salvaging merchandise, supplying
hospitals, extending informal credit to first responders) while central
leadership scaled a call center and logistics pipeline to support them
without micromanaging their decisions. Walmart's supplies arrived a full
day before the federal response.

This is the same design tension the [incident command
system](incident-command-system.md) exists to resolve: an incident
commander centralizes coordination and communication, but effective ICS
practice delegates technical execution to specialists and explicitly
assigns ambiguous cross-team work rather than routing every decision back
through the IC. Building [chronic unease](chronic-unease.md) and the
[Three Cs](incident-command-system.md) into a response structure only pays
off if the center's role stays logistical and coordinating rather than
becoming a bottleneck the frontline has to wait on.
