title: Restorative Just Culture

A **Restorative Just Culture** shifts the focus of incident aftermath from retribution ("Who broke the rule and how should they be punished?") to learning and repair ("Who was hurt, what are their needs, and who takes responsibility to fix the system?"). 

Five pillars support a restorative just culture:
1. **Ask What is Responsible**: Look at tools, tasks, and system design instead of pointing at [human error as a symptom](human-error-as-symptom.md).
2. **Incorporate "Messy Details"**: Involve peer practitioners who understand real operational trade-offs in [learning reviews](learning-reviews.md).
3. **Adopt Restorative Justice**: Prioritize dialogue, healing, and rebuilding trust over retributive punishment.
4. **Forward-Looking Accountability**: Define accountability as the responsibility to build a safer system going forward, rather than a retrospective penalty.
5. **Provide Second Victim Support**: Establish formal support programs for traumatized practitioners involved in accidents.

A restorative just culture encourages open reporting of mistakes, providing the foundation of psychological safety required for effective [blameless postmortems](blameless-postmortems.md).
