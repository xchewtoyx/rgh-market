title: SME Conduct During Incidents
description: The expected behaviors a subject-matter expert follows when joining an incident response, so the incident commander can integrate them without wasted time.
sources:
  - title: "Incident Management for Operations"
    resource: "Incident Management for Operations (Rob Schnepp, Ron Vidal, Chris Hawley), ch. 2"
---

A **subject-matter expert (SME)** is a technical responder the [incident
commander](incident-command-system.md) draws on because no single person can
know the whole stack — even if the SME outranks the IC in the normal
organization chart. Good SME participation during a response follows a
short set of norms: arrive and respond as fast as possible; identify
yourself by name and function the moment you join the channel (a database
engineer says "this is Phillip, database," not silence — "being stealthy on
the incident is not healthy"); wait for a briefing from the IC rather than
acting independently; keep your environment quiet; speak specifically and
factually rather than vaguely; respect the IC's timeline instead of
negotiating it; answer direct questions directly; have a backup plan ready
in addition to the primary one; and ask for more help the moment you need
it rather than struggling silently.

The underlying principle is that incident boundaries dissolve normal
organizational boundaries — during response, "everyone refocuses and starts
marching to the incident commander's beat," with egos set aside. An SME who
skips identification, wanders off without announcing it, or tries to run
the response themselves undermines the [command
presence](command-presence.md) the IC is trying to establish, even if their
technical judgment is good — see [responder behavior
patterns](responder-behavior-patterns.md) for how an IC handles SMEs who
depart from these norms.
