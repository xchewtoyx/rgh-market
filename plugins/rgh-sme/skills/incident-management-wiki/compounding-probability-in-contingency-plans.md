A multi-step plan where each step individually looks safe can still be
fragile as a whole: six sequential steps at 90% success probability each
combine to only about 53% overall success (0.9⁶ ≈ 0.53), even though every
individual step "feels" safe in isolation. This is a specific reason to
distrust an elaborate, many-branch [incident action
plan](star-incident-action-plan.md) just because each branch looks
individually well-reasoned — the right response to irreducible uncertainty
is not to plan every contingency in exhaustive detail (most of those
branches will be overtaken by events before they matter), but to act
decisively on the highest-value next step while staying flexible, the way
effective historical commanders are observed to do.
