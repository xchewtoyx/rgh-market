type: concept
title: Transfer of Command
description: The protocol for handing the incident commander role (or any other function) to another qualified responder mid-incident without losing continuity.
sources:
  - title: "Incident Management for Operations"
    resource: "Incident Management for Operations (Rob Schnepp, Ron Vidal, Chris Hawley), ch. 4"
---

Command — or any other job function in an incident response — can be
transferred to another qualified responder at any point in a long-running
incident. Common triggers: a natural break point like a shift change or
global handoff, commander fatigue where a rested replacement genuinely
serves the incident better, a struggling commander with a more capable
person available, or a business reason requiring a more senior person to
take over.

The protocol has two parts, in order. First, the outgoing and incoming
[incident commanders](incident-command-system.md) hold a private
conversation off the main channel — a full [CAN
report](can-report.md) on current conditions and actions, plus anything
sensitive the outgoing commander hasn't raised publicly, such as an
unresolved personnel issue with a difficult [responder behavior
pattern](responder-behavior-patterns.md). Only after that handoff briefing
does the outgoing commander announce the transfer publicly and introduce the
incoming commander to the group — the public announcement should never be
the first the incoming commander hears about the incident's state. This
keeps continuity intact even though the specific person in the role changes,
consistent with naming positions by function rather than by individual (see
[span of control](span-of-control.md)).
