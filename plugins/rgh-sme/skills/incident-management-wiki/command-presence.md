title: Command Presence
description: The ability of an incident commander to convey calm authority under pressure, which sets the emotional tone the rest of the response mirrors.
sources:
  - title: "Incident Management for Operations"
    resource: "Incident Management for Operations (Rob Schnepp, Ron Vidal, Chris Hawley), ch. 3"
---

**Command presence** is the ability to convey that you are in charge and
capable of leading — hard to define precisely but easy to recognize, and
built through repeated practice leading incidents rather than from reading
about it. It rests on a distinction worth holding onto: things are managed,
people are led. The [incident command system](incident-command-system.md) is
a management framework; command presence is the leadership that operates it.

The incident commander's emotional state is contagious: a calm, focused IC
sets the atmosphere for every other responder, and an agitated one breeds
the same agitation back. The "duck on a smooth lake" image captures the
target state — serene on the surface, paddling furiously underneath. Good
ICs stay assertive without being emotionally attached to their own idea of
the fix, remaining genuinely open to being told they're wrong by a [subject
matter expert](sme-conduct-during-incidents.md) while still driving toward a
decision on a deadline.

A few traps catch new incident commanders specifically: micromanaging by
having an opinion on every technical detail, and drifting into schedule
creep by letting a group discuss a fix with no deadline attached. Command
presence also does not require domain expertise — a commander who is also
the deepest technical expert on the affected system should decide whether
to command the incident or step into an SME role and hand command to someone
else, because trying to do both at once degrades the commanding half of the
job. Nor does command presence require answering every question personally:
a good commander draws options out of SMEs, synthesizes them into a plan,
and drives it forward, rather than either dictating everything or waiting
for the group to reach unanimous agreement — see [seeking support, not
consensus](incident-bridge-communication-patterns.md) for how that plays out
in practice, and [STAR](star-incident-action-plan.md) for the structure
command presence gets applied to.
