Traditional safety management, often called the **Old View** or **Bad Apple Theory**, assumes that systems are inherently safe and that failures are caused by erratic, unreliable human operators. Under this view, the solution to failure is to reprimand operators, impose more rules, or automate human roles.

In contrast, the **New View** of safety science (or the systemic/resilience view) claims that:
* Systems are not inherently safe; safety is actively and continuously created by practitioners who negotiate goal conflicts and resource constraints.
* Human error is an **effect or symptom**, not a cause of failure. It marks the starting point of an investigation, not the conclusion.
* Human behavior is systematically connected to the tools, tasks, and context in which it occurs.

Demanding that people "try harder" or introducing more safety bureaucracy does not resolve the underlying systemic constraints. To understand failure, investigators must move beyond individual blame and reconstruct why actions made sense to practitioners in real time by applying the [local rationality principle](local-rationality-principle.md). This helps bridge the gap between [work-as-imagined vs work-as-done](work-as-imagined-vs-work-as-done.md) and fosters a [restorative just culture](restorative-just-culture.md).
