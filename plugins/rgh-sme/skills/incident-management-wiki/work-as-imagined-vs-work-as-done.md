A fundamental concept in resilience engineering is the division between two views of work:
* **Work-as-Imagined (WAI)**: Procedures, rules, and workflows designed by managers, planners, or developers who are distant from the actual work. WAI assumes that tasks occur linearly, predictably, and under full operator control.
* **Work-as-Done (WAD)**: The actual, adaptive practices of sharp-end operators negotiating real-time resource constraints, goal conflicts, interruptions, and tool failures.

Planners often assume safety comes from strict compliance with WAI, treating departures as violations. However, safety science shows that procedures are flexible resources for action, not rigid rules. Because systems are inherently incomplete and complex, safety is created by the operator's skill in judging when and how rules apply.

Suppressing WAD or forcing compliance drives adaptations underground, accelerating [practical drift](practical-drift.md) and creating [safety bureaucracy](safety-bureaucracy.md).
