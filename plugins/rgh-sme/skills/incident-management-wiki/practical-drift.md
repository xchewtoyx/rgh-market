**Practical Drift** (originally formulated by Scott Snook) describes the slow, steady decoupling between how a system was designed (its official rules and procedures) and how it is actually operated. 

Operational dynamics drive practical drift:
* **Goal Conflicts**: Organizations push for efficiency and speed. Operators learn that they must adapt procedures to meet production demands.
* **Normalization of Deviance**: Because minor workarounds and departures from rules usually succeed without bad outcomes, operators perceive them as safe. Over time, these workarounds become the accepted local norm.
* **Erosion of Safety Margins**: Because safety margins are unquantifiable in daily work, they are incrementally eroded until a minor perturbation triggers a catastrophic failure.

Practical drift illustrates the gap between [work-as-imagined vs work-as-done](work-as-imagined-vs-work-as-done.md). To prevent drift from leading to disaster, organizations must maintain [chronic unease](chronic-unease.md) and actively study normal work, avoiding [survivorship bias in safety](survivorship-bias-in-safety.md).

[Rasmussen's safety boundary model](rasmussen-safety-boundary-model.md) formalizes the same dynamic: constant pressure pushes a system's operating point toward invisible boundaries of acceptable performance, economic failure, and unacceptable workload, and drift is what happens when nothing makes the approaching boundary visible in time to correct course.
