"Uncertainty" is a catch-all word covering several genuinely different
problems, and naming the specific kind in play points to a different fix:

- **Missing information** — never collected, or collected but not
  locatable when needed.
- **Unreliable information** — the source's credibility is in question,
  independent of whether the information happens to be accurate.
- **Ambiguous or conflicting information** — genuinely supports more than
  one reasonable reading.
- **Complex information** — individually clear facts that are hard to
  integrate across too many dimensions at once.

More monitoring data does not straightforwardly reduce uncertainty — it
tends to relocate it, from "no one collected this" to "no one can find this
in the volume of what we collected." A useful expectation to set going in:
better tooling generally compresses how fast decisions get made, not how
much genuine uncertainty remains at decision time — treat that as normal
rather than a sign the tooling failed.
