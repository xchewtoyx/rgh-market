The **Local Rationality Principle** asserts that practitioners do not set out to make mistakes. Instead, their actions are rational from their point of view at the time. What they did made sense to them given their:
* **Goals**: Competing objectives (e.g., maintaining production speed vs. safety).
* **Attentional Focus**: Cues and information that were salient in the moment.
* **Knowledge**: Their training, experience, and mental models of the system.
* **Constraints**: Resource limits, time pressure, and organizational demands.

To investigate incidents from the New View, analysts must shift from an "outside-in" retrospective stance to an "inside-out" reconstruction of the situation. Instead of asking what people *failed* to do (which is biased by hindsight), investigators must ask why it made sense to do what they did. This requires rebuilding the practitioner's perspective inside the "tunnel" of the event timeline, avoiding [hindsight and outcome biases](hindsight-and-outcome-biases.md) and [decontextualization traps](decontextualization-traps.md) to find the systemic drivers of [human error as a symptom](human-error-as-symptom.md).
