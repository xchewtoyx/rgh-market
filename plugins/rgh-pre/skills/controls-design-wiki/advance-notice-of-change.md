type: concept
title: Advance Notice of Change
description: >
  Establish a standing norm of receiving advance warning before changes to
  routine, schedule, or expectations, since it's the surprise of a
  last-minute change that's disproportionately costly, not the change
  itself.

The same change (a moved meeting, a shifted deadline, a new procedure) costs
much less to absorb when there's lead time to prepare for it than when it
lands without warning. Rather than treating disruption tolerance as
something to individually build up, the control is upstream: request or
establish, as a standing expectation with collaborators or an employer,
that changes come with advance notice by default. This converts an
unpredictable stream of surprises into a predictable stream of
already-flagged upcoming changes.

The same principle applies to planning a shared event, not just to
changing an existing plan: giving attendees concrete details ahead of time
(who else will be there, expected noise level, whether food will be
served) turns an unknown, anxiety-generating event into a known one before
it starts, the same way advance warning turns an unknown change into a
known one.

Workplace-specific applications: written meeting agendas and pre-read
materials distributed ahead of time; interview questions shared in advance;
when requesting a chat, add context like "nothing bad" to prevent anxious
spiraling; before an unplanned call, send a heads-up with topic and confirm
availability. Each converts an ambush into a prepared encounter — the same
control as calendar lead time for schedule changes.
