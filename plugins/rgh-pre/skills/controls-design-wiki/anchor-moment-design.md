---
type: concept
title: Anchor Moment Design
description: >
  Attach a new habit to a precise trailing edge of an existing reliable
  routine — matched by location, frequency, and theme — rather than relying
  on memory or mistimed alarms.
sources:
  - title: "Tiny Habits: The Small Changes That Change Everything"
    resource: "Tiny Habits (BJ Fogg), ch. 5, Appendix"
---

No behavior happens without a prompt. In Fogg's model, motivation and ability
vary, but prompts are binary — you notice one or you don't. Three prompt types:

- **Person prompts** (memory, bodily urges) — unreliable for non-survival
  habits; avoid when designing controls.
- **Context prompts** (sticky notes, alarms, notifications) — work for one-off
  tasks but desensitize with overuse; best when ability is high at fire time.
- **Action prompts (Anchors)** — an existing reliable behavior that reminds
  you to do a new one; Fogg's preferred mechanism for habits.

**Recipe card format** (index card): "After I ___ , I will ___ . To wire the
habit into my brain, I will immediately: ___" — fields for Anchor, Tiny
Behavior, Celebration. Collect and revise cards over time; revision is
expected practice, not failure.

Introductory anchors: **Maui Habit** — after feet touch the floor, say "It's
going to be a great day" (variant: "— somehow" on hard days); teaches feeling
successful. **Flossing habit** — after setting down toothbrush, floss one
tooth only; more teeth are extra credit.

**Recipe format:** "After I [ANCHOR], I will [NEW HABIT]." Sharpen fuzzy
anchors to the **trailing edge** — the last specific action in a routine:
"after I pee" → "after I flush the toilet"; "after breakfast dishes in sink"
→ "after I turn off the tap." The sensory finality makes the cue concrete.

**Matching criteria** (priority order):
1. **Location** — anchor and new habit in the same place (kitchen habit needs
   kitchen anchor).
2. **Frequency** — daily habit needs a daily anchor.
3. **Theme** — shared purpose strengthens pairing (watering a plant → drinking
   water; coffee as productivity ritual → open to-do app, not journal).

Mornings offer the most consistent anchors; evenings cascade disruption more
easily. Treat recipes as editable cards — revise until the pairing sticks.
Overlaps with [habit stacking](habit-stacking.md) (Clear's "After [CURRENT
HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]") and [concrete implementation plan](concrete-implementation-plan.md)
for time/location plans without an existing routine anchor.

**Anchor-first design** reverses the usual flow: start from a reliable
existing routine and ask what tiny behavior could follow it, instead of
starting from a desired habit and hunting for a slot. Useful once several
habits are running and you are mining for more opportunities.

**Business / service anchor discovery:** survey people who already perform the
target behavior reliably ("When in your day do you take blood pressure?"),
cluster answers (after morning coffee, after feeding a pet), and offer new
users a menu of proven anchors instead of generic notifications. Context
prompts (texts, app alerts) degrade under overload; routine-embedded action
prompts scale better for recurring behaviors.
