---
type: concept
title: Importance-Urgency Triage Criteria
description: >
  Pre-define criteria for distinguishing importance from urgency, so each
  newly arriving request or task is filtered against a known standard
  instead of being reactively treated as urgent by default just because it
  just arrived.
sources:
  - title: "The Canary Code: A Guide to Neurodiversity, Dignity, and Intersectional Belonging at Work"
    resource: "The Canary Code (Ludmila N. Praslova, PhD), Chapter 7: Work Organization — Productivity and Purpose"
---

In a reactive default, whatever request arrives most recently or most
loudly tends to get treated as the priority, regardless of whether it
actually matters more than what was already in progress — a "tyranny of the
urgent" that erodes sustained work on anything important but not urgent.
Deciding the triage criteria in advance (what actually counts as important,
independent of how urgent it's framed) turns each new request into a
lookup against a standard, rather than a fresh negotiation every time
something shows up demanding immediate attention. At organizational scale,
**proactive prioritizing decision trees** — written criteria for what counts
as important versus merely urgent — give managers and teams a shared filter
before reactive priority-shifting consumes energy on work that gets shelved
mid-completion. This is the intake-side counterpart to a [demand triage
traffic-light system](demand-triage-traffic-light-system.md), which
pre-sorts existing commitments rather than incoming new ones.
