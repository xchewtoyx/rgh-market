type: concept
title: Habit Stacking
description: >
  Attach a new small practice to an already-established routine's existing
  cue (e.g. noticing body sensations while holding a morning cup of tea)
  instead of introducing it as a freestanding task that needs its own
  reminder and motivation.
sources:

A new practice introduced on its own has to compete for attention: it needs
its own reminder, its own moment of deciding to do it, its own motivation.
Attaching it instead to a moment that already reliably happens — a hand
already wrapped around a warm mug, an already-existing morning ritual —
borrows that moment's existing reliability instead of building a new one
from scratch. Where no suitable existing moment is available to attach to,
a scheduled alarm/timer used purely as an external prompt (as in
[fixed schedule for unreliable body cues](fixed-schedule-for-unreliable-body-cues.md))
is the fallback, though it lacks the "already happening anyway" advantage of
a true habit stack. [Environmental reminder placement](environmental-reminder-placement.md)
is a third option — anchoring the cue to a location instead of a time or an
existing routine.

**Formula:** "After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]." Examples: after
pouring morning coffee, meditate one minute; after taking off work shoes,
change into workout clothes; after sitting down to dinner, say one grateful
thing; after putting on running shoes, text someone your route and ETA.
Chain small habits into morning or evening stacks so one behavior's momentum
carries into the next — a positive version of acquisition spirals where one
purchase triggers the next.

**Conditional stacks** tie action to events rather than fixed daily sequences:
when you see stairs, take them; when you walk into a party, introduce yourself
to someone new; when you want to buy something over $100, wait twenty-four
hours; when you serve a meal, put vegetables on the plate first.

**Cue selection rules:** match cue frequency to desired habit frequency; don't
stack onto a cue that only happens on Mondays for a daily habit; avoid chaotic
moments (kids running around) when the stack needs calm focus. Brainstorm
current daily habits (wake, shower, brush teeth, brew coffee, start/end work,
dinner, bed) and daily events (sunrise, text arriving, song ending) to find
anchors.

**Specificity is essential** — vague cues fail. "When I take a break for lunch,
I will do ten push-ups" is ambiguous; "When I close my laptop for lunch, I will
do ten push-ups next to my desk" removes the ambiguity. Bind tightly: "After I
close the door," "After I brush my teeth," "After I sit down at the table."
