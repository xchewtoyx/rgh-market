---
type: concept
title: Tiered To-Do Structure
description: >
  Split a flat task list into named tiers (must, could, delegate, later) so
  today's view excludes deferred and non-owned work instead of accumulating
  shame from one impossible pile.
sources:
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Ellie Middleton), ch. 1"
---

A single undifferentiated to-do list mixes critical work, nice-to-haves,
other people's jobs, and someday items — then reads as failure when the
whole pile isn't cleared. Tiering separates obligations by status:

- **Must do** — actually needs doing now
- **Could do** — helpful but not critical
- **To dump or delegate** — not yours to carry, or cut entirely
- **To do later** — captured without posing as a current demand

A "later" bucket is especially important for [demand-softening schedule
design](demand-softening-schedule-design.md): items stay recorded without
becoming today's command. Pair with duration sorting (quick ticks under five
minutes, tasks five to thirty, projects over thirty) when the next decision
is which tier to tackle first — see [task batching](task-batching.md).

Keep the list physically or digitally visible ([environmental reminder
placement](environmental-reminder-placement.md)) when object impermanence makes
out-of-sight lists disappear entirely.
