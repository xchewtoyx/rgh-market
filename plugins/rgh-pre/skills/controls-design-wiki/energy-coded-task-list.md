---
type: concept
title: Energy-Coded Task List
description: >
  Label each task with how much energy it costs, then match today's list to
  available energy instead of assuming uniform capacity.
sources:
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Ellie Middleton), ch. 5"
---

Traditional to-do lists ask what can fit in time, not what fits current
capacity. Color-code or tag tasks green (minimal energy), yellow (moderate),
or red (high). On a drained day after a last-minute disruption, prioritize
greens; on a high-energy morning, tackle reds first.

Adjusts plans to fluctuating executive resources without abandoning
progress — a scheduling control aligned with [energy rhythm aligned
scheduling](energy-rhythm-aligned-scheduling.md) and [bare minimum day
goals](bare-minimum-day-goal.md) when even yellow tasks are too much.
