---
type: concept
title: Distress-Tolerance Kit
description: >
  Assemble a physical kit of tangible coping objects (stress balls, fidget
  toys, soothing lotion, a photo, a meaningful note, a favorite book) in
  advance, so healthy coping tools are within reach the moment they're
  needed rather than having to be located under distress.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 3: Emotional Self-Care"
---

Assembled ahead of time and kept in one accessible place, the kit removes
two steps that are hard to do well under acute distress: deciding what
might help, and gathering it from wherever it's normally scattered. The
"tolerance" framing is deliberate — the kit's job is to make waiting out an
urge or a wave of distress more bearable, not to resolve its underlying
cause. It's the physical-object counterpart to the "comforting tools" entry
in a [sensory safety plan](sensory-safety-plan.md), and can be built from
items already identified via [environmental sensory accommodation](environmental-sensory-accommodation.md).
