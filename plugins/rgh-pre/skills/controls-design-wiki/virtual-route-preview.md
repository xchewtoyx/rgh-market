---
type: concept
title: Virtual Route Preview
description: >
  Rehearse an unfamiliar location via street-level imagery before going, to
  convert abstract anxiety into a concrete, predictable mental map.
sources:
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Ellie Middleton), ch. 5"
  - title: "Knowing Why: Adult-Diagnosed Autistic People on Life and Autism"
    resource: "Knowing Why (Autistic Self Advocacy Network), ch. 4 (Stuart Neilson); ch. 10 (A.C. Buchanan)"
---

New environments demand heavy processing — layout, crowds, sensory load —
without a reference image to prepare against. Virtual walk-throughs (e.g.
street view along the route and at the destination) supply that reference on
your own timeline before full immersion.

Also surfaces triggers early (busy intersections, bright entrances) so you
can plan headphones, alternate routes, or [advance notice of change](advance-notice-of-change.md)
requests. Hand-held GPS or mapping apps that recalculate continuously can
replace a rigid "one right way" belief with reassurance that a wrong turn
is recoverable — reducing travel anxiety even for people who never drive.
Mobile GPS on a tablet or phone is especially valuable when poor spatial
awareness, difficulty processing verbal directions, or zoning out while
walking or on a bus would otherwise lead to getting badly — sometimes
dangerously — lost. Works with the detail-oriented brain rather than demanding improvised
navigation under load — a [proactive transition
preparation](proactive-transition-preparation.md) variant for spatial
unknowns, often combined with [waypoint sequencing without
timetable](waypoint-sequencing-without-timetable.md) for multi-stop trips.
