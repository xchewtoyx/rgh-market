  Arrange artificial external motivators at the point of performance when
  internally generated drive is unreliable, and sustain them rather than

Internally generated motivation, drive, and willpower can be as deficient as
working memory in executive-function disorders. Complaining about laziness
or withdrawing support to "let natural consequences teach a lesson" does not
repair that deficit — it removes the prosthesis while the underlying gap
remains.

External motivators (token economies, immediate small rewards, visible
progress markers) function like physical prosthetics: they substitute for a
capacity that is weak or absent at the critical moment. Distinguish two
purposes of behavior management: informational training for skills not yet
acquired (support can fade once natural contingencies take over) versus
motivational sustaining for execution deficits (support must continue,
because the problem is timing and self-initiation, not knowledge). Removing
external motivation after improvement predictably regresses performance to
baseline.

Students with ADHD often need more frequent, salient reinforcement than
typical contingency schedules provide. Classroom token economies — points
exchangeable for backup rewards, sometimes with response cost — are
institutional-scale motivational prostheses. Teacher cueing systems (timer
devices, bingo chips tracking praise frequency) externalize the adult's own
attention to reinforcement so praise stays contingent and immediate.

Place motivators at the [point of performance](point-of-performance-intervention.md),
not as distant promises. [Streak tracking](streak-tracking.md) and
[periodic small rewards during demanding tasks](self-regulation-resource-replenishment.md)
are examples of sustained motivational scaffolding rather than one-time
incentives.
