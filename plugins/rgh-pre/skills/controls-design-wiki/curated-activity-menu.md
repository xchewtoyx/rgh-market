title: Curated Activity Menu
description: >
  Pre-build a large menu of concrete, low-barrier activity options spanning
  many life domains so that choosing what to do never requires generating
  ideas from scratch, which is exactly the step that's hardest when
  motivation or energy is low.
sources:
  - title: Back from the Bluez

  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"

The scaffold is the existence and structure of the menu, not any particular
entry on it: a long, standing list of concrete options (a worked example ran
to 365 items) that a person can pick two or three from for the coming week,
rather than having to invent activities in the moment. Idea-generation is
front-loaded once, ahead of time, when energy/motivation is higher — and
removed from the moment of choosing, when it's most likely to stall the
whole process.

Design details that make the menu actually usable:
- Options should be low-barrier and mostly low-cost or no-cost, so
  availability of the option never depends on having spare money or a big
  block of free time.
- Options should span many domains (outdoors, sensory/self-care, social,
  creative, physical, spiritual/reflective, novelty) so there's usually
  something matching current context or mood, rather than one narrow
  category that may not fit today.
- Achievement/mastery items (e.g. clearing an inbox, paying a bill) should
  be mixed directly into the same list rather than kept in a separate
  catalogue — see [balanced fun-and-achievement activity scheduling](balanced-activity-scheduling.md)
  for why the mix matters.
- The menu should be treated as extensible — a person adds their own items
  over time rather than being limited to the starting set.

Once items are picked from the menu, [weekly activity schedule template](weekly-activity-schedule-template.md)
provides the structure to actually place them in the week.

The same logic applies to a narrower menu built for comfort/recovery rather
than general activity choice — e.g. a pre-compiled playlist of beloved
songs, a list of go-to shows, or a dedicated physical spot for favorite
books, kept ready for fast access after a high-strain period. This matters
even more there: the state that creates the need (overload, burnout) also
tends to cloud memory access, so the moment the list is needed is exactly
the moment generating or even recalling options from scratch is hardest —
the list has to already exist and be quick to find.
