---
type: concept
title: Prompt-Ability-Motivation Troubleshooting Order
description: >
  When a wanted behavior fails, diagnose prompt first, then ability, then
  motivation last — reversing the common instinct to reach for willpower or
  penalties before fixing missing cues or excess friction.
sources:
  - title: "Tiny Habits: The Small Changes That Change Everything"
    resource: "Tiny Habits (BJ Fogg), ch. 2, ch. 8, ch. 9"
---

Most people assume stalled behavior means low motivation and reach for
threats, guilt, or bigger goals. Fogg's troubleshooting order reverses that:

1. **Prompt** — Is there a reliable cue? If not, design one ([anchor moment
   design](anchor-moment-design.md), [environmental reminder
   placement](environmental-reminder-placement.md)). A missing reminder alone
   often explains "forgetting" (poster board on the way home, morning
   meditation without an anchor).
2. **Ability** — Can the person actually do it right now? Ask what makes it
   hard; run [ability chain diagnosis](ability-chain-diagnosis.md) and
   simplify or demonstrate the exact steps.
3. **Motivation** — Only after prompt and ability are sufficient. Penalties,
   dirty looks, and self-blame are motivation levers of last resort.

The same order applies when **stopping** unwanted habits in reverse:
remove or avoid the prompt first, then make the behavior harder via
[friction shaping](friction-shaping.md), then address motivation — see
[behavior change masterplan](behavior-change-masterplan.md).

For **group** change, segment who already wants the behavior ([change
segment dolphin turtle crab clam](change-segment-dolphin-turtle-crab-clam.md))
before trying to motivate the unmotivated. The [Fogg Behavior
Model](fogg-behavior-model.md) has no "lazy" axis — treat misses as design
data, not character verdicts.
