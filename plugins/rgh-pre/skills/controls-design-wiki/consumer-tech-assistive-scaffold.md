---
type: concept
title: Consumer Tech Assistive Scaffold
description: >
  Use mainstream consumer hardware and software off-label as functional
  accommodation — to-do apps, GPS, headphones, calendars — rather than
  waiting for formally labeled assistive technology and its gatekeeping.
sources:
  - title: "Knowing Why: Adult-Diagnosed Autistic People on Life and Autism"
    resource: "Knowing Why (Autistic Self Advocacy Network), ch. 10 (A.C. Buchanan)"
---

Much of what makes basic self-care and work possible for some neurodivergent
people is ordinary consumer technology — a to-do list app, calendar apps,
noise-cancelling headphones, GPS, instant messaging, time-tracking tools,
email automation — not products marketed as "assistive technology." Using
mainstream tools off-label avoids assistive-tech price markups, purchase
barriers (medical clearance, retailers reachable only through unstructured
email), and the stigma of visibly disabled equipment. The trade-off: a
"commonly used item" is harder to justify as a necessary accommodation in
formal settings, because others assume the use matches their own casual
use, and partial compensation can be misread as an unfair advantage or
laziness rather than levelling the field.

The design posture is experimental trial: hardware and software defaults
built for neurotypical users often fail, so test alternatives, use software
off-label, and keep what works — sometimes building custom tools. Once
something works, rely on it heavily; imperfect solutions that restore
function are still functionally necessary. Stack complementary scaffolds
([fixed schedules for unreliable body
cues](fixed-schedule-for-unreliable-body-cues.md) via reminder apps,
[virtual route preview](virtual-route-preview.md) and live GPS,
[environmental sensory accommodation](environmental-sensory-accommodation.md)
via headphones, [recurring task automation](recurring-task-automation.md)
via calendars and standing reminders) rather than treating any single app
as the whole system.

When a dedicated device avoids a known failure mode — e.g. a separate MP3
player because streaming from a phone drains a battery that is already hard
to remember to recharge — prefer the reliable dedicated tool over the
converged default. [Lower-load modality choice](lower-load-modality-choice.md)
applies the same principle to communication format (typing or instant
messaging instead of handwriting when dyspraxia limits pen use). Where
social pressure pushes the "typical" method (handwritten notes, eye contact
instead of phone use, hand-signing instead of a pain-reducing stamp), treat
the chosen scaffold as a [personal bright-line
rule](personal-bright-line-rule.md) or enforce it with a [pre-rehearsed
boundary script](pre-rehearsed-boundary-script.md).
