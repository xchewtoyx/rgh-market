---
type: concept
title: Freeze New Commitments
description: >
  When trying to exit an overloaded role or situation, stop accepting any
  new obligations immediately, even before the existing ones are resolved,
  rather than treating the exit as a single all-at-once move.
sources:
  - title: "Letters on Ethics: To Lucilius"
    resource: "Seneca, Letters on Ethics (trans. Graver and Long), Letter 22"
---

Trying to extract oneself from an overloaded role all at once — settling
every existing obligation before allowing the situation to end — sets an
open-ended precondition that can be delayed indefinitely, since there's
always one more task that "should" be finished first. A lower-cost first
move is available immediately: stop the inflow. Commit to taking on
nothing new, starting now, while still working through what's already
been accepted. This doesn't resolve the overload by itself, but it puts a
ceiling on it — the situation can only shrink from here rather than
continuing to grow while the exit is being planned — and it's a rule that
can be adopted unilaterally and instantly, unlike negotiating an exit from
existing commitments, which usually can't be.
