title: Prepared Appointment Notes
description: >
  Write down questions and concerns before an important conversation or
  appointment, and consider asking to record it, so recall of what to raise
  or what was said doesn't depend on memory under the pressure of the
  conversation itself.

Two separate memory failure points in a high-stakes conversation (e.g. a
medical or therapy appointment): forgetting what you meant to raise, and
forgetting afterward what was actually said or agreed. Both are addressable
in advance rather than relied on to work in the moment. Writing down
concerns and questions before the appointment offloads the first — the list
exists independent of whatever anxiety or pressure shows up during the
conversation. Asking to record the session (where appropriate and
permitted) offloads the second, so the content doesn't have to be retained
in memory in real time while also participating in the conversation.

The same control also works from the other side of a recurring meeting:
whoever organizes it can send discussion questions or an agenda in advance,
so the other party isn't put on the spot to generate answers live and has
time to prepare a considered response beforehand.

A third variant offloads memory onto a person rather than paper or a
recording: bringing a trusted companion to the appointment, briefed in
advance to help recall details afterward and provide support during it —
useful specifically because someone who knows the person well can catch
details the person themselves might miss or forget under the pressure of
the conversation, without needing the appointment to be recorded at all.
