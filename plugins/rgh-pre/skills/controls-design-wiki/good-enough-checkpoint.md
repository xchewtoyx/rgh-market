---
type: concept
title: Good-Enough Checkpoint
description: >
  Adopt a specific, pre-agreed checkpoint phrase ("is this good enough?")
  to deliberately interrupt perfectionistic iteration and grant permission
  to stop, since it can be hard to trust one's own judgment that a task is
  finished without an explicit, previously legitimized signal to stop.
sources:
  - title: "Neurodiversity For Dummies"
    resource: "Neurodiversity For Dummies (John Marble, Khushboo Chabria, Ranga Jayaraman), Chapter 21"
---

Iterative refinement can continue indefinitely once a task has no natural
finishing line of its own — each pass reveals another small improvement to
make. Without an external signal, stopping can feel like settling for
something substandard, even when the work has long since cleared the bar
that actually matters. Introducing a specific check-in question, agreed on
in advance as a legitimate way to end the loop, gives permission to stop
that doesn't have to be re-earned by internal judgment each time — asking
"is this good enough?" and answering it counts as a real decision, not a
concession.
