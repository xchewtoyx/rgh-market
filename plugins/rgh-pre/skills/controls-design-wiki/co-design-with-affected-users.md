---
type: concept
title: Co-Design With Affected Users
description: >
  When designing an accommodation or system meant to serve a particular
  population, involve members of that population directly in the design
  process itself, rather than designing on their behalf from assumptions
  and only collecting feedback afterward.
sources:
  - title: "Neurodiversity For Dummies"
    resource: "Neurodiversity For Dummies (John Marble, Khushboo Chabria, Ranga Jayaraman), Chapter 22"
---

Designs aimed at a population rarely fail from lack of good intent or
investment — they fail because the people designing them weren't part of
the population and didn't ask early enough who should actually be steering
the decisions. A need that seems obvious in hindsight, once named by
someone who has it, is often invisible beforehand to someone without it,
no matter how carefully they try to imagine their way into it. The
practical difference from ordinary user feedback is timing and authority:
co-design brings affected users in while the design is still being shaped,
with real influence over decisions, not just as reviewers validating
choices already made. The question to keep asking through the process is
"whose voices are actually guiding this."

Physical workspace design benefits from the same involvement: neurodivergent
employees consulted during office planning (neutral palettes, varied quiet
spaces, flexible lighting and sound by zone) produce environments that fit
diverse sensory needs instead of one-size-fits-all open plans that increase
stress and reduce real collaboration.
