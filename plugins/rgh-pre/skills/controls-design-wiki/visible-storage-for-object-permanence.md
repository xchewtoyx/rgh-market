Some people lose track of objects — and tasks tied to them — once they leave
the visual field, analogous to weak object permanence. Hiding everything for
a "presentable" home trades aesthetics for forgotten medication, keys, gym
bags, and paperwork.

Invert the default: keys on a hook by the door, daily meds on the kitchen
counter by the toaster, ongoing paperwork in a tray on the fridge, bags on
corridor hooks. Apples in a crisper drawer rotted unseen; moving them to a
large bowl on the kitchen counter made daily eating automatic — visibility
changed behavior without extra willpower. A visible "clean-clothes doom pile" can outperform a tidy
wardrobe when clothes put away in drawers are effectively forgotten —
visibility beats hidden order for items that must be used daily. The same
pattern explains hyperfixation supplies abandoned in closed cupboards
("hyperfixation graveyards") and produce spoiling in fridge drawers: out
of sight, out of mind is a structural problem, not a moral one. Putting
something in a "safe place" often means forgetting both the item and the
safe place; prefer a consistent visible home over rotating hiding spots.
Allow some surface messiness rather than insisting everything live behind
closed doors when the cost is forgotten essentials.

Visibility at the [point of performance](point-of-performance-intervention.md)
passively prompts action and saves active self-reminding. This is
[environmental reminder placement](environmental-reminder-placement.md)
applied to physical objects, not just sticky-note messages.

For items too sensitive to display, use a [master storage directory](master-storage-directory.md)
instead of hidden-only storage with no index.
