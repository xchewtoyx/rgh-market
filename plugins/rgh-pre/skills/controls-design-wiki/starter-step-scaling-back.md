---
type: concept
title: Starter Step Scaling Back
description: >
  Make a habit tiny via a small first move (starter step) or a shrunken
  baseline version (scaling back), so low-motivation days still count as
  full success and the habit survives disruption.
sources:
  - title: "Tiny Habits: The Small Changes That Change Everything"
    resource: "Tiny Habits (BJ Fogg), ch. 1, ch. 4, Appendix"
---

Two ways to make behaviors tiny:

**Starter step** — a small first move that becomes the whole requirement:
put on walking shoes (not the walk); turn on the stovetop burner (not full
breakfast). Don't raise the bar prematurely — stopping at the starter step
on a hard day keeps the habit alive.

**Scaling back** — shrink the full behavior: floss one tooth instead of all;
walk to the mailbox instead of a mile; meditate three breaths instead of ten
minutes.

Overlaps [two-minute rule](two-minute-rule.md) (under-two-minute gateway)
but adds Fogg's rule: on low days, the minimum tiny version is **full
success** — one tooth of flossing counts, illness and travel shouldn't
shame-collapse the habit. Starter steps also work for one-off procrastinated
tasks: write the phone number on a Post-it and stick it to the phone — that
alone can generate momentum to complete the call.

When motivation is high, add skills or tools; when low, compensate by
making the behavior tinier. See [ability chain diagnosis](ability-chain-diagnosis.md)
for finding which link to address first.

**Why tiny beats big** (Fogg): under-30-second actions bypass "who has the
time" rejection; no prerequisites means you start from current capacity;
low visibility reduces sabotage risk; small roots can grow; and tiny habits
do not depend on motivation spikes or willpower — [law of least
effort](law-of-least-effort.md) does the work. Celebrating tiny wins wires
habits through positive emotion rather than shame.
