title: Behavior Change Masterplan
description: >
  A phased algorithm for stopping unwanted habits: build change skills with
  new positive habits first, then unwind one easy specific habit at a time
  using prompt → ability → motivation levers, and only then swap in a
  replacement.
sources:
  - title: "Tiny Habits: The Small Changes That Change Everything"
    resource: "Tiny Habits (BJ Fogg), ch. 8"
---

"Breaking" a bad habit by force usually fails and invites self-blame. Fogg
treats unwanted habits as tangled ropes — untangle the **easiest knot**
first, not the deepest one. Mechanics match good habits: behavior is always
motivation, ability, and prompt converging.

**Habit difficulty tiers** (for choosing approach):
- **Uphill** — effortful to maintain, easy to stop (alarm response, gym).
- **Downhill** — easy to maintain, hard to stop (snooze, scrolling); masterplan
  target.
- **Freefall** — needs professional help (substance dependence); Tiny Habits
  is not a substitute for treatment.

## Phase 1 — Build skills before attacking the painful habit

Create new positive habits in low-stakes domains (tidiness, relationships,
creativity) to learn the Skills of Change and accumulate evidence you can
change. Identity shifts from new habits can crowd out bad ones without a
direct fight (stairs instead of elevator, racquetball replacing TV).

## Phase 2 — Stop one specific habit

Replace abstract labels ("stop eating junk food") with a **swarm of specific
behaviors**; pick the easiest tangle to unwind first. Work levers in
[prompt-ability-motivation troubleshooting
order](prompt-ability-motivation-troubleshooting-order.md):

**Prompt** (strongest stop lever):
- *Remove* — one-time environment redesign ([cue exposure
  reduction](cue-exposure-reduction.md)): delete app, hide snacks, airplane
  mode.
- *Avoid* — skip places, people, or media that trigger the habit.
- *Ignore* — resist the cue by willpower; weakest because repetition depletes.

**Ability** — make the habit harder via the Ability Chain in reverse
([friction shaping](friction-shaping.md)): increase time (no ice cream at
home), money (taxes, charges), physical effort (TV in storage, no desk
chair), mental effort (hard password, calorie logging), or conflict with a
valued routine (morning surf displacing late-night scrolling).

**Motivation** (last resort for downhill habits):
- Reduce upstream drivers (meditate before leaving work; eat before a party;
  grayscale phone screen).
- Fogg does **not** recommend demotivators (public pledges, financial
  penalties, misery visualizations) — they invite an internal fight and guilt.
  Contrast [habit contract](habit-contract.md), which Clear positions for
  breaking habits; Fogg reserves social punishment for when structural
  levers fail.

If nothing stops the habit, **scale back the target** with [starter step
scaling back](starter-step-scaling-back.md): shorter trial period, fewer
instances, lower intensity — success at small scale opens bigger change.

**Omission habits** (lateness, procrastination) are behaviors *not*
happening — loop back to Phase 1 and add starter habits until the gap
closes.

## Phase 3 — Swap in a replacement (last)

Only after Phases 1–2. The replacement must beat the old habit on both
motivation and ability — a "golden" behavior, not merely virtuous. Remap
the old prompt to the new response and celebrate immediately. If the swap
fails, rehearse the new response at the old cue, or adjust all four levers
(raise new M/A, lower old M/A). Still stuck: try another replacement, a
short trial, or return to Phase 1.

The masterplan is an **ordered algorithm**, not a technique list — traditional
tools (motivational interviewing, accountability partners) slot inside it
rather than replacing it.
