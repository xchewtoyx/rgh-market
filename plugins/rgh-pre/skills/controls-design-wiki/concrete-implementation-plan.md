---
type: concept
title: Concrete Implementation Plan
description: >
  Convert a vague intention to act into a specific plan naming when, where,
  and how the action will happen, since vague intentions rarely convert
  into action on their own.
sources:
  - title: "Mindset: The New Psychology of Success"
    resource: "Mindset (Carol S. Dweck), Chapter 8: Changing Mindsets"
  - title: "Atomic Habits: Tiny Changes, Remarkable Results"
    resource: "Atomic Habits (James Clear), ch. 5"
---

A vague intention ("I'll deal with it tomorrow," "I should call them at some
point") leaves the actual moment of execution to be decided later, under
whatever conditions happen to exist at the time — which is exactly when
avoidance, distraction, or forgetting are most likely to win. Naming the
concrete when, where, and how in advance ("tomorrow during my break, I'll
get a cup of tea, close my office door, and make the call") removes that
later decision point entirely: there's no longer a question of *whether*
now is the right moment, only whether the already-specified moment has
arrived. This works whether or not the underlying task feels resolved
emotionally — the plan can be executed on schedule independent of how
motivated or confident the person feels when the moment comes.

**Implementation intention formula:** "I will [BEHAVIOR] at [TIME] in
[LOCATION]." In a 2001 exercise study, 91% of people who wrote a specific
when/where plan exercised at least once per week versus 35–38% who only
tracked workouts or read motivational material — many people lack clarity,
not motivation. Hundreds of studies show the same pattern for flu shots,
recycling, studying, sleep, smoking cessation, and voter turnout when plans
force route and time answers upfront.

Vague goals ("eat healthier," "write more") leave execution to chance. A
concrete plan removes the in-the-moment decision — follow the predetermined
plan instead of waiting for inspiration. Specificity also protects against
derailment: it's easier to say no to distractions when the habit already
has a time and space to live in the world.

This is a one-off counterpart to [habit stacking](habit-stacking.md) and
[anchor moment design](anchor-moment-design.md): those anchor a *recurring*
practice to an existing routine; an implementation intention anchors to
clock and place when no reliable routine exists yet. [Habit stacking](habit-stacking.md)
is the variant that pairs with a current habit instead of abstract time.
