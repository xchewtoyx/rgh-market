---
type: concept
title: Balanced Fun-and-Achievement Activity Scheduling
description: >
  When designing a recurring activity schedule, deliberately include both
  pleasurable/fun activities and achievement/mastery tasks rather than only
  one type, since each supplies a different missing positive feeling.
sources:
  - title: Back from the Bluez
    resource: "Back from the Bluez (Nathan, Rees, Lim, Correia), Module 2: Behavioural Strategies for Managing Depression — 'Behavioural Activation: Fun & Achievement'"
---

A schedule built only from pleasant/fun activities leaves out the sense of
purpose, mastery, and being "in control" that comes from achievement-type
tasks (e.g. paying off a bill, ironing, a household chore) — and vice versa.
Some activities supply both at once (e.g. making the bed gives pleasure from
a tidy result and achievement from an improved environment). The design rule
is to check, when populating a schedule, that it isn't skewed to only one
category — mix fun and achievement items deliberately rather than defaulting
to whichever comes to mind first.

Each scheduled item should still be sized so it's actually achievable —
see [graded task assignment](graded-task-assignment.md) for how to size an
achievement-type task down to something startable. A [curated activity menu](curated-activity-menu.md)
that mixes fun and achievement items together is one way to make this
balance the default rather than something to remember each time; a
[weekly activity schedule template](weekly-activity-schedule-template.md)
is the structure to place the chosen mix into.
