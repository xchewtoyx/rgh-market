---
type: concept
title: Eliminate Structural Overhead Costs
description: >
  When a fixed, seemingly-unavoidable overhead (like a commute) consumes a
  large share of daily capacity before any actual goal is addressed, look
  for ways to remove or reduce the overhead itself rather than only trying
  to fit everything else around what's left.
sources:
  - title: "The Canary Code: A Guide to Neurodiversity, Dignity, and Intersectional Belonging at Work"
    resource: "The Canary Code (Ludmila N. Praslova, PhD), Chapter 6: Work Environment — Flexible Spaces"
---

Some costs are paid regardless of what else is scheduled that day —
commuting is the clearest example: it takes a fixed bite out of the day's
total capacity before the day's actual purpose has even started, and for
someone with a limited total daily energy budget that bite can be
disproportionately large. Treating this overhead as fixed leads to trying
to squeeze the same amount of everything else into a smaller remaining
budget. The more effective move, where available, is to attack the
overhead directly — e.g. removing a commute via remote work — rather than
repeatedly re-optimizing around a cost that didn't need to be paid at all.
