title: Pre-Stocked Low-Energy Fallback
description: >
  Prepare and keep on hand a low-effort, no-decisions-required fallback
  option (e.g. frozen meals) reserved specifically for low-capacity days, so
  the routine has a degraded-but-working path instead of collapsing when
  energy or executive function is depleted.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 2: Physical Self-Care"
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"

---

A routine designed only for the person's typical-capacity day has no answer
for a low-capacity day — the whole routine (e.g. cooking a meal from
scratch) either gets forced through at high cost or skipped entirely. The
fix is to prepare, in advance and while capacity allows, a fallback version
that requires no planning, shopping, or multi-step execution in the moment —
e.g. stocking pre-prepared or frozen meals, or a meal-subscription service,
specifically earmarked for low-energy days rather than treated as the
everyday default. Include a "no-cooking cooking" tier — instant noodles,
microwave rice, steam-in-bag vegetables — for days when even reheating a
full frozen meal feels like too many steps. Meal-delivery kits reduce
shopping and decision paralysis when affordable. Pre-chopped frozen
ingredients and pre-prepped components lower the executive load on
better-capacity days when cooking does happen.

This complements [default option preselection](default-option-preselection.md):
where that pattern narrows everyday choice, the fallback is a separate,
even-lower-effort tier reserved for when even a narrowed choice is too much.
