type: concept
title: Time-Boxed Goal Setting
description: >
  Set a task's goal as a fixed duration to spend on it (e.g. "read for 5
  minutes") rather than a fixed amount to complete, so success is reachable
  even when initiation itself is the main barrier.
sources:
  - title: Back from the Bluez
    resource: "Back from the Bluez (Nathan, Rees, Lim, Correia), Module 2: Behavioural Strategies for Managing Depression — 'Start Simple'"
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 6: Professional Self-Care"
---

Amount-based goals ("read a chapter", "clear the flower bed") stay open-ended
until a variable amount of work is finished, so the size of the commitment is
unknown at the moment of starting — which is exactly when the activation
barrier is highest. Time-boxed goals fix the commitment instead: "read for 5
minutes" or "weed the garden for 10 minutes." The goal is met the moment the
timer ends, regardless of how much was produced, which makes the goal
achievable on demand and removes the need to estimate or negotiate scope
before starting.

This is one way to size the small step in [graded task assignment](graded-task-assignment.md)
when a task has no natural small unit to peel off (unlike, say, "wash five
plates," which is already amount-based and small). A
[scheduled worry period](scheduled-worry-period.md) uses the same
fixed-duration mechanism in the opposite direction — containing an unwanted
activity rather than making a wanted one easier to start.

Known elsewhere as the **five-minute rule**: commit to just five minutes on
a stalled or dreaded task, paired explicitly with permission to stop once
the timer ends. Stating the permission to stop matters — without it, "just
five minutes" is easily read as a trick to get started on the full task
regardless, which undermines the trust that makes the small commitment
easy to make in the first place. In practice, starting is usually the
hardest part, and continuing past the five minutes often happens anyway
once underway — but that has to be allowed to be a free choice, not the
hidden goal, or the technique stops working.
