---
type: concept
title: Comfort Edge Scaling
description: >
  As a habit becomes automatic, test slightly past your current comfort
  edge when motivated and scale back to baseline when sick, tired, or
  frustrated — flexibility in both directions protects the habit.
sources:
  - title: "Tiny Habits: The Small Changes That Change Everything"
    resource: "Tiny Habits (BJ Fogg), ch. 7"
---

Once a tiny habit is consistent, you naturally reach for more. **Comfort
edge scaling** is sensing that edge and nudging past it without triggering
pain or frustration that weakens the habit.

Example: two push-ups with focus on form → after a week, improved form on
the last rep signals readiness to add more → repeat over days and weeks. On
low-motivation days, baseline two still counts as keeping the habit alive.

Guidelines:

- Don't pressure past the tiniest version when sick, tired, or unmotivated;
  raise the bar again later ([starter step scaling
  back](starter-step-scaling-back.md)).
- Don't cap yourself when motivated — let motivation set the ceiling that
  day.
- If you overreach, celebrate extra hard to offset frustration and protect
  the habit ([Fogg behavior design maxims](fogg-behavior-design-maxims.md)).
- **Frustration, pain, avoidance** → too much too fast; scale back.
- **Boredom** → may be time to ramp up.

The edge moves non-linearly over time but the practical question is always
"what's the edge right now?" Part of Behavior Design step 7: troubleshoot,
iterate, and expand.
