---
type: concept
title: Energy-Rhythm-Aligned Scheduling
description: >
  Schedule demanding tasks during a person's natural high-focus windows
  (which may not match a conventional 9-to-5 pattern) rather than forcing
  focus-intensive work into a fixed external time slot that fights against
  it.
sources:
  - title: "The Canary Code: A Guide to Neurodiversity, Dignity, and Intersectional Belonging at Work"
    resource: "The Canary Code (Ludmila N. Praslova, PhD), Chapter 7: Work Organization — Productivity and Purpose"
---

Forcing demanding work into a schedule that ignores when a person's
attention is actually available (e.g. a distraction-heavy daytime block for
someone who focuses best in quiet early mornings or late evenings) produces
a bad trade: the fixed slot gets filled with low-quality effort, and the
person's real high-capacity hours end up spent compensating for it outside
the "official" schedule instead of counting toward it. Aligning the
schedule to the actual rhythm — doing demanding work when focus is
naturally available, lighter work when it isn't — makes the scheduled hours
themselves productive, rather than treating the schedule as fixed and
expecting the person's energy to conform to it.
