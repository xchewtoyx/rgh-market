---
type: concept
title: Group Behavior Design Process
description: >
  A seven-step method for changing household or team habits together —
  clarify aspiration, generate options, pick golden behaviors, ease ability,
  anchor prompts, celebrate success, and troubleshoot in P→A→M order.
sources:
  - title: "Tiny Habits: The Small Changes That Change Everything"
    resource: "Tiny Habits (BJ Fogg), ch. 9"
---

Treat the **household or team** as the unit of change, not only the
individual. One skilled designer can transform a group if they start close
to home (family, work team) and follow the [Fogg behavior design
maxims](fogg-behavior-design-maxims.md).

**Ringleader** — teaches Behavior Design openly (Swarm of Behaviors, Focus
Mapping). **Ninja** — applies the same steps covertly (break tasks into tiny
steps, celebrate wins without announcing the method). Both use the same
seven steps:

1. **Clarify aspiration together** — check buy-in on the shared outcome.
2. **Explore behavior options** — Magic Wanding or swarm worksheet; Ninja
   asks "What do you want to happen? If we had magical powers…?"
3. **Match golden behaviors** — group Focus Mapping on impact then
   feasibility; pick 1–5 upper-right behaviors. Ninja shortcut: "Which
   option can we realistically get ourselves to do?"
4. **Make it easy** — group [ability chain diagnosis](ability-chain-diagnosis.md);
   redesign checklists, environment, or lower-exertion alternatives.
5. **Prompt** — each person picks an [anchor moment](anchor-moment-design.md)
   ("After I return from lunch, I will…"); learn from members already
   succeeding and scale their prompt.
6. **Celebrate** — teach authentic celebration, supply Shine as authority
   figure, or let the group affirm each other. Use **Feedback Power Zone**
   feedback (domain they care about + area of uncertainty) only for sincere
   praise — critique in that zone lands as devastating rejection.
7. **Troubleshoot** — expect first-fit misses; follow [prompt-ability-motivation
   troubleshooting order](prompt-ability-motivation-troubleshooting-order.md);
   if stuck, pick a different golden behavior. Segment the group with
   [change segment dolphin turtle crab clam](change-segment-dolphin-turtle-crab-clam.md).

**Queen B Solution** for mandatory behaviors: keep the required action but
reframe around an aspiration that resonates with unmotivated segments
(homework tied to keeping pace with classmates, not grades).

**Recipe Maker** (shift work): list existing shift anchors in one column,
candidate tiny stress-reducers in another; each person mixes recipes (after
I park, three breaths; after I dock the computer, sip water). Nurses who
never drank water on 12-hour shifts built hydration without "try harder."

**Tiny-step escalation** (parenting example): ask only for step one of a
three-step chore; celebrate specifically; add steps after weeks of success —
[starter step scaling back](starter-step-scaling-back.md) applied to someone
else's behavior. A/B test variants ("your way five days, my way five days")
teaches Skills of Change, not just one fix.

Maxim: **change leads to change** — start where people already want to begin;
confidence opens further change.
