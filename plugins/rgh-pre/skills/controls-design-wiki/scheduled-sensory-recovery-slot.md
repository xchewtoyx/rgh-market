---
type: concept
title: Scheduled Sensory Recovery Slot
description: >
  Block recurring calendar time to deliberately meet sensory avoiding or
  seeking needs, rather than hoping recovery happens spontaneously after
  overload builds.
sources:
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Ellie Middleton), ch. 2"
---

Sensory needs vary day to day — after a busy office with overlapping sound
and bright light, recovery may mean a quiet dark room or white noise; on
another day the same person may seek pressure or stimulating input. Without
a scheduled slot, honoring those needs competes with everything else and
often loses, producing recurring burnout cycles despite other self-care
efforts.

Put sensory recovery on the calendar like any other commitment: a regular
check-in to ask which senses need avoiding or seeking right now, then act
on the answer (dim lighting, ear defenders, weighted blanket, crunchy
snacks, etc.). This is proactive [daily sensory load
budgeting](daily-sensory-load-budget.md) at the recovery end — planned
downshift after known high-load periods, not only capping input upstream.

Works alongside a [sensory safety plan](sensory-safety-plan.md) for acute
overwhelm; the scheduled slot prevents the steady accumulation that makes
acute plans necessary.
