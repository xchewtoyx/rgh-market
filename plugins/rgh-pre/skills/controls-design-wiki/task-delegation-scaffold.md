type: concept
title: Task Delegation Scaffold
description: >
  Hand off recurring or standardized tasks to another person or service when
  holding them in working memory is unsustainable, without treating support as
  failure.
sources:
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Ellie Middleton), ch. 8"
  - title: "Discipline Is Destiny: The Power of Self-Control"
    resource: "Discipline Is Destiny (Ryan Holiday), ch. 12"
---

Delegation removes items from the mental stack: personal assistants sending
pre-written replies, cleaners, laundry services, a partner choosing meals to
break decision-freeze, family collecting prescriptions. Independence norms
make this feel like cheating; the control frame is matching task ownership
to available capacity.

Pair with [recurring task automation](recurring-task-automation.md) for
machine-handled recurrence and with [what-by-when-and-why
instructions](what-by-when-and-why-instructions.md) when handing work to
others so delegated tasks do not bounce back for missing context.

Delegation is also **discipline against over-control**: high standards and
genuine enjoyment of a task are not reasons to keep doing everything
yourself — refusing to pass work others could do better, or to hire help
when affordable, wears down capacity and blocks comparative advantage.
Identify legacy tasks that can be automated or reassigned; know what an hour
of your time is worth; consider a buffer person who says "no" on your
behalf when willpower alone cannot protect focus. The payoff is time and
mental space for work only you can do — reflection, connection, and
strategic thinking that delegation purchases deliberately rather than hoping
to "gut it out."
