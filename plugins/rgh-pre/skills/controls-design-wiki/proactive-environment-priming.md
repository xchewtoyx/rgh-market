---
type: concept
title: Proactive Environment Priming
description: >
  After finishing an activity, reset the space and stage materials for the
  next wanted action so future effort starts at zero instead of hunting,
  deciding, or cleaning up first.
sources:
  - title: "Atomic Habits: Tiny Changes, Remarkable Results"
    resource: "Atomic Habits (James Clear), ch. 12"
  - title: "Tiny Habits: The Small Changes That Change Everything"
    resource: "Tiny Habits (BJ Fogg), ch. 7"
---

"Proactive laziness": reset the room after each use — return the remote,
arrange pillows after TV, wipe the toilet while the shower warms, throw away
car trash — not to clean up the last action but to prepare the next one.
Presort greeting cards by occasion so sending one is nearly frictionless
versus repeatedly missing the window to buy a card.

Practical priming: lay out skillet, spray, and plates the night before for
a healthy breakfast; keep drawing tools on the desk within reach; set out
workout clothes, shoes, gym bag, and water bottle ahead of time; batch-chop
fruits and vegetables on weekends for easy snacking during the week.

For habit-specific friction, ask proactively "How can I make this new habit
easier?" (move floss next to the toothbrush) and reactively "What is making
this hard?" (cucumbers unwashed or eaten by others). Question tradition
about where things "should" live — vitamins needn't stay in the kitchen.
Invest in gear that removes friction (cold-weather biking kit). At scale,
see [superfridge food environment](superfridge-food-environment.md) for
whole-fridge eating redesign.

This is [friction shaping](friction-shaping.md) applied across time —
investing a few seconds now removes minutes of friction later. Pair with
[environmental reminder placement](environmental-reminder-placement.md)
when the primed items also serve as visible cues, and [visible storage for
object permanence](visible-storage-for-object-permanence.md) when items must
stay in view to be used.
