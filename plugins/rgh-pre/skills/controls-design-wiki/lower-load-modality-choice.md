title: Lower-Load Modality Choice
description: >
  For a recurring interaction, choose the format with lower ongoing sensory
  or logistical cost (e.g. teletherapy over in-person visits) rather than
  defaulting to the standard modality when a lighter one serves the same
  purpose.

The default format for a given kind of interaction (an in-person clinic
visit, an in-person meeting) isn't always the lowest-cost way to get the
same outcome. When the interaction recurs regularly, the format itself is
worth treating as a design variable: a remote or otherwise lower-sensory
option (e.g. teletherapy for routine check-ins) can deliver the same
function at a lower standing cost, freeing capacity that would otherwise be
spent on travel, waiting rooms, or unfamiliar sensory environments every
time.

The same choice can be offered from the provider side of an interaction —
e.g. letting someone choose written versus face-to-face feedback delivery —
rather than only exercised by the person managing their own recurring
commitments.

For **motor and sensory load on output**, the same choice applies: typing,
dictation, or instant messaging instead of handwriting when pen use is
painful or limited to a sentence or two; a custom signature stamp at book
signings or similar events when hand-signing would cause pain or stress.
Others may frame the lighter modality as less personal or attentive; the
control is to pre-select the sustainable format and hold it under social
pressure rather than re-litigating each time.

The variable being chosen isn't limited to remote-vs-in-person: for social
connection specifically, "parallel play" — doing a solo activity
side-by-side with someone doing their own, rather than direct
face-to-face interaction — is a lower-demand format that still delivers
genuine connection, and can itself be conducted remotely (e.g. a video
call where both people work on separate projects simultaneously) for a
further reduction in cost.
