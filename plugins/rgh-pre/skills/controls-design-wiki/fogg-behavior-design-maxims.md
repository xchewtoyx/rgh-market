title: Fogg Behavior Design Maxims
description: >
  Two non-negotiable design requirements for durable behavior change —
  align interventions with what the person already wants to do, and engineer
  an immediate felt sense of success after each instance.
sources:
  - title: "Tiny Habits: The Small Changes That Change Everything"
    resource: "Tiny Habits (BJ Fogg), Appendix: Three Hundred Recipes for Tiny Habits"
---

Fogg distills a decade of behavior-design work into two maxims he treats as
hard requirements, not preferences: without both, a product, service, or
personal change plan will not engage people over time.

**Maxim 1 — Help people do what they already want to do.** Design around
existing aspiration and motivation rather than trying to install desire the
person does not have. In practice this means picking [golden
behaviors](golden-behavior-selection.md) that match stated goals, using
[preference-aligned routine design](preference-aligned-routine-design.md)
to choose variants that fit, and segmenting groups so effort goes first to
those who already want the behavior ([change segment dolphin turtle crab
clam](change-segment-dolphin-turtle-crab-clam.md)).

**Maxim 2 — Help people feel successful.** Wire the behavior by engineering
an immediate felt win after each instance — authentic celebration, Shine,
or specific affirmation — not deferred reward or abstract progress metrics.
Without that success signal, repetition does not consolidate into habit even
when the behavior technically occurred. Group settings use step 6 of the
[group behavior design process](group-behavior-design-process.md); solo
design pairs tiny achievable steps ([starter step scaling
back](starter-step-scaling-back.md)) with deliberate post-behavior
celebration.

Both maxims apply equally to designing for others and to designing change in
one's own life.
