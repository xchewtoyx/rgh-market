type: concept
title: Wind-Down Buffer
description: >
  Fix a cutoff time before a target routine (e.g. sleep) after which
  stimulating activities are pre-emptively swapped for calming ones, instead
  of deciding in the moment whether to stop.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 2: Physical Self-Care"
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition (Rhena Branch, Rob Willson), ch. 15"
---

Rather than judging in the moment whether a stimulating activity (e.g.
screens) should stop, set a fixed cutoff (e.g. no screens for at least an
hour before bed) and have a pre-decided substitute list ready for that
window — reading, gentle stretching, slow breathing, visualisation,
progressive muscle relaxation. Start winding-down rituals about 45 minutes
before bedtime and put the phone down. Limit blue light: night mode on
devices, blue-light-filter glasses, avoid screens in bed.

The decision of what counts as "too stimulating, this late" is made once, in advance, rather than re-litigated
each night when judgement and willpower are already lower. The buffer
complements [bed stimulus control](bed-stimulus-control.md) and a
[consistent sensory cue for routine transition](consistent-sensory-cue-for-routine-transition.md):
together they turn the run-up to sleep into a scripted sequence rather than
a series of in-the-moment choices.
