---
type: concept
title: Permissions-Not-Demands Work Frame
description: >
  Replace "should" self-talk that triggers shutdown with an explicit list of
  permitted tangential activities that count as legitimate parts of work.
sources:
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Ellie Middleton), ch. 7"
---

Perfectionism attaches moral weight to rigid rules (write every morning,
only "sellable" output counts). "I should be working right now" becomes a
demand that blocks even wanted tasks. List the shoulds, trace where each
came from, then write parallel permissions — daydream, read, tidy the desk,
talk to a friend about the project, follow current interest.

Tangential activities are often necessary incubation, not procrastination.
Treating them as allowed small wins restores dopamine and lowers initiation
threshold. Post the permissions list prominently; pick one item to re-enter
when stuck. Complements [demand-softening schedule design](demand-softening-schedule-design.md)
for calendar structure and [bare minimum day goals](bare-minimum-day-goal.md)
for daily scope.
