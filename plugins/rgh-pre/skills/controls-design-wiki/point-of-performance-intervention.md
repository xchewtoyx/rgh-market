---
type: concept
title: Point-of-Performance Intervention
description: >
  Design controls to act at the exact time and place a target behavior should
  occur, because interventions distant from that moment lose effectiveness.
sources:
  - title: "Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder: A Handbook for Diagnosis and Treatment, 4th Edition"
    resource: "Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (Russell A. Barkley, Ed.), ch. 16"
---

Executive-function deficits are primarily performance failures, not knowledge
gaps — people often know what to do but fail to deploy that knowledge at the
critical moment. Controls must therefore be engineered into the natural
setting where the behavior should happen, not delivered remotely and hoped
to transfer. The further an intervention sits from that moment in space or
time, the less it will change what actually occurs.

Weekly counseling or skill instruction without concurrent environmental
accommodations at key performance points is unlikely to succeed on its own.
Instrumental retraining (for example working-memory drills) may temporarily
raise the odds of self-regulation, but gains tend to decay unless the
support is periodically renewed. Pair remote skill-building with
[environmental reminder placement](environmental-reminder-placement.md),
[motivational prostheses](motivational-prosthesis.md), and other scaffolds
inserted where the behavior must run.
