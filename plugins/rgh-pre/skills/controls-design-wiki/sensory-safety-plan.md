type: concept
title: Sensory Safety Plan
description: >
  A written plan, prepared in advance while calm, listing calming strategies,
  safe spaces, comforting tools, and an exit strategy for sensory overwhelm —
  so none of that has to be worked out in the moment when overwhelmed.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 1: Cultivating Sensory Safety; Chapter 3: Emotional Self-Care"
---

The plan is written after identifying personal sensory triggers and
preferences, and covers four things ahead of time:
- calming strategies that are known to work
- safe spaces to retreat to
- comforting tools to have on hand (e.g. a weighted blanket, headphones, a
  favourite repeating song)
- an exit strategy for situations that can't be avoided but become
  overwhelming

The value of the plan is that it's authored while capacity is available,
before overload hits — overload itself degrades the ability to generate a
plan, so trying to improvise calming strategies, locate a safe space, or
decide on an exit route during overwhelm is exactly the wrong time to be
doing that thinking for the first time. Having the plan already written
turns "what do I do now" into "follow the plan."

A [pre-scripted accommodation request](pre-scripted-accommodation-request.md)
is a companion tool: a ready phrase for asking others to change the
environment, so the plan's calming strategies don't depend on first
composing a request under stress. A [distress-tolerance kit](distress-tolerance-kit.md)
is a physical-object version of the "comforting tools" item, assembled in
advance for quick reach. The plan also covers anticipatory dread of a known
upcoming high-sensory situation (e.g. a mall or dentist trip), not just
overwhelm that arrives unexpectedly; where the issue is the cumulative
number of such situations in a day rather than any single one, a
[daily sensory load budget](daily-sensory-load-budget.md) is the relevant
control instead.
