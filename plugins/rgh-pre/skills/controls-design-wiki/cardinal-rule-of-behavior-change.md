title: Cardinal Rule of Behavior Change
description: >
  What is immediately rewarded is repeated; what is immediately punished is
  avoided — the fourth law completes the habit loop by making the ending
  satisfying enough to drive the next repetition.
sources:
  - title: "Atomic Habits: Tiny Changes, Remarkable Results"
    resource: "Atomic Habits (James Clear), ch. 15"
---

**What is immediately rewarded is repeated. What is immediately punished is
avoided.** Positive emotions cultivate habits; negative emotions destroy
them. The first three laws in [four laws of behavior change](four-laws-of-behavior-change.md)
(obvious, attractive, easy) raise the odds a behavior happens *this time*;
the fourth (satisfying) raises the odds it happens *next time* — it closes
the loop.

Brains evolved in an **immediate-return environment** (eat now, sleep now,
avoid predator now) but modern life is mostly **delayed-return** (paychecks,
fitness, retirement). **Time inconsistency** means Present You overweights
certain reward now versus possible reward later — instant gratification
usually wins at the decisive moment. Good habits often cost in the present
and pay later; bad habits pay now and cost later. Design must work *with*
that bias: add immediate pleasure to habits that pay off long-term, and
immediate cost to ones that don't. [Temporal gap bridging](temporal-gap-bridging.md)
applies the same logic at the task-design level.

To get a habit to stick you need to feel successful even in a small way —
the ending of an experience is remembered most, so engineer a satisfying
finish via [habit completion reinforcement](habit-completion-reinforcement.md).
Extrinsic incentives can start a habit; identity sustains it once intrinsic
rewards (energy, mood, competence) accumulate — but early on "it's all
sacrifice," so immediate reinforcement bridges until delayed rewards arrive.
Pair with [Fogg behavior design maxims](fogg-behavior-design-maxims.md)
(maxim 2: help people feel successful).
