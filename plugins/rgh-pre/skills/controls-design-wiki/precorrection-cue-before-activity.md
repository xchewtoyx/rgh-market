---
type: concept
title: Precorrection Cue Before Activity
description: >
  State expected behavior immediately before a transition or activity
  starts, not only after a violation, so the cue is present at the point of
  performance.
sources:
  - title: "Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder: A Handbook for Diagnosis and Treatment, 4th Edition"
    resource: "Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (Russell A. Barkley, Ed.), ch. 24"
---

Correcting behavior after the fact teaches little when inhibition and
working memory are weak — the relevant moment has passed. Remind students
or yourself of the expected routine *before* entering high-risk transitions
(hallways, cafeteria, new task) and at activity onset, using brief verbal,
visual, or written cues.

Classroom pattern: posted schedules and rules, brisk instructional pace with
precorrection signals, explicitly taught transitions. Personal pattern:
state the next step aloud before starting, or read a [visual
checklist](visual-checklist-for-multistep-routines.md) entry before each
segment. Same logic as [environmental reminder placement](environmental-reminder-placement.md)
timed to precede the behavior, not follow failure.
