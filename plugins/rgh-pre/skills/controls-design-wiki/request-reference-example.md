---
type: concept
title: Request a Reference Example
description: >
  When facing a vague or open-ended task, ask for a concrete example,
  template, or prior similar work product before starting, rather than
  beginning from an ambiguous specification.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 6: Professional Self-Care"
---

Vague instructions leave the shape of the finished output, and the order of
steps to produce it, to be guessed at — which invites costly trial-and-error
before landing on what was actually wanted. Asking for a concrete example or
template up front (last year's version of the document, a step-by-step
illustration) supplies both a visual target and an implicit sequencing of
steps in one request, and heads off the rework that comes from guessing
wrong. Framing the ask around a stated need ("I work best from a concrete
example") makes it a reasonable, repeatable request rather than a one-off
favor.
