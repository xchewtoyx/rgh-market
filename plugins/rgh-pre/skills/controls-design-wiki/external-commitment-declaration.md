---
type: concept
title: External Commitment Declaration
description: >
  State a goal or deadline out loud to another person in advance, so
  follow-through is backed by external accountability rather than relying
  on internal willpower alone to sustain it.
sources:
  - title: "Neurodiversity For Dummies"
    resource: "Neurodiversity For Dummies (John Marble, Khushboo Chabria, Ranga Jayaraman), Chapter 12"
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Ellie Middleton), ch. 1"
---

Declaring a commitment to someone else ("I commit to finishing this by
Friday," told to a manager; sharing a personal goal with a friend) converts
an internal intention into an external one with a witness. The follow-
through no longer depends solely on the person's own in-the-moment
motivation holding up — there's now a second person who knows the
commitment exists and against whom slipping is visible, which raises the
cost of not following through independent of how motivated the person
feels on any given day. This is cheap to set up (a single sentence, said
once, ahead of the work) relative to the ongoing willpower it substitutes
for. Sharing a morning to-do list with an accountability partner applies
the same mechanism to daily task follow-through; teaching material to
someone else forces organization and retrieval that passive review does not
— another form of externalizing the commitment to remember and execute.
