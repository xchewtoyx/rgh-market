type: concept
title: Behavior Crafting Pace
description: >
  Start with about three super-easy habits and add roughly three per month
  unless your interests pull a different pace — learn by doing rather than
  perfecting one hard habit before starting another.
sources:
  - title: "Tiny Habits: The Small Changes That Change Everything"
    resource: "Tiny Habits (BJ Fogg), ch. 7"
---

**Behavior crafting** includes knowing how many new habits to start and when
to add more. Neither "master one hard piece before anything else" nor
haphazard addition works well — pace is learned by doing.

Default if undecided: **three super-easy habits** to start, then about
**three new habits per month**. Embrace variety (exercise, food,
productivity; different starter steps) to learn faster what fits. Stay
flexible — drop habits that no longer serve and add new ones as preferences
shift.

Use felt optimism and forward movement as the signal you're on track, not a
rigid formula. When a tiny habit cascades naturally (burner on → full meal
prep → counter clearing), multiplication can feel like "swimming with the
current" ([habit growth versus
multiplication](habit-growth-vs-multiplication.md)). Pair with [success
momentum](success-momentum.md) — many small wins beat one ambitious habit
that stalls.
