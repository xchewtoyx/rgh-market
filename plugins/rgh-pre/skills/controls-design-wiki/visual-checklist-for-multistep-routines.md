type: concept
title: Visual Checklist for Multi-Step Routines
description: >
  Externalize the steps of a multi-step self-care or task routine as a
  visual/written checklist, so the steps don't have to be held in memory or
  re-derived each time the routine is performed.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 2: Physical Self-Care"
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Ellie Middleton), ch. 6"
---

Multi-step routines (personal hygiene, meal prep, morning tasks) are prone
to stalling when the required steps have to be recalled from memory under
low executive-functioning capacity — a step gets skipped, or the whole
sequence feels too effortful to start because its shape is unclear. A
written or visual checklist (a dedicated app, a printed card, step-by-step
pictorial instructions for a recipe) removes that recall burden: the routine
becomes "do what the list says next" rather than "remember what comes
next." Break the routine into the same small, achievable steps used in
[graded task assignment](graded-task-assignment.md) before putting them on
the checklist, so each listed item is itself easy to start.

Visual week planners, photo sequences of planned activities, or symbol cards
for routines help adults who never received childhood accommodations — same
mechanism as classroom visual schedules, applied to [weekly brain dump
planning](weekly-brain-dump-planning.md) or a single troublesome morning or
bedtime chain.
