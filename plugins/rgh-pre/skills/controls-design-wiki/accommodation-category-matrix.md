title: Accommodation Category Matrix
description: >
  Classify supports by type — setting, presentation, scheduling, timing,
  response, and comfort — when designing educational or workplace
  accommodations for executive-function deficits.
sources:
  - title: "Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder: A Handbook for Diagnosis and Treatment, 4th Edition"
    resource: "Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (Russell A. Barkley, Ed.), ch. 33"
---

ADHD-related accommodation needs cluster around self-control, motivation,
and time management. The matrix crosses accommodation *type* with *setting*
(classroom, high-stakes test, workplace):

- **Setting** — private testing space, preferred seating, sound control,
  organized filing, administrative support
- **Presentation** — read-aloud, computer delivery, broken-into-parts tasks,
  clear oral plus written instructions
- **Scheduling** — modified deadlines and assignment dates
- **Timing** — extended time, extra breaks, shorter tasks, flexible hours
- **Response** — dictation, voice recognition, spell-check, movement-permitted
  tests
- **Comfort** — food, drink, headphones, special seating during long sessions
  (reduces discomfort without claiming to fix impairment)

Workplace "other" supports include frequent feedback, reminders and prompts,
novelty in tasks, job fit, weekly supervision, digital assistants,
incentives, and buddy systems. Use the matrix to catalogue gaps before
picking controls — many common accommodations lack strong ADHD-specific
evidence but remain reasonable engineering responses to known deficit
patterns. Pair with [motivational prostheses](motivational-prosthesis.md)
and [point-of-performance intervention](point-of-performance-intervention.md).
