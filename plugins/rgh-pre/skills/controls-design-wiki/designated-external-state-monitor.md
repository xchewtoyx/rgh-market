---
type: concept
title: Designated External State Monitor
description: >
  Arrange in advance for a trusted person to watch for signs of a state
  shift (mood escalation, overload, fatigue) that the person themselves is
  unlikely to notice in time, since the state being monitored for is often
  the same one that degrades self-monitoring.
sources:
  - title: "Neurodiversity For Dummies"
    resource: "Neurodiversity For Dummies (John Marble, Khushboo Chabria, Ranga Jayaraman), Chapter 9"
---

Some state shifts are hardest to notice from the inside precisely because
the shift itself impairs the faculty that would notice it — elevated mood
can outrun a person's own read on how keyed-up they are, and general
overload dulls the very self-awareness needed to catch overload early.
Rather than relying on in-the-moment self-monitoring, arrange the
monitoring externally, ahead of time: a trusted person agrees in advance to
watch for known warning signs and to say something (a plain check-in, a
prearranged phrase) when they see them, and the person being monitored
agrees in advance to treat that flag as credible rather than argued with in
the moment it's raised. Pairing this with planned downtime after
known-triggering events (e.g. a social gathering) catches the same drift on
a schedule, independent of whether anyone notices anything in real time.

This is the same "self-monitoring is unreliable during the state that
needs catching" logic behind [hyperfocus bumper
rails](hyperfocus-bumper-rails.md), generalized from a single bounded
activity session to an ongoing state (mood, energy) tracked over longer
stretches of time rather than one sitting.
