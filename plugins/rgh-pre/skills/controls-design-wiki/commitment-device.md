---
type: concept
title: Commitment Device
description: >
  A choice made now that locks in better behavior later by making the wanted
  path easier to continue than to abandon, or the unwanted path harder to
  start than to skip.
sources:
  - title: "Atomic Habits: Tiny Changes, Remarkable Results"
    resource: "Atomic Habits (James Clear), ch. 14"
---

Victor Hugo had his assistant lock away all his clothes except a shawl so he
couldn't leave home and was forced to finish *The Hunchback of Notre Dame*
ahead of deadline. Other examples: buy food in individual packages instead of
bulk; voluntarily add yourself to a casino ban list; leave your wallet at home
before weigh-in; use an outlet timer that cuts router power at 10 p.m.; ask the
waiter to box half your meal before it's served; pay ahead for a yoga class or
schedule a consulting call so bailing costs effort and money.

Mechanism: change the task so getting out of the good habit takes more work
than starting it, or starting the bad habit takes more work than skipping it.
Pair with [friction shaping](friction-shaping.md) for environmental friction
and [future-linked precommitment](future-linked-precommitment.md) when the
binding should activate on a future trigger rather than immediately.

Automation can go further than commitment — making good behavior inevitable
and bad behavior impractical. See [recurring task automation](recurring-task-automation.md)
and [one-time setup lock-in](one-time-setup-lock-in.md).
