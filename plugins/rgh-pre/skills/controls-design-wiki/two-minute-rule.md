---
type: concept
title: Two-Minute Rule
description: >
  Scale a new habit's first step to under two minutes so starting is easy,
  establishing the behavior before optimizing its duration or intensity.
sources:
  - title: "Atomic Habits: Tiny Changes, Remarkable Results"
    resource: "Atomic Habits (James Clear), ch. 13"
---

Nearly any habit scales down: read one page, take out the yoga mat, open
notes, fold one pair of socks, tie running shoes. The first two minutes must
be easy; what follows can be hard. Goal: master showing up — "standardize
before you optimize." Ritualizing the beginning (warm-up, creative ritual,
power-down routine) makes deep focus easier to enter.

Strong variant: do the activity for two minutes and **stop on purpose** — go
to the gym but leave after two minutes; meditate two minutes only. A reader
lost 100+ pounds by going daily but forbidding stays over five minutes; Greg
McKeown journaled by always writing less than he felt like. Hemingway: stop
when going good. One push-up beats no exercise; showing up five days running
casts votes for the identity you want.

Maps to a ladder: put on shoes → walk 10 min → 10,000 steps → 5K → marathon;
write one sentence → paragraph → article → book. Any large goal traces to a
two-minute behavior. This is a [decisive moment](decisive-moment.md) control
and pairs with [habit shaping](habit-shaping.md) for staged expansion.
