type: concept
title: Structured Engagement Check-In
description: >
  Pair systematic monitoring of engagement indicators with a trusted
  mentor who problem-solves when metrics cross risk thresholds, instead of
  waiting for crisis to surface disengagement.
sources:
  - title: "Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder: A Handbook for Diagnosis and Treatment, 4th Edition"
    resource: "Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder: A Handbook for Diagnosis and Treatment, 4th ed. (Russell A. Barkley, Ed.), ch. 6"
---

Dropout and disengagement often show up first in **leading indicators**
(attendance, homework completion, classroom participation) before grades
collapse. A structured check-in scaffold assigns a school-based coach to
collect those metrics regularly, build a genuine relationship, and **jointly
problem-solve** when thresholds are exceeded — the Check and Connect
pattern.

Success depends on the relationship, not the spreadsheet: the coach is a
consistent human anchor, not only a data collector. For adolescents, a
brief daily mentor check-in (homeroom teacher, counselor, or learning-
support teacher) keeps organization and assignment tracking from falling
through cracks when adult supervision drops after elementary school. Scale
frequency to need — one end-of-day check-in may suffice for mild cases;
more severe cases need additional coaching blocks.

Complements [daily report card scaffold](daily-report-card-scaffold.md)
(home–school feedback with rewards) and [task delegation
scaffold](task-delegation-scaffold.md) when the mentor role is partly
about offloading tracking the student cannot sustain alone.
