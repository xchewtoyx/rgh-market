---
type: concept
title: Outcome-Based Work Accountability
description: >
  Evaluate and reward work by quality and value delivered, not hours
  visible, meeting count, or productivity theater inputs.
sources:
  - title: "The Canary Code: A Guide to Neurodiversity, Dignity, and Intersectional Belonging at Work"
    resource: "The Canary Code (Ludmila N. Praslova, PhD), ch. 7"
---

Systems that measure bodies in seats, words typed, or meetings attended
reward looking productive over being productive — productivity theater that
displaces deep work. True output requires environments where people can do
their best work and take pride in results.

Design controls around outcomes: flexible schedules and workloads when the
deliverable is what matters; [protected uninterrupted time](protected-uninterrupted-time.md)
for deep work requiring multi-hour focus; [explicit async response
norms](explicit-async-response-norms.md) so shallow-message pressure does not
fragment concentration. Accountability ties to results and long-term value,
not personal style or presenteeism — including visual presenteeism on video
calls when audio-only suffices.

Organizational controls that reinforce outcomes over theater: base rewards
on quality and long-term value rather than visible activity; train managers
in deep-work protection and **energy management** (structuring work around
individual productivity cycles, not only clock time); use transparent async
systems that record contributions without invasive monitoring; and shift
evaluation away from face time to measurable progress on defined goals —
the main antidote to **proximity bias** that penalizes remote workers despite
often higher output. A results-only work environment, where supervisors
manage work rather than bodies-in-seats, is the extreme form when roles
support it.
