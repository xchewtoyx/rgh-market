---
type: concept
title: Risk Policy Advance Rule
description: >
  Adopt a class-wide decision rule in advance for a category of risky
  choices, so each individual instance is judged against the long-run policy
  rather than re-decided under loss aversion in the moment.
sources:
  - title: "Thinking, Fast and Slow"
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 33"
---

A **risk policy** is a pre-commitment for a whole class of decisions — e.g.
"always take the highest deductible on insurance," "never buy extended
warranties," "evaluate the investment portfolio only quarterly." Each single
loss still stings, but it is absorbed inside a broad frame: the policy is
financially advantageous over many repetitions, so one bad outcome does not
trigger a fresh narrow-framed renegotiation.

The same broad-frame move counters opposing biases: the outside view on
planning dampens exaggerated optimism; a risk policy dampens exaggerated
caution from loss aversion on marginal bets. Organizations often need
someone with portfolio-level authority to accept risks that every division
head rejects individually — the CEO who wants all divisions to take their
positive-expected-value bets because aggregation reduces overall risk.

Personal analogues: checking investments on a fixed schedule instead of
daily (limiting loss-averse reactions to noise); pre-deciding how to treat
sunk costs ("would I start this project today at zero?") before escalation
pressure hits. Differs from a [personal bright-line rule](personal-bright-line-rule.md)
in targeting **classes of probabilistic tradeoffs** rather than fixed
conduct; complements [importance-urgency triage criteria](importance-urgency-triage-criteria.md)
for reactive intake versus advance financial or risk posture.
