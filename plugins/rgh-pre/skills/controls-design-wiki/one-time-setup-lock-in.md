---
type: concept
title: One-Time Setup Lock-In
description: >
  A single upfront environmental or systems change that pays off repeatedly
  without further decisions — mattress, automatic savings, app deletion,
  smaller plates — automating future behavior by default.
sources:
  - title: "Atomic Habits: Tiny Changes, Remarkable Results"
    resource: "Atomic Habits (James Clear), ch. 14"
---

Some controls need one decision, then keep working: water filter, smaller
plates, good mattress, blackout curtains, TV removed from bedroom, unsubscribe
from email lists, turn off notifications, delete games and social apps, enroll
in automatic savings, set up automatic bill pay, cut cable, get vaccinated,
buy supportive shoes or chair.

John Henry Patterson's cash register automatically locked cash after each
transaction — employee theft vanished overnight because stealing became
impractical, not because employees were retrained. The best way to break a bad
habit is often to make it impractical: increase friction until the option to
act is removed entirely.

Technology extends one-time setup to infrequent behaviors technology can
"remember" — automatic prescription refills, wage-deduction retirement savings,
meal delivery, website blockers. Whitehead: civilization advances by extending
operations we can perform without thinking about them. Watch the downside:
autoplay and zero-effort delivery can automate unwanted impulses too; when
acting on a desire takes no effort, you slip from easy task to easy task
instead of harder, more rewarding work. Weekly password resets on social
accounts (or a friend holding passwords until Friday) can break the easy
default and reveal how little you actually need the habit.
