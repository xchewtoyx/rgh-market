---
type: concept
title: Structured Scoring Checklist
description: >
  Replace inconsistent holistic judgment with a fixed checklist of a few
  observable variables scored on a simple scale, then combined by a
  formula — often with equal weights — for reliable repeated decisions.
sources:
  - title: "Thinking, Fast and Slow"
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 22"
---

In low-validity environments (hiring interviews, clinical triage, quality
checks), experts are often **less accurate and less consistent** than simple
formulas built from the same cues — partly because humans re-weight factors
cleverly case by case, partly because the same rater disagrees with
themselves on repeat. The control is to **discipline measurement before
judgment**: pick a small set of relevant traits, assess each via a fixed
sequence of factual questions or observable checks, score each on a simple
scale (e.g. 0–2), then combine scores with a transparent rule — equal
weights often work as well as regression-optimized weights and are more
robust.

Worked examples:
- **Apgar score**: five newborn variables (heart rate, respiration, reflex,
  muscle tone, color), each 0/1/2; sum ≥8 means good shape, ≤4 triggers
  immediate intervention — a shared standard where practitioners previously
  attended to different cues.
- **Structured interviews**: six personality dimensions each rated 1–5 from
  standardized factual questions before any global "gut feel" score; the
  formula outperforms unstructured impression, though a final holistic
  rating can add value **after** the structured pass.

Override the formula only for rare decisive facts (Meehl's "broken-leg rule"),
not routine case-specific cleverness. Pair with [running decision log](running-decision-log.md)
when the checklist supports ongoing tracking rather than one-shot selection.
