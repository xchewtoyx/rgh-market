---
type: concept
title: Joint Sensory Needs Negotiation
description: >
  When two people who share a recurring context have opposing sensory or
  physical preferences, treat the mismatch as a joint design problem to
  solve together rather than framing either person's need as the flaw to
  be corrected.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 5: Social Self-Care"
---

Two people sharing a recurring context — a household, a relationship — can
have genuinely opposing sensory or physical needs: one touch-seeking, one
touch-avoidant; one wants firm pressure, the other soft; one is
sound-sensitive, the other craves movement and noise. The load-reducing
move is reframing: this is a mismatch to design around jointly, not a
character flaw in either party ("too loud," "too sensitive"). Process:
each person identifies their own needs explicitly, the pair pinpoints where
those needs actually clash (many needs won't), and they develop a solution
for each clash point together — treated as iterative, revisited and
adjusted over time rather than solved once and left fixed.

This differs from [co-design with affected users](co-design-with-affected-users.md)
in scale and stakes: co-design brings a population's members into
designing a system meant to serve them; this is a two-person negotiation
over a shared recurring environment, where both parties are simultaneously
the affected users and the designers.
