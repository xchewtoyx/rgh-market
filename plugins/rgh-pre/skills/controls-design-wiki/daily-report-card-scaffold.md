A Daily Report Card (DRC) — also called a classroom challenge card — lists
a small set of target behaviors (typically one to five) with **quantifiable
ratings** (points, frequency counts, or defined descriptors like "poor =
more than three rule violations"). The teacher rates **throughout the day**
by period or subject, not with a single end-of-day summary, so an early
morning slip can still be recovered later the same day.

Design controls that make the scaffold durable:
- Include at least one behavior the person already performs reliably so early
  success is possible.
- Start with a partial day (e.g. one hour) when problem rates are high, then
  expand coverage as behavior improves.
- Fade from daily to weekly or monthly reports only after sustained success;
  many students benefit from weekly reports maintained year-round.
- At home: parent inspects the card immediately on return, discusses
  positives first, keeps negatives brief and neutral, has the child plan
  how to continue positives tomorrow, then delivers a **privilege-based
  reward** (daily rewards matter most; weekly rewards supplement).

The card leverages home reinforcers that classrooms cannot match, targets
homework return and organization alongside in-class conduct, and requires
less teacher time than full classroom token systems. Treat a missing or
forged card the same as a bad report. For adolescents, evolve the same
structure into a **behavioral contract** with the teen co-designing goals,
rewards, and timeframes — see [smart goal specification](smart-goal-specification.md)
for measurable target wording.
