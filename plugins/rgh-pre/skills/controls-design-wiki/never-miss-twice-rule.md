---
type: concept
title: Never Miss Twice Rule
description: >
  After one missed instance of a habit, get back on track immediately so a
  single lapse doesn't spiral into a new bad habit of skipping.
sources:
  - title: "Atomic Habits: Tiny Changes, Remarkable Results"
    resource: "Atomic Habits (James Clear), ch. 16"
---

Every streak eventually breaks — illness, travel, life intervenes. **Never
miss twice:** one missed workout is an accident; two in a row starts a new
(bad) habit. The first mistake doesn't ruin you; the spiral of repeated
mistakes does.

All-or-nothing thinking ("if I can't do it perfectly, skip it") loses more
than successful days help — a 50% gain then 33% loss returns you to start.
Charlie Munger: never interrupt compounding unnecessarily. Bad workouts
matter: ten squats or one push-up preserves compound gains and reinforces
identity as someone who doesn't miss workouts — don't put up a zero.

On hard days, use [starter step scaling back](starter-step-scaling-back.md)
or [two-minute rule](two-minute-rule.md) to keep [streak tracking](streak-tracking.md)
alive without demanding full performance.
