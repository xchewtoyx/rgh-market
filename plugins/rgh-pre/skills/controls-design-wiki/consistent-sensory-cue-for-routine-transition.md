---
type: concept
title: Consistent Sensory Cue for Routine Transition
description: >
  Pair a specific, always-the-same sensory input (a scent, a piece of music)
  exclusively with the start of a target routine, so the cue itself comes to
  signal the transition and the brain shifts state on cue rather than by
  deliberate effort.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 2: Physical Self-Care"
---

Example: always using one specific essential-oil scent, or one specific
piece of soothing music, only in the run-up to sleep. Repeated pairing turns
the cue into a signal the brain learns to associate with "time to wind down,"
so triggering the cue does part of the work of shifting state that would
otherwise require deliberately calming oneself down. The cue must stay
reserved for this one purpose to keep working — using it in unrelated
contexts dilutes the association, the same way [bed stimulus control](bed-stimulus-control.md)
depends on keeping the bed reserved for sleep only.

Where there's no natural moment to introduce a new dedicated cue, attaching
the new practice to an already-existing routine instead — see
[habit stacking](habit-stacking.md) — is the more direct route.
