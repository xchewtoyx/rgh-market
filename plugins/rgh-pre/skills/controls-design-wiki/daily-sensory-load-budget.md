---
type: concept
title: Daily Sensory Load Budget
description: >
  Cap the number of high-sensory-demand tasks scheduled in a single day,
  treating sensory capacity as a finite daily budget to allocate rather than
  letting demanding tasks stack up unconstrained.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 3: Emotional Self-Care"
---

Each individual high-sensory-demand task (a crowded errand, a noisy
meeting, a bright unfamiliar venue) might look manageable when scheduled in
isolation, but scheduling several on the same day compounds the load in a
way that isn't obvious from looking at any one task on its own. Setting a
standing cap — e.g. no more than one or two high-sensory tasks per day — is
a scheduling-time control: it constrains the plan itself before the day
starts, rather than requiring the person to sense and react to accumulating
strain in real time (which is unreliable for exactly the population this
matters most for). This is a companion to the [sensory safety plan](sensory-safety-plan.md),
which covers what to do about a specific overwhelming situation once it's
in front of you.
