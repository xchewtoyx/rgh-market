title: Preference-Aligned Routine Design
description: >
  When choosing which form a routine activity takes (e.g. which kind of
  exercise), pick the variant that's genuinely enjoyable or sensory-fitting
  over the variant that's conventionally prescribed, so the routine survives
  without needing willpower to sustain it.

A prescribed "should" version of a routine component (e.g. gym workouts or
team sports for exercise) can carry sensory or social costs that make it a
poor fit for a given person, even though some version of that routine
category (movement, in this example) is genuinely wanted. Rather than
forcing the prescribed variant and relying on discipline to push through the
friction, swap in whichever concrete variant is actually enjoyable — for
exercise, this might be swimming, skating, repetitive walking, or dancing to
a favourite song. The category of the routine stays the same; only the
specific implementation changes to one the person will actually keep doing.

This is the same logic behind including a genuine mix of pleasurable options
in a [curated activity menu](curated-activity-menu.md): a routine component
chosen for fit is self-sustaining, while one chosen only because it's
"correct" has to be re-willed into existence every time.

The same fit-first move also works on tasks that aren't optional, not just
on discretionary routine components: infuse a required but neutral task
with a genuinely preferred element — e.g. making a mandatory data report
highly visual because visual data design is a personal passion — to borrow
intrinsic motivation the task wouldn't otherwise have, rather than treating
the task's form as fixed and separate from personal interests.

Where a task genuinely can't be made a better fit through its form,
consider rotating which tasks a person is assigned over time instead: some
people find repeated exposure to the same task draining regardless of how
well-suited any single instance is, and varying the assignment relieves
that even when no individual task can itself be changed.

Identify personal non-negotiables — what you need to feel functional
(reading, daily steps, rest) — and protect them in the calendar like
meetings, even when work pressure says they should yield. A monthly theme
word ("rest," "connections") can guide tradeoffs without balancing every
priority at once. Working backward from a five-year vision to this month's
step is covered in [backward milestone planning](backward-milestone-planning.md).
