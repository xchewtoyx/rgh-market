title: Commitment Pruning Protocol
description: >
  A five-step process for deliberately ending people, habits, jobs, or roles
  that no longer support growth — naming dead wood, planning for grief, and
  waiting for new space to fill.
sources:
  - title: "The Cherry Tree Theory: A Simple Guide to Stop F*cking Up Your Life"
    resource: "The Cherry Tree Theory (Rox Pink, Rich Pink), Step 3: Does it need pruning?"
---

Pruning is cutting away what cannot grow with you so new shoots have room —
exes, friendships, jobs, cities, or your own harmful patterns. Holding on
because emptiness feels worse than decay is common; the protocol makes the
cut deliberate instead of endless "one more try."

**Five steps:**

1. **Spot dead wood** — Not everything toxic; some things simply stopped
   growing (friendship that only works drunk, job you dread, partner who
   loved the old you). Ask: "Does this feel like my best future? Where would
   the best version of me grow?"
2. **Name the branch** — Write exactly what drains you and one sentence on
   how ("I leave this friendship anxious and doubting myself"). Ask what it
   once gave you and what it costs now.
3. **Prepare for the sap** — Pruning hurts; plan grief support (therapy,
   meetings, sober plans, trusted friends) before cutting. Do not reverse
   purely from fear; backtracking is only for genuine belief the future needs
   it, not to escape discomfort.
4. **Examine your own behavior** — Sometimes leaving is enough; sometimes you
   also prune your own repeating patterns (job-hopping, blowups). Not blame —
   noticing self-caused suffering you can stop.
5. **Stay patient for new growth** — Space opens before replacement arrives;
   loneliness in the gap is normal.

This is structural life design, not acute crisis response — pair with
[personal bright-line rule](personal-bright-line-rule.md) and [pre-rehearsed
boundary script](pre-rehearsed-boundary-script.md) when enforcing cuts.
Deciding to end something is honesty about the life you want, not punishment
of others. Contrast [behavior change masterplan](behavior-change-masterplan.md),
which untangles habits incrementally; pruning removes whole branches when
they cannot be rewired.
