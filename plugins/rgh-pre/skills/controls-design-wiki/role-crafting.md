---
type: concept
title: Role Crafting
description: >
  Proactively negotiate or reshape which tasks make up a role or set of
  responsibilities to match actual capacities and strengths, rather than
  treating the role's task list as a fixed given to be forced through
  regardless of fit.
sources:
  - title: "The Canary Code: A Guide to Neurodiversity, Dignity, and Intersectional Belonging at Work"
    resource: "The Canary Code (Ludmila N. Praslova, PhD), Chapter 5: Starting Off Right — Onboarding, Training, Accommodations, and Compensation"
---

A role's task list is usually treated as a fixed package to be accepted
whole, leaving mismatches between the role and the person's actual strengths
and constraints to be absorbed by extra effort. Role crafting treats the
task list itself as adjustable: swap, redistribute, or get support for the
specific tasks that are the poorest fit, while keeping the parts that
already work well — for example, arranging support specifically for a task
type that's disproportionately costly (note-taking, in the source example)
rather than accepting across-the-board strain from a role designed without
that constraint in mind. This is a structural fix at the level of what the
role contains, rather than a request to individually tolerate a bad fit.
