title: Scheduled Relationship Check-In
description: >
  Put a recurring, structured check-in on the calendar for an ongoing
  relationship, with fixed reflection prompts, so misalignment surfaces on
  a regular cadence instead of only once it's already become a problem.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 5: Social Self-Care"
---

A relationship's health is normally assessed reactively — noticed when
something has already gone wrong enough to prompt a conversation.
Scheduling a regular check-in ahead of time, independent of whether
anything currently feels off, catches drift earlier and makes reflection a
standing habit rather than a crisis response. Reusable prompts keep the
check-in from depending on either person generating a fresh agenda each
time, e.g.: "What is one new thing I learned about myself in our
interactions this week?" / "What is a small change we can make to enhance
our connection?"

This is the relationship-level counterpart to [scheduled recalibration
breaks](scheduled-recalibration-breaks.md): both put a fixed-interval check
on the calendar ahead of need rather than waiting for a reactive trigger,
one applied to a single person's regulation, this one to a dyad's ongoing
alignment.
