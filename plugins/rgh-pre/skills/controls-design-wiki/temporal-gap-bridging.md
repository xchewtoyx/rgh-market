---
type: concept
title: Temporal Gap Bridging
description: >
  Shorten the delay between action and consequence so behavior is governed
  by near-term contingencies instead of weak internal representations of
  the future.
sources:
  - title: "Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder: A Handbook for Diagnosis and Treatment, 4th Edition"
    resource: "Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (Russell A. Barkley, Ed.), ch. 16"
  - title: "Atomic Habits: Tiny Changes, Remarkable Results"
    resource: "Atomic Habits (James Clear), ch. 15"
---

Executive-function deficits often produce temporal myopia: behavior is
governed by the immediate context rather than by internally represented
future events. Human brains evolved for **immediate-return** conditions
(eat, sleep, avoid danger now) while modern life is mostly **delayed-return**
(paychecks, fitness, retirement weeks or years later) — **time
inconsistency** means Present You overweights reward now over possible
reward later. A month-long project with a single distant deadline leaves a
large gap between effort today and payoff later — the future consequence
cannot steer present action reliably.

Bridge or remove those gaps. Break long-horizon work into daily steps with
immediate feedback or incentives rather than one far-off outcome. Have
caregivers or systems bridge gaps to future events when the person cannot
hold them internally. [Graded task assignment](graded-task-assignment.md)
decomposes by size; temporal gap bridging decomposes by time — each segment
should close its own contingency loop (event → response → outcome) within a
horizon the person can actually respond to.

Pair with [motivational prostheses](motivational-prosthesis.md) at each
shortened segment so near-term rewards substitute for distant ones the
internal system cannot track.
