---
type: concept
title: Premortem Procedure
description: >
  Before committing to a plan, run a structured session imagining it has
  already failed and writing the failure history, to surface suppressed
  doubts and break premature group consensus.
sources:
  - title: "Thinking, Fast and Slow"
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 26"
  - title: "The Obstacle Is the Way: The Timeless Art of Turning Trials into Triumph, Expanded 10th Anniversary Edition"
    resource: "The Obstacle Is the Way (Ryan Holiday), ch. 29"
---

When a team has nearly decided but not formally committed, gather people
who know the plan and deliver a short premise: imagine it is a year later,
the plan was implemented as written, and the outcome was a disaster — spend
five to ten minutes writing a brief history of that disaster.

Two mechanisms make this work as a preventive control:
- It **legitimizes doubt** that groupthink would otherwise silence once a
  leader has tipped their hand and loyalty pressure treats objections as
  disloyalty.
- It **directs imagination** toward failure modes that optimistic planning
  skips — the same move the Stoics called *premeditatio malorum*
  (premeditation of evils): rehearse storms, delays, hostile audiences, or
  policy blocks before travel, a speech, or a launch, not only after.

Run while the plan can still change. It is not a complete guard against
nasty surprises, but it reduces damage from uncritical optimism and
overconfidence in the current story. Pair with [contingency rehearsal
script](contingency-rehearsal-script.md) to attach concrete "If X, then Y"
responses to each surfaced failure mode.
