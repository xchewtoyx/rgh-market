Painful or distressing content combined with a tendency to hyperfixate is a
costly combination — each individual piece of content might seem worth
engaging with in isolation, but the aggregate exposure adds up to a load
that outweighs the value of staying informed. Rather than evaluating each
news item or feed session on its own merits in the moment, set a standing
boundary on the channel itself (e.g. a time limit, a schedule for when news
is checked, or specific sources avoided) so consumption is capped by
default rather than by a fresh judgment call each time.

Concrete mechanisms for the same boundary, beyond a time budget: disabling
notifications so the channel stops initiating contact at all; reading
headlines only on a fixed cadence (e.g. every three days) rather than
continuously; a scoped trial reduction (a two-week full cutoff) with the
decision to extend it made afterward based on the actual effect on mood,
rather than negotiated in advance; and naming a trusted person to check in
with periodically, since a boundary that's reported on to someone else is
easier to hold than one that depends on private resolve alone.

Per-app notification audits (log a day's alerts, disable unhelpful ones) and
phone-wide do-not-disturb during focus blocks apply the same upstream cap to
interruption sources rather than resisting each ping. Schedule fixed check-in
slots for channels that genuinely need timely response instead of living in
always-on mode.
