---
type: concept
title: Friction Shaping
description: >
  Change the physical or logistical effort required to reach an unwanted
  option (remove it, put it out of easy reach) or a wanted one (pre-stage
  it, keep it close at hand), rather than relying on willpower to choose
  correctly each time the option is presented.
sources:

  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Ellie Middleton), ch. 3"
  - title: "Atomic Habits: Tiny Changes, Remarkable Results"
    resource: "Atomic Habits (James Clear), ch. 12"
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"

Relying on willpower to decline a tempting option treats every single
encounter with it as a fresh decision to be won by resolve alone — a
contest willpower reliably loses often enough to undo the times it wins.
Changing the option's accessibility instead moves the decision upstream,
to a moment when it's easier to make well: removing tempting food from the
house means the in-the-moment choice never has to be made at all; deciding
a restaurant order in advance (before arriving hungry and facing a menu)
does the same for a recurring situational temptation. The same shaping
works in the opposite direction for a wanted behavior — reducing the
effort to start it (keeping equipment pre-staged, a document already open)
makes the desired choice the path of least resistance instead of the one
requiring extra initiative.

**Increase friction** on unwanted habits: unplug the TV after each use and
only replug when you can name the show aloud; remove remote batteries; move
the TV to a closet; leave the phone in another room until lunch; hide beer
in the back of the fridge; delete social apps so redownloading delays use.
These won't curb true addiction but can tip the balance between sustaining
a good habit and sliding into a bad one. Ask someone to hold your phone for
a few hours when self-imposed distance isn't enough.

A standing scheduled exception (a weekly planned indulgence) is a
companion move: it gives the restricted option a legitimate, bounded outlet
so the restriction doesn't depend on never wanting it, only on not wanting
it *right now* — outside its scheduled slot.

For impulsive spending: delete saved card details, use separate accounts,
24-hour cart delays, reminder notes on cards, and guilt-free splurge pots
with caps — friction on unwanted purchases and a bounded outlet for wanted
ones.

**Urge-delay controls** for compulsive behavior: withdraw fixed cash before
shopping instead of carrying cards; remove credit cards to block online
gambling; delete addiction-related apps and numbers — force at least half an
hour between urge and ability to act. Temporary "patronize yourself"
restrictions in early recovery; see [urge delay friction
controls](urge-delay-friction-controls.md).
