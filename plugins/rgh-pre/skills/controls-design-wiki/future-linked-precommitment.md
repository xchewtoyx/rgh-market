---
type: concept
title: Future-Linked Precommitment
description: >
  Commit now to an automatic change that takes effect only on a future
  trigger (such as a pay raise), avoiding immediate loss while binding
  future behavior.
sources:
  - title: "Thinking, Fast and Slow"
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 40"
---

Immediate sacrifices feel like losses and get avoided; distant-linked changes
feel easier to accept. Save More Tomorrow (Thaler & Benartzi) applies the
pattern to retirement saving: employees pre-commit to raising their
contribution automatically with each future pay raise, with opt-out available
at any time.

Why it works as a control design: no reduction in take-home pay today;
increased saving is framed as a foregone future gain rather than a present
loss; automatic execution aligns with limited deliberation capacity at each
raise date. The person chooses once in a calm moment instead of re-deciding
under present-bias pressure every payday.

Generalize beyond finance: any recurring improvement that hurts if started
now (exercise blocks, bedtime shift, diet change) can be tied to a future
anchor ("starting the Monday after vacation," "when the new project kicks
off") with automatic rollout and a standing opt-out. Related to [temporal gap
bridging](temporal-gap-bridging.md) for shortening contingency loops, but
here the gap is deliberately placed to ease acceptance.
