Interruptions compound the cost already described in
[task batching](task-batching.md): every unplanned interruption forces an
unplanned task switch. Rather than relying on discipline to resist
distractions or on luck to avoid interruptions, remove the interruption
sources for a defined block: disable notifications, and where possible
formalize the block as protected time recognized by others (e.g. a
recurring no-meeting day reserved on a shared calendar) so the protection
doesn't depend on personally fending off each individual request as it
arrives.

Known elsewhere as **timeboxing** a deep-work block: reserving a fixed
block (e.g. three hours each morning) and communicating it to
collaborators in advance, so the protection is a known standing fact for
everyone else rather than something re-negotiated on the fly. The same
mechanism scales to a group — a whole team can adopt a shared distraction-
free window at the same time, multiplying the individual benefit, though
this requires the block to be sanctioned at the level that controls
competing demands (a manager or team norm), not just adopted individually.

**Collaboration overload** — when most of the day is meetings and ad-hoc
help requests — is a structural interruption problem, not a personal focus
failure. Controls include: treating collaboration as a tool rather than a
default; staffing teams with only needed skills; redistributing invisible
collaborative load; honoring Do Not Disturb and regular office hours for
non-emergencies; and using async ideation (brainwriting) so deep-work-wired
people are not forced into synchronous talk-over dynamics. See [explicit
async response norms](explicit-async-response-norms.md) and [outcome-based
work accountability](outcome-based-work-accountability.md).

Do the hardest creative work first in a short, bounded morning block before
email and meetings consume the day — discipline as showing up to a protected
slot, not marathon hours. A visible "NO" reminder beside the screen supports
declining interviews and meetings that would breach the block; see [freeze new
commitments](freeze-new-commitments.md) for the same protective stance at
capacity limits.
