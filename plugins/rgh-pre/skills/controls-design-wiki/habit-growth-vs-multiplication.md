---
type: concept
title: Habit Growth Versus Multiplication
description: >
  Scale habits either by growing the same behavior bigger or by keeping it
  fixed while it seeds additional habits — each plant has its own pace and
  ceiling.
sources:
  - title: "Tiny Habits: The Small Changes That Change Everything"
    resource: "Tiny Habits (BJ Fogg), ch. 7"
---

Two distinct scaling dynamics after a tiny habit roots:

**Grow** — the same habit gets bigger or longer (three breaths → thirty
minutes meditation; two push-ups → fifty; wipe one counter → clean the
kitchen). Each habit has a natural ceiling — extend only when you want more,
not on a fixed calendar. Use [comfort edge scaling](comfort-edge-scaling.md)
to find the next increment.

**Multiply** — the habit stays fixed in size but seeds others. The Maui
Habit (feet hit floor → "It's going to be a great day") doesn't grow, but
inspires making the bed, dishes before leaving, gratitude while brushing —
an upward trajectory for the day. A burner-turned-on habit can multiply into
full meal prep and counter-clearing without the starter habit ever changing.

Aspiration-driven design naturally mixes both: marathon training yields a
growth habit (distance) plus multiplication habits (hydration, vegetables).
No universal formation timeline — depends on person, behavior, and context.

Distinct from [habit shaping](habit-shaping.md), which ladders difficulty
in predefined stages across weeks.
