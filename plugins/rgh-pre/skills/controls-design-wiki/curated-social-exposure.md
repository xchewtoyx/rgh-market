---
type: concept
title: Curated Social Exposure
description: >
  Deliberately choose which people and environments to spend regular time in
  based on their emotional/energetic effect, rather than letting exposure be
  incidental to circumstance.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 3: Emotional Self-Care"
  - title: "Letters on Ethics: To Lucilius"
    resource: "Seneca, Letters on Ethics (trans. Graver and Long), Letter 7"
  - title: "That Little Voice in Your Head"
    resource: "That Little Voice in Your Head (Mo Gawdat), Chapter 3: Under Attack"
---

For someone who absorbs ambient emotional atmosphere readily (heightened
empathy, difficulty maintaining emotional distance from others' states),
who is regularly around is itself a load variable, not a neutral fact of
life. Treating it as a design choice — favoring company with positive
energy, and setting boundaries around exposure to draining people or
settings — is a steady-state control on emotional load, distinct from
having to individually regulate the impact of each difficult interaction
after the fact.

The same logic applies to unstructured crowds specifically, not just
individual relationships: Seneca advises treating "spend time with those
who improve you" as a standing selection rule for company in general, on
the grounds that even a strong character is gradually reshaped by whoever
it regularly keeps time with — deliberately choosing exposure prevents
that drift rather than requiring it to be corrected after the fact.

Making the choice on a standing recurring cadence — e.g. a monthly review
of everyone seen regularly, asking of each relationship whether it drains
or supports, followed by a conversation to try to improve it before
stepping back if unresolved — turns curated exposure into a maintained
practice rather than a one-time decision that quietly goes stale as
people's actual effect on you changes over time.
