type: concept
title: Multi-Format Information Provision
description: >
  Provide important information in more than one format (text, visual,
  audio) by default, since assuming a single processing style fits
  everyone systematically excludes whoever doesn't match the assumed
  default.
sources:
  - title: "The Canary Code: A Guide to Neurodiversity, Dignity, and Intersectional Belonging at Work"
    resource: "The Canary Code (Ludmila N. Praslova, PhD), Chapter 5: Starting Off Right — Onboarding, Training, Accommodations, and Compensation"
  - title: "Knowing Why: Adult-Diagnosed Autistic People on Life and Autism"
    resource: "Knowing Why (Autistic Self Advocacy Network), ch. 5 (Kelly Bron Johnson)"
  - title: "Unmasking Autism Audiobook Companion"
    resource: "Unmasking Autism Audiobook Companion (Devon Price, PhD), Common Autistic Communication Needs worksheet"

A welcome packet, a training video, or a set of instructions delivered in
only one format bakes in an assumption about how the recipient best takes
in information — an assumption that's wrong for a predictable fraction of
any audience (e.g. video without a transcript is overstimulating for some
and simply a worse fit than text for others). Producing the same content in
multiple formats up front costs more once, but removes the need to
identify and specifically accommodate each mismatch afterward — a specific
instance of the broader [universal design default](universal-design-default.md)
principle, applied to how information is presented.

The same move applies to **task instructions**: verbal command strings may
be heard and seem understood in the moment but not survive the walk back to
a desk. Default to written directions (notes, email, step-by-step documents
with measurable outcomes) rather than relying on auditory recall — reframed
as a processing-format mismatch, not a competence issue. Pair with
[what-by-when-and-why instructions](what-by-when-and-why-instructions.md)
when delegating or receiving multi-step work.
