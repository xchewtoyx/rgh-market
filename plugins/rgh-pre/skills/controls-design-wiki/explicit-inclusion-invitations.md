---
type: concept
title: Explicit Inclusion Invitations
description: >
  Address a person directly and explicitly to invite participation ("Hey
  Alan, want to join us?", "Amara, we'd love to hear your thoughts") rather
  than relying on implied cues (body language, an open pause) that assume
  everyone can read them the same way.
sources:
  - title: "Neurodiversity For Dummies"
    resource: "Neurodiversity For Dummies (John Marble, Khushboo Chabria, Ranga Jayaraman), Chapter 21"
---

An invitation or turn-taking opening that's only ever implied (a glance, a
pause left open, an assumption that "everyone knows they can join") is
invisible to anyone who doesn't reliably read that particular channel — the
person isn't excluded on purpose, but the invitation never actually reaches
them. Naming the person and stating the invitation directly removes the
ambiguity entirely; it costs the inviter almost nothing and doesn't
disadvantage anyone who would have picked up the implied version anyway.
This is the facilitation-side counterpart to
[requesting explicit norms](request-explicit-norms.md) — instead of the
person having to ask for the unwritten rule to be stated, whoever's
running the group states it unprompted.
