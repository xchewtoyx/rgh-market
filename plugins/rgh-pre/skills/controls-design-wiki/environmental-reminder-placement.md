type: concept
title: Environmental Reminder Placement
description: >
  Place a physical or visual reminder in the environment at the point where
  a recurring practice should happen, so the environment itself prompts the
  behavior instead of relying on memory or motivation to initiate it.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"

  - title: "Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder: A Handbook for Diagnosis and Treatment, 4th Edition"

  - title: "Atomic Habits: Tiny Changes, Remarkable Results"

Example: sticky notes placed around the living space as visual gratitude
reminders. The reminder isn't a note-to-self stored somewhere that has to be
remembered and retrieved — it's placed physically in the path of everyday
attention, so encountering it is incidental to normal movement through the
space rather than requiring a deliberate recall step. This differs from
[habit stacking](habit-stacking.md), which anchors a new practice to an
existing routine's timing; here the anchor is a location in the physical
environment instead.

When working memory and internal self-direction are weak, covert or
privately held information is a poor source of stimulus control — make
task-relevant information overt and public at the [point of
performance](point-of-performance-intervention.md). Post rules or signs in
the environment and refer to them during work; use recorded audio reminders
played during tasks; have the person state rules aloud before and during
activity. The goal is not to increase effort to remember but to fill the
immediate context with salient cues that outcompete distractors — a core
move in the [performance versus knowledge gap](performance-versus-knowledge-gap.md)
framing.

Cues that stand out get noticed — a fly-shaped sticker etched near urinal
centers drew aim and cut cleaning costs at Schiphol Airport. The same lever
for wanted habits: pill bottle by the faucet, guitar on a stand in the
living room, stationery on the desk, water bottles filled and placed around
the house. If a habit should be a big part of life, make its cue a big part
of the environment — not tucked in a closet or guest room where it never
enters the visual field.

Persistent unwanted habits often have **multiple redundant cues** (driving,
seeing a friend smoke, work stress). Apply the same multiplication for good
habits: sprinkle several triggers through the day so the practice comes to
mind repeatedly, not once. Pair with [visible storage for object
permanence](visible-storage-for-object-permanence.md) when the cue is a
physical object that must stay in view.
