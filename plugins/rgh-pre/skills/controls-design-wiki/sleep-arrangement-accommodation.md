---
type: concept
title: Sleep Arrangement Accommodation
description: >
  Choose the sleeping arrangement (e.g. separate beds or bedrooms) that
  actually protects both partners' sleep, rather than defaulting to a shared
  arrangement because of social expectations about what a healthy
  relationship's sleep setup should look like.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 2: Physical Self-Care"
---

Common relationship narratives treat sharing a bed as the default and
sleeping apart as a sign something is wrong. When one partner's sleep-
disrupting traits (snoring, late-night activity, sensory needs) conflict
with the other's sleep needs, insisting on the shared-bed default degrades
both people's sleep and, over time, the relationship — while deliberately
choosing separate sleeping arrangements can improve the relationship by
protecting both partners' rest. Treat the sleeping arrangement as a design
choice to be optimized for actual sleep quality, not as a proxy for
relationship health.
