---
type: concept
title: Pre-Scripted Accommodation Request
description: >
  Prepare a short, ready-to-use phrase for requesting a specific
  environmental accommodation in advance, so asking doesn't require
  composing a justification under stress in the moment.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 1: Cultivating Sensory Safety; Chapter 3: Emotional Self-Care; Chapter 6: Professional Self-Care"
---

Example: "I have light sensitivities. Is it okay if we sit in that corner
rather than under this light?" The script names the specific need and the
specific requested change together, as a single ready-made line, rather than
leaving the person to improvise a justification for the request while
already uncomfortable and under social pressure. Having the phrasing settled
in advance lowers the barrier to actually asking, which is often the harder
part of getting an accommodation compared to the accommodation itself.

General shape: **self-disclosure + request** (disclosure is optional — a
bare request, or a partial disclosure like naming only "sensory
differences" without detail, is a complete and valid script on its own).
Other worked examples: "I have auditory-processing differences. Can we
continue our conversation outside?"; "I thrive on direct communication.
Would you mind elaborating on your feedback?"

Build a small library of these in advance rather than writing one at a time:
review situations from the recent past where an accommodation would have
helped, and pre-write and rehearse a script for each, so the next time a
similar situation comes up the script already exists rather than needing to
be composed from scratch under the same conditions that made the original
situation hard.

In settings where disclosure has real stakes (e.g. a workplace), work
backward from the specific accommodation before deciding what to disclose:
identify exactly which environmental change would help (softer lighting,
relocating away from a scent, a schedule adjustment), then disclose only
the minimum needed to justify that specific ask, rather than defaulting to
full diagnostic disclosure. Where a formal process is involved (e.g. HR),
also check who actually gets access to what's disclosed before disclosing
it — the audience for a disclosure is often narrower than it appears.
