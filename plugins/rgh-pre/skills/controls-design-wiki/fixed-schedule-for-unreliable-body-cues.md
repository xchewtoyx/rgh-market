title: Fixed Schedule for Unreliable Body Cues
description: >
  Put basic needs (eating, drinking, using the bathroom) on a fixed external
  schedule rather than relying on internal body signals to prompt them, when
  those internal signals are known to be unreliable or register too late.

Some people have reduced interoceptive awareness — the internal sense of
hunger, thirst, needing the bathroom, or even emotional state is muted or
arrives too late to act on comfortably. Where the normal control loop
("feel the cue, then act") is unreliable because the cue itself is
unreliable, the fix is to stop depending on the cue: replace it with a
fixed, external schedule (e.g. "drink water every two hours," "eat lunch at
12:30 regardless of hunger signal") so the action happens on a timer instead
of waiting for a body signal that may not arrive in time.

Two concrete implementations of the same principle:
- **Timers/alarms as check-in prompts** — a recurring alarm ("has it been
  two hours? drink water") stands in for the missing internal prompt,
  turning "notice you're thirsty" into "respond to the alarm."
- **Concrete, quantified externalized targets** — replace a felt sense of
  "enough" with a visible, countable target, e.g. a marked 32-oz water
  bottle to empty by the end of the day. The target is checkable by looking
  at it, rather than by trying to feel whether the need has been met.
- **Structured interoception check-ins** — scheduled prompts that walk
  through a short question set (thirst, hunger, bathroom, rest) instead of
  a single generic alarm. For over-responsive interoception, regular
  check-ins ground the nervous system and confirm needs met at intervals
  (e.g. two cups of water since the last check-in toward a daily target).
  For discrimination difficulties, the check-in is intentional time to sit
  with the body and decide what's needed.

Hyperfocus can suppress even reliable cues — forgetting to eat, use the
bathroom, or leave at end of day. **Calendar and phone timers** for lunch,
going home, and periodic breathing or posture checks stand in for cues
crowded out by engrossing work, the same substitution logic applied to
attention rather than interoception alone. A **to-do list app with recurring
reminders** can carry the same function for whole self-care sequences —
shower, wash, medication — when short-term memory and organisation are the
bottleneck rather than a missing body cue alone.

When the missing cue isn't an internal body signal but an external hazard
signal (e.g. detecting spoiled food or air contaminants by smell), the same
substitution principle applies via [external hazard-detection substitutes](environmental-sensory-accommodation.md)
instead.
