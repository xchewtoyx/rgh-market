type: concept
title: Bed Stimulus Control
description: >
  Reserve the bed/bedroom for sleep (and sex) only, so that lying down there
  reliably cues the brain toward sleep instead of toward whatever else has
  been done there — physically partitioning the space if it must also serve
  as a retreat or safe space.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 2: Physical Self-Care"
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition (Rhena Branch, Rob Willson), ch. 15"
---

The brain forms associations between a place and what's habitually done
there (e.g. a bedroom repeatedly used for eating snacks while watching TV
becomes associated with wakeful activity, not sleep). If the bed is used for
sleep and nothing else, the association strengthens in the desired
direction: getting into bed itself becomes a cue for sleep, rather than one
context among several. Where the bedroom necessarily doubles as a retreat or
sensory-safe space during the day, physically partition it (a room divider
or curtain around the bed area) so the sleep-only association is preserved
for the bed specifically, even though the room serves other purposes.

If unable to fall asleep or return to sleep, don't lie in bed more than ten
minutes — get up and do something boring (dull reading, sorting laundry, warm
low-caffeine drink) until eyelids feel heavy. Avoid caffeine from mid-afternoon
onward; avoid alcohol until sleep is sorted (sedates then rebounds). In-bed
devices: soothing music or podcasts only, auto-off within ~30 minutes; easy
reading with the same limit. Bed is for sleeping, preparing to sleep, and sex
only — not eating, gripping books, shows, gaming, or work.
