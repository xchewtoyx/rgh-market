---
type: concept
title: Defined Primary End Filter
description: >
  Define a single primary contribution or definite end in advance, then
  treat every new commitment as a yes/no against that end rather than
  judging each opportunity on its own merits.
sources:
  - title: "Discipline Is Destiny: The Power of Self-Control"
    resource: "Discipline Is Destiny (Ryan Holiday), ch. 8"
---

Without a clear sense of the "main thing" — the most important contribution
or definite end the current season of life is aimed at — there is no stable
standard for what to accept or decline. Each "potential opportunity" or
"it'll only take a minute" request gets judged in isolation, which is
effectively winging it: no wind is favorable if the destination is
unknown. The control has two stages, done in order: first, step away while
capacity is available and name the primary end and most important
contribution; second, treat nearly everything else as out of scope until
that end is served.

Every yes to something peripheral is also a no to the main thing — to
family, to the people the work is meant to serve, and to a future self who
needed the protected focus. Framing intake this way turns "should I take
this on" into "does this serve the defined end," which is a lookup rather
than a fresh negotiation under social pressure. At intake time, pair this
filter with [importance-urgency triage
criteria](importance-urgency-triage-criteria.md) for requests that arrive
with urgency framing, and with [freeze new
commitments](freeze-new-commitments.md) when overload has already set in
and the first move is stopping the inflow. When the rule must be voiced to
someone else, [pre-rehearsed boundary
scripts](pre-rehearsed-boundary-script.md) supply the words; a [personal
bright-line rule](personal-bright-line-rule.md) can fix the answer entirely
for recurring categories of request.

The same dilution pattern appears when designing an intervention or
project: adding features or obligations to please everyone ends up pleasing
no one. [Protected core scope](protected-core-scope.md) is the design-side
counterpart — guarding the core concept from scope creep rather than
letting too much get jammed in.
