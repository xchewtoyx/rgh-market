Asserting a boundary — declining a request, exiting a conversation,
naming that a situation has become stressful and a pause is needed — is
hardest exactly when the pressure of the moment makes it hard to find
calm, socially-acceptable words. Writing the phrases down ahead of time and
rehearsing them aloud (building "muscle memory" the way lines are
rehearsed for a script) means the words are already available when the
moment arrives, rather than needing to be generated under the same
conditions that make the situation hard in the first place.

Worked examples, by purpose:
- Declining: "No, I'm sorry, I am not able to do that right now."
- Declining without over-apologizing: a plain "No" or "Better not" — owning
  the refusal rather than performing regret buys more power than always
  saying yes out of fear of missing out or seeming selfish.
- Exiting: "It's been lovely chatting with you."
- Naming overload and requesting a pause: "I'm finding this situation very
  stressful, and I need a moment to collect my thoughts. Can we pause for a
  few minutes?"

Fogg's recipe appendix treats scripted phrases as [anchor
moments](anchor-moment-design.md) for hard social moments — pre-written
lines remove in-the-moment composition: declining clubbing ("Thank you, but
not tonight"); ending a long call ("It's been great to talk, but I need to
wrap up. What haven't we covered yet that's important?"); declining PTA help
("Sorry I can't help this time around, but please ask me again in the
future"); refusing mid-focus interruptions ("I can't right now. Sorry.");
declining unhealthy travel snacks ("No, thank you").

This is the boundary-setting counterpart to a [pre-prepared response
bank](pre-prepared-response-bank.md): that note stocks answers for
spontaneous-verbal-demand situations where something must be said; this one
stocks phrases for the opposite demand — stopping, declining, or pausing an
interaction.

**Relationship boundaries** also need pre-decided rules, not fresh
negotiation each time: what you will and won't share, what treatment you
accept, what is not your responsibility, and keeping arm's length from
people who chronically drain capacity ("energy vampires") even when they are
talented, family, or old friends. Enforce with a [personal bright-line
rule](personal-bright-line-rule.md) where the answer is fixed in advance;
use scripts here when the rule must be voiced under pressure.
