title: Habit Completion Reinforcement
description: >
  Attach an immediate reward to the end of a target behavior so the finish
  feels satisfying and the brain learns to repeat it — distinct from cues
  that make starting obvious.
sources:
  - title: "Atomic Habits: Tiny Changes, Remarkable Results"
    resource: "Atomic Habits (James Clear), ch. 15"
---

**Reinforcement** uses an immediate reward to increase the rate of a
behavior. Contrast with [habit stacking](habit-stacking.md): stacking ties
a habit to an immediate *cue* (when to start); reinforcement ties it to an
immediate *reward* (how the finish feels). [Immediate start
reward](immediate-start-reward.md) bridges initiation; completion
reinforcement bridges the gap until long-term payoffs arrive.

Good habits often feel worthwhile only after results show up (gym before
visible fitness). Without a felt win at the end, repetition stalls even when
the behavior technically happened. Make the product or experience slightly
more pleasurable when consistency matters — pleasant-smelling soap raised
handwashing adherence in a Karachi slum study because sensory satisfaction
signaled "do this again," not because residents lacked knowledge.

Select rewards with [identity-aligned reward
selection](identity-aligned-reward-selection.md) so the reinforcer votes
for the identity you're building, not a conflicting one. [Temptation
bundling](temptation-bundling.md) is one form: the wanted activity follows
the needed one. [Streak tracking](streak-tracking.md) and crossing off
progress are other satisfying endings. For habits of avoidance (no spend, no
drink), use [avoidance habit savings transfer](avoidance-habit-savings-transfer.md)
because successful abstention produces no natural felt reward.
