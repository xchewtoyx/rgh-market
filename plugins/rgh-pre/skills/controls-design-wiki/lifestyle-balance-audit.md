  Periodically review daily and weekly habits across physical, psychological,
  and resource domains — flag excesses and deliberately substitute
  under-used replenishing activities before old unhelpful patterns return.

Healthy day-to-day living is steady-state relapse prevention: working too
late, poor eating, excess caffeine or alcohol, and isolation erode the base
that keeps symptoms away. A **lifestyle balance audit** reviews habits across:

- **Eating** — regular meals and snacks; spot gaps with a food diary, then
  fix the blocking attitudes or logistics.
- **Exercise** — at least three 20–30 minute sessions weekly (build toward
  five); walking and gardening count.
- **Leisure** — pleasure and satisfaction outside job and home obligations.
- **Social contact** — reinvigorate relationships that suffered during illness.
- **Absorbing interests** — causes and contributions that matter to you,
  including small everyday acts.
- **Resource management** — budgeting, bill systems, working hours,
  babysitting, hired help, earmarked relaxation time.

Everyone needs delineated time both to **replenish** psychological and
physical energy and to **get things done** — you can't sustain one without
the other. Scan for excess (late work, doom-scrolling, fast food) and
substitute under-used activities (exercise, family time, cooking, study).
Pair with [absorbing activity revival](absorbing-activity-revival.md) to
fill freed time constructively and [self-maintenance routine](self-maintenance-routine.md)
for baseline sleep, food, and movement.
