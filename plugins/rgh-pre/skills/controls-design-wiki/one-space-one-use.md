---
type: concept
title: One Space, One Use
description: >
  Assign each physical zone or device a single primary activity so the
  location itself cues one behavior, instead of mixing contexts that let the
  easiest habit win.
sources:
  - title: "Atomic Habits: Tiny Changes, Remarkable Results"
    resource: "Atomic Habits (James Clear), ch. 6"
---

When one place hosts multiple habits, the easiest or most practiced one
dominates — work from the couch or kitchen table blurs work and personal
time; a dedicated office room makes switching off easier because the
context no longer cues "always on."

Partition even a small apartment: a chair for reading, a desk for writing,
a table for eating. Each zone trains [context as cue](context-as-cue.md) for
one primary use. A writer who restricts his computer to writing, his tablet
to reading, and his phone to social/texting embodies the same rule for
devices: every habit should have a home.

Pair with [workspace processing surface](workspace-processing-surface.md)
for how the active work zone handles incoming items, and [bed stimulus
control](bed-stimulus-control.md) when the bedroom must stay sleep-only
despite serving other retreat functions.
