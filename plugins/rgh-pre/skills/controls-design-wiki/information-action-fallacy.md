type: concept
title: Information-Action Fallacy
description: >
  The mistaken belief that supplying correct facts or awareness alone will
  change behavior — when lasting change requires environmental redesign and
  tiny practiced habits instead.
sources:
  - title: "Tiny Habits: The Small Changes That Change Everything"
    resource: "Tiny Habits (BJ Fogg), ch. 1"
---

Professionals often assume "if people just knew the facts, they would change."
Fogg calls this the **Information-Action Fallacy**: information alone does
not reliably change behavior. Attitude shifts from education are weak
predictors of action.

For durable controls, pair information with structural levers Fogg treats as
the only practical routes besides accidental epiphany: **change the
environment** ([cue exposure reduction](cue-exposure-reduction.md),
[friction shaping](friction-shaping.md), [proactive environment
priming](proactive-environment-priming.md)) and **change habits in tiny
ways** ([starter step scaling back](starter-step-scaling-back.md), [anchor
moment design](anchor-moment-design.md)). A failed change attempt is more
often a design flaw than a personal flaw — diagnose with the [Fogg Behavior
Model](fogg-behavior-model.md) and [prompt-ability-motivation
troubleshooting order](prompt-ability-motivation-troubleshooting-order.md)
instead of adding more lectures.
