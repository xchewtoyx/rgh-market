  Proactively ask for an unwritten rule or expectation to be stated
  explicitly, rather than trying to infer it by observing subtle social or
  contextual cues.

Environments like workplaces run partly on unwritten norms (what an
informal team dinner is actually for, what "professional conduct" means in
practice) that most people absorb passively from ambient cues. Inferring
these norms by continuously monitoring tone, body language, and hierarchy
is expensive and error-prone for anyone who doesn't process that context
automatically — and a misread can carry real consequences. The lower-cost
alternative is to ask directly for the rule to be stated ("what's the
expectation for X") rather than deriving it from observation each time. A
[mentor as decoding resource](mentor-as-decoding-resource.md) is the
standing version of this for norms that are too diffuse or numerous to ask
about one at a time.

**Clear-expectation accommodations** make implicit norms explicit up front:
specific plans with time, place, and likely events; direct yes/no answers
instead of euphemisms; meeting agendas distributed and followed; reading
materials and questions provided before high-stress events; step-by-step
task instructions with measurable outcomes; explicit statements of feeling
("I am disappointed because…") rather than expecting inference from tone or
facial expression. Each removes a round of costly guesswork — a standing
instance of [multi-format information provision](multi-format-information-provision.md)
and [advance notice of change](advance-notice-of-change.md) applied to
social and task context.
