---
type: concept
title: Protected Core Scope
description: >
  When a core intervention, project, or routine is defined, resist adding
  peripheral features or obligations that dilute it — the personal analogue
  of product "feature creep."
sources:
  - title: "Discipline Is Destiny: The Power of Self-Control"
    resource: "Discipline Is Destiny (Ryan Holiday), ch. 8"
---

A founder who cannot protect a product's core concept lets too many
features get jammed in to please every stakeholder — and ends up pleasing
no one. The same failure mode applies to personal controls: a routine,
commitment structure, or intervention that started with a clear purpose
accumulates extras until the original function is buried. Scope creep is
not generosity; it is uncontrolled intake that erodes the thing that
actually mattered.

The preventive control is to name the core upfront — what this routine,
project, or season exists to accomplish — and treat every proposed addition
as a threat to that core unless it clearly serves the [defined primary
end](defined-primary-end-filter.md). "No" to peripheral additions is "yes"
to the main thing. When evaluating whether a control design is still
fit for purpose, ask whether new layers were added to satisfy momentary
social pressure rather than the stated end.
