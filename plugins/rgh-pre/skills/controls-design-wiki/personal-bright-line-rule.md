---
type: concept
title: Personal Bright-Line Rule
description: >
  Decide a fixed rule of conduct in advance and treat it as binding
  regardless of in-the-moment social pressure, rather than re-judging
  whether to follow it case by case.
sources:
  - title: "Discourses, Fragments, Handbook"
    resource: "Epictetus, Discourses, Fragments, Handbook (trans. Robin Hard), Handbook chapters 33, 50"
  - title: "Discipline Is Destiny: The Power of Self-Control"
    resource: "Discipline Is Destiny (Ryan Holiday), ch. 12"
  - title: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery"
    resource: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery (Sol Smith), ch. 10"
---

Re-deciding a behavioral question fresh every time it comes up (whether to
join in gossip, whether to have another drink, whether to comment on
something outside your business) means each instance is judged under
whatever social pressure happens to be present at that moment — exactly the
condition under which a bad call is most likely. Deciding the rule once,
in advance, while no particular instance is pressuring the decision, and
then simply holding to it "as if it were a law" removes the case-by-case
judgement call entirely: the question stops being "should I, this time"
and becomes "does the rule apply," which is far cheaper to answer under
pressure.

This differs from [default option preselection](default-option-preselection.md)
in shape: a preselected default narrows an open-ended choice down to a
short list to pick from, while a bright-line rule removes the choice
altogether by committing to a single fixed answer ahead of time. Where the
rule needs to be voiced to someone else in the moment it applies (declining
an invitation, refusing a request), a [pre-rehearsed boundary
script](pre-rehearsed-boundary-script.md) supplies the words to enforce it
without having to improvise them under the same pressure the rule exists to
resist.

Bright-line rules also define **what is not yours to carry**: other people's
dysfunction, gossip, or drama; tracking politics that aren't your job;
oversharing that trades privacy for attention. Without borders on
responsibility and disclosure, capacity bleeds into others' problems until
there is no telling where you end and they begin — the same structural move
as [information diet boundary](information-diet-boundary.md) applied to
relationships rather than media intake.

**Only set boundaries you will hold every time** — partial enforcement trains
others that limits are negotiable and erodes your own trust in the rule.
Discomfort at first is expected; consistent enforcement eventually yields a
more fully expressed relationship. If someone will not respect a stated
boundary after clear communication, [commitment pruning
protocol](commitment-pruning-protocol.md) may be the structural next step —
not because they are bad, but because the arrangement no longer fits.
