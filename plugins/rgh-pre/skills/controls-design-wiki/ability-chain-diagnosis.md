---
type: concept
title: Ability Chain Diagnosis
description: >
  Find which of five friction factors — time, money, physical effort, mental
  effort, or routine fit — is the weakest link making a behavior hard to do,
  then fix that link instead of adding motivation.
sources:
  - title: "Tiny Habits: The Small Changes That Change Everything"
    resource: "Tiny Habits (BJ Fogg), ch. 2, ch. 4"
---

A behavior is only as easy as its weakest link in the **Ability Chain**:
- **Time** — enough minutes available?
- **Money** — affordable?
- **Physical effort** — capable without strain?
- **Mental/creative effort** — heavy thinking required?
- **Routine fit** — aligns with current schedule?

Discovery question: "What is making this behavior hard to do?" A seven-minute
workout sounds easy until physical effort is the weak link on a low-energy
day. Fogg couldn't floss because close teeth made threading hard — thin,
slippery floss and one tooth as the target fixed the physical-effort link.

Big habits with shallow ability roots topple in storms; tiny easy habits
survive disruption and deepen over time. After diagnosis, use [starter step
scaling back](starter-step-scaling-back.md), skills, or tools per the
Breakthrough Question: "How can I make this behavior easier to do?" Pair
with [law of least effort](law-of-least-effort.md) — simplicity, not
motivation spikes, reliably changes behavior.

**Perceived ability** can be the weak link even when the task is objectively
doable — abstract requests ("wipe out the shower") feel hard without knowing
tools and steps. Ask "show me exactly what you want me to do" and watch a
demonstration; a ten-second walkthrough can move a behavior above the Action
Line in the [Fogg Behavior Model](fogg-behavior-model.md) without touching
motivation. Follow [prompt-ability-motivation troubleshooting
order](prompt-ability-motivation-troubleshooting-order.md): fix the cue and
clarify steps before blaming willpower.
