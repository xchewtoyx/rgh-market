type: concept
title: Habit Loop Replacement
description: >
  To change an existing habit, keep its cue and reward the same and swap
  only the routine in between, rather than trying to eliminate the cue or
  the underlying urge it triggers.
sources:
  - title: "Neurodiversity For Dummies"
    resource: "Neurodiversity For Dummies (John Marble, Khushboo Chabria, Ranga Jayaraman), Chapter 12"
---

A habit runs on a three-part loop: a cue (trigger — a time, place, person,
action, or emotional state), a routine (the behavior performed in
response), and a reward (the payoff that reinforces the loop). Trying to
break an unwanted habit by fighting the cue itself is fighting something
that's often outside easy control (a location, a stressful feeling) and
tends to fail. The more durable move is substitution: keep the cue and the
reward that make the loop reinforcing, and change only the routine between
them — e.g. replacing stress-induced unhealthy eating with a walk or a
short mindfulness practice, so the same stress cue now triggers a different
behavior but still resolves toward the same kind of relief.

This differs from [habit stacking](habit-stacking.md) in what problem it
solves: habit stacking borrows an existing cue's reliability to establish a
*new* practice; habit loop replacement uses an *existing* cue (one already
attached to an unwanted routine) to displace that routine with a better
one, without having to fight the cue itself.
