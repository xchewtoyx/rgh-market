Meditation is unusual among human activities: it is not primarily about
improving yourself or getting somewhere else, but "simply to realize where
you already are." It is better described as a **being** than a **doing.**
The Zen line captures it: "Nothing happens next. This is it."

People often come wanting relaxation, special states, stress relief, or habit
change — valid motives but problematic if you expect those outcomes just
because you're meditating. You may get caught wanting a special experience
or signs of progress, and doubt the path if nothing special happens quickly.
Unlike most learning domains, in meditation "the best way to get somewhere
is to let go of trying to get anywhere at all." Every state is a special
state, every moment a special moment.

Acceptance of the present moment has nothing to do with [acceptance vs
resignation](acceptance-vs-resignation.md) in the giving-up sense — it is a
clear acknowledgment that what is happening is happening. Acceptance does
not tell you what to do next; that comes from understanding the moment.

This attitude pairs with [doing mode and being mode](doing-mode-being-mode.md):
non-striving is how being mode holds experience without immediately launching
into fixing. [Mindfulness practice for its own sake](mindfulness-practice-for-its-own-sake.md)
extends the same spirit across the whole day.

Effort is still required — but **wise effort**: patience, commitment, and
trust rather than continually checking progress toward a imagined destination.
The butterfly metaphor captures it: hoping one lands on your shoulder and
trying to make it happen only agitates you and makes it less likely; eventually
you give up striving and see if it arrives on its own. If there is a "goal" at
all in formal practice, it is being fully present with openhearted spaciousness
with whatever arises — fully awake and fully alive. Pleasant feelings are a
welcome by-product, not the target.

**Try:** Remind yourself "This is it" and see if anything cannot have it
applied; contemplate whether this may actually be the best season of your
life and what that would mean.
