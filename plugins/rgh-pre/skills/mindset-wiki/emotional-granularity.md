---
type: concept
title: Emotional Granularity
description: >
  Multiple emotions are almost always present at once rather than one at a
  time, and the skill of separating that blend into its distinct
  components — rather than accepting a single compressed label — gives
  far more useful information than the compressed label alone.
sources:
  - title: That Little Voice in Your Head
    resource: "That Little Voice in Your Head (Mo Gawdat), Chapter 7: Can You Feel It?"
---

A pure, single emotion is rare — what's actually present at any moment is
usually a mix (e.g. simultaneously anxious about a deadline, glad about an
unrelated success, guilty about a neglected message, and mildly
uncomfortable physically). The brain tends to compress this mixture into
one dominant labeled emotion for efficiency, the same way it compresses
complex situations into simple rules under pressure — but the compression
obscures the actual components, making it harder to know what's really
going on or what to do about it.

Deliberately slowing down to separate the blend — like breaking a mixed
paint color back into its component pigments — surfaces information that
the single label hides: which specific thread is driving the strongest
reaction, which are minor background notes, and which might even be
positive amid an overall negative-feeling moment. This works together with
[the physical signature of emotions](physical-signature-of-emotions.md):
scanning the body for multiple distinct sensations, rather than one
overall feeling, is often how the separate components first become
noticeable.
