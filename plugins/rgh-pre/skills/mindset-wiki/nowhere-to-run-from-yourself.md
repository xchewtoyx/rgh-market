type: concept
title: Nowhere to Run From Yourself
description: >
  Changing jobs, towns, or retreats cannot escape patterns of seeing and
  behaving you carry with you — transformation requires working with the
  present situation rather than projecting problems outward.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 6: Wherever You Go, There You Are"
---

"There is no running away from anything" — things you try to escape or paper
over catch up with you, especially old patterns and fears. The romantic notion
is that the problem is external — the job, spouse, town, children — so
changing circumstance will let you start over. But "you carry your head and
your heart, and what some would call your 'karma,' around with you. You cannot
escape yourself." If problems stem largely from your own patterns of seeing,
thinking, and behaving, the same problems arise wherever you go.

Lives cease working because we cease working at life — unwillingness to take
responsibility for things as they are and work with difficulties, even
though "it is actually possible to attain clarity, understanding, and
transformation right in the middle of what is here and now." It is psychologically
easier to project involvement onto others, blame, or ultimately blame and give
up on oneself.

Meditation does not confer immunity: hopping techniques, teachers, or traditions
chasing a special high; unhealthy dependency on teachers; retreats used to stay
afloat rather than look deeply — transformation may be only skin deep before
reverting to the same pattern.

"There is no successful escaping from yourself in the long run, only
transformation" — whether via drugs, meditation, alcohol, divorce, or quitting
your job. Growth requires facing the present situation with [mindfulness](mindfulness.md),
allowing roughness to sand down rough edges. "You must be willing to let life
itself become your teacher" — this place, this relationship, this dilemma, this
job. Work with circumstances before deciding to cut losses and move on.

If you imagine a cave, beach, or retreat would be better — when you arrive,
there you are with the same mind, body, and breath. "Let go and admit that you
might as well be at home wherever you are." Right then, touch the core of your
being and invite mindfulness to heal.
