title: Triune Brain Model (as a Behavioral Heuristic)
description: >
  A three-layer model of the brain — reptilian (survival reflexes),
  mammalian (emotion and comfort-seeking), rational (planning and
  self-concept) — used as a practical lens for diagnosing which kind of
  reaction is driving a given moment of distress, not as a literal claim
  about brain evolution.
sources:
  - title: That Little Voice in Your Head
    resource: "That Little Voice in Your Head (Mo Gawdat), Chapter 3: Under Attack"
---

The model (originally Paul MacLean's, used here as a heuristic rather than
settled neuroscience) splits brain function into three layers with
different jobs: the reptilian layer runs autonomic survival reflexes —
fight-or-flight, triggered in a fraction of a second, with no conscious
input; the mammalian layer governs emotion, pleasure-seeking, and comfort
(reproduction, food, bonding, protecting offspring); the rational layer
handles logic, long-horizon planning, and the sense of being a distinct
self.

The layer that is currently driving a reaction determines what kind of
distress is present, and each layer maps onto one of the three [AAA
defenses](aaa-framework.md): the reptilian layer produces
[aversion](aversion.md), the mammalian layer produces [attachment to
comfort](attachment-to-comfort.md), and the rational layer produces [all-
pervasive dissatisfaction](all-pervasive-dissatisfaction.md). Naming which
layer is active in a given moment of unhappiness is a useful diagnostic
step before choosing which countermeasure to apply.
