type: concept
title: Loving-Kindness Meditation
description: >
  A mindfulness practice that repeatedly invites kindness, acceptance, and
  cherishing toward yourself — then outward in widening circles — as a way
  to counter low self-regard and sense interconnection.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 6"
---

Loving-kindness meditation starts with yourself: repeatedly invite kindness,
acceptance, and cherishing, bringing the mind back to that intention the way
you return to the breath in sitting practice — holding yourself "as a mother
would hold a hurt or frightened child, with completely available and
unconditional love." Posed questions: can you cultivate forgiveness of
yourself; is it possible to invite yourself to be happy; is it okay to feel okay?

Method (signposts, not the practice itself): center in posture and breathing;
from heart or belly, invite feelings or images of kindness to radiate through
the whole being, embodying benevolent mother and father energy — recognition
and kindness you may not have received enough as a child. Some use phrases
("May I be free from ignorance… May I not suffer. May I be happy") as
evocations of feeling, not literal formulas. Once established, dwell there;
then extend outward to family, partner, parents (forgiving limitations), people
known and unknown, and eventually those you dislike or who have hurt you. The
practice can extend further — toward all who are oppressed, caught in war or
hatred, or toward the planet itself (streams, forests, animals) — with no
natural limit; it is an ongoing realization of interconnectedness. Loving one
tree, person, or moment can hold all people, places, and suffering in that
moment. Love and kindness are always present, buried below fears, greed, and
the illusion of separateness; invoking them stretches against ignorance like
yoga stretches muscle — in the stretching, we expand and change ourselves and
the world.

**Important:** you are not trying to help anyone or change anything — you are
holding them in awareness, honoring them, wishing them well, opening to pain
with kindness and acceptance. If the practice calls you to act differently,
let those actions embody the same quality.

Kabat-Zinn frames interconnection (John Donne's "no man is an island") as the
basis: becoming a center of love in this moment adds to the world and benefits
self and others. He links Western "epidemic of low self-esteem" to outward
overdevelopment and inward poverty — a contrast with cultures where such
feelings are rare despite hardship.
