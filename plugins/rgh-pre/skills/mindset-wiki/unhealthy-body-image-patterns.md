  Rigid demands about appearance, linking worth to looks, comparison habits,
  and reassurance-seeking maintain body-image distress — distinct from healthy
  motivation to improve fitness or enjoyment.

Unhealthy body image rests on thinking and behavior patterns, not media alone.

**Thinking patterns:**

- Rigid demands about how others must judge your appearance.
- Linking self-worth to attractiveness — beautiful people are not superior or
  guaranteed charmed lives.
- Overrating appearance versus personality, values, humor.
- Overestimating how much others notice or evaluate your looks.
- Unrealistic standards (glamour-model comparisons) breeding chronic inadequacy.

**Behavior patterns:**

- Regular [social comparison](social-comparison-trap.md) on looks.
- Overpreparing before going out; constant dieting/exercising to feel worthwhile.
- Hiding from scrutiny (swimsuits, undressing, low lights).
- Reassurance-seeking and dismissing compliments.
- Checking/examining appearance (mirrors, selfies, touch).
- Vigilance for others' negative judgment; self-focused "inner magnifying glass"
  on perceived flaws.

**Healthy distinction:** improved appearance or fitness should increase
enjoyment and confidence — **not** worth as a human being. Wrong reasons to
change: believing looks make you more worthwhile, pleasing a critical partner,
overcoming jealousy, winning approval. Sound reasons: health, enjoyment,
mobility, reducing pain, genuine desire to reduce self-consciousness.

Self-acceptance and striving can coexist — accept natural shape while working
on fitness; see [unconditional self-acceptance](unconditional-self-acceptance.md).
A whole person exists inside the physical container; external presentation is
the conduit for internal qualities. List admired people who are not
conventionally good-looking to restore proportion.
