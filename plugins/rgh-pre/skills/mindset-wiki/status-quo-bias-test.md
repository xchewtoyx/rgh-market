---
type: concept
title: Status Quo Bias Test
description: >
  Checking whether a current situation would be actively chosen if it
  weren't already the default, to separate genuine preference from mere
  loss-averse inertia.
sources:
  - title: "The Scout Mindset: Why Some People See Things Clearly and Others Don't"
    resource: "The Scout Mindset (Julia Galef), Chapter 5: Noticing Bias"
---

The test's defining question: "if my current situation weren't already
the status quo, would I actively choose it?" This targets loss aversion —
the pain of losing something outweighs the pleasure of an equivalent gain
— which quietly biases people toward whatever already exists, independent
of its actual merits relative to the alternative. A worked example:
someone hesitant to leave a familiar city for a better opportunity
elsewhere can ask whether, if they were already living in the new city
doing the new job, they'd feel tempted to give it up and move back — if
the honest answer is no, the hesitation was defending the status quo
itself, not weighing the actual merits of each option.

This test pairs naturally with [the outsider test](outsider-test.md) —
both strip away the weight of "this is already how things are" — but
targets it more precisely at the specific bias of preferring what exists
simply because giving it up would register as a loss. It's a useful check
for any long-held commitment, belief, or self-story that has never been
actively re-chosen, only continued by default — since default continuation
is not, on its own, evidence that the thing is actually still the best
option.
