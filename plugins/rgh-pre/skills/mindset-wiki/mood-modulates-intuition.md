---
type: concept
title: Mood Modulates Intuition
description: >
  Good mood loosens vigilance and boosts intuitive, creative judgments;
  bad mood tightens analytic scrutiny — so your mental mode tracks
  perceived safety in the environment.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 6"
---

Mood shifts which cluster of mental habits is active. **Good mood** goes
with intuition, creativity, [cognitive ease](cognitive-ease-and-strain.md),
and greater reliance on [fast automatic thinking](fast-and-slow-thinking-heuristic.md)
— but also with lower vigilance and more logical errors. **Bad mood** goes
with suspicion, analytic effort, and [engaged monitoring](engaged-vs-lazy-monitoring.md).

On the Remote Association Test, subjects could judge within two seconds
whether a word triad had a shared association — above chance, before
consciously solving it. Manipulations that increase ease (priming, clear
font, pre-exposure) increased "linked" judgments. Inducing happy mood by
recalling positive life episodes more than doubled an intuition index;
unhappy subjects performed at chance — essentially losing access to the
faint associative signal.

Biologically this fits: good mood signals a safe environment where lowering
guard is fine; bad mood signals possible threat requiring vigilance.

When making important judgments while euphoric or drained, account for the
mode: happiness can make [heuristic substitution](heuristic-substitution.md)
and [affect heuristic](affect-heuristic.md) hits more likely; low mood can
help scrutiny but also narrow [window of tolerance](window-of-tolerance.md)
for social connection. Neither state is "wrong" — each is information about
which checks to add deliberately.
