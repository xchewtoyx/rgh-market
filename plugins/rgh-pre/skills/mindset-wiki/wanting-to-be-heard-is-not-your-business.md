---
type: concept
title: Wanting to Be Heard Is Not Your Business
description: >
  You cannot direct what others hear — only what you say and how you listen
  — so demanding family hear you on your terms keeps loneliness running.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 4"
---

"They should listen to me" fails reality ("There are twelve kids here;
give us a break!"). Believing it produces loneliness and distancing —
"Pretty hard to listen when you're way over there!" Turnarounds: "I should
hear me," "They shouldn't hear me [not unless they do]," "I should hear
them" — hear their song.

"I refuse to be left unheard" becomes "I'm willing to be left unheard" /
"I look forward to being left unheard" — the [judge your neighbor
worksheet](judge-your-neighbor-worksheet.md) willing/look-forward-to pattern
applied to control over others' attention.

"If I want my children to hear me, I'm insane. They're only going to hear
what they hear, not what I say... Direct their hearing. Hopeless." Go tell
the family what you've discovered "so that you can hear it," without
expecting them to listen. This is [staying in your own
business](staying-in-your-own-business.md): whose business is their
hearing? Yours is to speak honestly and inquire into the demand that they
receive it your way.
