---
type: concept
title: Conformity Test
description: >
  Checking whether a belief or preference would survive if the people
  whose agreement currently reinforces it turned out not to hold it,
  revealing how much of its appeal is really social conformity.
sources:
  - title: "The Scout Mindset: Why Some People See Things Clearly and Others Don't"
    resource: "The Scout Mindset (Julia Galef), Chapter 5: Noticing Bias"
---

The test's defining question: "if the people I currently agree with no
longer held this view, would I still hold it — would I feel comfortable
defending it to them directly?" A view or preference can feel like a
firsthand, independently-arrived-at conclusion while actually being held
largely because of who else holds it; imagining that social backing
removed (a trusted person revealing "I was just playing devil's advocate,
I don't actually believe this") often makes the felt strength of the
belief visibly shift in real time, exposing how much of it was conformity
rather than conviction.

This test applies to preferences as much as to factual beliefs — a
personal goal or life choice adopted partly because most people around
you make the same choice can be checked by imagining a world where the
popular default were reversed, and asking whether the goal would still
feel appealing on its own terms. This is directly relevant to [tall poppy
self-downplaying](tall-poppy-self-downplaying.md) and [motivated reasoning
for belonging](motivated-reasoning-for-belonging.md): the conformity test
is the concrete method for telling whether a stance (including a stance
toward one's own ability or accomplishments) is actually held, or is being
held to stay aligned with a group.
