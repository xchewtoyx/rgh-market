[Non-striving in meditation](non-striving-in-meditation.md) means broadening
focus beyond a particular goal and meeting "unacceptable" emotions with
acceptance rather than rejecting them. Mindfulness is "hardly passive
resignation" — it intentionally welcomes and turns toward whatever arises,
including inner experiences we would normally fight or escape.

Approach and avoidance mechanisms are wired into the brain. Mindfulness
embodies approach — interest, openness, curiosity (from Latin *curare*, "to
care for"), goodwill, and compassion. Christina Feldman: "The quality of
mindfulness is not a neutral or blank presence. True mindfulness is imbued
with warmth, compassion, and interest... where there is interest, a natural,
unforced attention follows." It is impossible to hate or fear anything or
anyone we truly understand.

The approach attitude is an antidote to instinctive avoidance that fuels
[rumination](rumination.md), offering a new way to relate to self and world
under stress. Regaining intentional control of attention rescues us from
being stuck in unhappiness. Pairs with [generosity in mindfulness
practice](generosity-in-mindfulness-practice.md) and [trust in mindfulness
practice](trust-in-mindfulness-practice.md).

Thich Nhat Hanh: "Peace can exist only in the present moment... If you are
not living in peace at this moment, you will never be able to. If you truly
want to be at peace, you must be at peace right now. Otherwise, there is
only 'the hope of peace some day.'"
