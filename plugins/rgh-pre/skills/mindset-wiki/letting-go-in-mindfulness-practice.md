type: concept
title: Letting Go in Mindfulness Practice
description: >
  Letting go is a conscious decision to release clinging to ideas, outcomes,
  and likes/dislikes — opening the palm so stillness and wisdom can arise in
  the present moment as it actually is.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 3: Letting Go"
---

Letting go means ceasing to cling to anything — an idea, a thing, an event,
a particular time, view, or desire. It is "a conscious decision to release
with full acceptance into the stream of present moments as they are
unfolding" — giving up coercing, resisting, or struggling in exchange for
allowing things to be as they are without getting caught in attraction or
rejection, the intrinsic stickiness of wanting, liking, and disliking. Like
opening your palm to unhand something you've been holding.

This applies to outer events and to holding on with the mind — clinging to
narrow views and self-serving hopes. Letting go means becoming transparent
to the strong pull of likes and dislikes and the unawareness that draws us
to cling — allowing fears and insecurities to play out in full awareness,
recognizing the lenses slipped unconsciously between observer and observed
that filter and bend our view (see [projection filters on
perception](projection-filters-on-perception.md)).

"Stillness, insight, and wisdom arise only when we can settle into being
complete in this moment, without having to seek or hold on to or reject
anything." Test it: when part of you wants to hold on, try letting go and
see whether it brings deeper satisfaction than clinging.

Central to [non-striving in meditation](non-striving-in-meditation.md) and
[doing mode and being mode](doing-mode-being-mode.md): letting go is how
being mode releases the grip of doing-mode fixing.
