---
type: concept
title: Affect Heuristic
description: >
  Judgments and decisions often consult emotion first — how strongly you
  like or dislike something — and that feeling substitutes for harder
  factual evaluation of risks and benefits.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 14"
---

People often answer "What do I think about it?" by consulting "How do I
feel about it?" — an instance of [heuristic
substitution](heuristic-substitution.md). Frightening thoughts and images
occur with particular ease, and fluent, vivid thoughts of danger
exacerbate fear — linking the [availability
heuristic](availability-heuristic.md) to emotional reaction.

In Slovic's surveys of technologies (fluoridation, chemical plants,
preservatives, cars), messages emphasizing either benefits or low risk
changed the emotional appeal — and, strikingly, shifted beliefs about the
*other* dimension without new evidence. People told only about benefits
also lowered perceived risks; people told only about mild risks also raised
perceived benefits. "The emotional tail wags the rational dog." The affect
heuristic simplifies life into a tidier world than reality: good
technologies have few costs, bad technologies have no benefits — whereas
real tradeoffs are often painful.

Ease-of-retrieval biases are stronger when System 2 is less engaged: during
another effortful task, in a good mood, among intuitive-trust scorers, when
reminded of a time you had power, or among knowledgeable novices (versus
true experts). Disaster preparedness shows the same pattern: concern and
insurance spike after earthquakes or floods, then memories dim and diligence
fades — protective action is usually designed only for the worst disaster
actually experienced.

When a strong gut reaction is driving a risk or benefit judgment, ask
whether you're evaluating evidence separately or letting one feeling color
both sides — pair with [emotional bias in
judgment](emotional-bias-in-judgment.md) and [naming cognitive
distortions](naming-cognitive-distortions.md).
