---
type: concept
title: Personalizing as Cognitive Distortion
description: >
  Interpreting events as directly about you while overlooking other
  contributing factors — leading to unwarranted hurt or guilt when people's
  behavior often reflects their own circumstances.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies (Rhena Branch, Rob Willson), ch. 2: Personalizing"
---

Personalizing interprets events as related to you personally while overlooking
other, unrelated factors — unwarranted hurt or guilt. Unable to cheer up an
upset friend → "I'm letting them down, a good friend could fix this"; friend
leaves quickly after a hurried hello → "they're avoiding me, I must have
offended them."

Countermeasures: consider what else might contribute outside your control (the
friend may have lost their job, be depressed, be in a hurry, be having a
difficult day); don't assume a response relates directly to you. People vastly
underestimate how much others have going on in their own lives.

Mindfulness framing: [taking things less personally](taking-things-less-personally.md)
and [selfing](selfing.md). CBT framing: listed in [naming cognitive
distortions](naming-cognitive-distortions.md).
