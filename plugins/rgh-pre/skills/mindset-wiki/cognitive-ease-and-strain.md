---
type: concept
title: Cognitive Ease and Strain
description: >
  Easy-to-process stimuli feel good and bias judgment toward trust and
  liking; strain mobilizes effortful thinking — so font difficulty can
  improve logic-test scores by waking up monitoring.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 6"
---

Cognitive strain and [effortful monitoring](engaged-vs-lazy-monitoring.md)
reinforce each other: strain is felt when System 2's operations engage, and
experiencing strain — from any source — tends to mobilize System 2, shifting
from casual intuition toward a more analytic mode.

**CRT font experiment:** On Shane Frederick's Cognitive Reflection Test
(bat-and-ball, widgets, lily pads), 90% of Princeton students made at least
one mistake in normal font versus 35% in a small, washed-out gray font that
was legible but strain-inducing. Harder reading improved performance by
prompting rejection of [fast intuitive wrong answers](fast-and-slow-thinking-heuristic.md).

**Pleasure of ease:** Subliminally priming object outlines before brief
exposures produced faint smiles and relaxed brows (facial EMG) when pictures
were easier to recognize — cognitive ease is intrinsically pleasant in
System 1. Easily pronounced company names, stock symbols, and words evoke
more favorable attitudes; investors even expect higher returns from
fluent-named stocks.

**Coherence is pleasurable:** Reading a coherent word triad (salt / deep /
foam → sea) produces a slight smile versus an incoherent triad, even when
subjects are not solving the puzzle — ease accompanying coherence feels good
on its own.

In self-regulation, notice when something "feels right" because it is
fluent, familiar, or easy to picture — not because evidence supports it.
Pair with [retrieval fluency versus
content](retrieval-fluency-vs-content.md) and [heuristic
substitution](heuristic-substitution.md). Deliberately adding friction
(slower reading, writing out steps) can be a tool when you suspect lazy
endorsement of a first impression.
