  it signals emerging difficulty before the mind catches up, prompting a
  breathing space or gentle holding of what arises.

The [internal barometer](experiential-avoidance.md) gives a continuous
pleasant/unpleasant/neutral readout. The **body barometer** (Trish Bartley)
makes that readout concrete for daily life:

1. Choose a part of the body — preferably in the trunk (chest, abdomen, or
   between) — especially sensitive to stress; you can rest a hand there.
2. Tune in regularly. Under pressure, anxiety, or irritability you may notice
   tension, tightness, shakiness, or discomfort varying with difficulty level.
3. With practice, subtle sensations signal something emerging long before the
   mind registers it — then come to the breath, do a [three-minute breathing
   space](three-minute-breathing-space.md), or simply monitor sensations
   moment by moment.

The challenge is remembering to check; a specific reminder and a few daily
checks for a couple of weeks establish it as a readily available resource.
Combining a general body area with a particular sensation pattern links
physical sensation to [feeling tone and reactivity](feeling-tone-and-reactivity.md)
and disrupts the cycles that fuel unhappiness.

Pairs with [direct sensing versus thinking about the
body](direct-sensing-vs-thinking-about-body.md).
