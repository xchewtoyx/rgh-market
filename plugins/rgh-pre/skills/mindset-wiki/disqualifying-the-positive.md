---
type: concept
title: Disqualifying the Positive
description: >
  Actively transforming positive events into something neutral or negative —
  discounting achievements and compliments so a negative self-belief survives
  even against overwhelming contrary evidence.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies (Rhena Branch, Rob Willson), ch. 2: Disqualifying the Positive"
---

Related to [mental filtering](brain-filters-information.md) but goes further:
not merely ignoring positive data but actively discounting it. A work promotion
dismissed as "anyone could get this"; a compliment dismissed as pity — "they're
only saying that because they feel sorry for me."

Countermeasures: become aware of responses to positive data; practice
acknowledging and accepting it (attribute the promotion partly to your hard
work); accept compliments graciously with a simple thank-you — rejecting a
sincere compliment is like turning down a gift; log overlooked positive data;
consider you might be wrong to doubt the compliment.

Repeated disqualification sustains a negative belief even against overwhelming
positive evidence. Common in [fixed mindset](fixed-mindset.md) patterns where
one failure is weighted more than many successes.
