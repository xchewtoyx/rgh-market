---
type: concept
title: Decorrelating Error
description: >
  When combining multiple judgments or evidence sources, keep them
  independent before aggregation so errors do not reinforce each other
  and averaging can actually reduce noise.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 8"
---

Individual estimates are often poor, but pooled or averaged judgments can
be remarkably accurate when errors are independent and uncorrelated —
individual mistakes tend to cancel out. This "magic of error reduction"
only works when observations are genuinely independent. Shared systematic
bias will not average away, and letting observers influence each other
effectively shrinks the effective sample size and degrades precision.

Kahneman's essay-grading fix illustrates the principle at personal scale:
after switching from grading one student's full booklet at a time to
grading all students' answers to each question before moving on, he often
found he'd given a top grade to one essay and a poor grade to the same
student's next — real inconsistency that sequential grading had hidden
behind a [halo effect](halo-effect.md). The discomfort of revealed
inconsistency is a good sign: earlier felt consistency was spurious.

The general rule: to derive the most useful information from multiple
sources of evidence, try to make those sources independent of each other
before they are combined or discussed. Good police procedure keeps
witnesses from discussing an event before testifying — not only to
prevent collusion but to prevent unbiased witnesses from converging on
similar errors, which reduces the total information value of their
testimony. For meetings and committees: before an issue is discussed, each
member should independently write a brief summary of their position. Open
discussion gives disproportionate weight to whoever speaks early and
assertively, causing others to line up behind them and correlating errors
that would have stayed diverse if captured first in private.
