---
type: concept
title: Fear as a Money Motivator
description: >
  The belief that fear and stress are necessary to earn or keep money keeps
  you sick with worry even when accounts are full — and blocks seeing that
  you already have enough for right now.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 6"
---

"I need fear and stress to motivate me" is a belief worth [inquiry as
thought-testing](inquiry-as-thought-testing.md): who would you be without
it? Katie describes being "sick with the fear" of losing money even when
she had a lot — concluding no amount of money is worth that kind of stress.

Living with "I need my money to be safe and secure" is hopeless in practice:
banks fail, markets crash, currencies deflate, people break promises. Even
millions bring no security under that story. After inquiry, she noticed she
"always had the perfect amount of money for me right now, even when I had
little or none." Without the safety thought, you might notice laws of
generosity — letting money go out fearlessly and come back fearlessly.
"You don't ever need more money than you have."

This challenges [self-criticism as a healthy
motivator](self-criticism-is-not-a-healthy-motivator.md) in the financial
domain: fear-driven earning is not the only path. See [happiness is a clear
mind](happiness-is-a-clear-mind.md).
