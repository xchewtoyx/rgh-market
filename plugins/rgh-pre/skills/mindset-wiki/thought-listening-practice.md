---
type: concept
title: Thought-Listening Practice
description: >
  A structured sitting practice — acknowledge each thought once, out loud
  and without analysis, and refuse to let any thought repeat — that trains
  the brain out of incessant rumination by exhausting its need to keep
  raising the same unresolved thought.
sources:
  - title: That Little Voice in Your Head
    resource: "That Little Voice in Your Head (Mo Gawdat), Chapter 6: Talk Talk Talk"
---

Distinct from open-ended meditation, this practice treats the inner voice
as something being deliberately listened to rather than suppressed or
followed. Sit quietly for an extended period (originally described as
around 25 minutes) and let thoughts arise freely, applying two rules: each
thought gets briefly acknowledged out loud, without analyzing or trying to
resolve it, then attention explicitly asks for the next one; and no
thought is allowed to repeat — if the same thought resurfaces, that
repetition is named out loud and a new thought is requested instead.
Treating the source of the thoughts as a separate entity being listened
to (rather than as oneself) reinforces [thought
defusion](thought-defusion.md) — "my brain is not me" — while doing the
practice.

The expected arc is that an initial chaotic rush of thoughts organizes
into a more orderly sequence once the mind notices it is being listened
to, then thins out once repeated thoughts are refused a repeat showing,
eventually producing a period of quiet. This directly targets
[rumination](rumination.md) at its mechanism: a ruminated thought persists
because it keeps being replayed without acknowledgment; a thought that has
been consciously heard once, and then blocked from repeating, has nothing
further to accomplish by resurfacing.
