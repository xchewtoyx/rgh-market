  emotional disturbance when reality does not comply.

Making demands embeds rigid, extreme beliefs containing "must," "should,"
"need," "ought," "got to," "have to," directed at self, others, or the world.
Ellis placed demands at the heart of emotional problems. Examples: "I must
have everyone's approval" → chronic social anxiety; "others ought to be as kind
as I am" → hurt when they aren't; "I absolutely should never let people down" →
chronic overwork and depression.

Countermeasures: replace must/need/should with prefer/wish/want; limit
approval-seeking (approval as preference, not dire need); accept the world
does not play by your rules and others have their own rulebooks; retain
standards and preferences but discard rigid demands about how things "have to"
be. Rigid demands leave no margin for deviation — trees that bend in a storm
survive better than rigid ones. Flexibility is not being a pushover.
