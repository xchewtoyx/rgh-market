---
type: concept
title: Extra Suffering From Should
description: >
  The fact of someone's incompetence may be manageable; the added belief
  that they should be competent is what fuels the emotional storm.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 6"
---

Gary judged employee Frank incompetent. "Frank should be competent" — tested
against experience: in Gary's experience, he isn't. "That's the only place
you can sanely come from: reality... when you understand this, you become a
lover of reality."

The [proof-of-truth technique](proof-of-truth-technique.md) on "he should be
competent" yields no real proof — "It's all a lie." Turnarounds: "Frank
should not be competent" (not yet — reality for now); "I should be competent"
(carry his portion if you want the job done).

Gary's synthesis: extra suffering came from the added belief "he should be
competent," not from incompetence itself — "By adding that he should be
competent, I work myself up into a fucking emotional tizzy." Without the
should, options open naturally — fire him for a better fit, or notice
unrecognized competence.

Katie: when you see it's only your thinking to work with, every problem
becomes a joy to bring to inquiry. Frank can be "the expert who brings you
to the highest level of competence" — a teacher. "In the moment you see him
as incompetent, you're incompetent. He's perfectly competent for what he
was supposed to bring you: clarity."

Pair with [arguing with reality](arguing-with-reality.md) and [being right
versus being free](being-right-vs-being-free.md).
