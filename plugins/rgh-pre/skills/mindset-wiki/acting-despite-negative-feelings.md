---
type: concept
title: Acting Despite Negative Feelings
description: >
  Constructive action after a setback does not require waiting to feel
  better first — a plan can and should be carried out regardless of the
  emotional state it's carried out in.
sources:
  - title: "Mindset: The New Psychology of Success"
    resource: "Mindset: The New Psychology of Success (Carol S. Dweck), Chapter 8: Changing Mindsets"
---

There's a common assumption that constructive action has to wait until
distress has resolved — that a person needs to feel steadier before they
can productively act. Growth-mindset-oriented people who were also
depressed showed the opposite pattern: the worse they felt, the more (not
less) they confronted problems and kept up their obligations (see
[rumination](rumination.md) for the contrasting fixed-mindset pattern,
where worse mood produced withdrawal). Feeling bad and acting
constructively are not mutually exclusive — a concrete plan (see [concrete
implementation plans](concrete-implementation-plans.md)) can be executed
on a day when the underlying feeling hasn't lifted at all.

The practical stance this supports is "the plan is the plan" — the
existence of ongoing anxiety, self-doubt, or a bad mood is not evidence
that the plan is wrong or that it isn't the right day to execute it; it's
simply a feeling running in parallel with the action. This doesn't mean
suppressing or ignoring the feeling (see [distress
tolerance](distress-tolerance.md) for staying in genuine contact with it)
— it means not treating "I don't feel ready" as a gate that has to open
before movement is allowed to happen.
