---
type: concept
title: Confirmation Bias
description: >
  The brain preferentially notices and remembers evidence that supports
  an existing belief while downplaying evidence against it, so a belief
  can feel well-supported by "the evidence" while the evidence was
  actually filtered by the belief itself.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 4, entry 18: Beware of Confirmation Bias"
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 8"
---

Confirmation bias is an efficient but distorting shortcut: once a belief
is in place, attention preferentially surfaces information that confirms
it and lets contradicting information pass by less noticed.

Understanding a claim also tends to begin with a brief attempt to believe
it — constructing the best possible interpretation automatically — and
only afterward can effortful doubt unbelieve it. When the effortful layer
is busy, tired, or depleted (see [mental energy
budget](mental-energy-budget.md)), the default "believe first" posture
wins more often. Asking "Is Sam friendly?" retrieves different memories
than asking "Is Sam unfriendly?" — a deliberate search strategy that still
mostly seeks compatible evidence rather than falsifying it. This runs
directly on self-beliefs — anxiety primes attention to notice
anxiety-confirming signals, a belief in one's own incompetence primes
attention to catch and remember mistakes while letting successes register
less strongly, a suspicion that someone dislikes you primes a search for
slights that would otherwise go unnoticed. The felt sense that "the
evidence clearly supports this" can therefore be an artifact of which
evidence got noticed in the first place, not an honest tally.

This is the same underlying mechanism as [soldier
mindset](soldier-mindset.md), applied specifically to ongoing self-belief
rather than to a single motivated conclusion. The practical countermeasure
is two steps: name the filter explicitly ("I notice I might be looking
for evidence that confirms X"), then deliberately seek out information or
perspectives that would challenge the belief rather than only ones that
support it — actively looking for disconfirming evidence rather than
waiting for it to surface on its own, since the bias specifically
suppresses its visibility.
