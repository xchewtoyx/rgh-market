**Mental reviewing** — sometimes called a postmortem — is going over a
conversation, presentation, date, or party interaction in your head after
it ends. It is central to social and performance anxiety, but common even
when you are only slightly outside your comfort zone at work or socially.

The review is usually negatively biased by embarrassment, self-criticism,
and concern about others' perceptions. That fuels a worse view of yourself
as a social actor, primes anxiety before the next similar encounter,
increases self-consciousness, and drives another review — a self-
perpetuating cycle distinct from [rumination](rumination.md) about life
events, though the mechanics overlap.

When you notice reviewing after social or work encounters, ask whether it
has ever actually helped. Even if the honest answer is no, a brief cost-
benefit tally of pros and cons can make the mind less willing to fall into
the trap again. Pair with [catching automatic
thoughts](catching-automatic-thoughts.md) and [naming cognitive
distortions](naming-cognitive-distortions.md) when the replay generates
[mind reading](mind-reading-as-cognitive-distortion.md) about what others
thought.
