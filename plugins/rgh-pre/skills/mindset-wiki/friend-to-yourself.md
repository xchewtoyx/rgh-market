title: Friend to Yourself
description: >
  Philosophical maturity includes extending to yourself the kindness and
  patience you would offer a friend — Seneca's benchmark for real progress.
sources:
  - title: "Discipline Is Destiny: The Power of Self-Control"
    resource: "Discipline Is Destiny (Ryan Holiday), ch. 15"
---

Seneca judged a lifetime's philosophical progress by one measure: "I have
begun to be a friend to myself." Stoicism is described as the kindest,
gentlest school — loving of humankind — "but of all people," including
yourself.

Being a friend to yourself means the same moves you'd make for someone you
care about: reassurance when they fail, honest advice without contempt,
refusing to let them call themselves worthless or quit. This is not
lowering standards — it is [self-discipline without self-
hatred](self-discipline-without-self-hatred.md). Cleanthes's interruption
of a self-berating stranger — "you're not talking to a bad man" — models
the stance outwardly.

Pairs with [attuned self-compassion
statements](self-compassion-statements.md) and [internalized ableism
guilt](internalized-ableism-guilt.md) when harsh standards were absorbed
from outside.
