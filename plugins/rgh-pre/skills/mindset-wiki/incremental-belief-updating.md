---
type: concept
title: Incremental Belief Updating
description: >
  Revising confidence in small steps as new evidence arrives, rather than
  treating a belief as all-or-nothing, makes updating far less stressful
  because each new piece of contrary information only requires a small
  adjustment instead of a wholesale reversal.
sources:
  - title: "The Scout Mindset: Why Some People See Things Clearly and Others Don't"
    resource: "The Scout Mindset (Julia Galef), Chapter 10: How to Be Wrong"
---

People who are unusually good at revising their views — studied directly
in forecasting-accuracy research, where top performers dramatically
outperformed both domain experts and each other — share a specific habit:
they adjust confidence gradually and often (sometimes dozens of times on
a single question) rather than holding a belief at fixed confidence until
some threshold forces a dramatic flip. A confidence level nudged from 80%
to 70% in response to one new fact is a minor correction; a belief held
rigidly at "definitely true" until it's forced all the way to "definitely
false" turns every piece of contrary evidence into a high-stakes threat to
the whole position, which is exactly the condition that makes people
defend a position past the point the evidence supports.

This is the same underlying move as [all-or-nothing
thinking](all-or-nothing-thinking.md)'s "gray thinking" countermeasure,
applied to beliefs and opinions rather than to self-judgment: treating
confidence as a dial that moves in small increments, not a switch that's
either fully on or fully off. People already do this instinctively in
some domains (confidence about a job offer naturally drifts up and down
with each new signal) but rarely apply the same fluidity to charged
topics — political views, deeply held opinions, self-assessments — where
the binary framing ("I was right" / "I was wrong") makes any movement
feel like a bigger event than it needs to be. Practicing incremental
updating on lower-stakes beliefs builds the habit of treating confidence
as continuously adjustable, which makes it available on the higher-stakes
topics where it matters most.
