---
type: concept
title: Positive Data Log
description: >
  A running record of evidence supporting a new healthy belief corrects the
  bias that keeps old beliefs well-fed — deliberately gathering confirming
  data strengthens and maintains conviction over months or years.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition (Branch, Willson), ch. 20"
---

Unhelpful beliefs soak up confirming evidence and discount disconfirming
evidence. A **positive data log** reverses the bias for the new belief:

1. Write the new belief at the top.
2. Record evidence it is helpful, including changes in emotions and behavior.
3. Record positive reactions from others when you act on the new belief.
4. Record experiences contradicting the old belief — be specific, including
   small details (a vendor's small talk counts against "I'm unlikable").
5. Keep collecting until the notebook is full; if the old belief still feels
   true, start with daily evidence it is not 100% true 100% of the time.

The log serves dual purposes: reminds you of good results from belief change,
and supports self-compassion during relapses — setbacks need only be temporary
when [practice makes imperfect](practice-makes-imperfect.md). Refer to it often
(daily or several times daily) while new beliefs are bedding down; keep it
accessible on desk, phone, or in your bag.

Complements [zigzag technique](zigzag-technique.md) and behavioral tests of
new beliefs via [acting as if for beliefs](acting-as-if-for-beliefs.md).
