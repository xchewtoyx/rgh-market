title: Reading Dialogues as Your Own Work
description: >
  When studying inquiry transcripts, read as if each worksheet were yours —
  get emotionally close to the participant's answers and discover where you
  have lived the same pattern.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 3"
---

Workshop dialogues are practice material, not entertainment about other
people's problems. Read as if the [Judge-Your-Neighbor
Worksheet](judge-your-neighbor-worksheet.md) had been written by you. Do
not only read the participant's answers — go inside and find your own.
Get as emotionally involved and as close to them as you can. Discover
where and when you have experienced what you are reading about.

Because [stressful thoughts are
universal](universal-stressful-thoughts.md), substitution works: if a
dialogue does not land, swap in a person who matters in your life and
run the same inquiry on your version of the story. [Self-facilitation of
inquiry](self-facilitation-of-inquiry.md) depends on this active
identification — watching someone else questioned while you question
yourself in parallel.

Katie's own answers and personal stories in the transcripts are from her
experience, not prescriptions for how you should live. If anything reads
as cold, uncaring, or unkind, treat it as material for your own inquiry
rather than instruction — see [inquiry does not condone
harm](inquiry-does-not-condone-harm.md).
