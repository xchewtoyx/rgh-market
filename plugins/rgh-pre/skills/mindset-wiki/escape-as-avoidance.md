Beyond happiness and unhappiness there is a third state: escape — using
fun, pleasure, or distraction to stop engaging with a difficult feeling
rather than working through it. It functions like a painkiller: it
suppresses the signal without addressing what generated it, can become
addictive, and — because it never resolves the underlying
[pain](pain-vs-suffering.md) — blocks the possibility of lasting
contentment. The tell is that the relief only lasts as long as the
distraction does, and the original difficulty is unchanged when it ends.

This differs from healthy [distress tolerance](distress-tolerance.md),
which aims to sit with a difficult feeling without being overwhelmed by
it; escape instead avoids contact with the feeling altogether.

A useful test for whether a chosen rest or coping activity is genuine
recovery or disguised escape: does it actually leave you rejuvenated
afterward, or only distracted for as long as it lasts and equally or more
drained once it ends? "Restorative rest" (an absorbing hobby, real
connection, something that matches personal rather than socially-expected
notions of relaxation) tends to pass this test; "cheap rest" (passive
consumption chosen for its numbing effect rather than its content) tends
to fail it even when it superficially looks similar from the outside —
the distinguishing question is the actual after-effect, not the activity
itself.

This doesn't make fun or pleasure something to avoid — the distinction is
between fun used as a permanent substitute for resolving what's actually
wrong, and fun used deliberately in two narrower, "wise" roles: as a
short, occasional circuit-breaker for a spiraling negative-thought episode
(a workout, music, a brief pleasant distraction that buys enough calm to
think more clearly), or as a regular, bounded maintenance practice — a
personal quota of scheduled, healthy pleasures kept small and consistent
rather than escalating. The difference from escape is that both uses stay
brief and don't get asked to do the work of actually resolving anything;
the moment fun starts being relied on as the whole strategy for feeling
better, rather than a short reset before addressing the actual cause, it
has crossed back into escape.
