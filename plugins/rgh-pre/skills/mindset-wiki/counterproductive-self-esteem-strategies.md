type: concept
title: Counterproductive Self-Esteem Strategies
description: >
  Ten common traps for managing low self-esteem — putting others down,
  proving superiority, chasing approval, perfectionism, avoidance, and
  control — dig the hole deeper rather than building genuine self-respect.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition (Branch, Willson), ch. 26"
---

Using these strategies to boost self-esteem is like digging out of a hole —
first realize you are digging deeper and put down the shovel.

1. **Putting others down** — temporary superiority does not fix self-attitude.
2. **Thinking you're special or superior** — masks inferiority, alienates;
   "if I'm not different, I'm nothing" is all-or-nothing thinking.
3. **Trying to get everyone to like you** — breeds anxiety; damages self-esteem
   when you let people walk over you.
4. **Placing yourself above criticism** — perfectionism and defensiveness;
   perfection is unreachable.
5. **Avoiding failure, disapproval, rejection** — papering over cracks postpones
   change.
6. **Avoiding emotions** — harms relationships and psychological health.
7. **Controlling others to feel significant** — reflects lack of control.
8. **Over-defending self-worth** — compulsive outrage over minor slights.
9. **Confusing responsibility with blame** — attributing problems to genetics,
   past, or others blocks change.
10. **Believing self-esteem is the road to happiness** — neglects relationships,
    compassion, balanced beliefs about others and world.

**Healthier directions:** self-respect over approval; human fallibility without
self-condemnation; appropriate assertion; accepting emotions; taking personal
responsibility for what keeps problems going; broader view including compassion,
relationships, values, and [distress tolerance](distress-tolerance.md).

Many traps stem from childhood messages ("Be the best," "Never admit you're
wrong," "Boys don't cry") — adults can reevaluate toward self-acceptance.
