---
type: concept
title: Conditioning and Context Mismatch
description: >
  A belief or emotional reaction formed under one set of circumstances
  often persists and gets misapplied long after the circumstances that
  produced it have changed, producing reactions that no longer fit reality.
sources:
  - title: That Little Voice in Your Head
    resource: "That Little Voice in Your Head (Mo Gawdat), Chapter 2: Garbage In..."
---

Conditioning is the accumulated sum of beliefs and past experience that
shapes perception, and it shapes perception more strongly than actual
present-moment circumstances do. The failure mode is not that the
conditioning was wrong when it formed — it may have been an accurate,
protective response to its original context — but that it keeps firing in
a new context where it no longer applies. A person conditioned by real
scarcity or danger in childhood can feel chronically unsafe as an adult in
objectively secure circumstances, because the conditioned response is
being run against the wrong context.

Conditioned beliefs also cascade: a single inherited rule (e.g. "success
matters more than happiness") generates a chain of corollary beliefs that
can end up dominating decisions for years without ever being independently
examined. Because a conditioned belief feels like settled fact rather than
an inherited rule, spotting it requires deliberately treating long-held
beliefs as hypotheses to test rather than as given truths — see [testing a
limiting belief](testing-a-limiting-belief.md) for a concrete technique,
and [recycled thoughts](recycled-thoughts.md) for the related pattern of
self-generated beliefs that resurface disguised as fact.
