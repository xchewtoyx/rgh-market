title: Generosity in Mindfulness Practice
description: >
  Generosity in meditation is inward giving of presence, vitality, and
  self-acceptance — sanding down fear-based self-cherishing rather than
  mindless outward giving for approval or control.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 3: Generosity"
---

Generosity is an attitudinal foundation for mindfulness practice alongside
[patience](patience-in-mindfulness-practice.md), [letting go](letting-go-in-mindfulness-practice.md),
[non-judging](non-judging-attitude-in-meditation.md), and [trust](trust-in-mindfulness-practice.md)
— usable as a vehicle for deep self-observation and inquiry as well as an
exercise in giving.

Start with yourself: give gifts that may be true blessings — self-acceptance,
unstructured time — and practice feeling deserving enough to receive them
without obligation. Get in touch with an inner core rich beyond reckoning,
let it radiate outward, and experiment with giving that energy away in small
ways toward self and others with no thought of gain or return. "Give more than
you think you can, trusting that you are richer than you think" — kingly
giving. This is not only money or possessions but sharing the fullness of
your being: enthusiasm, vitality, spirit, trust, openness — above all,
presence.

Notice resistance to giving — worries about the future, giving too much,
lack of appreciation, exhaustion, or not having enough yourself. These are
often forms of inertia, constriction, and fear-based self-protection — rough
edges of self-cherishing that rub against the world and cause pain, distance,
and diminishment. Giving sands down such rough edges and increases mindfulness
of inner wealth.

Caution: mindless giving is never healthy. Some giving is fear and lack of
confidence — hiding from pain, making others dependent, or giving so constantly
it is taken for granted. Scrutinize motives. It is not necessary to give
everything — "above all, generosity is an inward giving, a feeling state, a
willingness to share your own being with the world." Trust instincts, walk the
edge, take some risks — perhaps giving less, differently, to yourself first,
or a bit more than you think you can while letting go of expectations of return.
Initiate giving rather than waiting to be asked and observe the effects —
often greater clarity about self and relationships, and more energy rather
than less.

Pairs with [mindfulness practice for its own sake](mindfulness-practice-for-its-own-sake.md):
giving presence without performance or return.
