When the mind is too driven to sit still, use walking sensations as the anchor.
Mindful walking has accompanied breath practice since ancient times and is a
complete meditation in its own right — "meditation in motion."

Find a private lane; stand with feet parallel, knees unlocked, soft gaze. Bring
awareness to soles contacting ground; lift, move, and place each foot slowly,
noticing leg muscles and weight shifts. Expand to breath and whole body walking.
At the lane end, stop, turn with awareness, continue. When the mind wanders,
gently return to the chosen anchor; if very agitated, stop, stand, reconnect
with [breath as attention anchor](breath-as-attention-anchor.md), then resume.

Start slower than usual; experiment with faster speeds once settled — or begin
fast when agitated and let pace slow naturally. Small steps; feet know where they
are. Ten to fifteen minutes or longer. No destination — walking the same short
path embodies [beginning again in meditation](beginning-again-in-meditation.md):
nowhere to go, nothing to attain, only this step.

Transfer the quality into everyday walking or commuting: breathe with steps, pause
in the car aware of breathing before getting out. Slowing the body can help settle
even when you could walk quickly with awareness — subjective minutes lengthen when
present.

Moving practices (tai chi, yoga, mindful running) share the same shift from
mental rut to embodied presence when engaged with intentional attention.

**Formal walking** attends to footfall as a whole or to shifting, moving,
placing segments — coupled with breath. Having no place to go makes it easier
to be where you are. Same distractions as sitting are worked with the same way.
"Walking is stillness in motion, flowing mindfulness." Informal walking: sidewalk,
corridor, hiking, walking a dog — intention to recall you are here in your body.
"If you miss the here, you are likely also to miss the there."

**Standing meditation:** stand near trees; feel feet developing roots; body
sways gently; stay when impulse to move arises. Trees model being in touch —
roots with ground, trunk with air, leaves with wind.

**Lying-down meditation** risks sleep but may yield more restful sleep and
wakeful first moments. Allows deepest muscular letting go; whole-body awareness
reclaims the body as locus of being — you are not just a resident of your head.
The clinic's forty-five-minute scan moves attention through regions, directing
breath into a region on the inbreath and letting it dissolve on the outbreath.

**Emotional body:** lying-down practice accesses metaphorical heart — constriction,
grief, loneliness, love, joy. Solar plexus carries centeredness and vitality;
throat vocalizes emotions and reveals tonal qualities of speech. Honor "gut"
feelings; invite problematic regions back into membership with the whole body.
