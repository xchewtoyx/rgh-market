---
type: concept
title: Decision Paralysis
description: >
  Endless deliberation is often deferred consequences in disguise — even
  not choosing is a choice that surrenders agency and momentum.
sources:
  - title: "Courage Is Calling: Fortune Favors the Brave"
    resource: "Courage Is Calling (Ryan Holiday), ch. 6"
---

What scares us about decisions is often fear of being wrong, unintended
consequences, disagreement — the "what if" spiral. What feels like weighing
options can be paralysis by analysis while "somebody else is making progress."

A Spartan king marched across Greece asking each nation friendship or
enmity; one king deliberated so long the choice was made for him — "Let him
consider it, then, while we just march on." Even declining to decide is a
decision: "you are voting for the status quo... voting to give up your own
agency."

Kierkegaard: "What cowardice fears most of all is the making of a resolution,
for a resolution instantly dissipates the mist." Deliberation defers
consequences. "Can't lose if you don't choose?" — false; you lose the moment,
momentum, and ability to look yourself in the mirror.

At the top, "there are no easy choices. All are between evils, the
consequences of which are hard to judge" (Dean Acheson) — but waiting for
certainty is its own evil. Pair with [fear-setting](fear-setting.md) to
define dread without freezing, and [paralysis before
obstacles](paralysis-before-obstacles.md) when the block is situational
rather than deliberative.
