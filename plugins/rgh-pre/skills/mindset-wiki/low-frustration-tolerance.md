Low frustration tolerance rates something merely difficult as intolerable,
magnifies discomfort, and fails to tolerate temporary discomfort even when
doing so serves longer-term benefit. Examples: procrastinating until last-minute
lower-quality effort because starting feels like "too much hassle"; abandoning
graduated exposure to travel anxiety with "this is horrible, I can't stand it";
venting anger immediately rather than tolerating restraint, risking regrettable
communication.

"Can't stand it" self-talk increases focus on discomfort and leads you to
underestimate your ability to cope. Countermeasures: push yourself to do
uncomfortable things anyway; ask whether you've withstood similar feelings
before; experiment with deliberately tolerating frustration to build emotional
control. The goal is cultivating high frustration tolerance.

Overlaps [distress tolerance](distress-tolerance.md) — the mindfulness/CBT skill
of bearing discomfort without immediate escape — and [acting despite negative
feelings](acting-despite-negative-feelings.md).

**High frustration tolerance (HFT)** is the counterpart: willingness to
tolerate short-term discomfort or hard work toward a longer-term goal.
[Unconditional self-acceptance](unconditional-self-acceptance.md) often
requires HFT — self-condemnation and resignation ("this is just the way I
am, I may as well give up") feel easier in the moment but block improvement;
accepting yourself as worth the effort makes enrolling in the IT course or
seeking help for addiction more likely than hiding in shame.
