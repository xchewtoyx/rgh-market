---
type: concept
title: Concrete Implementation Plans
description: >
  A vague intention to act rarely produces action; specifying exactly
  when, where, and how a planned action will happen makes it dramatically
  more likely to actually occur.
sources:
  - title: "Mindset: The New Psychology of Success"
    resource: "Mindset: The New Psychology of Success (Carol S. Dweck), Chapter 8: Changing Mindsets"
---

Vague vowing ("I'll do it tomorrow," "I'll try harder") is a weak
predictor of whether the thing actually gets done — it leaves every
detail of execution to be figured out in the moment, when motivation may
have already faded. What reliably works instead is a concrete
implementation plan that specifies the exact when, where, and how: "Tomorrow
during my break, I'll get a cup of tea, close the door to my office, and
call the graduate school" — a script vivid and specific enough that
following it doesn't require summoning fresh motivation at execution time,
only recognizing the trigger conditions it named.

This is the practical bridge from a [growth-mindset
diagnosis](learning-vs-proving-orientation.md) of a situation (identifying
that some concrete action would help) to that action actually happening —
insight and intention alone, without a concrete plan for when/where/how,
tend to evaporate. When a first plan runs into an obstacle, the correct
response is to make a *new* concrete plan with the same level of
specificity, not to abandon planning and fall back on vague intention.
