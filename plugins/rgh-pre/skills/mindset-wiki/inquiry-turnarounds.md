After [the four questions of inquiry](the-four-questions-of-inquiry.md),
turn the original statement around and find examples — in that same
situation — of how each turnaround is as true as, or truer than, the
original. "Try on each turnaround as if you are trying on a new pair of
shoes."

**Purpose:** as long as the cause of the problem is believed to be "out
there," the situation is hopeless — it keeps you in the role of victim.
"Inquiry combined with the turnarounds is the fast track to self-
realization." The power lies in discovering that what you think you see
outside is a projection of your own mind; in finding the innocence of the
person judged, you recognize your own innocence.

**Always inquire first:** apply the four questions to the original
statement *before* turnarounds. Skipping straight to turnarounds makes them
"feel harsh and shameful"; done after inquiry, they land as "revelations
rather than mental gymnastics." The Work is not about shame, blame, or
proving who's wrong.

From "Paul doesn't listen to me," three standard turnarounds:

- **To the self:** "I don't listen to myself" — while accusing Paul, you
  may be so focused on being right you cannot hear yourself.
- **To the other:** "I don't listen to Paul" — are you listening to him
  while thinking about him not listening to you?
- **To the opposite:** "Paul does listen to me" — even while saying he
  doesn't care about his health, he may put out his cigarette.

From "Paul should be kind to me": "I should be kind to myself" — believing
he should be kind and isn't makes you angry and resentful, which is unkind
to yourself; "If I were kind to myself, I wouldn't have to wait for
kindness from others." "I should be kind to Paul" — the anger from the
original belief leads to treating him unkindly, especially in your mind.
"Paul shouldn't be kind to me" can be truer in that moment: "He shouldn't
be kind in that situation, because he isn't. That's the reality of it."

From "Paul is unkind": "I am unkind" — was your own reaction (tensed body,
racing heart, judgment, replaying his insult over and over) itself unkind?
"Who is more unkind: Paul (who insulted you once today) or you (who
multiplied his insult over and over again in your mind)?"

From "Paul should appreciate me": to yourself — "I should appreciate myself"
(it's my job, not his); to the other — "I should appreciate Paul"; to the
opposite — "Paul shouldn't appreciate me" (sometimes that's just reality).

If a turnaround doesn't fit literal behavior, look for it in *thinking* —
"Paul should stop smoking" → "I should stop smoking" can mean mentally
"smoking" with anger every time you picture him smoking, possibly more
often than he actually smokes. Substitute a parallel compulsion of your own
(food, drugs, credit cards) for "smoking" if needed.

Test each turnaround by asking if it's as true as or truer than the
original, and find authentic examples. If it's hard to own fully, add
"sometimes" and see if it's true even just in the moment you believed the
original. The goal isn't the most turnarounds but whichever "penetrate the
deepest."

Only turnarounds of the *original* statement count — not turnarounds of a
turnaround. "He shouldn't waste his time" turns to "I shouldn't waste my
time," "I shouldn't waste his time," and "He should waste his time" — but
"I should waste my time" is a turnaround of a turnaround and is not valid.

Worksheet statement 6 uses a different pattern: replace "I don't ever want
to…" with "I am willing to…" and then "I look forward to…" — e.g., "I don't
ever want Paul to lie to me again" → "I am willing to have Paul lie to me
again" / "I look forward to having Paul lie to me again." "I am willing
to..." creates open-mindedness and flexibility, softening resistance
instead of applying willpower to push a situation away. "I look forward
to..." actively opens you to life as it unfolds — going further than mere
acceptance toward loving what is. Uncomfortable feelings, when they recur,
become reminders that call you back to inquiry rather than problems to
avoid. Willingness doesn't force any particular outcome — it means you stop
closing yourself off from reality.

After one statement is complete, repeat the full process for every statement on the
worksheet. Stay as still as possible; the authentic answer "will rise from
a stillness that's deeper than thought." First attempts may wobble — keep
going; effects are often felt only later. "Everyone is a mirror image of
your own thinking coming back at you." Pair with [insight requires
action](insight-requires-action.md) for living turnarounds as amends and
behavior change.
