---
type: concept
title: Depersonalizing Critique
description: >
  Treating a critic's job as finding flaws — rather than treating a
  critique as a personal judgment of worth — makes it possible to extract
  useful information from harsh feedback without it triggering a collapse.
sources:
  - title: "Mindset: The New Psychology of Success"
    resource: "Mindset: The New Psychology of Success (Carol S. Dweck), Chapter 8: Changing Mindsets"
---

A harsh, detailed critique can read as a verdict on personal worth,
producing paralysis that lasts far longer than the critique itself
warrants. A specific, reusable reframe: "it's not about you — finding
every flaw is their job. Your job is to learn from the critique." This
separates the critic's role (surfacing problems, which is what a rigorous
review is supposed to do) from any implied statement about the recipient
as a person — a critique that is exhaustive because that's the job is not
the same thing as a critique that is exhaustive because the work (or the
person) is uniquely bad.

This is a direct, repeatable application of [mindset as the frame behind
the internal monologue](mindset-as-internal-monologue.md): instead of
letting a critique trigger a judging question ("what does this say about
me"), it redirects to a learning question ("what in here is useful").
Applied consistently, this same reframe defuses both failure modes at
once — the impostor-style collapse into "I'm not good enough" and the
defensive dismissal of feedback as unfair or hostile — because neither
response is actually required once the critique is understood as
information about the work rather than a ruling on the person.
