---
type: concept
title: Misapplied Self-Care Advice
description: >
  Standard burnout and depression recommendations can backfire when they
  ignore how a person's nervous system actually works — adding depleting
  activities in the name of self-care worsens the deficit instead of
  closing it.
sources:
  - title: "Knowing Why: Adult-Diagnosed Autistic People on Life and Autism"
    resource: "Knowing Why (ASAN), ch. 2: Erin Human, Emerging From Burnout"
---

Generic wellbeing advice assumes a replenishment profile that may not
match the person receiving it. For many autistic people in burnout, common
prescriptions actively deepen the [wellbeing
deficit](autistic-burnout-as-wellbeing-deficit.md):

- **More in-person socializing** (date nights, babysitters, outings) —
  even when human connection has value, face-to-face engagement costs heavy
  executive-functioning and social energy; piling on "fun" outings followed
  by weeks recovering from the fun is a net loss.
- **Salon, spa, and body-care rituals** — hairdressers, manicures, and
  massages can be sensory ordeals (touch, smell, small talk) and are
  financially inaccessible for many disabled people.
- **Meditation and present-moment focus** — for some autistic people,
  attending to the present lets in unfiltered sensory information that
  becomes overwhelming rather than calming; the practice that calms many
  neurotypicals can intensify overload.

The corrective is not rejecting all connection, movement, or attention
practice but trial-and-error discovery of what actually replenishes:
text-based connection instead of in-person socializing, home-based
movement instead of gyms, or [deep focus as
restoration](deep-focus-as-restoration.md) instead of seated meditation.
Sensory self-care (noise-canceling headphones, leaving overload
environments) matters for adults as much as for children — sensory
processing equally affects energy, mood, and cognitive capacity.
