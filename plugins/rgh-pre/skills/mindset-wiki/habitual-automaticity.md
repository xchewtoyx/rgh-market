type: concept
title: Habitual Automaticity
description: >
  The mind's default sleep of unconscious, automatic reacting — driven by
  likes, prejudices, and fear — that mindfulness practice counterbalances by
  waking deliberate present-moment awareness.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 1: What Is Mindfulness?"
---

Much of ordinary waking life runs on automatic pilot: actions and
perceptions shaped by unconscious habits rather than open attention to what
is actually happening. Kabat-Zinn compares this state to an extended dream
rather than full wakefulness — a severely limited mode of consciousness
that leaves little room simply to be without constantly doing.

This automaticity is not weak; it is "exceedingly tenacious" and strong
enough that sustained practice is needed just to capture moments in
awareness. Habitually ignoring the present in favor of future moments
erodes understanding of how the mind shapes perceptions and actions and
how a person is embedded in the web of life around them.

[mindfulness](mindfulness.md) and [deliberate attention
practice](deliberate-attention-practice.md) target this layer directly:
paying attention openly without immediately falling prey to likes and
dislikes, opinions, projections, and expectations opens new possibilities
and frees attention from what Kabat-Zinn calls the "straitjacket of
unconsciousness." [Catching automatic thoughts](catching-automatic-thoughts.md)
applies the same move at the level of individual cognitions rather than the
whole background state.
