Mindfulness is not neutral attention. Whether mindful awareness produces a
fundamental shift depends on whether **friendliness and compassion** are
brought to what is attended to. "Attending without kindness can be ineffective
or even harmful."

Christina Feldman: "The quality of mindfulness is not a neutral or blank
presence. True mindfulness is imbued with warmth, compassion and interest…
where there is interest, a natural, unforced attention follows."

MBCT treats participants as guests, not patients — kindness and self-compassion
are among the most important things people learn. The instructor's personal
warmth is a necessary condition for the program's structured elements.

## Indirect routes to self-compassion

MBCT deliberately omits formal [loving-kindness meditation](loving-kindness-meditation.md)
in the eight-week course because: (1) kindness should come primarily through
the instructor's embodiment; (2) clinical populations may hear phrases like
"happy" or "free from harm" as calls to strive, triggering painful comparisons;
(3) aiming to produce loving feelings can reinforce "unable to love or be loved"
if feelings do not arise.

Indirect routes remain: developing mindfulness is "a vital portal into
self-compassion" — "to know the mind and its ways is the beginning of befriending
the mind." Curiosity, kindness, calmness, and steadiness strengthen together as
"part of the fabric of compassion." Gently turning toward the present moment is
itself a powerful gesture of self-care — all MBSR/MBCT practices are acts of
self-compassion.

Pairs with [non-judging attitude in meditation](non-judging-attitude-in-meditation.md)
and [friend to yourself](friend-to-yourself.md).
