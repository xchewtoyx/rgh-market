Feeling excessively bad usually means thinking badly, or at least unhelpfully —
mostly without intending to or being aware of it. "Thinking errors" are common
slips that distort facts, cause jumping to conclusions, and sustain beliefs
unhelpful to emotional health. Recognizing them lets you spot and correct
unhelpful thoughts more quickly.

Cognitive distortions function like tinted glasses — they skew everything
seen through them uniformly, which is exactly why they're hard to notice
from the inside; the fix is to identify and remove the distortion rather
than to argue point-by-point against whatever it's currently generating.
A working vocabulary of common patterns makes them easier to catch in the
moment: [catastrophizing](catastrophizing.md); [all-or-nothing
thinking](all-or-nothing-thinking.md); [overgeneralization as cognitive
distortion](overgeneralization-as-cognitive-distortion.md); jumping to
conclusions; emotional reasoning (treating a feeling as proof of a fact,
related to [emotional bias in judgment](emotional-bias-in-judgment.md));
[mind reading as cognitive distortion](mind-reading-as-cognitive-distortion.md);
future telling ([predictions treated as
fact](predictions-treated-as-fact.md)); [labeling](labels-strip-context.md);
[making demands as cognitive distortion](making-demands-as-cognitive-distortion.md);
[mental filtering](brain-filters-information.md); [disqualifying the
positive](disqualifying-the-positive.md); [low frustration
tolerance](low-frustration-tolerance.md); and [personalizing as cognitive
distortion](personalizing-cognitive-distortion.md).

Self-monitoring when upset — record thoughts and review against this list —
surfaces which errors you are most prone to and which situations trigger
them (see [catching automatic thoughts](catching-automatic-thoughts.md)).

The practical technique is simply naming the distortion aloud or silently
the moment it's noticed — "right now my mind is doing mind reading about
what they think of me" — which doesn't require resolving whether the
thought is accurate, only recognizing which named pattern is currently
running. Naming reliably loosens a distortion's grip even without further
argument, the same effect produced by [noticing the story your mind is
telling](noticing-the-story-your-mind-is-telling.md).
