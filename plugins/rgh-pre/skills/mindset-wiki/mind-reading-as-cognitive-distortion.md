type: concept
title: Mind Reading as Cognitive Distortion
description: >
  Assuming you know what others are thinking — usually that they hold
  negative views about you — without evidence, often projecting your own
  fears onto their imagined judgments.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies (Rhena Branch, Rob Willson), ch. 2: Mind Reading"
---

Mind reading assumes knowledge of others' thoughts, usually that they hold
negative views or motives about you, without evidence. Examples: someone
yawns during conversation → they find you boring; boss suggests vacation
time → they want to replace you; unfriendly neighbor → they're reporting
your dog.

Countermeasures: generate alternative explanations; consider whether the
guess reflects your insecurity more than the other person; get more
information if appropriate (ask); if prone to assuming negative judgment,
practice assuming favorable judgment instead. "You tend to mind read what
you fear most... based on what's already in [your mind]."

A specific case of [assumptions filling perceptual gaps](brain-fills-gaps-with-assumptions.md),
named in [naming cognitive distortions](naming-cognitive-distortions.md).
Pairs with [taking things less personally](taking-things-less-personally.md).
