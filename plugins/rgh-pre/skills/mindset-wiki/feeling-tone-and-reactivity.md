title: Feeling Tone and Reactivity
description: >
  Every experience carries an automatic pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral tone;
  the mind's habitual next-moment reactions — clinging, pushing away, or tuning
  out — are the lever mindfulness practice learns to see and loosen.
sources:

Every experience — sound, sight, smell, taste, body sensation, thought —
automatically evokes a **feeling tone**: pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral. The
mind–body registers this constantly, like a barometer, often below awareness.

Something habitually happens immediately after:

- **Pleasant** → attachment: hang on, get more.
- **Unpleasant** → aversive reactivity: get rid of it, prevent recurrence.
- **Neutral** → tuning out, boredom, restlessness.

Session work in MBCT often begins with **recognition** of aversive reactivity
(the mind's "not wanting"), which is at the root of rumination and relapse when
applied to internal experience. Evolution equipped us to escape external threats;
applying the same push-away to unwelcome thoughts and moods co-opts
problem-solving into [doing-mode rumination](doing-mode-rumination.md) with
counterproductive results.

The central theme underneath attachment and aversion: **needing things to be
different from how they are right now.** The first skillful step toward unpleasant
states is being present with them; from fuller awareness, further action can be
chosen. Curiosity and compassion toward bodily reactions let experience settle
and pass in its own time.

Pairs with [second-arrow suffering](second-arrow-suffering.md), [attachment to
pleasant meditation states](attachment-to-pleasant-meditation-states.md),
[experiential avoidance](experiential-avoidance.md), and
the [AAA framework](aaa-framework.md) — which uses "aversion" and "attachment"
in a related but broader self-defence sense.
