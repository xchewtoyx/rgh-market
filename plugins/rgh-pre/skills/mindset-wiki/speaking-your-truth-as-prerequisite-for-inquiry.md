---
type: concept
title: Speaking Your Truth as Prerequisite for Inquiry
description: >
  Inquiry can stall when fear of someone's reaction keeps you from saying
  what you actually think — "I broke my word to me" is often the belief
  underneath a relationship crisis.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 4"
---

Barbara's mid-session insight on why solo Work had failed before: "If I
don't dare to speak my truth, then I can't do The Work." Fear or threat of
violence can keep you from speaking your real truth to a partner — which
surfaces as "I broke my word to me" on [inquiry
turnarounds](inquiry-turnarounds.md). Staying silent with someone violent
can also be wise self-protection; the inquiry is into the belief driving
the silence, not into forcing unsafe disclosure.

Turn "I want him to acknowledge his breaking his word" to "I want me to
acknowledge that I broke my word to myself" and "...that I broke my word
to him" — not saying things you ought to have said. Dependence on his
explanation puts you "in a place of being dependent on his actions or his
words"; without that thought, you can focus on your life and let him "breathe
freely, not having to explain anything." "He doesn't owe me that."

"He kept his word to me" can be truer: he was being honest — "speaking
truthfully. It was his truth." Even "He broke his word to me" can land as
relief when you would never have made that choice yourself. See [being
right versus being free](being-right-vs-being-free.md).
