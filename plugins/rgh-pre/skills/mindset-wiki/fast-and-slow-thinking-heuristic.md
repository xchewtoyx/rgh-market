Kahneman's System 1 / System 2 language is a deliberate fiction: there are
no such entities in the brain, and sentences like "System 2 calculates
products" are shorthand descriptions, not explanations. They work because
readers already know what is being described — voluntary mental arithmetic
that takes effort, shouldn't be done while turning left, and shows up in
dilated pupils and faster heart rate.

The labels are worth using anyway because agent-shaped sentences are easier
to grasp than abstract process descriptions, and because System 1 is
especially good at constructing stories about agents with habits. Treat
the names as nicknames — like calling automatic reactions "Bob" and
effortful monitoring "Joe" — rather than as neuroscience claims. The same
posture applies to other layered models in this bundle, such as the
[triune brain model](triune-brain-model.md): useful for diagnosing what
kind of reaction is running, not a literal map of brain anatomy.

In self-regulation practice, the heuristic maps cleanly onto familiar
distinctions: **System 1** operates automatically and quickly, with little
effort or sense of voluntary control — detecting anger in a face, completing
"bread and…", orienting to sudden sounds. **System 2** allocates attention
to effortful activities — complex computation, monitoring social
appropriateness, searching memory — and is associated with agency, choice,
and concentration. System 1 continuously generates suggestions; System 2
normally adopts them with little modification, but mobilizes when surprised,
when System 1 hits a problem it can't answer, or when self-monitoring detects
an error about to be made.

**Attention budget:** you dispose of a limited budget of attention.
Effortful activities interfere with each other — you cannot compute 17×24
while making a left turn into dense traffic. Easy undemanding tasks can run
in parallel (conversation on an empty highway). Only the effortful layer
follows rules, compares objects on several attributes, and makes deliberate
choices between options; the automatic layer lacks those capabilities even
when it excels at stereotype fit and single-topic integration — see [task
sets and executive control](task-sets-and-executive-control.md).

**Invisible gorilla:** about half of viewers counting basketball passes miss
a woman in a gorilla suit crossing the court for nine seconds — "we can be
blind to the obvious, and we are also blind to our blindness." Seeing still
requires some attention allocated to the stimulus.

System 1 generates fast impressions, associative stories, and impulses;
System 2 can monitor, doubt, and override — but only when engaged, and only
at the cost of [mental energy](mental-energy-budget.md). System 1 has
systematic biases and cannot be turned off — shown a word in a language you
know, you will read it unless attention is fully engaged elsewhere.

When automatic and intentional responses conflict — not staring at a
stranger, steering into a skid, biting back a cruel reply — [self-control
is the job of the effortful layer](buffer-between-feeling-and-action.md),
not of wishing the impulse away.
