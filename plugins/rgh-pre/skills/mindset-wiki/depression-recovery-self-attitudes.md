title: Depression Recovery Self-Attitudes
description: >
  Recovery from depression needs accepting the illness without liking it,
  self-compassion instead of self-bullying, and daily practice of a hopeful
  outlook — principles shared by CBT and acceptance-and-commitment therapy.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies (Rhena Branch, Rob Willson), ch. 11"
---

**Acceptance.** Depression's feelings and thoughts are deeply unpleasant;
no one chooses it. Depression differs from a cynical worldview — often the
opposite, which makes the shift from enjoying life to feeling defeated so
hard to accept ("I no longer recognize myself"). Acceptance means treating
depression as an illness — usually temporary even when long-lasting, not a
personality change; personality is intact, merely masked. Demands like "I
must not feel this way," "this is intolerable," "I have to get better now!"
add pressure and reinforce bad feelings — "like banging your head against a
wall." Accepting self-statements: "I hate being depressed but unfortunately
I'm not immune." / "Depression is hard to bear but I am tolerating these
unpleasant feelings." / "I want to get better now, but I can't bully myself
out of depression, so I'll be patient but determined." Give yourself credit
for coping even when it doesn't feel easy.

**Compassion.** You can't bully yourself out of depression — self-criticism
for being low is kicking yourself when you're down; many depressed people
berate and neglect themselves in an insidious cycle. With flu you don't
usually call yourself weak for being ill; depression invites extra
self-criticism. Offer compassion instead: credit efforts against depression,
benchmarking small daily improvements from where you are now (out of bed one
day, shopping or answering the phone the next); compliment and kindly cajole
yourself, calling to mind good points. Beware "false friends" — drugs, alcohol,
or food for brief respite at high cost (worse comedown, dependency, possible
medication interference).

**Outlook.** Depressed outlook stays bleak even as recovery steps begin.
Construct and practice optimistic outlook daily — even without full initial
belief; belief builds with time. Examples: "I look forward to feeling better
soon." / "I may not see the light at the end of the tunnel, but I know it's
there." / "This will pass." / "I'll deal with the future when it comes." /
"Hanging on is worth it because things will get better." Recovery is rarely
steady — setbacks and difficult days are normal, not a return to square one;
"saddle up and get back on the horse."

**ACT angle.** Acceptance and commitment therapy (third-wave CBT) emphasizes
observing negative thoughts without judgment and letting them pass — see
[thought defusion](thought-defusion.md) and [eight ways to unhook from painful
thoughts](eight-ways-to-unhook-from-thoughts.md) — plus commitment to personal
values. CBT and ACT share these three attitude pillars even when technique
differs on whether to dispute thoughts directly.
