title: Interrupting Habit Momentum Through Mindfulness
description: >
  Sitting with impulses without acting on them reveals they are passing mental
  events — refashioning the action-consequence chain so the present moment can
  become a new beginning rather than old momentum replayed.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 7: Karma"
---

Karma is often misunderstood as fixed destiny; operationally it is an accumulation
of tendencies locking you into behavior patterns that generate more of the same.
It is possible to change — but only in **now**, the one time you ever have to do
it.

When sitting in meditation, impulses arise and pass without being translated into
action. You see they have a life of their own — "not you but just thinking" —
and need not rule you. Not feeding or reacting to destructive impulses while
holding creative ones in awareness **refashions the links** between actions and
consequences, unchaining you from momentum that otherwise keeps the present from
becoming a fresh start.

Long relationships or habits practiced for years (anger, isolation, blaming)
can imprison through **mindlessness** — being out of touch, reacting, not seeing.
Stopping outward activity to practice stillness already breaks old momentum and
creates healthier patterns; the instrument to start with is your own mind, through
which thoughts, feelings, and impulses become actions.

Only what happens now happens later — without [mindfulness](mindfulness.md),
equanimity, or compassion now, they are unlikely to appear magically under stress.
See [buffer between feeling and action](buffer-between-feeling-and-action.md)
and [sitting beside the thought stream](sitting-beside-the-thought-stream.md).
