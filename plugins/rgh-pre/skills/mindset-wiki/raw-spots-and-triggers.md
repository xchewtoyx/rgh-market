title: Raw Spots and Triggers
description: >

  present, otherwise-minor stressor — naming the raw spot when it's
  activated separates the outsized reaction from the small present
  trigger that set it off.
sources:

A disproportionately strong reaction to a small present event is often a
sign that an old, unresolved sensitivity has been activated rather than
that the present event itself warrants the full intensity of the
reaction — the current stressor lands on top of accumulated historical
pain and gets amplified by it. Identifying a raw spot involves reviewing
past instances of strong reactions for a common pattern (context,
reaction, the thought running underneath), noticing recurring bodily or
mental responses to a specific category of situation, and naming the
deeper story the raw spot is actually tied to (often about safety,
belonging, or worth).

The practical technique is naming the raw spot explicitly when it
activates — "my fear of being dismissed is activated right now" — which
does the same separating work as [thought
defusion](thought-defusion.md): it distinguishes the old pattern from the
present moment, making it visible as a historical reaction being replayed
rather than an accurate read of the current situation. This is a specific
instance of [conditioning and context
mismatch](conditioning-and-context-mismatch.md) — the raw spot is exactly
the kind of conditioned response formed in one context and now firing,
disproportionately, in a different one.
