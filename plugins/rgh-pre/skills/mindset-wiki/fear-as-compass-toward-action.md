type: concept
title: Fear as Compass Toward Action
description: >
  The qualm you feel before a worthy act often points at exactly what needs
  doing — listen closely, then do the opposite of what fear demands.
sources:
  - title: "Courage Is Calling: Fortune Favors the Brave"
    resource: "Courage Is Calling (Ryan Holiday), ch. 6"
---

Theodore Roosevelt hesitated before inviting Booker T. Washington to dine at
the White House — the first Black guest of a sitting president — fearing
southern backlash. He went ahead precisely because of the qualm: "The very
fact that I felt a moment's qualm on inviting him because of his color made
me ashamed of myself, and made me hasten to send the invitation."

"Our fears point us, like a self-indicting arrow, in the direction of the
right thing to do." Fear alerts to danger and to opportunity — "that tinge
of self-preservation is the pinging of the metal detector going off." Fear
always has a reason for inaction; absent occasional hesitation, you're
probably not pushing enough.

Eleanor Roosevelt: do the thing you cannot do — it's almost always what you
should do when something tells you you're not allowed. "They say not to take
counsel of your fears, but perhaps that's exactly what we should do. We
should listen closely and then do the opposite."
