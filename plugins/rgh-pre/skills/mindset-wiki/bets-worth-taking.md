---
type: concept
title: Bets Worth Taking
description: >
  Committing fully to a difficult goal doesn't require believing it will
  succeed — it requires judging that the attempt is worth making given its
  real odds and payoff, which is a different and more honest source of
  motivation than manufactured confidence.
sources:
  - title: "The Scout Mindset: Why Some People See Things Clearly and Others Don't"
    resource: "The Scout Mindset (Julia Galef), Chapter 8: Motivation Without Self-Deception"
---

The "self-belief model" of motivation holds that convincing yourself
you'll succeed is what makes persistence possible, and that acknowledging
real odds of failure will sap that persistence. An alternative used by
people who took on famously long-odds ventures is to openly hold a low
probability of success while still committing fully — motivated not by
"this will succeed" but by "this is a bet worth taking." The reasoning
resembles expected value: even a low probability of success can justify a
full attempt if the payoff is large enough and the downside is bounded
and survivable, in the same way a single unlikely bet can still be a good
one if the potential win is disproportionately large relative to the
likely loss.

An accurate picture of the real odds is not demotivating — it does three
useful things a falsely inflated picture can't: it helps in choosing
between competing goals (a goal only looks like the single obvious choice
when its true odds and cost are obscured); it allows the plan to be
revised as new information arrives, rather than treating the original
target as fixed regardless of changing circumstances; and it calibrates
how much to actually stake on the attempt — overestimating the odds
tends to justify betting far more than the situation warrants, risking
real, unbounded harm rather than an acceptable, bounded one. This connects
to [mindset determines confidence trajectory](mindset-confidence-trajectory.md):
in both cases, full commitment does not require confidence about the
outcome — a clear-eyed acceptance of the odds is sufficient basis for
action.
