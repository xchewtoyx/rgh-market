---
type: concept
title: Belief in Small Numbers
description: >
  Intuition treats small samples as if the law of large numbers already
  applied — producing extreme, spurious results and confident causal stories
  where only sampling variation is at work.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 11"
---

**Kidney cancer counties:** US counties with the *lowest* kidney-cancer
rates are mostly rural, sparsely populated, and Republican-leaning — inviting
a story about clean living. Counties with the *highest* rates share the same
profile — inviting the opposite story about rural poverty. Rurality cannot
cause both extremes; the relevant feature is **small population**: small
samples yield more extreme highs and lows than large ones.

System 1 automatically links events with causal stories, even spurious
ones, but is weak with "merely statistical" facts that change probabilities
without causing outcomes. Collections of random events are highly regular
in aggregate — you can predict repeated draws from an urn as confidently as
you can predict what happens when you hit an egg with a hammer — minus the
satisfying sense of causation.

**Jack and Jill:** Drawing four marbles repeatedly, homogeneous
(all-red or all-white) outcomes occur far more often from Jack's samples of
four than Jill's samples of seven. Rural counties are Jack — extreme
observed rates are expected artifacts of method, not facts about those
counties that will repeat identically next year.

**Shallow versus deep knowledge:** Most people "know" large samples are
more trustworthy but are surprised by how much more variable small samples
are — e.g., an eightfold difference between samples of four and seven.
Truly understanding the law of large numbers means seeing that "large
samples are more precise" and "small samples yield extreme results more
often" are the same statement.

Kahneman and Tversky coined **belief in the law of small numbers** from
surveying researchers who chose sample sizes so small they risked a 50%
chance of failing to confirm true hypotheses — intuition satisfied the law
of large numbers for small numbers as well. Countermove: treat statistical
intuitions with suspicion; replace impression with computation when
possible. Pair with [representativeness heuristic](representativeness-
heuristic.md), [causal versus statistical base rates](causal-vs-
statistical-base-rates.md), and [statistics versus individual
cases](statistics-versus-individual-cases.md).
