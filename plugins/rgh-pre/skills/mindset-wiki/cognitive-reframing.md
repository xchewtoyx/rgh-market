Reframing intervenes on the perception side of the [happiness
equation](happiness-equation.md): once a thought has been noticed (see
[catching automatic thoughts](catching-automatic-thoughts.md)), it can be
tested and revised rather than taken at face value. The goal is not
forced positivity but accuracy — checking whether the first interpretation
that occurred to the mind actually matches the evidence, or whether it was
shaped by habitual distortion.

This is the general technique that specific bias-countering practices
apply to particular distortions. See [the two-handles
technique](two-handles-technique.md) for a compact version of this move
suited specifically to interpersonal grievances, where an unworkable and
a workable description of the same situation are both equally true. A useful default question, applied
consistently: "have I ever debated this claim, or only claims other people
make?" — most people readily interrogate what other people tell them but
rarely apply the same scrutiny to their own [recycled
thoughts](recycled-thoughts.md).

## Lightweight reframes

Hard habits become more attractive when associated with positive experience via a
slight mindset shift. Change **"have to" into "get to"** — both are equally
true; you choose which evidence-supported mindset to adopt. Reframe exercise as
building endurance; saving as freedom; meditation distractions as chances to
practice returning to breath.

**Physiological arousal reframe:** pre-event jitters (quick breathing, faster
heart rate) can be interpreted as threat (tense up) or excitement ("adrenaline
to help me concentrate") — interpretation shapes whether the response is fluid
or tense.

These shifts are not magic but change feelings associated with a habit. For
conditioned cues that summon a state on demand, see [motivation
rituals](motivation-rituals.md).
