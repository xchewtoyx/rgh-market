---
type: concept
title: Voluntary Simplicity
description: >
  Intentionally doing one thing at a time and being fully present for it —
  saying no to the impulse to squeeze in one more task — nourishes at a deep
  level that multitasking and numbing consumption cannot reach.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 3"
---

An impulse to squeeze another task into the moment — "just this phone call,
just stopping off here" — scavenges to fill time and keeps you unconscious,
lulled in fog, missing breakfast while overfilling the belly. Voluntary
simplicity counters this: **doing fewer places in one day rather than more,
seeing less so you can see more, doing less so you can do more, acquiring
less so you can have more.**

Practices: one walk fully present; time with the dog where you are really
with the dog; staying with a child rather than answering the phone; not
reacting to inner impulse to call someone; not acquiring on impulse; sitting
and doing nothing for an evening; reading; walking alone or with family;
restacking the woodpile; looking at the moon.

"Saying no to keep my life simple" is an arduous discipline — tricky because
real needs and opportunities must still be met. It is a delicate balancing
act, always needing retuning. Choosing simplicity whenever possible adds
deepest freedom and discovers that less may actually be more.

Thoreau: "Simplicity, simplicity, simplicity! … In the midst of this chopping
sea of civilized life… simplify, simplify."
