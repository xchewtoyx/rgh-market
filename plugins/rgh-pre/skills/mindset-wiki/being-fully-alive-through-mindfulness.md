---
type: concept
title: Being Fully Alive Through Mindfulness
description: >
  Mindfulness does not erase difficulty but enlarges the present moment until
  worries and memories are only part of experience — making room to trust,
  accept, and savor the life already here instead of living only toward
  imagined futures.
sources:
  - title: "The Mindful Way Through Depression"
    resource: "The Mindful Way Through Depression (Williams, Teasdale, Segal, Kabat-Zinn), ch. 10"
---

Mindfulness practice does not neutralize difficult situations, worries,
memories, or people, nor make you indifferent to them. The space made for them
by present-moment awareness grows bigger — big enough that they become only
part of experience, not the whole of it.

Practice can make more room to realize and embody the full spectrum of who you
already are: trusting yourself in new ways, discovering you are okay as you are,
accepting yourself, feeling gratitude for the life you already have rather than
grasping at a fantasized one, and choosing to savor unfolding life moment by
moment.

When the mind stays preoccupied with rewards or dangers at journey's end, you
cut yourself off from life's richness and from recognizing it in each moment's
texture. In any one moment that may seem no great loss — but "a whole life of
lost moments is a whole life lost." The tragedy for many is not that lives are
too short, but that we take so long before we start to live them.

The wisdom cultivated through [mindfulness](mindfulness.md), if allowed, reveals
how much suffering stems from unawareness — and the deep peace at the heart of
each moment, if you have the courage to cultivate awareness here and now. Pairs
with [informal attention practices](informal-attention-practices.md) and
[non-striving in meditation](non-striving-in-meditation.md).
