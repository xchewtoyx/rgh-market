type: concept
title: Mountain Meditation Imagery
description: >
  Imagining the body as a mountain — still, rooted, unmoving through changing
  inner weather — cultivates stability and non-personal relating to thoughts
  and storms.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 5: The Mountain Meditation"
---

Mountains carry archetypal significance — elemental, rock-hard, rock-solid,
guardian and protector. The meditation borrows these qualities to bolster
intentionality and hold the moment with elemental purity.

**Practice:** Picture a personally resonant mountain; merge body and image —
head as peak, shoulders and arms as slopes, legs as rooted base. Become a
breathing mountain, unwavering stillness, centered presence.

**Weather analogy:** As light and shadow shift on the mountain through day,
night, seasons, and storms, "the mountain just sits, simply being itself."
Hold this image to embody unwavering stillness through everything that changes
in life. Thoughts, feelings, preoccupations, emotional storms, and external
events are like weather on the mountain — we tend to take them personally, but
their strongest characteristic is impersonal. Inner weather is not to be ignored
or denied — encounter, honor, feel, know it, hold in high awareness — doing so
reveals deeper silence, stillness, and wisdom within the storms.

Humans exceed mountains in complexity — "breathing, moving, dancing mountains"
capable of being hard and unmoving and soft and flowing. The image is a device
pointing toward experience — "we still have to look, then go."

Pairs with [surfing the mind's waves](surfing-the-mind-waves.md) (work with weather
rather than stop it) and [patience in mindfulness practice](patience-in-mindfulness-practice.md).

**Try:** Hold the mountain image in formal sitting; carry it into daily life;
see the mountain in others.
