---
type: concept
title: Retrieval Fluency Versus Content
description: >
  When recalling examples to judge yourself or a situation, ease of
  retrieval often outweighs how many examples you actually found — and
  unexpected difficulty can flip the judgment the opposite way.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 13"
---

Listing instances to rate a trait pits two cues against each other: how many
examples you retrieved versus how easily they came to mind. Ease usually
dominates. People who listed twelve assertive behaviors rated themselves
*less* assertive than those who listed six — despite twice as many
examples — because the last instances were hard to generate. Listing twelve
*non*-assertive behaviors made people feel quite assertive for the same
reason.

The paradox extends beyond self-rating: more recalled bicycle trips → felt
less frequent use; more supporting arguments → less confidence in a
choice; more ways an event could have been avoided → less confidence it was
avoidable; more car advantages → less impressed. Sometimes the opposite
holds — students asked to list *more* ways to improve a course rated it
*higher* because struggling to find suggestions signaled the course was
already good.

The key is *unexpected* difficulty: people expect fluency to decline, but
the drop between six and twelve instances is steeper than expected — so
they infer "if I'm having more trouble than expected, I can't be very
assertive." Giving a spurious explanation for the difficulty (background
music said to impair memory, curved text boxes, screen color) disrupts the
heuristic — subjects stop using fluency as a cue.

Personal stakes shift which cue dominates: students with a family history
of cardiac disease rated risk by *number* of behaviors retrieved (many safe
behaviors → felt safer), while others without family history followed the
standard fluency pattern. Pair with [availability
heuristic](availability-heuristic.md) and [fast-and-slow thinking as a
behavioral heuristic](fast-and-slow-thinking-heuristic.md).
