---
type: concept
title: Doing-the-Dishes Practice
description: >
  When overwhelmed by decisions or mental combat about what should happen
  next, reduce scope to the single next action in front of you and do it
  with full attention — one dish, one bill, one step.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 9"
---

After a shift in understanding, Katie describes hearing an inner directive
— "Do the dishes" — and simply washing the next dish, paying the top bill,
one thing at a time, until at day's end everything was done without a
master plan. "Doing the dishes" names that practice: loving the task
immediately in front of you instead of fighting a story about who should
have done it, what it means about you, or what the whole future requires.

When the directive isn't followed, an internal war erupts — "I'll do them
later," "It's not fair," "People will think less of me" — producing
mental combat fatigue unrelated to the physical work. The alternative is
open, waiting, trusting: one thing to do now, whether you have ten dollars
or ten million; life does not get structurally harder than that.

For decision paralysis about money or other high-stakes futures, the same
move applies: notice the urge to decide everything at once, inquire on
the thought driving panic, then return to the next concrete step that is
actually here. Decisions arrive in their own time; the suffering is often
the story that you must force them early from anxiety (see [stressful
feeling as inquiry signal](stressful-feeling-as-inquiry-signal.md)). Pair
with [single-tasking](single-tasking.md) and [flow
state](flow-state.md) for attention on the current micro-task rather than
the whole unfinished pile.
