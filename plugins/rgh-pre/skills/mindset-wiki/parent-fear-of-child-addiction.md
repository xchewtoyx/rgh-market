type: concept
title: Parent Fear of a Child's Addiction
description: >
  A parent's terror about a child's substance use often tracks uninvestigated
  thoughts — "she's dying of drugs, you're dying of a thinking overdose."
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 11"
---

Charlotte was consumed by thoughts about her daughter Linda's drug addiction.
On "Her drug addiction is killing her" — can you absolutely know? No. Under
the thought: angry, afraid, pushing Linda away. Without it: relaxed, less
mean, less reactive. Turnarounds substitute "my thinking": "My thinking is
killing me"; "It's killing our relationship." "She's dying of a drug overdose,
and you're dying of a thinking overdose. She could last a lot longer than you."

"Her addiction endangers my granddaughter" — turnarounds: "My thinking
addiction is endangering my life"; "I'm so much in her business." "She's
addicted to drugs, and you're addicted to mentally running her life. She's
your drug." "It's insane to mentally be in my children's business."

On ruining Linda's life — not knowable even long-term. Katie's daughter's
addiction later made her life "very rich." "If she died, I'd still be able
to see that." Without the story, Charlotte would be "a better mother."

**Child as teacher:** "Your daughter has zero to do with your problem...
her job is to use drugs; my job is to drink my tea now." "When you understand,
she'll follow you... Hell here, hell there. Peace here, peace there."

"I'm afraid of Linda's drug addiction because it changes her personality" →
"I'm afraid of my thinking because it changes my personality... and therefore
Linda's." "Always trying to change the projected rather than clear the
projector."

When Charlotte shuts Linda out while using, "There's no listener in your home
now; just a teacher of fear." Closing: willing to have Linda around whether
using or not — "available to her when she's using, instead of shutting off."
