---
type: concept
title: Probability Neglect
description: >
  The mind either ignores small risks entirely or overweight them — rarely
  calibrating concern to actual likelihood — so vivid numerators without
  denominators hijack judgment.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 14"
---

With low-probability harms, people often **either ignore the risk completely
or give it far too much weight — nothing in between**. A parent waiting up
for a late teenager knows there is almost nothing to worry about yet cannot
stop imagining disaster.

Concern is not adequately sensitive to probability: people imagine the
tragic numerator (the story from the news) without considering the
denominator. Sunstein called this **probability neglect**. Combined with
[availability cascades](availability-cascade.md), it produces gross
exaggeration of minor threats — apple-juice-as-toxic-waste calls, costly
regulation driven by fear rather than lives saved per dollar.

Kahneman sits between two policy poles: experts who weigh lives and costs
objectively, and the public's richer distinctions (good deaths versus bad
deaths, voluntary versus random risk) — but agrees that **fear is painful
and debilitating whether or not it is rational**, so policy cannot simply
override widespread dread.

Countermove when dread feels total: name the base rate, separate vividness
from likelihood, and ask whether [affect](affect-heuristic.md) is doing the
math. Pair with [catastrophizing](catastrophizing.md) and [fear-
setting](fear-setting.md) — define the scenario without letting imagination
substitute for odds.
