**TICs** (task-interfering cognitions) are thoughts, attitudes, and beliefs that
impede progress on a goal. **TOCs** (task-orienting cognitions) are constructive
alternatives that help move toward the goal.

Procedure: (1) identify the goal or task; (2) list TICs in the left column; (3)
write a TOC response to each TIC in the right column. Example — goal: filling out
university application forms:

- TIC "If I start I'll get too stressed" → TOC "Doing this is a hassle, but if I
  take it one step at a time I'll cope."
- TIC "I'm bound to be rejected" → TOC "I've got a good chance, and I'll really
  regret it if I miss the deadline."

Use whenever progress on a goal or self-help task stalls.

## Positive principles for sticking with change

CBT steps are simple but not easy — application requires diligence, repetition,
and determination. **Be optimistic:** challenge predictions like "I'll never
change" or "CBT won't work for someone like me" as you would for a friend.
Clinging to "no one can understand or help me" protects against disappointed
hope but becomes self-fulfilling — risk possible disappointment for the chance
of success.

**Stay focused on goals:** renew commitment regularly; use [cost-benefit analysis
for change](cost-benefit-analysis-for-change.md) when ambivalence sets in (staying
anxious can feel easier than changing). Set reachable sub-goals along the way.

**Persevere and repeat:** one attempt is rarely enough; new thinking and behavior
feel unnatural at first — like switching to your non-dominant hand, skill develops
with repetition and need not be abandoned once familiar.

**Common sand traps and reframes:**

- *Fearing change* — relationships organized around your problem may shift; getting
  well opens more fulfilling independence.
- *[Low frustration tolerance](low-frustration-tolerance.md)* — retreating (back to
  bed) leaves the same problems waiting; tolerance increases only by sticking with
  discomfort.
- *Being passive* — waiting for someone else or a miracle instead of doing the work.
- *Fear of being bossed around* — trying a suggestion is always your own choice.
- *Being fatalistic* — test whether biology, fate, or past fully determine mood by
  making a real effort.
- *"Love is the drug"* — rescuing relationships are not the only path; healthiest
  partnerships involve two self-sufficient people.
- *Waiting to feel motivated* — see [motivation follows
  action](motivation-follows-action.md); positive action is the best remedy for
  lethargy and hopelessness.
