It is hardest to be mindful when mindfulness would help most — under pressure,
feeling bad, with no moment to spare. MBCT's **three-minute breathing space** is
a mini-meditation for those tipping points: adopt erect, dignified posture;
close eyes if possible.

**Step 1 — Becoming aware:** What is my experience right now? Acknowledge
thoughts as mental events, feelings (turn toward discomfort), body sensations
(tightness, bracing). Disentangles from [driven-doing mode](driven-doing-mode.md).

**Step 2 — Gathering:** Redirect attention to physical sensations of breathing
in the belly — follow breath all the way in and out, using [breath as attention
anchor](breath-as-attention-anchor.md). Hourglass narrows here.

**Step 3 — Expanding:** Expand awareness to the body as a whole, posture, facial
expression. Breathe into discomfort on the in-breath, out from it on the out-breath.
Optional: "It's okay for me to feel this; whatever it is, it's already here."

From MBCT Session 5 onward, Step 3 can expand further: if discomfort, tension,
or resistance is present, breathe into and out from it with "It's OK to feel
this. Whatever it is, it's already here. Let me feel it" — see [allowing and
letting be](allowing-letting-be.md). Step 1 can add brief labeling ("A feeling
of anger is arising"; "Self-critical thoughts are here"); Step 2 can note
"Breathing in... breathing out" or count breaths 1–5.

Practiced three minutes, three times daily when learning; then deployable from
one or two breaths to ten minutes when noticing unpleasant feelings, tightening,
or overwhelm. In MBCT it is "the spine" of the program — a brief practice carrying
longer formal sittings into everyday life. **Program progression:** after Session 3,
three times daily preprogrammed; Session 4 adds responsive use when unpleasant
feelings arise; Session 5 adds opening to difficulty through the body; Session 6
adds relating differently to thoughts; Session 7 adds skillful action. Instructors
should keep instructions spare — importing longer-practice detail undermines
directness. Postural shift at the start embodies waking from autopilot. Anchor to
daily activities (morning coffee, meals, computer) rather than leaving timing vague.

Not a mere time-out to relax before returning to busyness — it is
**turning to face** difficulty (rain-shelter analogy: step into the rain rather
than huddle hoping it stops).

Parsing experience into thoughts, feelings, and body sensations — rather than
one undifferentiated blob — is itself valuable; wholehearted acknowledgment of
what is already present is often all that's necessary.

Like all meditation, it takes effort but goal-orientation backfires — responsibility
is for the input (giving oneself to the practice), not the outcome. Thoughts like
"this isn't working" are themselves what needs acknowledging. Practice is forgiving:
when hassled, that is the cue — even after missing it all day.

Distinct from [three-minute worry period](worry-period-technique.md) and general
[informal attention practices](informal-attention-practices.md) — this is a
formal structured gear-shift tool.

## Four doors after the three steps

After completing the three steps, choose how to proceed:

**Reentry:** Consider the practice complete and mentally reenter the situation
that prompted it, now with [being mode](doing-mode-being-mode.md) in place.
Unpleasant content may remain, but a spacious perspective can make all the
difference — avoiding tunnel vision and automatic reactions that add fuel.
The shift is often subtle (still tired or fed up, but no longer a disaster)
yet opens new possibilities: working one task at a time, feeling regret
without self-flagellation, feeling down without adding anger or "I'll never
get over this." Repeating the whole breathing space is an option, but risks
goal-orientation — using it to "fix" the situation. If repeating, limit to
two cycles on any one occasion; the aim is not to dispose of negative states
but to access a mode of mind in which you can be with them more effectively.
It is never too late for a breathing space, even after the triggering event;
what is trained is orientation and willingness to practice next time.

**Body door:** Direct open, friendly attention to where resistance shows up as
sensation (tension, tightness, pressure). Breathe into the area on the in-breath,
out from it on the out-breath; optional phrase: "Softening, opening, embracing."
Release on the out-breath is not forced — if it happens, fine; if not, equally
fine. Two ways to stay: (a) hold sensations in soft, wide, spacious awareness
with the breath; or (b) investigate in detail — edges, where most intense,
how it changes moment to moment. Bringing awareness to "the not wanting" itself
may change intensity. If overwhelming, [work the edge](working-the-edge.md) —
approach as far as possible with a light touch, then shift to a stabilizing
focus (breath alone, or "breathing with" the feeling in the background) until
ready to approach again. Rumination actively inhibits sensory processing, so
even a small opening to body sensations can begin an important shift.

**Thought door:** If judgmental or self-critical thoughts dominate, relate
differently: record on paper; watch them as [mental events rather than
facts](thoughts-as-mental-events.md); note mood state (anxiety, sadness,
exhaustion); recognize familiar tapes even when camouflaged. Lessens thinking's
"command element" so negative thoughts move through rather than consuming energy
to hold in place.

**Skillful action door:** Acknowledgment is only the first self-care step. Choose
action from wider awareness: **pleasure activities** (special coffee, music,
shared meal) and **mastery activities** (paying bills, errands — no action too
small). Skillful action need not wait until you want to do it; treat it as an
experiment when mood is unmotivated — see [motivation follows
action](motivation-follows-action.md) and [behavioral
activation](behavioral-activation.md). Intention matters: the same action
differs depending on whether it is driven by [avoidance versus
approach](approach-versus-avoidance-in-emotions.md) or by genuine self-care.
For difficult waking moments, ask: "How can I best be kind to myself right
now?" "What is the best gift I can give myself at this moment?" "What would I
do for someone I cared about who felt this way — how can I look after myself
the same way?" Even a 1% shift in how a difficult moment is approached can
compound across subsequent moments into a large downstream effect. Regular
practice reveals a moment's pause and previously unseen choices — "an idea
comes to me and then I say yes or no."

## Relapse action plan

From Session 7 onward, when feeling down the **first step is always a breathing
space**, then choose what to do next — body door, thought door, or **skillful
action**. Key questions: "What do I need for myself right now? How can I best
take care of myself right now?" Depressed mood demands specificity — a choice
must always be made about what to do next.

**Three-step relapse structure** (building on earlier trigger/warning-sign work):

1. Always take a breathing space
2. Gather using past helpful practices (audio track, class reminders, essential
   program message, noting that intense feelings now need the same skills as then)
3. Take action — especially past pleasure or mastery activities, even when
   futile-feeling — in small steps

Some participants write themselves a sealed letter listing actions to take when
depression starts, to open only in that state — pre-loading wisdom that may be
inaccessible when mood drops.

**Waking vulnerability:** mornings are high-risk even without depression history;
for those with a depression history, waking can mimic episode symptoms and evoke
relapse fear. Self-statement: "Just because I am depressed now does not mean I
have to stay depressed." Old thought habits (overgeneralization, "forever,"
"back to square one") undermine action — a breathing space widens perspective on
both the pull of those habits and available skillful action. Shift attention into
the body with gentleness before choosing next action; even a 1% improvement in
handling one difficult moment compounds across subsequent moments.
