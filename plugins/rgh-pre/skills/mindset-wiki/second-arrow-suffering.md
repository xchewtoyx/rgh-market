title: Second-Arrow Suffering
description: >
  The first arrow is unavoidable pain; the second arrow is the added suffering
  of anger, fear, grief, or self-attack layered onto it — and we are the ones
  who fire it, so we can learn to stop.
sources:
  - title: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition"
    resource: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression (Segal, Williams, Teasdale), ch. 11"
---

Being hit by an arrow causes physical pain. For most people, a **second arrow**
follows — added suffering from anger, fear, grief, or distress reacted onto the
first pain. The second arrow often causes the greater unhappiness.

The crucial point: we fire the second arrow at ourselves. Hence we can learn to
free ourselves from it.

In depression-related rumination, getting upset about having negative thoughts
("I should not feel like this; why am I so stupid?") is a second arrow on top of
the first unpleasant feeling. [Feeling tone and reactivity](feeling-tone-and-reactivity.md)
and [three-minute breathing space](three-minute-breathing-space.md) help catch the
sequence before the second arrow lands.

Pairs with [making extra unhappiness](making-extra-unhappiness.md) and
[familiar thought tape](familiar-thought-tape.md).
