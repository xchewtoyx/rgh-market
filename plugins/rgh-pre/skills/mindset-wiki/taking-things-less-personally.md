type: concept
title: Taking Things Less Personally
description: >
  When upset arises, checking whether the event was aimed at you or simply
  happened — and whether the reaction is about the trigger or about feeling
  unheard — reduces selfing and pointless conflict.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 7: Selfing; Cat-Food Lessons"
---

A practical counter to [selfing](selfing.md): when something happens, try to
see it without self-orientation — "maybe it just happened. Maybe it's not
aimed at you." Watch the mind for "I this" and "me that."

Kabat-Zinn's cat-food anecdote illustrates the move in domestic life: his
revulsion at cat dishes in the sink escalated through a sense of betrayal —
"someone in my family didn't respect my request, and I am taking it very
personally." The cat food itself was less the source of anger than not feeling
listened to and respected. His wife and kids saw it differently — he was
making a big deal out of nothing and they would comply only when it felt
reasonable, sometimes without thinking about him at all. Stopping taking it
personally ended the fights; catching the reaction with "This is it," observing
anger as it starts, and staying with the initial revulsion for a second or two
let it dissipate.

People vastly underestimate how much others have going on in their own lives,
making self-referential explanations feel more compelling than they should —
"you really aren't the center of the universe."

Pairs with [personalizing as cognitive distortion](personalizing-cognitive-distortion.md)
and [mind reading as cognitive distortion](mind-reading-as-cognitive-distortion.md)
from the CBT tradition — same pattern, different framings.
