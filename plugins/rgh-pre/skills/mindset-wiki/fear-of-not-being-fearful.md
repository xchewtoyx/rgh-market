title: Fear of Not Being Fearful
description: >
  Beginners in inquiry often believe anger and stress are necessary
  motivators — a stumbling block that confuses managed fear with effective
  action.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 6"
---

One of the biggest stumbling blocks for inquiry beginners: "the fear of not
being fearful" — the belief that without stress or anger "they wouldn't act,
they would just sit around in bliss with drool running down their chins."
Margaret, angry at corporations, initially frames depression alternating
with anger as productive: "it motivates me to act, so it's good to have
some stress. We need it to get things moving."

Tested: "I need violence to motivate me" — eventually no; anger leaves her
"depressed and wiped out." Alternative: "I don't need anger or violence to
accomplish things... If I were to feel anger, I would do The Work on the
thought behind it. This leaves love as the motivator."

Parallel to [fear as a money motivator](fear-as-money-motivator.md) and
[self-criticism is not a healthy
motivator](self-criticism-is-not-a-healthy-motivator.md). See [clarity
over anger in action](clarity-over-anger-in-action.md).
