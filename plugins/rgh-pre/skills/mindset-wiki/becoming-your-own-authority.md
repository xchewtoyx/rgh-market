Health promotion at its best challenges people to become their own authorities
— taking more responsibility for their lives, bodies, and health. Each person is
already the world authority on themselves, potentially, if they attend mindfully;
the information needed to grow, heal, and choose effectively is "right beneath
our noses." What's missing is often careful listening and trust in what body,
mind, and feelings report — a complement to expert medical care, not a replacement.

Authoring your own life requires believing in yourself — something many lack deep
down. **Mindful inquiry can heal low self-esteem:** low self-estimation is often a
miscalculated, mood-colored view. Observing breath or body in meditation reveals
ongoing competence you take for granted while magnifying shortcomings and
projecting onto others that "they are okay and we are not."

When others project wisdom or calm onto a teacher, reflect their positive energy
back as theirs — they need to keep and use it rather than give their power away.
