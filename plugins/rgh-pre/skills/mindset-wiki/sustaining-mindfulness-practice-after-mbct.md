title: Sustaining Mindfulness Practice After MBCT
description: >
  Maintaining gains after an eight-week program needs realistic daily practice,
  values-linked intention, and informal cues — brief regular practice beats
  infrequent long sessions because tools must stay oiled before crisis hits.
sources:
  - title: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition"

Session 8 is "the end of the beginning" — Kabat-Zinn: "the real Session 8 is
the rest of our lives." Theme: **planning for a new way of living** — maintaining
a mindful, caring way of being requires clear intention linked to personally
significant values or positive reasons for self-care.

**Link practice to what you value.** Resolutions fail unless tied to something
you care about. Contemplate: "What is most important to me that this practice
might help with?" — let the question drop like a stone down a well; an answer
may come, then drop further for a deeper one. Example: Joanne found practice
made her more available for her children despite earlier worry it stole family
time — she linked maintenance to remaining available for them. At base, a core
reason recurs: **because I care for myself.** The Welsh *trugaredd* (from
*caru*, to love) — mercy, lovingkindness, unfailing affection — is what
depression opposes (self-criticism, self-loathing). *Trugaredd* "never works
in one direction only": greater self-compassion brought greater compassion for
others.

**Realistic maintenance plans** vary: 30 minutes regular practice; alternating
sitting and yoga; breathing space through the day plus weekend formal practice;
guided meditation from class materials; bells-only unstructured sitting.
**Regular, daily, brief practice** is preferable to longer infrequent practice
— everydayness builds momentum (like keeping a foreign language alive by
speaking it often). Stress is unpredictable; home practice correlates with
benefit — keep tools oiled for when needed. If using the breathing space
regularly, one or two at fixed daily times support using it when difficulty
strikes.

**Larry Rosenberg's informal tips:** (1) when possible, do just one thing at
a time; (2) pay full attention; (3) when mind wanders, bring it back; (4)
repeat step 3 several billion times; (5) investigate distractions. Daily cues
include: five breaths on waking and before sleep; notice posture transitions;
use sounds as bells of mindfulness; pause before eating; notice feet on ground
while walking; scan bodily tightness and breathe into it; mindful routine
activities; mindful listening and speaking.

Hold intention — not striving for goals — to explore "where we are" moment to
moment and step into [being mode](doing-mode-being-mode.md) rather than
[driven-doing mode](driven-doing-mode.md). Endings remind participants to
live alongside felt brokenness and respond to fragility with gentleness.

Pairs with [mbct relapse action plan](mbct-relapse-action-plan.md) and
[informal attention practices](informal-attention-practices.md).
