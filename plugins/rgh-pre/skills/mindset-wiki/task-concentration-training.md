title: Task Concentration Training
description: >
  Deliberately redirecting attention from self-monitoring (bodily sensations,
  thoughts, performance anxiety) toward the external task and environment —
  while still feeling discomfort — reduces anxiety and improves functioning
  without thought suppression.
sources:

Modern CBT addresses not only thought content but **where attention goes**.
For most emotional problems, random distressing thoughts are not the core
problem — the meaning and importance you attach to them is. Viewing "I'm a
hopeless case" as a thought rather than a fact [defuses](thought-defusion.md)
it. Emotional problems also make the mind overfocus on threat and self;
attention can be deliberately steered toward chosen features of experience.

**Task concentration** means becoming adept at redirecting attention away from
yourself (bodily sensations, thoughts, mental images) toward your external
environment and what you are doing — not how well you are doing. Especially
useful when anxious, since anxiety pulls focus inward and toward threat.

This is **not thought suppression**: you control where attention goes while
still feeling uncomfortable; suppression "usually works only briefly, if at all."
The goal is not less concentration overall but concentrating harder on different
(external) aspects.

**Graded rehearsal** mirrors learning to drive on quiet roads before busy ones:

1. **Nonthreatening situations** (little anxiety) — park walk, quiet train,
   close friends.
2. **Challenging situations** (moderate–severe anxiety) — grocery store, rush-hour
   transit, party of strangers.

Progress gradually from list 1 to list 2 as skill develops. Build a hierarchy
of ~10 anxiety-provoking situations from least to most threatening; practice
outward focus at each real-life step.

**Core drills:**

- **Listening:** partner tells a ~2-minute story; summarize it; estimate
  percentage of attention on self / task / environment (must total 100%). Repeat
  face-to-face with deliberate mid-story self-distraction, then redirect back.
- **Speaking:** same structure while you tell the story, refocusing on content
  and listener reactions after self-distraction.
- **Sensory walk:** 20+ minutes cycling attention through each sense in turn,
  then integrating all senses — proves attention is directable; sustained
  outward focus reduces self-consciousness.
- **Eyewitness mode:** imagine police will ask you to describe the scene later;
  deliberately absorb maximal environmental detail — shows how much more you
  retain when attention is outward.

Log practice on a record sheet: who/where/what; self/task/environment
percentages; redirection actions; feelings; learnings; outcome. Social anxiety
often improves when self-monitoring (blushing, sweating) gives way to task focus
(packing groceries, eye contact with cashier, conversation content).
