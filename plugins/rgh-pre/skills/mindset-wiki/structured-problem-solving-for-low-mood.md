type: concept
title: Structured Problem Solving for Low Mood
description: >
  When practical problems pile up during depression, a five-step written
  process — define, brainstorm, evaluate, try in small steps, review —
  makes neglected chores manageable without waiting for motivation to return.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies (Rhena Branch, Rob Willson), ch. 11"
---

Practical problems — bills, letters, neglected tasks — accumulate during
inactivity and deepen overwhelm. Set aside specific daily time for neglected
chores; allocating time makes things feel more manageable.

**Five steps** (apply to each problem after listing under categories such as
relationships, isolation, interests, employment, finances, legal issues,
housing, health):

1. **Define the problem** — write it down clearly.
2. **Brainstorm solutions** — how did you handle similar problems before?
   How have others coped? How would you tackle it if not depressed? How would
   someone else approach it? What professionals or voluntary services could
   help?
3. **Evaluate solutions** — list pros and cons; select the most realistic.
4. **Try out a solution** — break into smaller steps even when the solution
   is good (e.g., financial problems: ask friends for accountant recommendation
   → gather tax returns → contact accountant about fees).
5. **Review** — how much did it help? Further steps, different solution, or
   next problem?

This is [doing-mode](doing-mode-being-mode.md) applied constructively to
external tasks, not [doing-mode rumination](doing-mode-rumination.md) on
mood. Pair with [behavioral activation](behavioral-activation.md) when
inactivity is the primary trap and [antirumination action-focused
thinking](antirumination-action-focused-thinking.md) when the mind loops
on unsolvable "why" questions instead of solvable "how" steps.
