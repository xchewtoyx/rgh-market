---
type: concept
title: Delayed Interoception
description: >
  When body signals for hunger, tiredness, temperature, or pain arrive
  only at crisis threshold, wellbeing requires deliberate periodic
  check-ins rather than waiting to feel the need.
sources:
  - title: "Knowing Why: Adult-Diagnosed Autistic People on Life and Autism"
    resource: "Knowing Why (ASAN), ch. 2: Erin Human, Emerging From Burnout"
---

Interoception — sensing internal body states — is uneven. Some autistic
people do not register hunger, tiredness, cold, or heat until they are at
the very brink of a breakdown. Relying on felt signals alone then
guarantees running the [wellbeing economy](autistic-burnout-as-wellbeing-deficit.md)
into deficit: by the time thirst or exhaustion registers, recovery costs
far more than prevention would have.

The operational fix is scheduled self-check-ins for unmet body needs —
asking deliberately whether food, water, rest, temperature regulation, or
sensory relief is overdue, on a timer if necessary, rather than assuming
the body will prompt in time. This pairs with proactive sensory management
(noise-canceling gear, leaving overload environments before shutdown) and
[reducing demands over trying harder](reducing-demands-over-trying-harder.md)
when the check-in reveals chronic overextension.
