title: Sitting With Dignity
description: >
  An erect, self-supporting meditation posture embodies wakefulness and
  affirms inherent worth — a return to dignity often taught away by past
  wounds or future anxiety.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 4"

Sitting meditation is "mind sitting" — a posture toward the mind, not merely
a body shape. **Taking your seat** carries intentional energy; with right
attitude you can sit anywhere and be at home. **Dignity** is the apt word:
slumping signals low energy; ramrod stiffness signals over-effort; dignity
balances alignment without tension — "think of yourself as a mountain."

Feelings of unworthiness were often taught; sitting with dignity is return
to original worthiness. The posture affirms life's strength even when
depressed or confused — awareness mirrorlike, reflecting what is without
being identical to mental state. Practice builds foundation for stress;
you cannot save mindfulness only for crisis — big events overwhelm without
trenches dug in ordinary moments.

**Hand mudras** radiate inward and outward: palms down (self-containment),
palms up (receptivity), fists (anger — opening to prayer position over heart
can tame anger energy). **Coming out of meditation** deserves the same
mindfulness as the sitting — notice "who" ends it, linger with transition,
apply to life's endings.
