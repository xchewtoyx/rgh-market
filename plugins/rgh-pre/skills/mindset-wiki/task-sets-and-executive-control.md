---
type: concept
title: Task Sets and Executive Control
description: >
  Effortful thinking programs memory to follow instructions that override
  habit — the executive-control capacity that lets you compare, choose, and
  combine ideas System 1 cannot hold at once.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 3"
---

Effort is required when several ideas must stay active at once while each
demands a separate action or must be combined by a rule — rehearsing a
shopping list while entering a store, choosing between entrees, or merging
a surprising survey result with knowledge that the sample was small.

[Fast automatic thinking](fast-and-slow-thinking-heuristic.md) detects simple
relations and integrates information about one thing well, but does not
juggle multiple distinct topics or handle bare statistics. It will match a
meek, tidy description to a librarian stereotype; only the effortful layer
can combine that intuition with base-rate knowledge about how rare
librarians are in the population.

A crucial effortful capability is adopting a **task set**: programming
memory to obey an instruction that overrides habitual responses — counting
every "f"" on a page, monitoring social appropriateness, or searching
memory deliberately. Psychologists call adopting and dropping these sets
**executive control**; it engages whenever conflict must be resolved.

Modern working-memory tests that alternate between demanding tasks while
holding partial results correlate with general-intelligence scores — but
**attention-control efficiency** also predicts air-traffic-controller and
pilot performance beyond IQ alone. Attention control is related to, but
not identical with, raw intelligence.

When several ideas must be held simultaneously, time pressure intensifies
the strain: material decays in memory like balls a juggler cannot set down.
Kahneman's summary: "The most effortful forms of slow thinking are those
that require you to think fast." Pair with [task switching
cost](task-switching-cost.md), [mental energy
budget](mental-energy-budget.md), and [law of least effort in
thinking](law-of-least-effort-thinking.md).
