title: Refusal of the Call
description: >
  The gap between hearing what you are called to do and acting on it is
  almost always filled by fear — and that refusal can last years while
  the cost of inaction compounds.
sources:
  - title: "Courage Is Calling: Fortune Favors the Brave"
    resource: "Courage Is Calling (Ryan Holiday), ch. 2"
---

The hero's journey pattern: a call to adventure is almost always followed
by refusal — too hard, too scary, surely the wrong person was picked.
Florence Nightingale received her "call" at sixteen — a sense that
something was expected of her beyond the constraining roles available to
women — yet let it sit unaddressed for eight years, then sixteen more,
while banal pressures (china, reading to her father, finding a husband,
gossip) outweighed urgent human need she could see clearly.

"The bonds which bound her were only of straw, but she did not break them."
Fear of being unusual kept her home even after encouragement to "go forward."
Family reaction to her choice: treated "as if I had come from committing a
crime." She wrote: "What a murderer am I to disturb their happiness."

"Fear does this. It keeps us from our destiny. It holds us back. It freezes
us." Nightingale later: "How very little can be done under the spirit of
fear." The turn came when she recalled a brief earlier moment of not being
afraid and seized that power again — taking what she needed because it
would not be given.
