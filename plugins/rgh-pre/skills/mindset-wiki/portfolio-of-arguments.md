---
type: concept
title: Portfolio of Arguments
description: >
  A collected set of arguments against an old unhelpful belief and in support
  of a new healthy one — kept in a notebook or phone to consult whenever
  conviction in the new belief wanes.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition (Branch, Willson), ch. 20"
---

Old beliefs are long-standing; expect to argue with yourself several times
before replacement sticks. A **portfolio** gathers arguments on both sides
for quick review — not only when the unhealthy belief is triggered, but
regularly to reaffirm commitment.

**Against the unhelpful belief**, interrogate:

- Is it untrue or inconsistent with reality? (Separate "consistent with my
  upbringing" from "consistent with reality.")
- Is it rigid? Replace "must" with "prefer."
- Is it extreme? One late appointment does not mean always late forever.
- Is it illogical? Wanting approval does not require getting it.
- Is it unhelpful? Trace practical costs — anxiety, depression, misreading
  neutral behavior as disapproval.

**Supporting the helpful alternative**, apply the same five checks (true,
flexible, balanced, logical, helpful). Imagine presenting to a jury; fill
one side of a sheet per belief. Fold in evidence from ABC forms, behavioral
experiments, and positive results from living by new beliefs.

Part of raising [strength of conviction](head-to-heart-problem.md) alongside
[zigzag technique](zigzag-technique.md) and [positive data
log](positive-data-log.md).
