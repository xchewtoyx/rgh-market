type: concept
title: Trigger Self-Inquiry Questions
description: >
  Four questions to run after a trigger fires — separating objective event,
  felt response, historical echo, and the meaning you are assigning —
  so the reaction can be updated for who you are today.
sources:
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Ellie Middleton), ch. 4, Tip 20"
---

Triggers mirror something you have not fully understood about yourself —
not pure external attacks. Before cognitive work, discharge bodily energy
(shake hands, hips) and take a double breath in to signal safety, then
ask:

1. **What is actually happening here?** Objective facts, stripped of story.
2. **What am I feeling as a result?** Name sensations in body and emotion.
3. **What am I actually reacting to?** The specific hook — self-dislike,
   past resonance, or intensity of feeling.
4. **What meaning am I placing on the trigger?** Is the response sized for
   today, or an old pattern replaying?

Example: not being invited to an event — locate the feeling in the body,
hear its message, choose a self-supportive response rather than global
self-condemnation. Reframe: "Triggers are not happening to me; they are
happening with me — I hold the right to decide."

Pair with [raw spots and triggers](raw-spots-and-triggers.md), [stressful
feeling as inquiry signal](stressful-feeling-as-inquiry-signal.md), and
[inquiry on specific incidents](inquiry-on-specific-incidents.md) when a
single scene needs structured testing.
