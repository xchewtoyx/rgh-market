title: Trust in Mindfulness Practice
description: >
  Cultivating trust in one's ability to observe, reflect, and grow from
  experience — and in the present moment itself — stabilizes practice enough
  to persevere when the mind is noisy or doubtful.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 3: Trust"
---

Trust is a feeling of confidence that things can unfold within a dependable
framework of order and integrity. We may not understand what is happening,
but trusting ourselves, another, a process, or an ideal provides a
stabilizing element — security, balance, openness — that intuitively guides
and protects from harm or self-destruction (when not based on naivete).

In mindfulness practice, trust in our ability to observe, be open and
attentive, reflect on experience, and grow from it is necessary for
perseverance; without it, cultivated abilities wither. Part of practice is
cultivating a trusting heart — beginning by looking deeply into what we can
trust in ourselves, dwelling longer in stillness if needed.

If unaware of much of our own behavior and dissatisfied with outcomes, pay
closer attention to choices and their consequences. Experiment with trusting
the present moment — accepting what is felt, thought, or seen right now —
and taking a stand there, letting go into the full texture of now. Repeated
experiments may build a new sense that somewhere deep within resides a
profoundly healthy and trustworthy core, and that intuitions — deep
resonances of the actuality of the present moment — are worthy of trust.

One attitudinal foundation alongside [patience in mindfulness
practice](patience-in-mindfulness-practice.md), [letting go in mindfulness
practice](letting-go-in-mindfulness-practice.md), and [non-judging attitude
in meditation](non-judging-attitude-in-meditation.md).
