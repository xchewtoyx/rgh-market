---
type: concept
title: Mental Travel Into Others' Lives
description: >
  Worrying about someone else's choices often means mentally leaving your
  present and rehearsing their disaster — "unnecessary travel" you can
  stop without controlling them.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 4"
---

"I want them to avoid unnecessary travel during the pandemic" turns to "I
want me to avoid unnecessary travel" — sitting at your kitchen table while
in your mind you're "in a crowded airplane full of covid-19... That's
unnecessary travel. You don't have to go there. It's not necessary, and
it's not helpful."

Past ER trips and imagined futures (hospitals full, dying alone) fuel [catastrophizing](catastrophizing.md). The suffering cause Gloria names:
"Thinking that I know. Thinking that I know what's best." Turnarounds of
shoulds become advice to the self: "I should stay home [in myself, out of
their business]," "I shouldn't go on packed airplanes [in real life or in
my imagination]."

"Stop traveling into your son's life... Your business is to be at peace
wherever you are. When we believe that our children are responsible for our
peace, we suffer." See [staying in your own
business](staying-in-your-own-business.md) and [children are not
responsible for your peace](children-are-not-responsible-for-your-peace.md).
