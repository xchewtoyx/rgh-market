---
type: concept
title: Systematic Biases as Cognitive Design
description: >
  Predictable judgment errors come from how fast thinking is built, not
  mainly from emotion corrupting reason — naming biases helps spot them in
  yourself and others.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 1"
---

Kahneman's heuristics-and-biases program aimed to enrich vocabulary for
judgment errors — like medical labels, naming a pattern (halo effect,
availability) makes it easier to anticipate and recognize. **Biases** are
systematic errors recurring in particular circumstances; focusing on error
does not deny that most judgments are usually fine.

Much mental life is invisible to introspection — impressions arise without
knowing how. The work producing intuitions runs "in silence."

Kahneman and Tversky studied their own deficient statistical intuitions
despite teaching statistics — experts at a conference exaggerated small-sample
replication likelihood. Method: treat shared intuitive answers as the object
of study; intuitions two researchers shared would likely be shared widely.

1974 *Science* article challenged two assumptions: people are generally
rational, and irrationality is mainly emotional. Instead, errors trace to
**the design of cognition** rather than corrupted thought. Including full
demonstration questions let readers observe their own biases directly.

For self-regulation: richer bias vocabulary powers "intelligent gossip" about
your own mistakes — often more motivating than resolutions. Pair with
[representativeness heuristic](representativeness-heuristic.md),
[availability heuristic](availability-heuristic.md), and [fast-and-slow
thinking as a behavioral heuristic](fast-and-slow-thinking-heuristic.md).
