---
type: concept
title: Taking Yourself Away From You
description: >
  When caught up in a story about someone else's life, you mentally leave
  your own — a turnaround that names how rumination on others abandons the
  self in the present.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 8 (Zeffi dialogue)"
---

A stressful belief about others — "she took my friend from me" — can pull
attention so completely into their relationship that you are "over there
living Zoë's life," leaving nobody to live your own. The [inquiry
turnaround](inquiry-turnarounds.md) names this directly: "I took me away
from me."

The suffering isn't only that the other person did something; it's that
you've vacated your own experience to monitor theirs. Question four ("who
would you be without the thought?") often surfaces this by listing what
becomes available again — other friends, other activities, other parts of
school or life that shrink to invisibility while the story runs.

Pair with [rumination](rumination.md), [mind reading as
distortion](naming-cognitive-distortions.md), and [rejection
sensitivity](rejection-sensitivity.md) when exclusion stories trigger
sleep loss, dread before social situations, and reading reluctant "sure"
as proof you're unwanted.
