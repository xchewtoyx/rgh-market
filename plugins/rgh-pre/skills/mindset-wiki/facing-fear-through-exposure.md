type: concept
title: Facing Fear Through Exposure
description: >
  Deliberately confronting feared situations and staying until anxiety
  habituates — without safety behaviors — proves you can bear discomfort
  and breaks the avoidance cycle that keeps fear alive.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies (Rhena Branch, Rob Willson), ch. 10"
---

Anxiety originates in the threat system; trying to **control** anxiety while
confronting fears usually intensifies and prolongs it. The effective move:
accept and tolerate anxiety while doing nothing to stop it — let it pass like
waves shrinking to ripples. For generalized worry, relegate anxiety to the
back burner and carry on with mundane tasks.

**FEAR = Face Everything And Recover.** Exposure/desensitization means
deliberately confronting a fear and staying until anxiety noticeably reduces —
typically 20–60+ minutes. The main goal is overcoming avoidance and
**safety behaviors** that maintain the problem, not perfect anxiety readings.
Repeated confrontation reduces anxiety more quickly each time; aim for daily
repetition when starting.

Build a **graded hierarchy**: list feared triggers ranked mildest to most severe
(0–10 anticipated anxiety). After each exposure, rate actual anxiety and adjust
the next step — situations are often less bad than expected. Exposure should be
challenging but not overwhelming; too-gentle exposure reinforces the idea that
anxiety is unbearable. Jumping in at the deep end (top of hierarchy, no safety
behaviors) can work if you stay long enough to learn nothing terrible happens.

**Shed safety behaviors** — short-term fear reducers that keep anxiety alive
long-term. "Turn your anxiety upside down" by inviting it to do its own thing.
Record session length and anxiety at beginning, middle, and end.

[solution becomes the problem](solution-becomes-the-problem.md).
