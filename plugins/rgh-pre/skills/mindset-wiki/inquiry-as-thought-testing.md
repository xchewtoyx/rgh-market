Inquiry (Byron Katie's "The Work") is putting a thought or story up
against structured questions and turnarounds rather than treating it as
settled fact. The binary framing: you're either attaching to your thoughts
or inquiring — there is no third option. Inquiry rests on the premise that
needed answers are already available inside you; the method is to expose
confusion by testing what you have been believing without examination.

A belief is a thought you have been attaching to, often for years. Suffering
comes not from thoughts appearing but from believing them without inquiry.
Meeting a painful concept with understanding — rather than trying to force
it away — lets it release on its own: "I don't let go of my thoughts; I
meet them with understanding. Then they let go of me." You cannot reliably
drop a painful thought by willpower alone; it returns with the same fear
until met with inquiry — realization, not suppression, is what changes
the pattern. With practice,
inquiry can appear alongside thoughts as their balance, leaving a person
more able to listen without resentment, criticism, or grudges.

Inquiry always leaves you with less of your uncomfortable story. The
structured questions are detailed in [the four questions of
inquiry](the-four-questions-of-inquiry.md); at this level, the move is
simply to choose testing over automatic belief when a thought [argues with
reality](arguing-with-reality.md) or a [stressful
feeling](stressful-feeling-as-inquiry-signal.md) points at one.

When overwhelmed by judgments, don't try to undo all beliefs at once —
investigate the one causing stress now; there's never more than one.
Thoughts are visiting neighbors you misunderstood; everyone is welcome.
A judgment returning a dozen or a hundred times is always an opportunity
to see what attachments remain.

Freedom follows inquiry "in its own way" — you may not recognize it, and
it may not show on the issue you wrote about. A worksheet on your mother
can leave an unrelated irritation gone a day later, or cooking suddenly
loving a week later; release sometimes arrives in the shower the morning
after a session that felt small at the time.

Inquiry appears to be thinking but is actually a way to *undo* thinking.
Thoughts lose power when you realize you aren't doing the thinking anyway
— thoughts simply appear; they're not personal. See [thought
defusion](thought-defusion.md). When inquiry stalls, see [inquiry blocking
patterns](inquiry-blocking-patterns.md).
