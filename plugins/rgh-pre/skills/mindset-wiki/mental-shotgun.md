---
type: concept
title: Mental Shotgun
description: >
  Automatic thinking often computes more than you asked for — extra
  associations, comparisons, and meanings intrude on a focused task and
  can slow or distort judgment without you noticing.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 9"
---

System 1 runs many computations at once. Some are continuous background
assessments — building a 3-D visual field, monitoring for violated
expectations — with no intention required. Others fire only when you
deliberately ask a question (your happiness, your wealth, a politician's
prospects).

Control over which computations run is imprecise: you often compute far
more than the task demands. Kahneman calls this excess the **mental
shotgun** — like pellets scattering beyond the target, it is almost as hard
for [fast automatic thinking](fast-and-slow-thinking-heuristic.md) to
_do only_ what [effortful monitoring](engaged-vs-lazy-monitoring.md)
charges it to do.

**Rhyme task:** hearing word pairs and judging rhyme only, participants were
slower when rhyming words were spelled differently (VOTE–GOAT vs VOTE–NOTE)
— spelling was compared even though the task was sound-only.

**Literal-truth task:** judging whether sentences are literally true,
"Some jobs are jails" was rejected more slowly than equally false but
nonsensical sentences — metaphorical meaning was computed and interfered,
though never requested.

In self-regulation, the shotgun explains why a simple check ("Is this
thought literally true?") can still pull in emotional associations,
memories, and [heuristic substitutions](heuristic-substitution.md). When
you notice extra material crowding a narrow question, naming the intrusion
— "my mind is also running X" — is a first step before
[thought defusion](thought-defusion.md) or inquiry.
