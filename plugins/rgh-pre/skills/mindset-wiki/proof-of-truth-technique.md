type: concept
title: Proof-of-Truth Technique
description: >
  To surface hidden beliefs behind a judgment, stay inside the story and
  list every reason that proves it true — the list reveals the deeper
  assumptions driving the suffering.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 9"
---

One of the best ways to discover [underlying beliefs](underlying-beliefs.md):
for question 1 of inquiry, write your "proof of truth." Rather than moving
immediately to "I can't really know anything," stay in the story — stay
believing what you wrote is true — and list every reason that proves it.
From that list, underlying beliefs become evident.

Worked example — "I am angry at Bobby, Ross, and Roxann because they don't
really respect me." Proof items and the beliefs they reveal:

1. They ignore me when I ask them to put things away → Children should
   respect adults; people should follow my directions; if someone ignores me,
   they don't respect me.
2. They fight noisily when I'm on the phone → There is a time and place for
   everything; clients are more important than children; what people think
   about my children matters.
3. They make fun of things I care about → People shouldn't have fun at my
   expense; children should care about what parents care about.
4. They walk in unannounced expecting attention → Children should wait;
   others are responsible for my happiness.
5. They don't appreciate food I prepare → I need to be appreciated; people's
   tastes should shift when I say so.

After listing proof, inquire into each item with [the four questions of
inquiry](the-four-questions-of-inquiry.md), then return to the original
statement. This is the chapter-scale version of the "Where's your proof?"
prompt from question 2 — applied systematically to excavate [underlying
beliefs](underlying-beliefs.md) rather than stopping at surface facts.
