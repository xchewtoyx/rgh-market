"We think that courage means being unbreakable. Nah. It means getting back
up when you've been broken." The obstacle is often not will to continue but
fear of admitting vulnerability — of needing repair after setback.

Hemingway: "The world breaks every one and afterward many are strong at the
broken places. But those that will not break, it kills." Confusing strength
with invulnerability invites destruction; [Stoicism](inner-citadel.md) helps
you recover by focusing on response, repair, lessons, preparation, helping
others, requesting help, and sacrifice for a greater good.

**Kintsugi:** fifteenth-century Japanese repair fuses broken pottery with
gold- or silver-mixed lacquer so the object becomes more beautiful than
before — scars of the break displayed, not hidden. Legend: a tea bowl
returned from China merely glued prompted the invention. The world's test:
"Death or kintsugi? Will you find a way to become stronger at the broken
places? Or will you so cling to your old ways that you will be shattered?"

Audie Murphy, despite PTSD, vowed not to be defeated by war — to learn faith,
love, and "finally — like countless others — learn to live again." Pair with
[imperfect recovery after setback](imperfect-recovery-after-setback.md),
[friend to yourself](friend-to-yourself.md), and [adversity as
training](adversity-as-training.md).
