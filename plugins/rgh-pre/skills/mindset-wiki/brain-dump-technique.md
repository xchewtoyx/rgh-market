type: concept
title: Brain Dump Technique
description: >
  Transcribing the current whirlwind of thoughts and emotions onto paper
  releases the felt obligation to keep holding them in mind, freeing
  attention for whatever actually needs it.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 3, entry 6: Clear Emotional Clutter with a Brain Dump"
---

A significant amount of the mental load from an overloaded mind comes not
from actually processing anything but from the effort of holding tasks,
worries, and half-formed thoughts in memory so nothing gets lost. Writing
everything down — a task dump, a free-association dump of whatever comes
to mind, a dump of current stressors, or an emotional dump noting current
feelings without judging or analyzing them — externalizes that memory
load, which reduces the background sense of pressure even before anything
on the list has actually been acted on. A useful variant for someone whose
thinking is naturally associative rather than linear is a spatial version
— write a central topic in the middle, branch out related sub-threads and
connections rather than a linear list — which can surface patterns a
straight list would miss.

This pairs well with [the worry period technique](worry-period-technique.md):
where a worry period bounds *when* worrying happens, a brain dump gives a
place to actually put everything currently circulating, so a busy mind —
especially before sleep — has somewhere to set thoughts down rather than
continuing to cycle them purely in memory.
