title: Cost-Benefit Analysis for Change
description: >
  Listing short- and long-term pros and cons of a feeling, thought, or
  behavior — for yourself and others — clarifies whether change is worth
  temporary discomfort and supports motivational flashcards when drive flags.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies (Rhena Branch, Rob Willson), ch. 9"
---

People often maintain unhelpful patterns because short-term benefits at the
moment of behavior (avoiding crowded-commute anxiety) outweigh remembered long-
term costs (habitual lateness). A **cost–benefit analysis (CBA)** examines
pros and cons across four lenses: short term, long term, for yourself, for
other people.

Apply to behaviors, emotions, thoughts/attitudes, or practical options. Write
paired statements — advantages and disadvantages of the current state **and**
of the healthier alternative (e.g. speaking spontaneously vs. rehearsing every
reply in your head).

After completing a CBA, review critically — "benefits" of staying the same may
not survive scrutiny. Boosting the sense that change pays off increases
motivation. Write a **motivational flashcard** summarizing benefits of change and
costs of staying the same; reread when drive flags. Accepting temporary discomfort
for long-term gain is central to CBT work and life goals alike.

**Recording progress:** every one to two weeks, rate problem intensity and life
interference (0–10), state the goal in the same theme, and rate progress toward
the goal (0–10). Look for trends, not day-to-day noise.
