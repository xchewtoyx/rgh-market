type: concept
title: Overgeneralization as Cognitive Distortion
description: >
  Drawing global conclusions with "always," "never," or "people are..." from
  one or a few events — like seeing one black sheep and assuming the whole flock
  is black.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies (Rhena Branch, Rob Willson), ch. 2: Overgeneralizing"
---

Overgeneralizing draws global conclusions from one or a few events: car won't
start → "things like this are always happening to me, nothing ever goes right";
one delayed train passenger → "other people are so stupid"; one instance of
yelling at your child → "I'm a rotten parent."

Countermeasures: get perspective (how many others might have the same problem
right now); suspend judgment; be specific rather than drawing global
conclusions from single incidents — forgive singular errors while still
working to change what's changeable.

Listed in [naming cognitive distortions](naming-cognitive-distortions.md);
often pairs with [all-or-nothing thinking](all-or-nothing-thinking.md) and
[labeling](labels-strip-context.md).
