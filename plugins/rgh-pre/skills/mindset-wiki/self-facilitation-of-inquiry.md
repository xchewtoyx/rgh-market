title: Self-Facilitation of Inquiry
description: >
  The Work does not require a facilitator — you are the teacher and healer
  you have been waiting for, and learning from others' dialogues works by
  looking inside for your own answers.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 3"
---

There is no essential difference between a facilitator guiding someone
through [inquiry as thought-testing](inquiry-as-thought-testing.md) and a
person doing The Work alone. A facilitator can be powerful, but is not
necessary. Watching someone else questioned while looking inside for your
own answers is also a valid way to learn — the method is designed for
self-facilitation.

The book's workshop transcripts are edited records of volunteers sitting
with a facilitator, reading from a [Judge-Your-Neighbor
Worksheet](judge-your-neighbor-worksheet.md), and being guided through [the
four questions of inquiry](the-four-questions-of-inquiry.md) and
[turnarounds](inquiry-turnarounds.md) into self-induced realizations. The
reader's job is not to collect Katie's answers but to use each dialogue as
practice material (see [reading dialogues as your own
work](reading-dialogues-as-your-own-work.md)).

After practicing, the questions become internalized — any single question,
inquired into deeply, can be sufficient to shift a stuck belief.
