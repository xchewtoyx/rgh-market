title: Arguing With Reality Always Loses
description: >
  Clinging to how someone should have lived or died keeps you in a war
  you lose every time — inquiry shifts from changing the past to returning
  to your own present life.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 12"
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 13"
---

"Sam should have stuck around" — not true: "He left. He died." Believing it
feels tired, sad, separate. "That's how it feels to argue with what is...
I notice that I lose, one hundred percent of the time. It's hopeless. We
take these concepts to the grave with us if they're not examined. Concepts
are the grave we bury ourselves in."

Without the thought: present in your own life, "even let him die in his
own time." Turn "I need Sam back" — admitted not true — to **"I need
myself back"** and **"I don't need Sam back."** "You keep going back to
that cliff and falling off with Sam... So come back yourself."

"What I love most about reality is that it's always the story of a past.
And what I love most about the past is that it's over... I don't argue
with it." How do you know he lived a full life? "It's over. He lived it
to the end — his end, not the end you think he should have had."

The same move applies to smaller disappointments: if you were perfectly
clear, what you want is what already arrived — the wrong dish, the lost
arm, the conversation that happened — because [arguing with what
is](arguing-with-reality-always-loses.md) is arguing with a past that
cannot change. Clarity then frees [effective action in the
present](clarity-enables-effective-action.md): restate the order, adapt the
task, inquire into the story that says you cannot cope.

Grieving parents may feel inquiry betrays the child; many aren't ready yet,
"and that's as it should be." When ready, see [grief inquiry after
loss](grief-inquiry-after-loss.md). "You move totally away from reality
when you believe that there is a legitimate reason to suffer."
