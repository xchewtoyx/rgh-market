---
type: concept
title: Regression to the Mean
description: >
  Extreme performances tend to be followed by less extreme ones by chance
  alone — so praise after success and criticism after failure can look
  causal when they are not.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 41 (Tversky & Kahneman, 1974)"
---

Regression toward the mean: extreme outcomes on one occasion tend to be
followed by less extreme outcomes next time, purely by chance. People
don't expect this intuitively because they expect outcomes to stay as
extreme as the inputs that produced them ([representativeness
heuristic](representativeness-heuristic.md)).

Flight instructors: praise after a smooth landing is often followed by a
worse landing; criticism after a rough landing by improvement. Conclusion
that punishment works better than praise is unwarranted — the pattern is
exactly what regression predicts regardless of any causal effect.

General consequence: "by chance alone, one is most often rewarded for
punishing others and most often punished for rewarding them," and people
don't notice this contingency. Spurious causal stories get invented
whenever regression is noticed.

Practically: before crediting a intervention (or blaming yourself) for a
swing from a very good or very bad period, ask whether the previous period
was unusually extreme and whether chance alone would predict a return toward
average — especially relevant when evaluating [process vs. outcome](process-vs-outcome-evaluation.md)
after a single dramatic result.
