---
type: concept
title: Look at Your Feet
description: >
  When anxiety fires about a future problem, checking where your feet actually
  are tests whether the worry is actionable now — a pause button enabled by
  mindfulness's gap between self and thought.
sources:
  - title: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery"
    resource: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery (Sol Smith), ch. 8"
---

The brain does not distinguish well between a problem happening now and one
that may happen later — a big assignment can trigger the same response as
being chased. **Look at your feet:** your feet tell you where and when you
can actually address a problem. Feet in bed at 3 a.m. — not time to solve
work or money worries. Feet at your desk during work hours — work on work
problems then. Worried about eviction — are your feet currently being forced
out by a sheriff? If not, you're worrying about something not presently
happening.

Worrying weeks ahead rarely reduces sting when crisis arrives; process takes
time; how you cope on the day will differ from imagination. "On average,
everything is average" — most days until a crisis day, you can function with
a fraction of the stress currently spent.

Requires the gap [watch the watcher](watch-the-watcher.md) cultivates between
you and anxious thoughts. Pairs with [worry period technique](worry-period-technique.md)
for deferring non-actionable worries and [present task focus against
catastrophizing](present-task-focus-against-catastrophizing.md).
