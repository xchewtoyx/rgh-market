---
type: concept
title: Familiar Thought Tape
description: >
  Naming a recurring negative thought pattern as a familiar "tape" loosens its
  adhesive grip — you may still hear it again, but you no longer treat it as
  fresh fact requiring action.
sources:
  - title: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition"
    resource: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression (Segal, Williams, Teasdale), ch. 14"
---

Larry Rosenberg's analogy: watching the same recurring thought pattern is like
watching *Gone with the Wind* a twelfth time — eventually you just don't care.
Naming helps: "this is my 'I can't stand my boss' tape." Naming won't necessarily
switch the tape off (it will likely recur, like a holiday children's movie), but
it changes the relationship — from treating the content as fact requiring serious
action to treating it as a tape that keeps running until the batteries run down.

Acknowledging familiar patterns and the hurt they cause can free you from the
adhesive quality that ironically comes from **aversion** to them — fighting the
tape keeps you glued to it. This is a specific application of [thoughts as mental
events](thoughts-as-mental-events.md) and [rumination](rumination.md) recognition.
