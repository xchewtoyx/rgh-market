title: Linguistic Tells for Cognitive Distortions
description: >
  Each of the brain's habitual perceptual distortions leaves a
  characteristic linguistic signature — a verb tense or word choice — that
  makes it possible to spot which distortion is active in a thought as
  it's being formed or spoken.
sources:
  - title: Solve for Happy
    resource: "Solve for Happy (Mo Gawdat), Part 3, Chapter 9: Is It True?"
---

A quick detection method for the perceptual distortions covered by
[filtering](brain-filters-information.md), [assumptions](brain-fills-gaps-with-assumptions.md),
[predictions](predictions-treated-as-fact.md), [memory
distortion](memory-distortion-through-blending.md), [labels](labels-strip-context.md),
[emotional bias](emotional-bias-in-judgment.md), and
[exaggeration](exaggeration-and-peak-shift.md): each tends to surface with
a recognizable linguistic marker. A genuinely verified statement uses
direct-observation verbs ("I saw," "I heard," "I was told by X
specifically"). An assumption uses hedged, internally-sourced verbs ("I
guess," "I assume," "I'm sure," "I feel like"). A memory-based claim
appears in past tense generalized as ongoing truth ("this is how it
always is," "that's just how she is"). A prediction appears in future
tense stated with unwarranted certainty. A label appears as a short,
confident, single-word judgment ("idiot," "boring," "lucky"). Emotional
bias appears attached to phrases like "I love," "I hate," "she's a pain."
Exaggeration appears as absolute or blanket words ("always," "never,"
"everyone," "completely").

The practical use is as a fast self-check on any strong, confidently-held
thought: which of these markers is present, and what would the same
statement sound like with the marker stripped out — restated as only what
was actually, directly observed? A statement run through this filter
typically ends up sounding notably drier and more limited than the
original felt conviction ("she's drop-dead gorgeous" reduces to "she has
symmetrical features and long hair"), which is itself useful information
about how much the original statement was carrying beyond the verified
facts. This pairs directly with [the "Is It True?"
technique](is-it-true-technique.md) as the diagnostic step before applying
it.
