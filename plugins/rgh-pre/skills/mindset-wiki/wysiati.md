---
type: concept
title: WYSIATI (What You See Is All There Is)
description: >
  The mind builds the best coherent story it can from whatever is currently
  active — without pausing for missing information — so confidence tracks
  story coherence, not evidence completeness.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 8"
---

WYSIATI — "what you see is all there is" — names a core limitation of
fast associative thinking: it constructs the best possible coherent story
from currently activated ideas and cannot allow for information it does
not have. The quantity and quality of the data behind the story matter
little to how good the story feels.

Told only that someone is intelligent and strong, people quickly judge her
a good leader without asking what else might matter — and without waiting
for possible later facts like "corrupt and cruel." The story revises if
new information arrives, but there is no discomfort in the gap; [first
impressions](halo-effect.md) keep disproportionate weight.

Several familiar distortions follow from the same mechanism:

- **Overconfidence** — subjective confidence depends mostly on internal
  coherence, not on how much evidence is actually in hand.
- **Framing effects** — "90% survival" feels better than "10% mortality"
  because only one formulation is seen at a time.
- **Base-rate neglect** — a vivid description feels like the whole case;
  statistical base rates that should temper it often never come to mind.

In one-sided legal scenarios, hearing only one lawyer's spin can produce
*more* confidence than hearing both sides — because a single coherent
story is easier to build than a balanced one. The practical countermove is
to ask explicitly what is missing: what would change this read if it were
known? Pair with [brain fills gaps with
assumptions](brain-fills-gaps-with-assumptions.md) and [high-stakes bias
vigilance](high-stakes-bias-vigilance.md).
