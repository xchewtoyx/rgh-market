---
type: concept
title: Outward Emotion Is Valid
description: >
  Crying, flapping, needing time to process, or showing anger safely are
  legitimate communication — not proof of crisis or failure to regulate.
sources:
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Ellie Middleton), ch. 4"
---

"It's okay to show emotions outwardly." No obligation to perform being
"fine." Crying can signal "there is a lot going on right now" rather than
requesting rescue — but society reads crying as last-resort after failed
suppression, so onlookers often over-respond.

"A list of things that are absolutely fine": crying in public; flapping
when joyful; asking for time out; admitting you're having a hard time;
difficulty regulating tone when emotions run high; screaming into a pillow;
needing time to process rather than "getting over it" immediately; actually
feeling feelings instead of suppressing them.

Brené Brown: "Staying vulnerable is a risk we have to take if we want to
experience connection." Pairs with [no bad feelings](no-bad-feelings.md)
and [buffer between feeling and
action](buffer-between-feeling-and-action.md) — expression is not the same
as acting out harmfully.
