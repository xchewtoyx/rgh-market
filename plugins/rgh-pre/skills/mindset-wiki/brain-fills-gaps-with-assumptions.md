---
type: concept
title: Assumptions Fill Perceptual Gaps
description: >
  Where information is missing — from raw visual gaps to social and
  interpersonal unknowns — the brain automatically fills in a plausible
  story and presents it as if it had actually been observed.
sources:
  - title: Solve for Happy
    resource: "Solve for Happy (Mo Gawdat), Part 3, Chapter 9: Is It True?"
---

The brain doesn't leave gaps visible as gaps — it fills them, and the
fill feels like direct perception rather than inference. This happens at
the literal physiological level (the retina's blind spot, where the optic
nerve attaches, is invisibly patched using surrounding visual data) and at
the level of well-known visual illusions where a "corrected" perception
persists even after being shown proof it's wrong. The same mechanism
extends to social judgment: the same underlying fact ("my boss missed her
target last quarter") can be filled in with entirely opposite stories
(she's a threat to the team; she's an ally facing pressure), and the brain
presents whichever fill it lands on with the same felt certainty as
something actually witnessed.

Because an assumption feels identical to an observation from the inside,
the useful discipline is checking the actual source of any confident
belief: was this literally seen or heard, or was it filled in around
something that was only partially observed? The [linguistic tells for
cognitive distortions](linguistic-tells-for-cognitive-distortions.md)
technique gives a concrete way to catch this distinction in real time —
assumption-based statements characteristically use words like "I guess,"
"I assume," "I'm sure," rather than "I saw" or "I heard."
