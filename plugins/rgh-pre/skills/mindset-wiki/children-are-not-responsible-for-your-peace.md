---
type: concept
title: Children Are Not Responsible for Your Peace
description: >
  Needing children to act safely or agree with you so you can feel secure
  makes their choices your emotional business — inquiry redirects care
  toward your own fear instead of their freedom.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 4"
---

"What do you get if they cancel their trip?" → feeling they're safe, feeling
secure, "they've made my life easier." "So it's really all about you...
Nothing there for your son." A parent's desire for a child's safety can be
"a subtle form of selfishness" once you see it as managing your own fear.

Honest script while respecting autonomy: tell him you'd worry and grieve
but he's free to go — "And I prefer that you're in my life. I love you.
And yes, I'm going to worry, and that's for me to take care of." Katie's
model: if a son said he's jumping off a cliff, she might ask if he thinks
that's a good idea; if he still jumps, respect that "he's doing the best
that he can"; if he asks what she thinks, say the truth.

"How dependent am I on my children?" "You have to give him his freedom?
It's not yours to give. He has his freedom." Believing they're reckless
can make you deceptive — calling without revealing you'd seen the Facebook
post. Their silence may be kindness: sparing your feelings. See [mental
travel into others' lives](mental-travel-into-others-lives.md) and
[peace requires only one person](peace-requires-only-one-person.md).
