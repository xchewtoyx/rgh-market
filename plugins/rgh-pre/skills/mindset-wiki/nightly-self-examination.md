type: concept
title: Nightly Self-Examination
description: >
  A brief, structured daily review of one's own actions and reactions —
  conducted alone, honestly, and without an audience to perform for —
  catches patterns and lapses that would otherwise go unexamined simply
  because nothing forces them into view.
sources:
  - title: "Letters on Ethics: To Lucilius"
    resource: "Letters on Ethics (Seneca), Letter 28"
  - title: "Discourses, Fragments, Handbook"
    resource: "Discourses (Epictetus), Book 3.10 (quoting the Pythagorean self-examination verses)"
---

A short daily habit, done at day's end while the day's events are still
fresh: review what was done and said, and ask where it went wrong,
where it went right, and what was left undone. The Pythagorean version
frames it as a question run through in order — "where did I go wrong?
What did I do? What duty did I neglect?" — before allowing sleep. The
practice works specifically because it's private and unaudienced: run
as an honest internal proceeding rather than a performance, it surfaces
things a person would otherwise never notice about their own patterns,
the way [catching automatic thoughts](catching-automatic-thoughts.md)
surfaces a thought that would otherwise pass unnoticed in the moment it
occurred.

A more adversarial version of the same practice treats the review as a
structured internal trial: take the role of the accuser first, stating
the case against one's own conduct as forcefully as it can honestly be
stated, then the judge weighing the case fairly, and only last the
advocate offering whatever genuine defense the conduct deserves —
deliberately in that order, so the defense doesn't get to go first and
soften what actually happened. The point isn't self-punishment; it's
accuracy — the same discipline [is it true?](is-it-true-technique.md)
applies to a single thought, run instead across a whole day's conduct on
a fixed daily schedule, so a pattern of small lapses becomes visible
before it hardens into something larger and harder to notice or correct.
