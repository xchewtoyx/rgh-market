title: Anti-Anxiety Thinking Attitudes
description: >
  Realistic probability estimates, moderate language, accepting common
  anxiety sensations, and tolerating discomfort — rather than catastrophizing
  or rating anxiety as unbearable — reduce the vicious thought-feeling cycle.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies (Rhena Branch, Rob Willson), ch. 10"
---

Anxious thinking and anxious feeling fuel each other in a vicious cycle.
Facing fears requires adopting attitudes that cool the heat:

**Realistic probability:** anxious focus overestimates how likely bad events
are. If you always imagine the worst, deliberately assume things are likely to
be okay — like steering a bike with offset handlebars.

**Avoid extreme labels:** "awful," "horrible," "the end of the world" ramp up
reactions. Rate events as bad, unfortunate, inconvenient, or unpleasant instead.

**Take the fear out of fear:** strong bodily sensations (nausea, derealization)
are common in anxiety, not proof of impending catastrophe or "going crazy."
Understanding normal anxiety physiology stops you adding fear on top of fear.
Check with a doctor if concerned about physical symptoms before deliberate
exposure.

**Tolerate discomfort:** evaluating anxiety as unbearable ("I can't stand it")
makes it worse; tolerance improves outcomes — and the less you avoid, the weaker
anxiety becomes.

tolerance](low-frustration-tolerance.md), and [facing fear through
