Cognitive therapy taught patients to catch negative thoughts and test them as
hypotheses. MBCT adds an explicit relationship shift: stop relating **from**
thoughts and start relating **to** them as objects in awareness — "mental
events" arising with depressed mood that need not be taken personally. Session
6's phrase: **"thoughts are not facts"** — including "even the ones that say
they are not." Thinking is usually reliable, which is why we rarely question it;
the point is that every thought mixes bare fact with inference.

Thoughts feel especially compelling when they demand immediate action ("I ought
to phone Mary") or mesh with a prevailing negative mood ("I can't do this; I
may as well give up"). In depression, thoughts become "propaganda directed
against yourself" — contrary information is ignored, consistent information
noticed, self-reinforcing.

Metaphors for decentering: cinema screen (watch thoughts appear and vanish);
[train of association](train-of-association-metaphor.md) (hop on unknowingly,
wake up miles from where you boarded); stage with wings; sky and clouds (mind
as sky, thoughts as weather); sounds as bare auditory sensations before shifting
to thoughts as passing events — see [choiceless awareness](choiceless-awareness.md).
When charge is high, risk being pulled into the
story — first step is always body awareness: surround intense sensation with
friendly interest, soften resistance, return to breath — the move to stand
[behind the waterfall](awareness-versus-thinking.md) where cascading thoughts
are felt but "are not you."

Not everyone thinks verbally; some experience image-thoughts. Some thoughts
arrive "from behind" like theater whispers — harder to catch; practice briefly.
Naming a familiar pattern ("this is my 'I can't stand my boss' tape") changes
relationship from fact requiring action to a recurring tape — see [familiar
thought tape](familiar-thought-tape.md).

**Schoolchild story:** suffering abandonment waiting for a father who was never
coming (wrong day remembered) — relief when the mix-up is realized though
nothing external changed. We react to explanations, not bare facts; thoughts
become "carved in stone" rather than "written on water."

**Ear analogy:** mind is to thoughts as ear is to sounds — truck noise is not
part of you; thoughts can be discrete events in awareness.

**Depression landscape:** automatic negative thoughts are symptoms of mood like
aches in flu — universality ("This is depression — it's not me") de-isolates.
Analyzing when low can intensify fear; watching thoughts like clouds is wiser.
Label recurring patterns ("Critical Mind") as visitors, not self. Beneath
thoughts lie body feelings — explore with affectionate awareness after
decentering from thought content.

Pairs with [thought defusion](thought-defusion.md), [three-minute breathing
space](three-minute-breathing-space.md), and [emotion as entangled
package](emotion-as-entangled-package.md).

**Mood gives birth to thoughts:** Session work on ABC-style links often
foregrounds thoughts→feelings; the reverse arrow matters equally. Ask "What
am I feeling now, specifically?" — surfacing feelings not otherwise apparent
offers another place to stand relative to rumination's pull. Identical
objective situations produce radically different interpretations depending
on the frame of mind brought to them — "just because we think something
doesn't make it so."

Interpretations arise almost instantaneously, so we do not notice them
coloring reactions. Thoughts born from context and feeling are camouflaged
by that context and seem simply real.

**After a breathing space**, if negative thoughts persist, four options:
(1) watch thoughts come and go without following them; (2) view them as
mental events, not facts — decide how true and how to deal; (3) write them
down for wider perspective; (4) focus with compassion on feelings giving
birth to the thought — "What feelings are here? What physical sensations?
How can I best look after myself right now?"

Caution: using a breathing space merely to muster counter-arguments ("answering
back") reinforces hopelessness and differs from standing in a different
**relationship** to thoughts. "Thoughts are not facts — even the ones that say
they are not" carries a different tone from self-critical arguing thoughts down.

See [welcoming decentering](welcoming-decentering.md) for the allowing stance
beyond thought content alone.
