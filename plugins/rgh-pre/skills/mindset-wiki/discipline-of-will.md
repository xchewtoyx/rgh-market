---
type: concept
title: Discipline of the Will
description: >
  Will is what you control completely when perception and action fail —
  enduring, contextualizing, and finding meaning in what cannot be flipped.
sources:
  - title: "The Obstacle Is the Way: The Timeless Art of Turning Trials into Triumph, Expanded 10th Anniversary Edition"
    resource: "The Obstacle Is the Way (Ryan Holiday), ch. 27"
---

Will is internal power the outside world cannot touch. In an unchangeable
negative situation, you still choose what to do with it — breakthrough,
learning, humility, comfort for others. It must be cultivated: preparing for
adversity, learning [the art of acquiescence](amor-fati.md), practicing
cheerfulness in dark times.

Not simply "how badly you want" — true will includes surrender: "God willing"
as quiet humility, resilience, flexibility versus breakable ambition.

**Lincoln as exemplar:** lifelong depression, poverty, losses, repeated
defeats — struggles he believed prepared him for greater work. "This too
shall pass" for any situation. Inner fortress from living with depression
let him endure the Civil War as "an unpleasant duty... as smoothly as
possible." Compassion from his own suffering; patience from knowing hard
things take time; purpose in a cause larger than himself.

If Perception and Action are disciplines of mind and body, **Will** is
discipline of heart and soul — the one thing always in your control, unlike
perception (partly mitigatable) or action (thwartable). Fortitude and wisdom
about where obstacles fit — strength to endure what cannot be overcome ("flip
the unflippable").

Hardest discipline hierarchy: easier to control perceptions than to give up
controlling others; easier to persist in action than endure pain; easier to
think and act than practice wisdom. These harder lessons are most critical
for wresting advantage from adversity.

**Practice litany:** prepare for harder times; accept what you cannot change;
manage expectations; persevere; [love your fate](amor-fati.md); protect the
inner self; submit to a larger cause; remember mortality; begin again.

Pair with [equanimity under pressure](equanimity-under-pressure.md) and
[fear-setting](fear-setting.md).
