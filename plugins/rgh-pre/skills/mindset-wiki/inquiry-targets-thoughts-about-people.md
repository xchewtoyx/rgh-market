title: Inquiry Targets Thoughts About People
description: >
  The Work is always on your thoughts about people, not on changing the
  people themselves — which is why inquiry on one relationship can free
  patterns that show up elsewhere.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 3"
---

"We think we are doing The Work on people, but actually we're working on
our thoughts about people." The person in the story is the hook; the
beliefs are the target. This is why a worksheet focused on your mother can
later improve your relationship with your daughter — the same uninvestigated
thoughts were attached to both roles without your noticing.

The [image in your head is not the
person](image-in-your-head-is-not-the-person.md): inquiry meets the mental
picture and the judgments layered on it, not a puppet you can control through
sheer belief change. [Staying in your own
business](staying-in-your-own-business.md) follows naturally — stress
comes from mentally living someone else's choices while neglecting your own.

This reframing pairs with [close relationships as
teachers](close-relationships-as-teachers.md): recurring friction is signal
about which thought is active, not proof that the other person is uniquely
the problem.
