type: concept
title: Behavioral Tests for Scout Mindset
description: >
  Feeling objective, being smart, or believing oneself fair-minded are not
  evidence of practicing scout mindset — only concrete past behavior is,
  because motivated reasoning feels like clear thinking from the inside
  regardless of which mindset is actually running.
sources:
  - title: "The Scout Mindset: Why Some People See Things Clearly and Others Don't"
    resource: "The Scout Mindset (Julia Galef), Chapter 4: Signs of a Scout"
---

Three common self-assessments are unreliable signals of [scout
mindset](scout-mindset.md): feeling calm and dispassionate while reasoning
(everyone's motivated reasoning feels this way from the inside, and
believing yourself objective can even make you trust your own snap
intuitions more, not less); being smart or knowledgeable (intelligence is
a tool usable in service of either [scout or soldier
mindset](soldier-mindset.md) — more knowledgeable people are, if anything,
better equipped to construct sophisticated justifications for what they
already wanted to believe); and believing you're the type of person who
readily changes their mind (easy to assert, rarely checked against actual
track record).

Since self-image is unreliable, the real test is behavioral, checked
against specific past instances rather than a general self-rating:

- Have you told someone else, unprompted, that they were right and you
  were wrong about something specific?
- Do you have an actual track record of acting on personal criticism, or
  making it structurally easier for people to criticize you — not just a
  stated belief that you "value honesty"?
- Can you point to a real case where you set out to check a belief of
  your own and found it didn't hold up?
- Do you take active precautions against fooling yourself — deciding
  success criteria in advance so they can't be moved after the fact,
  or seeking out an assessment before it's known which answer would be
  convenient?
- Can you name a specific critic of your views, profession, or life
  choices whom you consider thoughtful and good-faith, even though you
  think they're wrong?

The single most telling sign may be the reverse of what it seems: being
able to point to specific past occasions when you were clearly in
[soldier mindset](soldier-mindset.md) yourself. Since motivated reasoning
is universal, never being able to identify it in your own past behavior is
much better explained by low self-awareness than by genuinely superior
objectivity.
