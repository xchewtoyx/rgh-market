---
type: concept
title: Coming Back to Mindfulness
description: >
  Lapses from regular practice are themselves informative — noticing stiffness,
  impatience, or mindlessness after time away teaches more than uninterrupted
  practice alone.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 5"
---

Kabat-Zinn: "It is in the coming back to mindfulness that seeing lies." Skipping
yoga or meditation for a while and returning reveals effects by contrast — you
learn more by coming back than by never stopping, if you notice bodily stiffness,
impatience, or resistance to staying with the breath. The same applies to life
outside formal practice: track how lack of regular practice affects anxiety about
time, results, and relationships; trace mindless patterns and their triggers.

"Not practicing is an arduous practice" — forgetting mindfulness can teach more
than being mindful all the time, since our mindless tendencies are robust. Most
people needn't worry about lacking lapses. Floor practice (lying down for body
scan or mindful yoga) can break habitual neurological patterning by putting the
body in an unfamiliar position, opening a "body door" into the present.
