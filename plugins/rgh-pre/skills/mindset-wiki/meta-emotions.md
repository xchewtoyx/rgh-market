type: concept
title: Meta-Emotions
description: >
  Feelings about feelings — such as guilt about depression — add a second
  layer of unhealthy emotion that can block work on the primary problem until
  the meta-emotion is recognized and addressed.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition (Branch, Willson), ch. 7"
---

"Meta" (beside/after) emotions are feelings about your feelings. You can give
yourself a **second helping** of unhealthy emotion by holding rigid demands
about which emotions are acceptable.

Common example: guilt about depression arising from demands like "I mustn't
let others down" or "I mustn't put strain on loved ones." Typical guilt-
producing thoughts in depression:

- "I should be contributing more to running the home."
- "I must demonstrate love and care to my children."
- "My family is worried and I'm making them suffer."
- "I shouldn't be neglecting my friends."

Recognizing meta-emotions matters because they block work on the primary
emotional problem — resolving guilt about being depressed frees you to work
on the depression itself more effectively. Check whether another emotion is
getting in the way when identifying a first emotion (e.g., shame about anger).

Related to [making extra unhappiness](making-extra-unhappiness.md) (unhappiness
about unhappiness) and [healthy versus unhealthy negative
emotions](healthy-vs-unhealthy-negative-emotions.md).
