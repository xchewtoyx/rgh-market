---
type: concept
title: Cloud of Forgetfulness
description: >
  The habitual drift into robotlike seeing and doing that breaks contact with
  the present moment — the crossroad of here and now obscured by preoccupation
  with past and future.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 1"
---

We stand at "the crossroad of here and now," but a **cloud of forgetfulness**
about where we are sets in and we get lost — momentarily losing touch with
ourselves and the full extent of our possibilities, falling into robotlike
seeing, thinking, and doing. Left unchecked, those clouded moments can
stretch out and become most of our lives.

To be truly in touch with where we already are requires pausing long enough
to let the present moment sink in — feel it, see it in fullness, hold it in
awareness — before accepting, learning, and moving on. Instead we are
preoccupied with past or future, looking for someplace else to stand where
things will be better.

Not knowing you are in such a dream is what Buddhists call ignorance or
mindlessness; being in touch with not-knowing is [mindfulness](mindfulness.md).
Waking up is the work of meditation — systematic cultivation of present-moment
awareness. Others may try to help, but "waking up is ultimately something
that each one of us can only do for ourselves."
