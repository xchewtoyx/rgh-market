---
type: concept
title: Work and Money Problems as Thinking Problems
description: >
  Apparent work or money crises usually trace to uninvestigated beliefs
  about security, success, and motivation — not to the numbers or job title
  themselves.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 6"
---

"If our thinking is clear, how can work or money be the problem? Our
thinking is all we need to change. It's all we can change." Katie reports
never seeing a work or money problem that did not turn out to be a
[thinking problem](inquiry-as-thought-testing.md) once investigated.

Corporate settings can use the same method: employees and bosses judging
one another on paper, then doing The Work and turnarounds, reportedly
producing more clarity, honesty, responsibility, and a more productive
workforce — because the friction was in unquestioned stories about roles
and fairness, not in the org chart itself.

Money fears often rest on beliefs like "I need money to be happy" or "I
need my money to be safe and secure" — states that banks, markets, and
other people cannot actually guarantee. Living inside those thoughts is
"a hopeless state of mind" no matter the balance sheet. See [fear as a
money motivator](fear-as-money-motivator.md), [success as
concept](success-as-concept.md), and [happiness is a clear
mind](happiness-is-a-clear-mind.md).

Loyalty stories at work can hide resentment: keeping an underperforming
employee out of guilt teaches everyone that children count more than
competence — [guilt is expensive](guilt-is-expensive.md) compared with
honest conversation or a better-fitting role.
