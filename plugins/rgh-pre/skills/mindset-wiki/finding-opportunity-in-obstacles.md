---
type: concept
title: Finding Opportunity in Obstacles
description: >
  Most obstacles contain a usable advantage for those who refuse to be
  rattled and deliberately look for what the situation offers — even when
  the package is poorly wrapped.
sources:
  - title: "The Obstacle Is the Way: The Timeless Art of Turning Trials into Triumph, Expanded 10th Anniversary Edition"
    resource: "The Obstacle Is the Way (Ryan Holiday), ch. 13"
---

"A good person dyes events with his own color…and turns whatever happens
to his own benefit." Step one: refuse to be overwhelmed, rattled, or
discouraged — as most problems (and intimidation tactics) want you to be.
Step two: adaptability — "You're just what I was looking for."

The defeat is obvious; the opportunity "requires a little squinting."
Preconceptions about how things should be make us assume disadvantage when
"every situation is an opportunity for us to act." A bad boss becomes a
safe laboratory: experiment with tactics, add skills, update a résumé, try
new communication, stand up for yourself — with quitting as a backstop.
A difficult rival keeps you alert, raises stakes, motivates proof, hardens
you, and shows whom not to become. Erased work is a chance to start fresh
and emerge twice as skilled at the task.

**Reframes for others' negative behavior:** rude people underestimate you
(an advantage); conniving people mean no apology needed when you make an
example of them; critical people set expectations you can exceed; lazy
people cede control and credit.

Elite athletes after serious injury often report adversarial or
post-traumatic growth — added perspective, desire to help others,
realization of strengths, and greater ability in the same area that once
triggered fear. "That which doesn't kill me makes me stronger" is fact,
not cliché: the extent of the struggle determines the extent of the growth.

"The obstacle is an advantage, not adversity. The enemy is any perception
that prevents us from seeing this." Fighting, denying, or running away
doesn't remove the obstacle or the benefit beneath it. "The package may be
poorly wrapped... the gift—the opportunity—is what happens after you tear
all that off."

Distinct from [obstacle as fuel](obstacle-as-fuel.md), which emphasizes
actively converting difficulty into material to work with in the moment.
Pair with [steadying your nerves](steadying-your-nerves.md) and [context
and framing in perspective](context-and-framing-in-perspective.md).
