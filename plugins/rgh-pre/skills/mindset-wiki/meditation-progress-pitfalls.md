title: Meditation Progress Pitfalls
description: >
  Claiming you are getting somewhere or getting nowhere in meditation both
  arrest practice — self-inflation and attachment to boredom or staleness
  betray the essence of allowing yourself to be where you already are.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 8"
---

The thinking mind is the biggest obstacle on the lifelong mindfulness path.

**Pitfall 1 — claiming "getting somewhere."** After a satisfying transcendent
moment, the ego lays claim and takes credit. "You are no longer into meditation
but into advertising." Even genuine insight, once claimed by self-serving
thinking, clouds over and loses authenticity. Reminder: "I," "me," and "mine"
are currents of thinking liable to carry you from your heart and direct
experience.

**Pitfall 2 — claiming "getting nowhere."** Staleness and boredom are not
inherently wrong — practice may be deepening even as it feels flat. The pitfall
is inflating such experiences and getting attached to them as special.

**Try when either arises:** "Where am I supposed to get?" "Who is supposed
to get somewhere?" "Why are some mind states less valid to observe than others?"
"Am I inviting mindfulness into each moment, or mindlessly repeating forms?"
"Am I using meditation as a technique?"

Meditation is the one human activity where you are not trying to get anywhere
else — allowing yourself to be where and as you already are. Bitter medicine
when you dislike what is happening, but especially worth swallowing then.
