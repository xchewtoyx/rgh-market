type: concept
title: Self-Forgiveness for Past Missteps
description: >
  Releasing ongoing blame for a past mistake by recognizing it was made
  with whatever resources, knowledge, and capacity were actually available
  at the time — not by minimizing that it caused harm.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 4, entry 14: Forgive Yourself"
---

A past mistake — including one that genuinely hurt someone, or came from
a coping strategy that wasn't healthy — can stay lodged as an ongoing
source of shame long after the situation itself has passed, distinct from
[rumination](rumination.md) in that the content here is specifically
self-blame for something already done rather than a replayed hypothetical
or worry. The reframe that unlocks release isn't denial or minimization of
the harm caused — it's recognizing that the decision was made using
whatever resources, knowledge, and capacity for self-regulation were
actually available in that moment: "I was doing the best I could with
what I had at the time."

This is compatible with genuinely making amends where that's still
possible, and with learning from the mistake going forward — it targets
specifically the ongoing, unproductive self-punishment that continues
long after any useful learning or repair has already happened. It differs
from [blame vs. ownership after failure](blame-vs-ownership-after-failure.md)
in what it addresses: that concept is about not denying responsibility in
the moment of a setback; this is about not indefinitely re-litigating
responsibility for something already owned and already in the past.
