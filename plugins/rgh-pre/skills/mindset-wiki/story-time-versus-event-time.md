---
type: concept
title: Story-Time Versus Event-Time
description: >
  A traumatic event may last minutes or weeks in reality while the story
  about it replays for decades — and the prolonged suffering is almost
  always the story, not the event.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 12 (Willem dialogue)"
---

When a child survives bombing — house collapsing, concussion, crawling to
safety — the actual danger may last days or weeks. The story can run for
fifty-five years: "the bombs have been falling inside you for fifty-five
years. And in reality only for part of six years... who is kinder, war or
you? Who is making war unceasingly?"

The arithmetic matters for [pain versus suffering](pain-vs-suffering.md):
reaction to the remembered story — fear for decades with no bombs and no
houses actually falling — versus life without the story: "free... especially
free of fear." The child didn't understand how the mind works; "it's just a
story that's scaring them."

Inquiry on the worst moment asks: apart from thinking, was it actually
okay in that instant? Progressive answers often find survival at each step
until the only remaining "not okay" is a thought — not knowing whether
family survived. Exercise: watch the child "without your story, without the
story of bombs and parents... you can have your story back later."

Turnarounds on war statements often map to inner conflict: "I don't like
war because it brought fear" → "I don't like my thinking about war because
it brought fear"; "international conflicts should be resolved peacefully"
→ "my inner conflicts should be resolved in a peaceful way." "Fear teaches
fear. Only peace can teach peace."

stories](reality-kinder-than-your-stories.md), [inquiry on specific
incidents](inquiry-on-specific-incidents.md), and [inner
