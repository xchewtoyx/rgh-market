---
type: concept
title: Fear Lives in Memory and Anticipation
description: >
  Fright in a charged moment often comes from remembered or imagined scenes,
  not from what is literally happening now — staying in the present dissolves
  the charge the story adds.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 4"
---

"Peter scared me" can fail in the specific moment — "he didn't scare me in
that moment," even though she felt scared. The distinction: "Peter said
what he said. That's one thing. But you believed your thoughts about what
he said, and that's what scared you." Images when believing the thought:
him hitting her again (past and imagined future), blaming her. Turnaround:
"Peter didn't scare me... It happens in here [pointing to her head]."

"You can't be frightened if your mind stays in the present. The
remembering is where the fear is. The anticipation is where the fear is."
This extends [anger requires past-and-future
images](anger-requires-past-and-future-images.md) and [pain versus
suffering](pain-vs-suffering.md): the event passes; fear often rides on
replay and forecast. Pair with [grounding techniques](grounding-techniques.md)
when the body is reacting to a story rather than to now.
