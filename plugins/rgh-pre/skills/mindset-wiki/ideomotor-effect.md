---
type: concept
title: Ideomotor Effect
description: >
  Ideas and body postures influence each other reciprocally — acting calm or
  kind tends to produce the corresponding feeling, not only the reverse.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 5"
---

The ideomotor effect: an idea primes associated behavior without awareness
(thinking old → walking slowly). The link runs both ways — walking slowly
for five minutes speeds recognition of aging-related words. Reciprocal
priming produces coherent reactions: amusement makes you smile; smiling
makes you feel amused.

Pencil-in-teeth studies: holding a pencil to force a smile shape makes
cartoons seem funnier; a frown shape strengthens upset at disturbing images.
Head nods while hearing a message increase acceptance; head shakes increase
rejection — without participants noticing.

Practical folk advice gains support: "act calm and kind regardless of how
you feel," since the posture feeds the feeling. Part of [priming
effect](priming-effect.md) and [fast and slow thinking
heuristic](fast-and-slow-thinking-heuristic.md) — System 1 links body and
mind beneath System 2's narrative of control.

Pair with [grounding techniques](grounding-techniques.md) and [buffer
between feeling and action](buffer-between-feeling-and-action.md).
