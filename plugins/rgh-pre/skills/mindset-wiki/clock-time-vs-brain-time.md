---
type: concept
title: Clock Time vs. Brain Time
description: >
  A quick filter for catching unproductive rumination in the moment — a
  thought tied to real logistics and bounded by an actual task is "clock
  time" and worth having, while open-ended emotional replay of the past
  or speculation about the future is "brain time" and safe to release.
sources:
  - title: Solve for Happy
    resource: "Solve for Happy (Mo Gawdat), Part 2, Chapter 6: Does Anybody Know What Time It Is?"
---

Not every past- or future-directed thought is [rumination](rumination.md)
— estimating travel time to an appointment or planning tomorrow's tasks is
genuinely useful and doesn't need to be suppressed. The distinction that
matters is whether a thought is **clock time** (bounded, tied to a real
logistical decision, resolves into a concrete action) or **brain time**
(open-ended, emotionally loaded, doesn't resolve into anything actionable
and just keeps circulating). A thought about a past event that isn't
being used to inform any current decision, or speculation about a future
outcome that isn't attached to a concrete next step, is brain time even
though it may feel urgent or important while it's happening.

The practical check, applied whenever a thought is noticed pulling
attention away from the present: is this actually solving a concrete
problem right now, or is it just replaying/rehearsing without moving
toward any action? If the former, follow it through — that's ordinary
useful planning. If the latter, it's a candidate to release rather than
continue turning over, since continuing it produces the felt weight of
"thinking hard about something" without any of the benefit. This gives
[catching automatic thoughts](catching-automatic-thoughts.md) a concrete
sorting question to apply the moment a past- or future-tagged thought is
noticed, rather than needing to decide case by case from scratch each
time whether a given thought is worth continuing.
