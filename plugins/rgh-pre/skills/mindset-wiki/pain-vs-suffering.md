---
type: concept
title: Pain vs. Suffering
description: >
  Pain is an uncontrollable external signal that something is wrong, while
  suffering is the optional, repeated act of reliving that pain through
  thought after the triggering event has passed.
sources:
  - title: That Little Voice in Your Head
    resource: "That Little Voice in Your Head (Mo Gawdat), Introduction"
---

Pain is triggered by external conditions and is out of our control — it
happens *to* us, and it is useful: it focuses attention, teaches, and keeps
us safe in the moment it occurs. Suffering is different in kind: it is the
choice to replay or ruminate on a painful event through thought when the
event itself is no longer happening. "Pain is out of our control; it
happens to us. Suffering is a choice."

The stronger claim behind this distinction: no event in life has the power
to make a person unhappy until they choose to grant it that power by
turning it into a thought. It is the thought *about* the event — not the
event — that generates ongoing unhappiness. This reframes unhappiness as
something produced downstream of an event, at the point where attention
keeps returning to it, rather than as an unavoidable consequence of the
event itself.

Practically, this separates two different jobs: let pain do its job (feel
it, learn from it, respond to the immediate situation), and notice when
the mind has moved from that immediate response into repeated replay —
that shift is where suffering begins and where intervention is possible.
See [rumination](rumination.md) for the mechanics of that repeated replay,
and [catching automatic thoughts](catching-automatic-thoughts.md) for
noticing the thought before it compounds into suffering.
