---
type: concept
title: Choiceless Awareness
description: >
  Resting in open awareness itself — without directing attention toward any
  particular object — unifies prior mindfulness practices as facets of the
  same boundless knowing that can hold even difficult experience.
sources:
  - title: "The Mindful Way through Depression: Freeing Yourself from Chronic Unhappiness"
    resource: "The Mindful Way through Depression (Williams, Teasdale, Segal, Kabat-Zinn), ch. 8"
---

Prior formal practices — breath, body scan, walking, pleasant/unpleasant feelings,
sounds, thoughts — are different facets of the same underlying awareness, not
genuinely separate domains.

**Choiceless awareness** lets go of any and all objects of attention and rests in
open awareness itself, without directing toward anything other than awareness —
"no need even to think that you are meditating or that there is a 'you' to meditate;
even these are seen as thoughts that dissipate, like touching soap bubbles." Sounds
easy; genuinely challenging because there is nothing specific to focus on.

Objects of attention (breath, sensations, sounds, thoughts) are like celestial
bodies in space; in choiceless awareness "we become the space that holds whatever
condenses momentarily within it." Awareness is boundless, not itself subject to
pain though bearing empathic witness to pain — potentially revealing that awareness
is already free, intrinsically whole, and deeply knowing.

**Practice structure:** begin with breath, expand to body, sounds, thoughts, and
feelings; then let go of any particular object and let awareness be open to
whatever arises — "effortlessly apprehending whatever arises from moment to
moment," with no agenda other than embodied wakefulness. Like an empty mirror
reflecting whatever comes before it. Practice in short periods initially,
alternating with return to breath or another anchor.

**Mindfulness of hearing and thinking** precedes this: attend to sounds as bare
auditory sensations (pitch, timbre, loudness) rather than meaning; then feature
thoughts as mental events arising and passing — movie-screen or [clouds across the
sky](thoughts-as-mental-events.md) metaphors. If mind scatters, return to breath
as anchor.

Pairs with [awareness versus thinking](awareness-versus-thinking.md) and
[watch the watcher](watch-the-watcher.md).
