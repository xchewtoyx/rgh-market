type: concept
title: Inquiry Blocking Patterns
description: >
  Inquiry stalls when you rush the conscious mind, answer from a hidden
  agenda, or treat peace as the goal — and resumes when you wait for honest
  answers and let turnarounds surface in their own time.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 13"
---

When [inquiry as thought-testing](inquiry-as-thought-testing.md) feels
like it "isn't working," question the claim itself first — "I've done The
Work many times" — with [the four questions of
inquiry](the-four-questions-of-inquiry.md). Common blocking patterns:

- **Rushing the conscious mind** before a gentler, less argumentative
  answer can surface.
- **Moving into story** instead of fully experiencing the answer and its
  feelings; answers that begin "Well, yes, but…" signal you've shifted
  away from inquiry.
- **Hidden motives** — trying to prove an existing painful answer valid,
  or inquiring to heal the body or solve a problem rather than for love of
  truth. Acceptance, peace, and less attachment to suffering are
  *effects* of The Work, not goals; other motives miss "the wonder and
  grace of inquiry."
- **Rushing the turnaround** — wait for new answers to surface; optionally
  make a written list of every way [inquiry turnarounds](inquiry-turnarounds.md)
  apply to you. Living turnarounds, reporting them to others, and making
  amends speed the process.

How to know inquiry is working: when the thing you feared happens and you
notice little or no stress or fear.

When you notice blocking, keep going if you're up for it — even one small
honest answer or turnaround "opens a world you don't even know exists." If
the intention is to be [right rather than
free](being-right-vs-being-free.md), it's fine to stop: the story you're
sticking to is more valuable to you now than your freedom, and that's
okay. You may not be suffering enough yet. Be gentle; life will bring what
you need.
