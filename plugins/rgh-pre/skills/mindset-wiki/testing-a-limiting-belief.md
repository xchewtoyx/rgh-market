title: Testing a Limiting Belief
description: >
  A three-question check for examining whether a long-held belief actually
  serves a real goal, before continuing to act as though it is settled
  fact.
sources:
  - title: That Little Voice in Your Head
    resource: "That Little Voice in Your Head (Mo Gawdat), Chapter 2: Garbage In..."
---

To test whether a belief is a genuine guide to action or an unexamined
piece of [conditioning](conditioning-and-context-mismatch.md), ask three
questions: (1) Is this belief actually working for, or against, the real
goal it's supposedly in service of? (2) If pursuing the goal under this
belief meant guaranteed misery, would it still be worth taking — versus
adjusting or giving up the goal in exchange for guaranteed contentment?
(3) Is there a different target, or a different path to the same target,
that gets the result without requiring the belief's cost?

The point of the check is not to declare all beliefs false, but to convert
a belief from an unexamined assumption into something that has been
weighed against its actual consequences at least once. A belief that
survives the check can be kept deliberately rather than by default; one
that fails it becomes a candidate to revise via [cognitive
reframing](cognitive-reframing.md), followed by committing to a concrete
change in action rather than only a change in opinion.
