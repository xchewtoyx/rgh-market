title: Rejection Reframe Worksheet
description: >
  A six-step self-coaching sequence for rejection-sensitive spirals —
  naming the worst thought, testing its evidence, tracing its consequences,
  and building an opposite read with supporting proof.
sources:
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Ellie Middleton), ch. 4, Tip 17"
---

When the brain jumps from a slight or ambiguous cue to catastrophic
rejection ("I'm the worst person, no one will ever love me"), a structured
worksheet slows the jump:

1. **What is the thought bothering me?** Brain-dump the worst version
   honestly — embarrassment allowed; no audience.
2. **What proof do I have for this thought?** List what actually triggered
   it (e.g., partner upset about a forgotten anniversary).
3. **What happens if I choose to believe this thought?** Trace downstream
   — withdraw, self-attack, doubt other relationships.
4. **What is the absolute worst case if it's true? How would I cope?**
   Fully indulge the catastrophe, then spell out a survivable plan (space,
   friends, parents, bath, music) to remove the thought's power.
5. **What opposite thought could I choose?** Direct inversion ("I am a good
   person, and I am loved").
6. **What proof supports the opposite?** List evidence for the kinder read.

The goal is not positive thinking for its own sake but exiting a
rejection-sensitive dysphoria loop with a more proportionate story. Pair
with [predictions treated as fact](predictions-treated-as-fact.md),
[mind reading as distortion](naming-cognitive-distortions.md), and
[testing a limiting belief](testing-a-limiting-belief.md).
