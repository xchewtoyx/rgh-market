type: concept
title: Six-Page Inquiry Exercise
description: >
  When upset but unable to name the triggering thought, six structured
  pages — situation, wants, shoulds, needs, judgments, and never-again —
  surface charged statements to inquire into.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 10"
---

Use this when a [stressful feeling](stressful-feeling-as-inquiry-signal.md)
is present but the specific belief behind it won't surface. Take six blank
sheets:

1. **Situation** — After "sad, disappointed, ashamed, embarrassed, afraid,
   irritated, angry," write "Because ___" with the fact. Circle relevant
   emotions. After "And it means that ___," write the interpretation,
   including worst-case thoughts.
2. **Want** — "I want ___" statements: play God and describe the perfect
   version. Near the bottom, check whether you've written what you really
   want.
3. **Should** — "So-and-so should/shouldn't ___." If none come, list what
   would restore justice and order.
4. **Need** — "I need ___" for comfort and security. Ask what you would
   have once all needs are filled; write that at the bottom.
5. **Judge** — Merciless evaluation of the person or situation.
6. **Never again** — What you vow never to live through again.

Underline statements with the highest emotional charge and do [inquiry](inquiry-as-thought-testing.md)
on them one by one, then work the rest. Backup if the story stays elusive:
five-minute freewrite without stopping; if stuck, repeat the last phrase
until ready to continue. Underline painful phrases and transfer to the
fitting page. Walk away overnight, reread, underline again — "Now you
know where to begin."

This excavates [underlying beliefs](underlying-beliefs.md) and [staying in
your own business](staying-in-your-own-business.md) violations (wants,
shoulds, needs) when a single sentence won't capture the upset. For issue-
focused statements, apply [my-thinking turnaround](my-thinking-turnaround.md)
after the four questions.
