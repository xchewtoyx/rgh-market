---
type: concept
title: Acting as If for Beliefs
description: >
  You do not need full gut conviction before behaving according to a new
  belief — acting as if it is already true grows conviction through lived
  experience that rewires habitual thinking.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition (Branch, Willson), ch. 20"
---

A version of "fake it 'til you make it" for belief change. When the
[head-to-heart problem](head-to-heart-problem.md) shows up, act as if you
already hold the new belief:

- How would I behave if I truly considered this belief true and helpful?
- How would I handle challenges to it if I truly endorsed it?
- What would someone who already lives this belief do in awkward moments?

For social anxiety or self-acceptance: act consistently with the new belief
(specific body language, conversation moves); troubleshoot lulls and awkward
silences as if the belief were true; observe and imitate people who already
seem to endorse it.

Acting in accordance with a belief reinforces its truth — the more you
experience a belief in action, the more you appreciate its effects on
emotions. Try even when it feels wishful or silly; actions speak louder than
words. Pairs with behavioral experiments and [positive data
log](positive-data-log.md) entries documenting outcomes.
