title: Masking as a Burnout Mechanism
description: >
  Chronic masking — working against one's own neurology to appear
  neurotypical — depletes the wellbeing budget until burnout forces the
  question of why functioning costs so much more than it seems to for others.
sources:
  - title: "Knowing Why: Adult-Diagnosed Autistic People on Life and Autism"
    resource: "Knowing Why (ASAN), ch. 2: Erin Human, Emerging From Burnout"
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Ellie Middleton), ch. 9"
---

Undiagnosed autistic people who "fly under the radar" often dismiss their
differences as things to overcome, grow out of, or hide. Seeing others'
relative ease becomes evidence that something is wrong with *them* rather
than with the world's fit — producing a lifelong habit of working against
one's own neurology and a belief that trying harder will eventually make
one "normal."

This pattern — pushing beyond neurological boundaries for too long to keep
up with neurotypical-lifestyle demands — is a common route to [autistic
burnout as wellbeing deficit](autistic-burnout-as-wellbeing-deficit.md).
Perfectionism and overcompensation that look like ambition from outside
may be adaptations to an invisible disability, carrying the cost of a
push-until-crash-then-push-harder cycle.

Unmasking means catching inherited "should" rules and replacing them with
what actually fits (see [saying goodbye to
should](saying-goodbye-to-should.md)). Recovery often requires [reducing
demands over trying harder](reducing-demands-over-trying-harder.md) —
accepting disability as real rather than believing more effort will make
it go away.
