title: Catastrophizing
description: >
  Jumping from an uncertain or merely uncomfortable event to a worst-case
  chain of consequences, treating the worst case as the expected outcome
  rather than one improbable branch among many.

Catastrophizing takes a real but bounded risk (leaving a stable job,
having a difficult conversation, missing a deadline) and mentally runs it
forward into an extreme, low-probability disaster narrative — financial
ruin, total rejection, irrecoverable failure — then reacts to the
narrative as though it were the likely outcome. In recovery circles the
same structure is glossed as F.E.A.R. — false evidence appearing real:
explore the impression, break it down logically, and look at what is
actually present rather than what the voice extrapolates (see [scare versus
fear as ruling state](scare-vs-fear-as-ruling-state.md)). The distortion is not that
change carries no risk, but that the imagined chain of consequences is
wildly out of proportion to the actual odds and severity involved.

Catastrophizing is [aversion](aversion.md)'s typical expression when a
change is being considered: fear of the unfamiliar manufactures a specific
disaster story to justify staying put. The [safety reality
check](safety-reality-check.md) is the direct countermeasure — replacing
the imagined worst case with an honest look at actual probabilities,
historical base rates, and existing safety nets.
