---
type: concept
title: The Body Is Never the Problem
description: >
  Bodies don't think or shame themselves — suffering about the body comes
  from uninvestigated thoughts; physical care is the body's business,
  inquiry is yours.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 11"
---

Bodies "don't think, care, or have any problem with themselves" — they try
to stay balanced and heal. Where there's no thought, there's no problem.
"It's the story we believe, prior to investigation, that leaves us confused."
The body is never your problem; "our problem is always a thought that we
innocently believe."

If the body gets sick, go to the doctor — "My body is his business. My
thinking is my business." In that clarity you know what to do; the body
becomes fun because "you're not invested in whether it lives or dies" — a
projected image, a metaphor of thinking mirrored back.

During sudden full-body paralysis (1986), Katie stayed calm because she
had no story that the body should move fluidly. Thoughts arose ("Something
terrible is happening") but inquiry met them at arising: "Can you really
know you'll never walk again?" After an hour the body relaxed. "My body can
never be a problem if my thinking is healthy."
