---
type: concept
title: Mental Autopilot
description: >
  Overlearned mental habits that take control when attention is elsewhere —
  handing the reins to automatic pilot so old patterns run actions and moods
  without full awareness.
sources:
  - title: "The Mindful Way through Depression: Freeing Yourself from Chronic Unhappiness"
    resource: "The Mindful Way through Depression (Williams, Teasdale, Segal, Kabat-Zinn), ch. 3"
---

It is possible to eat for weeks without tasting food — eating while talking,
reading, or thinking of other things. Dieticians suggest this contributes
to overeating because we do not attend to satiation signals. The same
applies to mental patterns that keep us stuck in unhappiness: they are old,
overlearned habits pulled from memory when we are not fully awake and
present. We hand the reins to the automatic pilot in the mind, letting
subconscious mechanisms operate freely.

Everyday lapses: driving to a familiar route instead of a needed detour;
buying groceries missing the one item you went for; driving to an old address;
reaching to wipe a smudge from a child's face when he is now 27. Eating a whole
bag of sweets without noticing, or driving a familiar route and forgetting an
intended stop — the body does one thing while the mind, hijacked by thoughts,
memories, plans, or feelings, does another.

For people with depression history, automatic pilot is especially dangerous:
fragments of negative thinking go unnoticed and coalesce into patterns leading
to stronger sadness — by the time thoughts surface, they are often too strong
to handle easily. An important relapse-prevention step is recognizing, then
intentionally stepping out of, automatic pilot through mindfulness practice.
"[Driven-doing mode](driven-doing-mode.md) can take on a life of its own,
especially when we're unhappy. That's why the shift to mindfulness requires
intention and takes practice."

Awareness keeps autopilot habits from having the final say and lets us spot
them coming — even viewing ruminative patterns with detached, self-compassionate
amusement like everyday lapses. Doing mode leaves us only vaguely aware of
what is happening; [being mode](doing-mode-being-mode.md) is characterized by
awareness of immediate, sensory, direct knowing of what we are doing as we
do it. Without that awareness we stay in a deepening groove — automatic
thought patterns triggering the same actions and worsening feelings, "every
part of the anatomy of depression triggering the others and the whole."

Goal-focused doing mode also produces **change blindness**: when attention
locks onto a problem-solving goal, irrelevant information gets filtered out
— even the identity of the person you are speaking to. The same narrowing
explains why tastes and sensations of eating go unattended when the mind is
absorbed in planning, rehashing, or rehearsing.

Countermeasure: [mindfulness of routine activities](mindfulness-of-routine-activities.md)
and recognizing autopilot the moment it starts, then intentionally stepping
into a more spacious awareness.
