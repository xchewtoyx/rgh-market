---
type: concept
title: Intentional Causality Perception
description: >
  Minds automatically attribute goals, emotions, and personality to moving
  shapes — a separate causal mode from physical push-and-pull, present from
  infancy.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 7"
---

Heider and Simmel: viewers of a film with triangles and a circle around a
house irresistibly perceive bullying, fear, alliance, and pursuit — personality
and intention from geometry alone. Infants under one year identify bullies
and victims and expect pursuers to take direct paths. Most people affected
by autism do not experience this automatic social story.

This is distinct from Michotte's physical causality — seeing one object
"launch" another — and from freely willed action felt as caused by a
"disembodied you" who wanted salt, not by hand motion alone.

We perceive objects and minds as separate worlds — making soulless bodies and
bodiless souls imaginable. That dual perception may underlie widespread
religious intuitions about immaterial agency. For everyday wellbeing, the
lesson is how fast [spurious causal
narratives](spurious-causal-narratives.md) attach motives to others —
useful for empathy, hazardous when unchecked — motives are inferred faster
than verified.
