type: concept
title: Inquiry Does Not Condone Harm
description: >
  Seeing through stressful stories is not permission for harmful action —
  misreading inquiry as justification for unkindness is itself a belief
  worth questioning.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 3"
---

The Work does not condone harmful action. To see it as justifying anything
less than kind is to misinterpret it. Inquiry releases attachment to
punishing stories; it does not license cruelty, neglect, or staying in
unsafe situations when action is needed.

When a dialogue reads as cold, uncaring, unloving, or unkind, the
invitation is to be gentle with the reaction, breathe through it, and use
the discomfort as material for your own [inquiry as thought-
testing](inquiry-as-thought-testing.md) — not to copy the facilitator's
words as a lifestyle prescription. [Reading dialogues as your own
work](reading-dialogues-as-your-own-work.md) means extracting the
questioning move, not importing someone else's conclusions about how to
treat people.

[Close relationships as teachers](close-relationships-as-teachers.md) is a
lens for practice when safety allows, not a mandate to endure harm because
"suffering is only in the mind."
