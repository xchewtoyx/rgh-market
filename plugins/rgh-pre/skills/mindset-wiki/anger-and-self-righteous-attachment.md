---
type: concept
title: Anger and Self-Righteous Attachment
description: >
  Self-tinged anger often indulges the need to be right at the cost of trust
  and connection — attachment to righteousness can trample what matters more
  than the passing mood.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 7: Anger"
---

Kabat-Zinn recounts getting out of the car at his daughter's friend's house,
registering the look of silent pleading on her face not to get angry — but not
enough to rein in rising annoyance at being kept waiting. He was caught in an
eddy of self-righteous indignation — his "I" did not want to be taken advantage
of. The matter dissolved quickly, but memory still carries her face that he
was not able to read quickly enough to be fully present for; had he been, "the
anger would have died then and there."

The cost of attachment to being right: his passing mood mattered far less than
her trust, "but in that moment, her trust got trampled all the same." Self-tinged
anger may be something we indulge and surrender to far too often; "the
collective pain we cause others and ourselves bleeds our souls."

This is [selfing](selfing.md) in action — the constructed "I" defending its
position. [Patience in mindfulness practice](patience-in-mindfulness-practice.md)
and [letting go in mindfulness practice](letting-go-in-mindfulness-practice.md)
offer the attitudinal counterweight; [distress tolerance](distress-tolerance.md)
addresses tolerating the discomfort of restraint rather than venting immediately.

Anger still arises in practice; Kabat-Zinn elsewhere notes it can be harnessed
to nourish patience, compassion, and wisdom — but only when not blindly
identified with righteousness.
