type: concept
title: Seeing Without Your Story
description: >
  Briefly picturing a person or scene with the judgment suspended — just
  looking at what is there — often surfaces the belief driving the charge
  and tests it against present reality.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 4"
---

Question 4 of [the four questions of inquiry](the-four-questions-of-inquiry.md)
asks who you would be without the thought — imagine the person doing what
you judge, eyes closed, unable to think the judgment. A related guided move:
picture the person (and anyone else in the scene) "without your story,"
just looking at his face.

In a jealousy dialogue this surfaced "He wants to be with her," then:
"Who's he with?" → "Oh! He's with me." The suffering came from mentally
having him elsewhere while he was physically present. The same technique
appears when Barbara, without "He broke his word to me," looked at Peter's
face directly: "Honestly? I'd be relieved."

Use when [noticing the story your mind is
telling](noticing-the-story-your-mind-is-telling.md) has fused observation
with interpretation — separate the image from the narrative, then inquire
into the narrative. Pair with [agency over
interpretation](agency-over-interpretation.md): the event and the story are
separable.
