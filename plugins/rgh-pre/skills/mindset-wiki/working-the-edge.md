type: concept
title: Working the Edge
description: >
  When discomfort arises — in yoga, sitting meditation, or difficult emotions
  — approach the limit with gentle curiosity rather than avoiding or forcing
  through; modulate intensity like dipping a toe to test the water.
sources:
  - title: "The Mindful Way through Depression: Freeing Yourself from Chronic Unhappiness"
    resource: "The Mindful Way through Depression (Williams, Teasdale, Segal, Kabat-Zinn), ch. 7"
---

Three responses to discomfort (physical stretch or emotional difficulty):

1. **Avoidance** — back off at first discomfort, redirect attention elsewhere.
2. **Unkind forcing** — grit teeth, push through, numb out awareness.
3. **Mindful option** — direct attention into the discomfort with breath as
   vehicle; explore with curiosity; linger at the limit without forcing;
   modulate intensity directly.

In yoga, tension in areas not mechanically needed for a pose (jaw, forehead)
often signals aversion to discomfort — breathing nurturing attention into
those areas on the in-breath and releasing on the out-breath yields felt ease.

In sitting meditation, strategies when intensity grows:

- Shift attention within the region toward an area of lesser intensity.
- Breathe with discomfort while keeping background breath awareness.
- If overwhelming, focus on breath alone.
- Move posture with continuity of awareness — kindness and intelligence, not
  failure.

Do not throw yourself in all at once — "more like dipping just the big toe
in the water to test the temperature." The same skill transfers from physical
to emotional discomfort: breathing with tension rather than ignoring it builds
compassion toward the body and self-acceptance.
