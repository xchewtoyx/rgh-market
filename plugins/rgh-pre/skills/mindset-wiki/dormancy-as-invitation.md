"Dormancy is not death; it only looks like it." A barren tree may still have
green under dry bark — one flexible shoot proves nutrients still flow. Humans
can feel "dead inside" without being clinically dead: numbness, joylessness,
drudgery, dreading wake-up, wanting it over, present but disconnected. That is
**dormancy in human form** — it feels like no way back, but if there is still
breath, potential remains. "Dormancy isn't the end. It's an invitation for the
beginning."

**Finding signs of life:**

1. **Check beneath the surface** — is there still something you care about,
   however small (person, dream, hope)? One thing that matters counts.
2. **Notice the conditions** — dormancy often follows shock (grief, burnout,
   addiction, loss); shutdown means recovering, not gone.
3. **Watch for small shoots** — laughter, intrigue, a blue sky; in a dark season
   a tiny glimmer is enough.
4. **Remember seasons change** — numbness today does not mean joy will never
   return; hard days feel permanent but are not.
5. **Hold your sign of life** — a face, phrase, song, or simply waking today;
   cling to it as proof you are still here, ready for something different.

The first step of change: realize you are alive and that there are things to
fight for. Pairs with [gardener mindset for change](gardener-mindset-for-change.md)
