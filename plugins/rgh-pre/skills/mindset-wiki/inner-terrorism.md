---
type: concept
title: Inner Terrorism
description: >
  Catastrophic thoughts about future harm often do more damage than the
  feared event — the "terrorist" on the subway may be your own mind.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 12"
---

"A terrorist could kill me on the subway" — not absolutely knowable, only
possible. Believing it: terrified, shut down; mentally seeing children's
faces "with you dead" — you're in your children's business. Without the
thought: as comfortable as before. Turnaround: **"I can kill myself on the
subway"** — "The killing is going on in your mind. The only terrorist on
the subway in that moment is you, terrifying yourself with your thoughts."

Turn "The terrorists are evil, ignorant, successful, powerful" to **"I am
so ignorant in my hatred and my need to feel powerful"** — contingency
planning to feel powerful, hatred that "empowers me for the moment" then
consumes. "My thoughts are like locusts... everywhere, hiding, waiting to
hurt me, disrupt me, kill me."

"I don't see any terrorists in this moment except the one you live with:
yourself... We all deserve to end our own terrorism." A teacher of fear
can't bring peace; the person who finds peace inside and lives it teaches
what true peace is. Pair with [catastrophizing](catastrophizing.md),
[mental travel into others' lives](mental-travel-into-others-lives.md), and
[high-stakes bias vigilance](high-stakes-bias-vigilance.md) after collective
trauma — without outsourcing safety entirely to contingency fantasies.
