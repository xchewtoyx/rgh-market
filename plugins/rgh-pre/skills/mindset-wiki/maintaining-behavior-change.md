---
type: concept
title: Maintaining Behavior Change
description: >
  Improvement produced by a new strategy tends to reverse if the strategy
  is dropped once things start going well, because the improvement was
  caused by the strategy, not by having "arrived" at a fixed better state.
sources:
  - title: "Mindset: The New Psychology of Success"
    resource: "Mindset: The New Psychology of Success (Carol S. Dweck), Chapter 8: Changing Mindsets"
---

A common failure pattern: a new approach (praising a specific behavior,
noticing and rewarding a partner's positive actions, following a concrete
plan) produces real, visible improvement — and then gets quietly dropped
once the improvement arrives, on the [fixed-mindset](fixed-mindset.md)
assumption that the better state should now just sustain itself. It
usually doesn't; the improvement was an ongoing product of the strategy,
not a permanent trait that was unlocked, and dropping the strategy lets
things drift back, often with added disappointment on top of the relapse.

The corrective stance treats any change as needing continued maintenance
indefinitely rather than as a problem solved once — the same logic behind
programs that deliberately keep people identifying with an ongoing
vulnerability ("you'll always be vulnerable") specifically to prevent the
complacency that stopping maintenance invites. Practically, this means
building a repeatable check-in rather than treating a single successful
period as the finish line: periodically asking what's currently being
done to sustain a gain, not just what produced it originally.
