type: concept
title: Lake Meditation Imagery
description: >
  Imagining the mind as a lake — receptive basin holding rippling surface
  weather above calmer depths — cultivates identifying with awareness itself
  rather than only with passing thoughts and feelings.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 5"
---

Sibling to [mountain meditation imagery](mountain-meditation-imagery.md): the
image is not fundamental but can deepen practice. Water is equally elemental,
in a sense stronger — "water wears down rock." Its virtue is receptivity: it
parts to allow anything in, then resumes itself (hit a lake with a hammer and
you get a rusty hammer).

Picture a lake held in a receptive basin — deep or shallow, clear or muddy.
Windless, the surface mirrors trees and sky; wind stirs ripples and reflections
vanish, yet light still sparkles; in winter it may freeze while life moves below.

Merge with the image lying down or sitting: let mind and heart be open and
receptive, reflecting whatever comes near. Notice complete stillness versus
disturbance; observe thoughts, feelings, and reactions as ripples that come
and go.

Key reframe: can a rippled surface be seen as intimate to being a lake? Can you
identify not only with surface content but the entire body of water — stillness
below even when the surface is whipped to froth? Applied to mind: identify not
only with thoughts and feelings but the vast unwavering reservoir of awareness
below the surface. The lake sits held by the earth; its vitality is highlighted
by the very air and wind that disturb it.

**Try:** Use the lake image to support stillness, noting when the mind reflects
versus when embroiled, and the calm below the surface.

Pairs with [surfing the mind's waves](surfing-the-mind-waves.md) and
[non-striving in meditation](non-striving-in-meditation.md).
