title: Problem and Goal Statements
description: >
  CBT frames change as moving from a three-part problem statement (emotion,
  trigger situation, behavior) to a parallel goal statement targeting healthier
  emotion and action at appropriate intensity.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies (Rhena Branch, Rob Willson), ch. 9"
---

A **problem statement** has three components: (1) feelings/emotions, (2) the
situation or theme that triggered them, (3) how you tend to act when feeling
the problem emotion.

Define the alternative by targeting [healthy versus unhealthy negative
emotions](healthy-vs-unhealthy-negative-emotions.md) — not elimination of all
negative feeling. Feeling sad and disappointed about a marriage ending is
realistic; aiming to feel "fine" in a genuinely tough situation may not fit.
Aim for healthy emotions at appropriate intensity.

**Goal statement template:**

> To feel ____________ (emotion) about ____________ (theme or situation) and
> to ____________ (behavior).

Example: feel concerned about saying something foolish at a dinner party and
stay at the table to make further conversation.

Goals can include mental activities — refocusing attention outward, letting
catastrophic thoughts pass without engagement. Pairs with [SPORT goal
setting](sport-goal-setting.md).
