---
type: concept
title: Grief Inquiry After Loss
description: >
  After bereavement, inquiry targets the thoughts that argue with how life
  works — not to erase love, but to stop the mind from turning presence
  into absence and peace into war with reality.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 4"
---

Joyce senses her husband Stanley at the kitchen table, then argues because
he's "not in his body." Believing "Stanley didn't have to die" means
"you argue with life... angry at him and very sad that he left me... you're
not grateful that he appears to you in your mind." Turnaround: "Stanley did
have to die. He really did, and I wanted him to. I didn't want him to
suffer." "There were two of you at the table, and only one of you was
suffering. It wasn't Stanley."

"I want Stanley to change the way life works" → "I want me to change the
way life works and come back to me in my body, in my current enlightened
state." "Stanley doesn't need you to work on him anymore. Let's just work
on you." Believing he shouldn't be okay while he appears blissful leaves
you "alone and abandoned and lost, with him right there in front of me."
Without the thought: "Joyous and blissful and in deep contact with him...
It's only my mind that's driving me crazy... It's certainly not affecting
Stanley."

"It's a terrible situation" → "It's not a terrible situation... It's a
wonderful situation." Self-turn: "I need me to figure out how to fix this,
where I have this wonderful experience and turn it into not wonderful."
On missing someone deeply: "that somebody you care about so deeply is
always going to be yourself. What you were thinking and believing kept you
from this self that could even see your beloved."

"If you're really in that kind of grief... you have a way out of it. You
can fill in another Worksheet." Otherwise "you're living in a tantrum. 'No
one can die till I say so!' And every time someone you love dies, you go to
war. You go to war with [arguing with reality](arguing-with-reality.md),
and it's very painful." See [stages of grief](stages-of-grief.md) for
naming patterns; inquiry works the thoughts on top of the bare loss.

Answer [the four questions of inquiry](the-four-questions-of-inquiry.md)
with yes/no only — "Yes, because..." or "Yes, but..." means you haven't
felt the answer; give it time without interrupting.
