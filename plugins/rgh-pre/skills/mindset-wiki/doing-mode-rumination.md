Persistent depression is not primarily about getting sad — sadness is natural.
The problem is what happens **next**. [Aversion](aversion.md) toward inner
experience activates avoidance circuitry: body tenses, mind contracts, experience
narrows. Mood functions as internal context — like underwater for divers —
reactivating memories and thinking patterns from previous times in that mood,
often without recognizing them as memories.

When reawakened thinking says we are the problem, we recruit [doing
mode](doing-mode-being-mode.md) problem-solving: figure out what's wrong, close
the gap between how things are and how they should be. Applied to emotions,
this becomes **[driven-doing mode](driven-doing-mode.md)**: focusing on mismatch
between current sadness and desired happiness makes us feel worse — "I should
be feeling happy on this lovely day" deepens misery.

Mental time travel adds past failures and feared futures. Self-hammering questions
— "Why do I always react this way?" — constitute **brooding rumination**:
fruitless preoccupation with unhappiness and its causes, meanings, and consequences.

## Discrepancy-based processing

Reaching for a physical object requires computing the gap between current
position and target, then closing it through monitored action. Evolution
reused this format for "in the head" problem-solving: **discrepancy-based
processing** — comparing current state (A) to desired state (B) and
considering routes from A to B.

Applied to moods, the gap becomes the difference between the mood you're in
and the mood you want. Monitoring progress against a happiness standard is
very unhelpful — waking up feeling bad and matching that against a "better
mood" standard worsens mood further ("I wish I didn't feel this bad… Why do I
always feel this way?"). The natural drive for happiness itself creates
brooding rumination. People are unlikely to abandon rumination voluntarily
until they have sampled an alternative — [being mode](doing-mode-being-mode.md)
— that holds difficulty in awareness without instantly solving it.

The default mode network hijacks daydreaming into negative problem-searching that
feels productive but deteriorates problem-solving ability — like checking distance
to a destination and finding the car moved farther away each time.

**Spilt milk parable:** mixing water with spilt milk makes a uniform bigger mess —
our attempts to clear mood can add misery indistinguishable from the original mood,
which may have already moved on.

**Body-triggered spiral:** physical sensations, thoughts, feelings, and behavior
coalesce in a self-reinforcing loop — morning sluggishness surfaces thoughts
about not getting anything done, frustration and sadness follow, the effort to
rise meets heaviness, self-critical thoughts strengthen, and bodily heaviness
intensifies. Every link (body→thoughts, thoughts→feelings, feelings→body) is
an opportunity to redirect the sequence by bringing mindful awareness to the
body, particularly through [direct sensing versus thinking about the
body](direct-sensing-vs-thinking-about-body.md).

**Alternative:** [being mode](doing-mode-being-mode.md) — awareness itself rather
than critical problem-solving. [Mindfulness](mindfulness.md) contrasts with rumination:
intentional vs. automatic; experiential vs. abstract; nonjudgmental vs. evaluating.
See [rumination](rumination.md) for general mechanics; this note is the MBCT-specific
doing-mode pathway into it.
