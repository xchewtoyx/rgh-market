description: >

  aversion, attachment, or all-pervasive dissatisfaction — that need
  right-sizing rather than elimination.
sources:

If a person's basic needs are met and they are not in physical pain but
still unhappy, the cause traces to one or more of three patterns, each
tied to a layer of the [triune brain model](triune-brain-model.md):
[aversion](aversion.md) (fear-driven refusal to leave or change a bad
situation), [attachment to comfort](attachment-to-comfort.md) (clinging to
the familiar regardless of its actual value), and [all-pervasive
dissatisfaction](all-pervasive-dissatisfaction.md) (a background sense
that life should be more, independent of any real unmet need).

None of the three is inherently bad — some aversion (basic safety
precaution), some attachment (to what genuinely sustains life), and some
dissatisfaction (drive to fix real problems) are healthy and necessary.
The brain is also biased toward over-triggering all three: it is
evolutionarily safer to falsely flag something as a threat than to falsely
mark a real threat as safe, so all three defences run hot by default. The
goal is not to eliminate them but to catch them firing in excess —
becoming an observer of one's own reactions well enough to notice when a
defence is responding to a story about danger/loss/lack rather than to
anything actually true right now.
