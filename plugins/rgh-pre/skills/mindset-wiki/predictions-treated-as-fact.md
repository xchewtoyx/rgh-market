title: Predictions Get Experienced as Fact
description: >
  The brain fills the unknown future the same confident way it fills any
  other gap, extrapolating from a handful of data points into a forecast
  that feels certain — and a confidently-held prediction can go on to
  shape behavior in ways that make it self-fulfilling.
sources:
  - title: Solve for Happy
    resource: "Solve for Happy (Mo Gawdat), Part 3, Chapter 9: Is It True?"
---

Facing the biggest gap of all — what hasn't happened yet — the brain
extrapolates from limited past data into a confident-feeling forecast, the
same way it fills in any other missing information. A pattern seen in a
small number of cases (a friend's relationship ending in infidelity, a
dramatic story) can generalize into a sweeping belief ("people like this
always do X"), which then narrows into a specific prediction about a
current, unrelated situation. The danger compounds because a strongly held
prediction can change behavior toward the predicted person or outcome in
ways that make the prediction more likely to come true — suspicion
generated by an ungrounded prediction can itself produce the friction that
then seems to confirm it.

This overlaps with, but is broader than, [catastrophizing](catastrophizing.md):
catastrophizing specifically escalates to a worst-case narrative, while
this is the more general habit of treating any extrapolated forecast —
positive, negative, or neutral — with the same confidence due to a
directly witnessed fact. The corrective is to notice future-tense
confidence as future-tense confidence rather than present fact — see
[linguistic tells for cognitive
distortions](linguistic-tells-for-cognitive-distortions.md) for how
predictions characteristically surface in language.
