---
type: concept
title: Present-Task Focus Against Catastrophizing
description: >
  When danger feels overwhelming, narrowing attention to the next concrete
  actions in front of you — not the full tally of threats — reduces fear's
  grip and preserves capacity for effective response.
sources:
  - title: "Courage Is Calling: Fortune Favors the Brave"
    resource: "Courage Is Calling (Ryan Holiday), ch. 4"
  - title: "The Obstacle Is the Way: The Timeless Art of Turning Trials into Triumph, Expanded 10th Anniversary Edition"
    resource: "The Obstacle Is the Way (Ryan Holiday), ch. 11"
---

Demosthenes, facing attack by sea and land together, told his men not to
"show his wit by exactly calculating all the perils that encompass us"
but to close with the enemy — "In emergencies like ours calculation is
out of place; the sooner the danger is faced the better."

Marcus Aurelius, despite ruling an empire beset by plague, coups, and
border wars: "Don't let your reflection on the whole sweep of life crush
you. Don't fill your mind with all the bad things that might still
happen. Stay focused on the present situation and ask yourself why it's so
unbearable and can't be survived." Wilfred Owen from the trenches: "Happy
are these who lose imagination: / They have enough to carry with
ammunition." [Catastrophizing](catastrophizing.md) and imagining
everything is what makes us most miserable; focusing on what we have to
carry keeps us too busy to worry.

Chris Hadfield's spacewalk crisis — blindness from tears freezing — his
move: "There are six things that I could do right now, all of which will
help make things better," while holding "there's no problem so bad that
you can't make it worse also." Energy spent fearing you'll make it worse
is energy not spent making it better. You can't perform well with a
divided mind — half-preparing for failure while listing reasons an idea
is bad.

Stoic "first impressions" — stick with what's here, not everything that
may someday relate to it: this call, this check, this step. Pair with
[single-tasking](single-tasking.md), [mindfulness](mindfulness.md), and
[mental travel into others' lives](mental-travel-into-others-lives.md).
Balance with advance [safety reality check](safety-reality-check.md) —
Seneca's premeditation softens hard times without feeding today's fear.

This moment is not your life — it's just a moment in your life. Ignore
what it "represents," "means," or "why it happened to you"; focus on
what's in front of you right now.
