"We take weakness for granted, assuming our disadvantages are permanent,
and atrophy from there." Some people remake body and mind through exercise
and activity to prepare for a hard road they hope never to walk but want to
be ready for regardless.

Ancient philosophers treated themselves as mental athletes — training the
brain through repeated exercises until muscle memory let them respond to
obstacles intuitively. No one is born a gladiator; strength of will is built
the same way as any other skill. Roosevelt conceived life as an arena and
himself as a gladiator needing to be strong, resilient, fearless — willing
to risk personal harm and expend massive energy to build hardiness. "You'll
have far better luck toughening yourself up than you ever will trying to
take the teeth out of a world that is — at best — indifferent to your
existence."

This is the construction project behind the [inner
citadel](inner-citadel.md): daily reinforcement during good times, not a
single heroic moment. Self-assessment prompts: Are you okay being alone?
Strong enough for a few more rounds? Comfortable with challenges? Does
uncertainty bother you? How does pressure feel? Life will demand answers —
preparation is armor plating, not invincibility.

**Preparation replaces fear:** Other people are not naturally braver — they
are better prepared. The WWII Army handbook: "There is more mental comfort,
more personal satisfaction, in knowing your place and part in this Army than
in any other single thing you can now do for yourself." At the Caudine Forks
(321 BC), a trapped Roman army sat paralyzed until one soldier began
building fortifications; others followed without orders. The act of doing
something — falling back on training — lifted stupor and hardened resolve;
the enemy, seeing discipline, made terms rather than attack.

Epictetus: the goal in adversity is to say "This is what I've trained for,
for this is my discipline." Seneca: if you do not want to flinch when danger
comes, train before it comes. Fear leads to aversion and cowardice;
repetition leads to confidence and courage. What you have not practiced has
an advantage over you; what you have prepared for, you can answer. Practice
— mental rehearsal, muscle memory, drills, tough questions on purpose,
threshold training — lets you know what to do under pressure because you
did it a thousand times when there wasn't. Pair with [steadying your
nerves](steadying-your-nerves.md) and [adversity as training](adversity-as-
training.md).
