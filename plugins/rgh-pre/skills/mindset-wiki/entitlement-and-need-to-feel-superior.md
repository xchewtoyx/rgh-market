---
type: concept
title: Entitlement and the Need to Feel Superior
description: >
  A fixed mindset can curdle "feeling special" into needing to feel better
  than others — an entitled, superiority-seeking stance that is distinct
  from, and does not require, genuinely arrogant behavior toward others.
sources:
  - title: "Mindset: The New Psychology of Success"
    resource: "Mindset: The New Psychology of Success (Carol S. Dweck), Chapter 2: Inside the Mindsets"
---

Under a [fixed mindset](fixed-mindset.md), "feeling smart" often gets
defined relationally — feeling different from and better than other
people — rather than by any absolute measure of growth or accomplishment.
Taken to an extreme this produces entitlement: "the problem is when
special begins to mean better than others. A more valuable human being. A
superior person." This is a specific, describable failure mode (constant
need for validation, intolerance of criticism, treating others' success as
a threat), not a vague character flaw — and it is a consequence of the
mindset, not an unavoidable companion to genuine ability or achievement.

The useful contrast is between two people who are both, in fact,
accomplished: one whose mindset makes them need to broadcast and defend
specialness (fragile, defensive, uncomfortable when others succeed), and
one who is comfortable being "a human being like everyone else" despite
real accomplishment — reacting to skill or praise as encouragement to keep
developing rather than as proof of superiority to maintain. This is
directly useful for anyone worried that stating a genuine strength or
accomplishment reads as arrogant: the behaviors actually associated with
entitlement (needing validation, dismissing others' input, treating
success as zero-sum) are separable from simply being accurate and
unapologetic about one's own competence. Stating a fact about one's
ability is not the same act as needing to be seen as superior — the
first is compatible with a [growth mindset](growth-mindset.md) and
[accurate self-knowledge](mindset-and-self-knowledge-accuracy.md); the
second is the fixed-mindset failure mode.
