Adults often want their own way as much as children — just less honestly. The
fairy-tale warning about three wishes and the joke "You can't get there from
here" both point to a life-direction truth: **you can only get there if you are
fully here.**

The central inquiry: "What exactly is my way?" — my Way with a capital W —
alongside questions rarely lingered on: Who am I? Where am I going? What path am
I on? Is this the right direction? What is my yearning? What do I truly love?

Do **not** seek an answer: "Better not to think at all." Persist in asking;
let any answers formulate and dissolve on their own — watch, listen, note, let
be, let go, regenerate the question. Remain open to not-knowing, including
"I don't know," and relax into that rather than condemning yourself.

Sustained inquiry eventually "does you" rather than you doing it — permeating
the bland and routine with vitality. The human lifespan is a mythic hero's
journey between birth and death; the challenge is to meet what arises, find
your own way, and live it.

## Spirit of inquiry in daily life

"The spirit of inquiry is fundamental to living mindfully." Inquiry is not just
problem-solving — it keeps contact with the basic mystery of life. Questions
like "What is this?" "Who am I?" "What is my job on the planet with a capital
J?" are carried without expecting quick answers: ponder, let wondering percolate,
come in and out of awareness. Inquiry and mindfulness can occur simultaneously
during dishes, walking, or conversation.

When problems arise, meet them with inquiry: "What is this thought, feeling,
dilemma?" "Am I willing to deal with it or even acknowledge it?" Acknowledging
a problem is the first step. There is no timetable — inquiry is "like a pot on
your shelf," ready when you are. Listen to the thinking questioning evokes, as
if sitting beside the stream of your own thoughts.
