type: concept
title: Patience in Mindfulness Practice
description: >
  Patience in meditation means letting experience unfold in its own time —
  balancing push and pull in the present moment rather than letting anxiety
  about results dominate awareness.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 3: Patience"
---

Patience is an attitudinal foundation for [mindfulness](mindfulness.md)
practice, cultivated directly by sitting and attending to the breath. It
extends into openness and patience with one's moments in daily life: "We
know that things unfold according to their own nature. We can remember to
let our lives unfold in the same way," rather than letting anxieties and
desire for particular results dominate the quality of the moment, even when
things are painful.

"When we have to push, we push. When we have to pull, we pull. But we know
when not to push too, and when not to pull." The aim is balance in the
present moment — "understanding that in patience lies wisdom, knowing that
what will come next will be determined in large measure by how we are now."

Complete compassion rests on right mindfulness and right understanding; it
must be practiced. Anger still arises, but its energies can be harnessed
to nourish patience, compassion, harmony, and wisdom.

**Try:** When impatience, frustration, or anger arise, adopt the perspective
that things unfold in their own time — "try not to push the river in that
moment but listen carefully to it instead." If it tells you nothing, breathe,
let things be, let go into patience, keep listening; if it tells you
something, do it mindfully, then pause and listen again. In formal practice,
when the mind pulls to fill time or change what's happening, sit patiently
with the breath and keen awareness of what's unfolding without imposing
anything — just watching, embodying stillness, becoming patience.

Pairs with [letting go in mindfulness practice](letting-go-in-mindfulness-practice.md)
and [non-striving in meditation](non-striving-in-meditation.md).
