title: Metacognition About Mental Acts
description: >
  Beliefs about your own worrying, ruminating, or suppressing thoughts —
  what you think those mental activities are achieving — shape whether you
  keep doing them; mapping intended versus actual consequences opens room to swap strategies.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies (Rhena Branch, Rob Willson), ch. 3"
---

**Metacognition** is cognition about cognition — the thoughts and beliefs held
about one's own mental activities: worrying, [rumination](rumination.md),
mental reviewing, thought suppression, comparing. A simple identification
question: in what way does your mind believe it is helping or protecting you
by engaging in this activity?

Use a four-column chart for each unhelpful mental activity:

| Mental activity | Intended consequence | Unintended consequence | Alternative to try |

Work through what each activity is meant to achieve versus what it actually
costs, then identify alternatives.

**Tracking when, where, and with whom** you overthink is key to planning
interruption — log moments across the week (time of day, context) to build a
plan of attack. See [catching automatic thoughts](catching-automatic-thoughts.md).

**Swapping strategies:** plan alternatives in advance — music, podcast,
exercise, calling a friend, a household chore, directing attention to a bodily
sensation (knot in stomach), or [task concentration
training](task-concentration-training.md). The aim is redirecting attentional
focus to disrupt unhelpful mental processes. Persistence matters — breaking a
mental habit works like breaking a behavioral one. Practice switching attention
in less-stressful situations first before relying on swaps under peak stress.

Pairs with [label assess reassign for rumination](label-assess-reassign-for-rumination.md)
and [rumination interruption techniques](rumination-interruption-techniques.md).
