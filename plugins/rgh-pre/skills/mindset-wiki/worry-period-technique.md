title: Worry Period Technique
description: >
  Confining worry to a fixed, short daily time slot — and deliberately
  deferring intrusive worries to that slot when they arise elsewhere —
  lets worry do its useful planning work without letting it run
  unbounded throughout the day.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 3, entry 19: Harness the Power of Effective Worrying"
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies (Rhena Branch, Rob Willson), ch. 3: Overthinking Processes"
---

Worry isn't inherently unproductive — anticipating challenges and
thinking through risk is a real form of problem-solving. It becomes
harmful when it's unbounded, intruding at any moment and looping without
resolution. The technique: set aside a consistent daily slot (roughly
10–20 minutes) specifically for worrying — write worries down during that
window, then sort each into controllable (something an actual step can be
taken on) and uncontrollable (nothing to be done, so it can be set down).
When a worry intrudes outside the scheduled period, the move is not to
suppress it but to note that it will be addressed at the next worry
period and let it wait — a form of mindfulness-based deferral rather than
avoidance.

This gives worry a bounded container instead of either letting it run
continuously or trying to eliminate it outright, similar in spirit to
[the interrogation technique](the-interrogation-technique.md) for a
specific fear, but aimed at the general daily flow of intrusive worries
rather than one identified fear at a time. Sorting into
controllable/uncontrollable during the period is itself a form of
[cognitive reframing](cognitive-reframing.md): a worry that turns out to
have no available action attached to it stops being useful to keep
replaying.

Worry in CBT terms comes from two factors meeting: the mind's creative
"what if…" planning capacity and **intolerance of uncertainty** — the
urge to eliminate ambiguity by thinking it through. In a complex world,
each settled plan invites another twist, so overthinking cannot actually
remove uncertainty; the sustainable move is learning to live with (even
embrace) not knowing, and confining problem-solving to the right time and
place (work worries at the desk during work hours, not in bed at night).
Concerns crossing the mind are normal; what is controllable is how far you
follow and work on them.

Do not [catastrophize](catastrophizing.md) about worry itself — fearing
that worrying will make you ill or "drive you crazy" only adds a second
layer of anxiety on top of the first. Worry is normal; excess worry is
changeable with steady practice.
