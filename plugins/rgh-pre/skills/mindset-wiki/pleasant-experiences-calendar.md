title: Pleasant Experiences Calendar
description: >
  A daily MBCT homework exercise recording thoughts, feelings, and body
  sensations during pleasant moments — deconstructing experience and tuning
  into positive life already present without scheduling more events.
sources:
  - title: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition"
    resource: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression (Segal, Williams, Teasdale), ch. 9"
---

Notice one different pleasant experience each day — ideally while it occurs —
and record thoughts, feelings, and body sensations as soon as possible afterward.
Write thoughts as if spoken aloud; describe feelings and sensations in detail.

**Why it matters:**

1. Brings awareness to the earliest valence reaction (pleasant/unpleasant/neutral)
   — a pivotal trigger point before rumination or "needing things to be different"
2. Helps notice positive things already occurring — **not** pleasant-events
   scheduling to add more events, but awareness of what is already there
3. Deconstructs experience into thoughts, feelings, and sensations rather than
   one indistinguishable blob — supporting [decentering](thoughts-as-mental-events.md)
4. Nonthreatening entry to the "felt" dimension for those with experiential
   avoidance — encountering elements without the threat unpleasant material carries

Body sensations act as a sensitive barometer of affect; many people overlook
subtle bodily signals until trained. A trivial moment (hearing a bird after
shift) can contain relief, pleasure, and uplift when fully noticed — "day by day
we sell ourselves short."

Pairs with [informal attention practices](informal-attention-practices.md) and
the Unpleasant Experiences Calendar counterpart in [approach versus avoidance in
emotions](approach-versus-avoidance-in-emotions.md).
