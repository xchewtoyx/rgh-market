---
type: concept
title: Selective Skeptic Test
description: >
  Checking whether a piece of evidence would be found credible if it
  supported the opposite conclusion, to reveal whether scrutiny is being
  applied evenhandedly or only to unwelcome evidence.
sources:
  - title: "The Scout Mindset: Why Some People See Things Clearly and Others Don't"
    resource: "The Scout Mindset (Julia Galef), Chapter 5: Noticing Bias"
---

The test's defining question: "if this same evidence supported the
opposite side, how credible would I find it?" Methodological objections
("the study was poorly designed," "the sample was too small," "they don't
have real insider knowledge") are often perfectly valid — but [soldier
mindset](soldier-mindset.md) tends to deploy that same level of scrutiny
selectively, applying it only to evidence for an unwelcome conclusion
while waving through equally weak evidence for a welcome one. The test
catches this by explicitly imagining the identical piece of evidence
pointing the other way and asking whether the same objection would still
feel disqualifying.

A common personal application: dismissing critical feedback by noting the
critic "doesn't understand the full context" or "lacks insider details" —
a check worth running is whether that same objection would be raised
against equally under-informed *praise*. If flattering feedback from an
outsider is accepted without the same scrutiny that critical feedback
receives, the scrutiny is tracking whether the conclusion is welcome, not
whether the evidence is actually strong — the same asymmetry [the double
standard test](double-standard-test.md) targets, applied specifically to
evidence quality rather than to behavior.
