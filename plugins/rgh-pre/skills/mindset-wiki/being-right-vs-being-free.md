---
type: concept
title: Being Right Versus Being Free
description: >
  Clinging to a belief about how others should behave keeps you fighting
  reality; letting the belief go — or changing your own action to match what
  you want — buys freedom even when you "lose" the argument on facts.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 4"
---

A rigid rule like "my children should pick up their socks" can absorb years
of preaching, nagging, and punishing while the floor stays unchanged —
the belief never matched reality. The shift is seeing who actually had the
problem and the solution: if you want socks picked up, you can pick them
up; if you want peace more than you want to be right about the rule, you
can stop fighting what is.

"I realized that I could be right or I could be free." Being right means
defending a should-statement against daily evidence; being free means
dropping the war with reality and acting from what is actually in your
control — often a [turnaround to the
self](inquiry-turnarounds.md) ("I should pick up the socks") done without
resentment because it is for you, not as a lesson to them. When the chore
stops being a battle, it can become ordinary care; others sometimes mirror
that change without being asked.

This is operational [agency over
interpretation](agency-over-interpretation.md) in close relationships: the
event (socks on the floor) and the suffering (mental screaming about it)
are separable. Pair with [staying in your own
business](staying-in-your-own-business.md) when the distress comes from
trying to control what isn't yours to control.
