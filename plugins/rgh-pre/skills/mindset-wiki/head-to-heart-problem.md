title: Head-to-Heart Problem
description: >
  Knowing a new belief makes sense intellectually without yet feeling true
  in your gut produces cognitive dissonance — the critical CBT work is raising
  strength of conviction, not parroting rational philosophies.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition (Branch, Willson), ch. 20"
---

"I know what I should think, but I don't believe it!" — understanding in
your head without gut conviction is the **head-to-heart problem**. The
discomfort of cognitive dissonance (old thinking not fully wrong, new belief
not yet convincing) is a sign change is underway, not failure.

Alternative beliefs usually target yourself, other people, or the world, in
three formats:

- **Flexible preference** instead of rigid demand — "I'd prefer approval,
  but there's no reason they absolutely must give it."
- **Alternative assumption** — "If I don't get an A, I'm fallible like
  everyone else, not a failure."
- **Global healthy belief** — "I'm basically okay" rather than "I'm worthless."

**Strength of conviction (SOC)** is rated 0–100% after formulating a helpful
belief. Tools to raise it: [acting as if for beliefs](acting-as-if-for-beliefs.md),
[portfolio of arguments](portfolio-of-arguments.md), [zigzag
technique](zigzag-technique.md), [positive data log](positive-data-log.md),
and behavioral tests in familiar triggering situations.

A parrot can repeat rational philosophies without understanding or believing
them — the real work turns intellectual understanding into something you know
in your gut to be true.
