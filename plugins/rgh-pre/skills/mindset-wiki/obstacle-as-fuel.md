---
type: concept
title: Obstacle as Fuel
description: >
  An obstacle can be treated as material the current effort works with and
  is strengthened by, rather than as opposition to be merely endured —
  the way a well-tended fire burns brighter for whatever is thrown on it,
  including material that would smother a weaker flame.
sources:
  - title: Meditations
    resource: "Meditations (Marcus Aurelius, tr. Gregory Hays), Book 4.1, 5.20, 8.35"
  - title: "The Obstacle Is the Way: The Timeless Art of Turning Trials into Triumph, Expanded 10th Anniversary Edition"
    resource: "The Obstacle Is the Way (Ryan Holiday), ch. 31"
---

An "inward power" properly aligned with its own purpose doesn't just
tolerate what gets in its way — it converts it into material to work
with: "fire adapts itself to whatever is thrown into it... the fire
seizes on all these and, rising higher, is strengthened by them." Applied
to difficulty generally, this reframes an obstacle from something
standing between the current effort and its goal into something the
effort can actively use — a mistake becomes information, an interruption
becomes an unplanned test of adaptability, a difficult person becomes
practice for a skill that will be needed regardless.

This differs from simply enduring an obstacle or accepting it after the
fact (see [committed acceptance](committed-acceptance.md)) — it's a more
active stance: instead of asking only "how do I get past this," it asks
"what can this specific obstacle actually supply me with right now." A
companion formulation makes the same point about intentions rather than
actions: an obstacle can block a specific planned action, but "the mind
adapts and converts to its own purposes the obstacle to our acting...
what stands in the way becomes the way" — when one path is blocked, the
blockage itself becomes the next piece of material to work with, rather
than a pure loss.
Whether an obstacle ends up functioning as fuel or as genuine
obstruction depends less on the obstacle's size than on whether attention
is already oriented toward converting difficulty into use — the same
underlying disposition [growth mindset](growth-mindset.md) brings to a
setback, applied at the moment the obstacle is first encountered rather
than only in how it's judged afterward. [Amor fati](amor-fati.md) pushes
this further: not merely using the obstacle but loving it as necessary
fuel.
