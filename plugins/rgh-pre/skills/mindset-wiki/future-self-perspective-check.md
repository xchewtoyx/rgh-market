---
type: concept
title: Future-Self Perspective Check
description: >
  Imagining looking back on a decision from many years in the future — not
  whether it succeeded, but whether the attempt itself will have been
  worth having made — clarifies which regret is actually more durable.
sources:
  - title: "The Scout Mindset: Why Some People See Things Clearly and Others Don't"
    resource: "The Scout Mindset (Julia Galef), Chapter 8: Motivation Without Self-Deception"
---

Facing an uncertain, high-stakes choice (leaving a secure position to
pursue a long-odds goal), a useful check is to imagine looking back on the
decision from decades in the future and asking which choice would produce
more regret — not "will this succeed," but "will I be glad I tried,
regardless of outcome, versus glad I played it safe." Applied by people
weighing genuinely long-odds ventures: a foregone opportunity (a bonus
missed, a comfortable job left) tends to matter little in hindsight,
while a foregone attempt at something significant tends to matter a great
deal, independent of whether the attempt would have succeeded.

This works as a companion to [bets worth taking](bets-worth-taking.md):
where that concept evaluates a decision by its expected value in the
moment, the future-self check evaluates it by which version of not-yet-
known regret is more tolerable in hindsight — "if it failed, I'd still be
proud I tried" is a legitimate basis for committing to a bet even when the
near-term odds look unfavorable. It's a way of accessing [scout
mindset](scout-mindset.md) on a decision that current anxiety is
distorting: the future vantage point strips away the immediate fear of a
bad short-term outcome and asks what will actually have mattered.
