---
type: concept
title: Statistics Versus Individual Cases
description: >
  Surprising aggregate facts often fail to update how you judge individuals;
  surprising individual cases generalize better than statistics alone.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 17"
---

Nisbett and Borgida taught students the NYU helping experiment: only 4 of 15
responded immediately to a feigned seizure (diffusion of responsibility).
Students then watched bland interview videos of two participants and predicted
how quickly each had helped.

**Bayesian prior:** ~27% helped immediately; uninformative videos should keep
predictions near the base rate.

Students who knew the statistic predicted the same as students who didn't —
both expected the nice-looking individuals rushed to help. "They learned
nothing at all." Nisbett and Borgida: unwillingness to deduce particular from
general matched only by willingness to infer general from particular.

**The fix:** tell students these two specific individuals had *not* helped,
then ask them to guess group results — guesses became extremely accurate.
Surprise from individual cases produced correct generalization; surprising
statistics did not transfer to individual judgment.

Test of learning psychology: whether your understanding of situations you
encounter has changed, not whether you learned a new fact. Even compelling
causal statistics may not change beliefs rooted in personal experience;
people quietly exempt themselves and friends unless confronted with concrete
surprising cases.

Teaching implication: to teach psychology you must surprise students — but
surprise must come from a particular case, not an aggregate statistic alone.

For self-regulation: knowing base rates (how often people help, how often
plans fail) may not change your felt prediction about *this* person or *this*
situation until a vivid case lands. Pair with [causal versus statistical base
rates](causal-vs-statistical-base-rates.md) and [catastrophizing](catastrophizing.md).
