---
type: concept
title: Communicating World-Uncertainty Well
description: >
  Three concrete moves make an honest expression of uncertainty read as
  expertise rather than as incompetence — show the uncertainty is
  justified, give an informed estimate rather than a shrug, and pair it
  with a concrete plan.
sources:
  - title: "The Scout Mindset: Why Some People See Things Clearly and Others Don't"
    resource: "The Scout Mindset (Julia Galef), Chapter 9: Influence Without Overconfidence"
---

Given [two kinds of uncertainty](two-kinds-of-uncertainty.md), the
practical question is how to voice genuine uncertainty about an
unpredictable situation without it reading as a personal gap. Three moves
help: first, show the uncertainty is justified — set the expectation that
no one could reasonably know more (e.g. pointing out that even the best
experts in a field couldn't have predicted a specific past outcome, so
similar unpredictability here isn't a personal failing). Second, give an
informed estimate rather than a bare "I don't know" — grounding a range or
a best guess in whatever real analysis or data is available ("there's no
hard data on this exact case, so here's the average estimate from three
people who've dealt with something similar") demonstrates the uncertainty
comes with real work behind it, not an absence of thought. Third, pair the
uncertainty with a concrete plan for acting despite it — a next step, a
monitoring approach, a specific test that would narrow the estimate —
since open uncertainty followed by "and here's what I'd do about it" reads
as command of the situation rather than as being stuck.

Together these let someone state "I'm not certain" honestly while still
projecting competence — the uncertainty itself isn't the problem, an
uncertainty presented with no context, no grounding, and no plan is. This
gives a direct, practical answer to worrying that admitting a limit or an
open question will undercut credibility: it's the surrounding delivery,
not the admission itself, that determines how it lands.
