---
type: concept
title: Anger Defusion Checklist
description: >
  A short set of standing reminders to run through when angry at someone
  else's behavior, each attacking a different part of what makes anger
  feel justified, so at least one is likely to land even when others
  don't.
sources:
  - title: Meditations
    resource: "Meditations (Marcus Aurelius, tr. Gregory Hays), Book 11.18"
---

Rather than relying on a single argument to defuse anger at someone
else's conduct, several independent reminders are kept ready, on the
premise that different ones will land depending on the situation:
remembering that you and the other person share a common purpose or
community; picturing the ordinary, driven, non-malicious humanity behind
their action rather than only the action itself; recognizing that if
they're actually right you have no grounds to be angry, and if they're
wrong they most likely acted from [error rather than
malice](is-it-true-technique.md) — believing, however mistakenly, that
what they did was reasonable; recalling comparable mistakes of your own,
made or narrowly avoided; admitting that fully judging whether something
even *was* a mistake requires more context than is usually available in
the moment; noting how quickly the whole situation will be forgotten
regardless; and noticing that what's actually causing distress is your
own reaction to the event, not the event itself (see [thoughts trigger
emotions](thoughts-trigger-emotions.md)) — and that the anger and grief
generated in response typically do more damage than the original offense
did.

A further, more demanding item completes the set: responding with
genuine kindness, sincerely rather than performed to win a point, on the
grounds that kindness reliably disarms in a way retaliation doesn't. The
checklist works less by any single item being individually decisive and
more by its redundancy — an angry moment that resists one reframe often
gives way to another, so having several ready increases the odds that at
least one actually interrupts the reaction before it's acted on (see
[buffer between feeling and
action](buffer-between-feeling-and-action.md) for holding that gap open
long enough for the checklist to actually be run).
