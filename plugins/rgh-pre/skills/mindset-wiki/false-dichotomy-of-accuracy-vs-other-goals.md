---
type: concept
title: The False Dichotomy of Accuracy vs. Other Goals
description: >
  Comfort, self-esteem, motivation, and influence are often assumed to
  require some degree of self-deception to obtain, but for most of these
  goals there is a way to get them honestly — it just takes more
  deliberate effort to find than defaulting to distortion.
sources:
  - title: "The Scout Mindset: Why Some People See Things Clearly and Others Don't"
    resource: "The Scout Mindset (Julia Galef), Chapters 7-9"
---

The implicit bargain behind [soldier mindset](soldier-mindset.md) is that
accuracy has to be traded away to get something else that's needed —
comfort, self-esteem, morale, or influence over others (see [what the
soldier is protecting](chestertons-fence-for-beliefs.md)). Framing the
choice as only "distort the truth" or "give up the benefit" is itself a
false dichotomy, akin to a bully offering only "pay up" or "take a
beating" as options, when changing the situation entirely (learning to
push back, finding allies, leaving) was also available. In most of the
cases examined — [morale built on bets worth
taking](motivated-reasoning-for-morale.md) instead of inflated odds,
[influence built on social confidence and honest, well-communicated
uncertainty](epistemic-vs-social-confidence.md) instead of false
certainty — an honest path to the same underlying benefit exists, it's
just less automatic than reaching for a comforting distortion.

The practical implication is that whenever self-talk seems to require
choosing between "feel good/look good/stay motivated" and "be accurate,"
that framing itself deserves scrutiny before accepting the trade-off — the
question worth asking is not "which do I sacrifice" but "what's the
honest way to get this same benefit," which is often available with a
bit more deliberate work than the default distorted shortcut.
