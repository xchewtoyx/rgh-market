---
type: concept
title: Peace Requires Only One Person
description: >
  Inner peace does not wait on another person's agreement or behavior —
  continuing your own inquiry is what changes how family and close ties
  appear, without imposing the method on anyone.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 13"
---

"Peace doesn't require two people; it requires only one. It has to be
you." Family may read inquiry-driven shifts as detachment or uncaring
because question 3 can dissolve agreements that held the group together —
once you stop enforcing a should ("Charlie should brush his teeth"), the
family you trained by example may shame *you* for not shaming him:
"you're betraying the family religion."

Your family is an echo of your own past beliefs — a projected image of
your thinking. Continuing [inquiry as thought-testing](inquiry-as-thought-testing.md)
means they will eventually see as you do; until you can love them
unconditionally even while they shame Charlie, self-love isn't possible
and your Work isn't done.

Do not impose inquiry uninvited ("Is it true?", "Turn it around") on
friends or family who haven't asked — that risks alienating them, though
their irritation can lead you deeper into inquiry or deeper into suffering.
See [close relationships as
teachers](close-relationships-as-teachers.md) for using proximity as
practice material when safety allows, and [staying in your own
business](staying-in-your-own-business.md) when the distress is trying to
control others' minds.
