Autistic stress discourse names four distinct states:

- **Burnout:** exhaustion from pushing too hard or prolonged overwhelm; loss
  of skills/function and increased sensory aversions. See [autistic burnout as
  wellbeing deficit](autistic-burnout-as-wellbeing-deficit.md).
- **Inertia:** difficulty transitioning between activities — hard to stop once
  engaged, hard to start if not; not laziness. See [five-minute rule for task
  initiation](five-minute-rule-for-task-initiation.md).
- **Meltdown:** capacity exceeded; regulation impossible — increased stimming,
  crying, erratic behavior, irrational thoughts. Often follows ignored burnout.
- **Shutdown:** pushed past coping, often after burnout or meltdown; speech and
  cognition slow; seeks quiet; may become nonresponsive or sleep excessively.

Hyperconnectivity and masking intensify exhaustion; with [alexithymia as emotional
blindspot](alexithymia-as-emotional-blindspot.md), internal states may register
hours late — near meltdown before you notice.

**Tracking:** log stimuli (sensory, social, cognitive) and responses over time to
see which states you enter and what precedes them. Mood/environment charts reveal
patterns (e.g. work hours, hugs, insults) so you can add replenishing activities
when fatigue accumulates.

**Coping when irrational:** plans must assume you cannot think clearly mid-crisis.
Identify what ends the state for you (quiet dark space, being held, rocking) and
practice consistently — having a plan can reduce anxiety about sensations and
even prevent some episodes. Professional support matters for severe meltdowns.

Pairs with [delayed interoception](delayed-interoception.md) and [window of
tolerance](window-of-tolerance.md).
