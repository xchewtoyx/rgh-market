People assess how common or likely something is by the ease with which
instances come to mind — the **availability heuristic**: judging a
category's size or an event's frequency by how fluently examples can be
retrieved. Frequent classes are recalled faster, so the heuristic is often
valid — but retrievability, salience, and search ease distort it. Like other
judgment shortcuts, it is a case of [heuristic
substitution](heuristic-substitution.md): the target question is "how
common is this?" but the answered question is "how easily do instances come
to mind?"

No instances need actually be retrieved to get an impression of ease.
People know almost immediately — without generating any words — that one
scrambled letter set offers far more word possibilities than another, just
as they sense which countries appeared more in the news without recalling
specific stories.

**Retrievability:** A class with more famous or vivid instances is judged
more numerous at equal actual frequency. Seeing a burning house affects
subjective fire risk more than reading about one.

**Salience and drama:** Attention-grabbing events (Hollywood divorces,
political scandals) are retrieved easily, so their frequency is exaggerated.
A widely covered plane crash temporarily alters felt safety of flying; a car
burning roadside makes the world feel more dangerous for a while.

**Personal vs. vicarious experience:** Personal experiences, pictures, and
vivid examples are more available than incidents that happened to others,
mere words, or statistics — a judicial error affecting you personally
undermines faith in the justice system more than an equivalent case read
about in the news.

**Search-set bias:** Words starting with "r" feel more common than words
with "r" in third position, even when the opposite is true — because
searching by first letter is easier.

**Imaginability:** Committees-of-k problem: people estimate more possible
2-person committees than 8-person committees from 10 people (medians 70 vs.
20), when the true answer is 45 for both — small committees are easier to
construct mentally. Expeditions seem more dangerous when many difficulties
are vividly imagined.

**Illusory correlation:** Judges overestimate co-occurrence of "natural
associates" (e.g. suspiciousness and peculiar eyes in drawings) even when
true correlation is negative — judged frequency tracks associative strength,
not observed frequency.

**General diagnostic:** when a frequency judgment feels off, list factors
other than actual frequency that make instances easy to retrieve — each is
a potential source of bias. Resisting these biases is possible but tiresome:
deliberately reconsider impressions with questions like "Is our belief that
teen theft is a major problem due to a few recent local instances?" or "Do
I feel no need for a flu shot just because no acquaintance got the flu last
year?"

**Credit-allocation bias:** spouses estimating their personal percentage
contribution to household tasks typically produce self-assessments that sum
to more than 100%. Each remembers their own efforts far more vividly than
the partner's. The same effect explains why collaborative team members
commonly feel they did more than their share. Knowing that self-assessed
contributions usually exceed 100% in total is often enough to defuse
credit-allocation disputes.

**Death-frequency distortions:** strokes cause almost twice as many deaths as
all accidents combined, but 80% of respondents judged accidental death more
likely; tornadoes were seen as more frequent killers than asthma (20× gap).
Estimates track media coverage biased toward novelty and poignancy. See also
[retrieval fluency versus content](retrieval-fluency-vs-content.md) and
[affect heuristic](affect-heuristic.md).

When anxiety spikes after vivid news or imagination, check whether
availability is inflating perceived risk — pair with
[availability cascade](availability-cascade.md), [probability
neglect](probability-neglect.md), [catastrophizing](catastrophizing.md),
and [exaggeration and peak shift](exaggeration-and-peak-shift.md). The
same mechanism makes [focusing illusion](focusing-illusion.md) overweight
whatever is currently in attention.
