---
type: concept
title: Task Switching Cost
description: >
  Shifting from one deliberate mental program to another is effortful —
  the just-acquired habit fights the new instruction, which is why
  multitasking under time pressure feels hurried and error-prone.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 3"
---

After programming yourself to count every "f" on a page, switching to count
commas on the next page is harder than either task alone — you must
overcome the tendency you just built. [Task sets and executive
control](task-sets-and-executive-control.md) are not free to swap; under
time pressure, switching is especially costly.

This is one reason demanding working-memory exercises feel frantic: several
digits must be held while each sits in a different processing state —
queued for transformation, being transformed, or ready to report. The
juggler cannot pause without dropping a ball.

For self-regulation, notice when you stack incompatible effortful jobs
(decision-making while rehearsing a difficult conversation, planning while
monitoring emotional tone). [Breaking work into single-purpose
steps](law-of-least-effort-thinking.md) and finishing one [task
set](task-sets-and-executive-control.md) before loading the next reduces
switching tax. Pair with the [attention budget in fast-and-slow
thinking](fast-and-slow-thinking-heuristic.md).
