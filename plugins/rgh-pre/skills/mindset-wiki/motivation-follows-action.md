title: Motivation Follows Action
description: >
  Motivation waxes and wanes like the moon — you often do not need to feel
  motivated before starting; positive action frequently generates the drive
  that was being waited for.

Motivation is not a reliable prerequisite for change. People often "get into"
something once they've started rather than waiting to feel ready.

**Sources of inspiration** (used as models, not unfavorable comparisons):
role models with traits you aspire to; stories of overcoming adversity; images
and metaphors (a tree withstanding wind); quotes and proverbs; imagining "You
2.0" a year ahead — how you live, rise to challenges, and care for mind and
body.

**Focusing on benefits of change:** record why short-term effort is worth it and
refer to it often — see [cost-benefit analysis for change](cost-benefit-analysis-for-change.md).

This is the motivational framing behind [behavioral activation](behavioral-activation.md):
action is the first step, not motivation. The five-minute rule and graded tasks
apply the same sequence at smaller scale.

**Depression reverses the sequence:** normally you wait until you want to do
something before doing it; in depression you often must do something before you
can want to. Depressed tiredness is misleading — ordinary tiredness calls for
rest that refreshes; depressed fatigue can increase with rest and calls for
short increased activity instead. "Stay in the game" — keep participating even
when mood and thoughts insist there is no use. Perform nourishing actions as
experiments without pre-judging how you will feel afterward.

Even thinking about previously pleasant activities can feel aversive in low
mood — taking action anyway, with courage, is sometimes what body and mind need.
See [exhaustion funnel](exhaustion-funnel.md) for how nourishing activities get
dropped first under stress.

Pairs with [concrete implementation plans](concrete-implementation-plans.md) and
[self-discipline without self-hatred](self-discipline-without-self-hatred.md).
