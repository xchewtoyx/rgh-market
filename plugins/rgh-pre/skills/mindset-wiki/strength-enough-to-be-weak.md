type: concept
title: Strength Enough to Be Weak
description: >
  What looks like invulnerability is often a facade covering fear; allowing
  vulnerability, grief, and appropriate openness is where real strength lies
  in meditation and daily life.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 3"
---

Strong-willed people often project invulnerability — isolating themselves and
others. Fathers and people in power positions are especially prone to the
"Rock of Gibraltar" persona that blocks real feelings behind image and aura.

Meditation practice can create a similar trap: believing in the role of
"supremely invulnerable, correct meditator" arrests development without
knowing it. We all have an emotional life; walling ourselves off is perilous.

When you notice self-promotional talk about meditation wisdom or invincibility,
bring mindfulness to whether you are running from vulnerability, grief, or
fear. "If you are truly strong, there is little need to emphasize it." Better
to feel, even cry, hold fewer opinions, and be appropriately open about
feelings. What looks like weakness is where strength lies; what looks like
strength is often weakness covering fear.

**Try:** When impulse is to be hard, experiment with soft; when withholding,
with generous; when closing up, with open. Let grief and sadness be here;
release labels on crying or vulnerability. Ride waves of up/down, good/bad,
weak/strong until labels prove inadequate. Trust deepest strength: to be
present and wakeful.
