---
type: concept
title: No Prizes for Hard Mode
description: >
  Choosing the harder route when an easier one achieves the same outcome
  adds suffering without adding value — ease is not cheating.
sources:
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Ellie Middleton), ch. 9"
---

A common cultural script treats difficulty as virtue: hiring a cleaner,
using pre-cut vegetables, or listening to audiobooks instead of "proper
reading" can feel like cheating or laziness. The phrase "an easy way out"
implies that worthwhile actions must cost extra energy.

Middleton challenges this: if the outcome is the same — a clean home,
adequate nutrition, knowing how a story ends — the harder path adds no real
value. The point of life is not to maximize struggle; it is to find ways of
living that are as easy, enjoyable, and pleasant as possible while still
getting what matters done.

You do not have to force unnecessary struggle to earn status as a Good
Person, or act against your wants and natural ways of being because someone
once said you should. Pair this with [saying goodbye to
should](saying-goodbye-to-should.md) when guilt appears around choosing
support, tools, or accommodations that work.

This is not avoidance of all challenge — it is refusing **gratuitous**
hardship when [willpower needs strategy](willpower-needs-strategy.md) and
[mental energy](mental-energy-budget.md) are finite.
