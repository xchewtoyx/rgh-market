title: The Apology Reflex
description: >
  Reflexively apologizing for one's own presence or minor, faultless
  situations (rather than for an actual mistake) reflects an internalized
  belief that one is a burden, and can be interrupted by noticing the
  distinction between a genuine-fault apology and an existence-apology.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 3, entry 11: Curtail the Apology Reflex"
---

There's a meaningful difference between apologizing for something one
actually did wrong and reflexively apologizing for existing in a space —
saying "sorry" when someone else bumps into you, when asking a reasonable
question, or when simply taking up attention. The reflexive version tends
to reflect an internalized sense of being a burden or an imposition,
built up over time rather than a response to any actual fault in the
moment.

A concrete technique for interrupting the pattern: in situations with no
actual fault, substitute a neutral phrase ("excuse me," "pardon me") for
"I'm sorry" — this keeps the social function (acknowledging a minor
interruption) while removing the self-deprecating content the reflexive
apology carries. Because the words used repeatedly shape which
self-narrative gets reinforced (see [self-talk as a trainable
pattern](self-talk-as-trainable-pattern.md)), the substitution is not
just cosmetic — reducing how often "I'm sorry" gets paired with faultless,
everyday situations reduces how often that self-narrative gets rehearsed.
