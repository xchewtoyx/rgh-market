---
type: concept
title: Everyone's Path as Their Music
description: >
  Treating each person's chosen way of life as their own "music" — neither
  higher nor lower than yours — reduces the war over approval and makes
  room for mutual respect without conversion.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 4"
---

"I yearn for my family to respect my music" turns to "I yearn for me to
respect my music" and "I yearn for me to respect their music." Everyone's
chosen path or way of life is their music — "There's no path that's higher
than another."

When paths collide, a communication script that honors both songs: "I love
it that your way makes you happy. Thank you for wanting to share it with
me." Regarding being heard without demanding it: "I'm happy for you, and
I'm happy for me."

If thirteen people can't convince you to walk their path, what makes you
think you'll convince all thirteen of yours? "If this is war, you're
outnumbered." Turn "I want them to accept me as I learn my own truth" to
"I want me to accept myself as I learn my own truth" and "I want me to
accept them as they learn their own truth... They're just doing what
you're doing. We're all doing the best we can." Whose business is it who
you love? Whose business is it who they love? See [staying in your own
business](staying-in-your-own-business.md).

Reconnect practically: go home, sit with or help a busy parent, listen to
her talk about her path "without letting your story interfere." "No one
can deprive me of my family — no one but me... There's no family to save.
No family to convert. There's only one, as it turns out — you." See also
[peace requires only one person](peace-requires-only-one-person.md).
