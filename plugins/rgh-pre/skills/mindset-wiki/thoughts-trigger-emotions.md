Emotions are not random or purely biological events; a specific thought
precedes and triggers each one (with unconditional love treated as a
possible exception). Any further flood of thought that follows an emotion
is the mind analyzing or reacting to the emotion, not its origin — the
thought comes first. This makes emotional reactions describable as
roughly predictable functions of the gap between two mental states, in the
same spirit as the [happiness equation](happiness-equation.md): for
example, fear as the gap between a felt sense of safety right now and an
imagined sense of safety at some future moment, or anxiety as the gap
between what feels needed to be safe and what one believes oneself
capable of.

The practical use of this is diagnostic: an emotion that seems to come out
of nowhere almost always has an identifiable thought behind it, and
finding that thought is the first step toward addressing the emotion at
its source rather than only managing its symptoms. This is also the
oldest form of the claim on record: the Stoic position that it is
"neither death, nor exile, nor distress... that causes us to do
something, but rather our judgements and opinions" — the same event
(a loss, a threat, an insult) produces entirely different reactions in
different people, or in the same person at different times, because the
judgment about it, not the event, is what actually generates the
reaction. This is the same move
as [catching automatic thoughts](catching-automatic-thoughts.md), applied
specifically to tracing feeling back to its trigger rather than only
noticing the thought itself.

A concrete version of this diagnostic: when a strong emotional reaction
occurs, acknowledge the feeling without resisting it, then trace it
backward to ask why this specific situation triggered this specific
emotion — often the immediate trigger (a minor annoyance) turns out to be
connected to an older, unrelated thought or memory it happens to echo.
Once the actual originating thought is identified, reframing that thought
tends to reframe the emotion attached to it directly — the emotion isn't
independently stubborn, it's downstream of a specific interpretation that,
once seen clearly, is often revealed as disproportionate to the present
situation.
