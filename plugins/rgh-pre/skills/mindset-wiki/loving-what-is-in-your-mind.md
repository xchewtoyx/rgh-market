type: concept
title: Loving What Is in Your Mind
description: >
  Mental peace means welcoming every thought that appears — arguing with
  them is what turns ordinary cognition into victimhood and pain.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 4"
---

"It doesn't matter what thoughts are in your head: do you love them yet?
Is everything welcome there? Is your mind at home in itself? Are you loving
what is? ... That's why all thoughts have a right to live. It's not as
though they're not living in your head anyway. And the more you argue with
them, the more painful it is, the more identified as a victim you become."

When grief turns a felt presence into argument — "going crazy" — "identify
the crazy thought, write it down, question it, and see what's there...
what's true." An "ascended master" is "someone who's totally fine with
what is" — which can show up as the deceased at breakfast, already okay.

This is [inquiry as thought-testing](inquiry-as-thought-testing.md) applied
to inner hospitality rather than selective suppression. Pair with [thought
defusion](thought-defusion.md) and [grief inquiry after
loss](grief-inquiry-after-loss.md).
