---
type: concept
title: Recycled Thoughts
description: >
  A self-generated, unexamined thought that resurfaces repeatedly over time
  and disguises itself as settled fact, spawning further unverified
  thoughts that cascade from it.
sources:
  - title: That Little Voice in Your Head
    resource: "That Little Voice in Your Head (Mo Gawdat), Chapter 2: Garbage In..."
---

Unlike a thought triggered by something external, a recycled thought is
generated by the brain itself and then the brain retroactively finds
justification to support it — the conclusion arrives before the evidence.
Its distinctive signature is repetition: it resurfaces again and again
across unrelated situations, each repetition making it feel more like an
established fact rather than a single, once-generated interpretation.
Left unexamined, one recycled thought tends to spawn a cascade of further
unverified thoughts built on top of it (e.g. "they don't love me anymore"
cascading into "I'm unlovable," "I'll never find love again").

Recycled thoughts are close kin to [conditioning](conditioning-and-context-mismatch.md)
— both present as fact rather than as hypothesis — but a recycled thought
is generated internally and repeated by the person's own mind rather than
inherited from an external source. The countermeasure is the same
discipline used for any unverified claim: question it as deliberately as
one would question a claim made by someone else, rather than exempting
one's own recurring thoughts from scrutiny. See [catching automatic
thoughts](catching-automatic-thoughts.md) for noticing the thought as it
arises, and [cognitive reframing](cognitive-reframing.md) for testing it
once noticed.
