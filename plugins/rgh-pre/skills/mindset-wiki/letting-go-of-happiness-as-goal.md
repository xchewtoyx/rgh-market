---
type: concept
title: Letting Go of Happiness as Goal
description: >
  Craving to be elsewhere than you are — comparing your state to a wished-for
  peaceful one — creates the suffering it tries to escape; releasing happiness
  as a goal can allow it to appear on its own.
sources:
  - title: "The Mindful Way through Depression: Freeing Yourself from Chronic Unhappiness"
    resource: "The Mindful Way through Depression (Williams, Teasdale, Segal, Kabat-Zinn), ch. 10"
---

The doing mind constantly measures the gap between how things are and how you
want them to be. Comparing your actual state to a mindful, peaceful state you
long for — especially during difficult "head" work — generates further
unhappiness. This is **craving**: longing for things to be other than they are.

Recognizing you are creating this suffering yourself can prompt a compassionate
response: "Why not do yourself a favor and let go?" The words "I do not need
to be happy" can bring lightness — as if a burden carried too long lifts — and
happiness may follow.

Mindfulness is more than attending to pleasant sensory detail; it discerns which
patterns of mind serve you and which create suffering. **Letting go of
happiness as a goal can pave the way for happiness to appear on its own.**

mode breaks](head-tasks-mode-breaks.md).
