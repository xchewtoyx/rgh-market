type: concept
title: Direct Contact Versus Secondhand Ideas
description: >
  We often live through borrowed images and opinions about reality instead
  of checking what is actually present — closing off fresh encounter and
  impoverishing experience until deliberate attention restores contact.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 6"
---

We carry ideas and images of reality gathered secondhand — from other
people, courses, books, television, newspapers, culture in general — and
as a result often see our thoughts, or someone else's, instead of what is
right in front of us or inside us. We may stop checking how we feel because
we think we already know, closing ourselves to the wonder of fresh
encounters, and can even forget that direct contact is possible at all —
living in a "dream reality of our own making" without sensing the loss,
spiritually and emotionally impoverished by it.

Physicist Victor Weisskopf, invited to lecture at the University of
Arizona, asked to look directly through the Kitt Peak Observatory telescope.
Told it was impossible — booked constantly for photography and research —
he replied that in that case he would not give the talks; within days the
visit was arranged. On a clear night he viewed Saturn and galaxies with his
own eyes, seeing details he had only known from photographs. Astronomers
attached to the observatory who had never looked directly at objects of
their own research took turns at the eyepiece too.

**Practice prompt:** Your own life is at least as interesting and miraculous
as the moon or the stars. What stands between you and direct contact with
your life? What can you do to change that?

This is the experiential counterpart to [non-judging attitude in
meditation](non-judging-attitude-in-meditation.md): interest in what is
actually here, not in the story about it. It complements [becoming your own
authority](becoming-your-own-authority.md) — the information needed is often
"right beneath our noses" once secondhand certainty loosens — and
[informal attention practices](informal-attention-practices.md) that restore
contact in ordinary moments.
