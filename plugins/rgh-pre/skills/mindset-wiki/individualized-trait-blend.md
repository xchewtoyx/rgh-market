---
type: concept
title: Individualized Trait Blend (Not Fitting a Category)
description: >
  Personality and cognitive traits exist on continuous, individually
  unique scales rather than as binary categories, so measuring oneself
  against a category one is expected to "fit" is itself a source of
  unnecessary suffering.
sources:
  - title: That Little Voice in Your Head
    resource: "That Little Voice in Your Head (Mo Gawdat), Chapter 5: Both of You"
---

Traits commonly treated as opposing categories (analytical vs. intuitive,
disciplined vs. spontaneous, and similar pairs) are not binary — each
person carries a unique blend and intensity of both ends of every such
scale. Reducing a person to one label in the pair ("you're just not a
detail person," "you're too emotional") is a simplification, and "an
approximation of the truth is not the truth... it misleads." The only
category any person precisely belongs to is their own specific
combination of traits.

This reframes the felt need to fit into a recognizable category — a
personality type, a professional archetype, a social role — as a
manufactured pressure rather than a real requirement: measuring against a
category's expectations, instead of noticing one's own actual blend of
traits, generates unnecessary self-judgment. It also supplies a direct
counter to the impulse to suppress a genuine trait for fear of it not
matching an expected mold (e.g. downplaying a strength for fear of not
fitting a modest/humble category, or downplaying intuition for fear of
not fitting an analytical one) — the trait belongs to the individual blend
regardless of which category it seems to violate.
