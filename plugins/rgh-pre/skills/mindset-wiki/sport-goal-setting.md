description: >
  CBT goals work best when Specific, Positive, Observable, Realistic, and
  Time-bound — framing the destination you want rather than only what you flee.
sources:

---

Aaron Beck defined CBT as "whatever helps you move from your problems to your
goals." The **SPORT** acronym sets clear, actionable targets:

- **Specific** — precise about where, when, or with whom you want to feel or
  behave differently.
- **Positive** — state what you want more of, not less of ("gain social
  confidence" not "be less anxious"). Focus on the destination, not only what
  you flee.
- **Observable** — include a behavioral change others could notice; if stuck,
  ask how someone who knows you well would see a positive mood change.
- **Realistic** — achievable through your own change, not others' or the
  world's.
- **Time** — timeframe for focus; use flexibly for conditions like depression
  where rigid schedules can themselves cause distress. Integration into routine
  until automatic matters more than hitting arbitrary deadlines.

**Mercurial desires:** constantly abandoning goals for new whims can mask
avoidance — use SPORT to assess durability before switching direction.
