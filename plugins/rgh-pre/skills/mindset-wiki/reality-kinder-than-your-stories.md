"Reality is always kinder than the stories we tell about it." When Gloria
admits "They're being reckless" may not be absolutely knowable, she names
why that's scary: "The ego doesn't want to let go of its beliefs. That's
dangerous to its existence." Letting go of "I know what's best" opens
turnarounds like "They're being safe" — same information, considered adult
decision, their own priorities.

A twelve-year-old in a collapsing house during bombing may be alive at
each step — falling, crawling out, reaching safety — until the only
remaining "not okay" is a thought about family survival. "Little boys don't
understand how the mind works. They can't know that it's just a story
that's scaring them." Asked if he even felt the roof falling: "Probably
not, because I was in so much fear" — the story can be louder than the
sensation.

Question 3's physical inventory (chest tightening, shallow breathing,
sweaty hands) links belief to real physiological cost — emotions affect
blood pressure, diabetes, blood sugar — making the story's price visible.
