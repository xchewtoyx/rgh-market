There is no one "right way" to practice meditation, though there are pitfalls
to watch for. Each moment deserves freshness: look deeply, then let go into the
next without holding the last — each breath a new beginning and letting be.
Meditative experience, like a sunset, should not be forced or over-narrated;
"best not to speak at all in moments such as these."

Like feet finding their own way over rough trail — each step choosing among
many possible placements, committing with full weight, then releasing as the
next foot chooses — your practice finds its own footing. Children on a mountain
path never ask which rock to step on; they each discover their own way rather
than simply imitating an adult's steps. This does not mean there is no wrong
way — care and sensing of footing still matter — but eyes and brain are very
good at rapid terrain assessment, so stepping on rough ground becomes balance
in motion. Ordinarily we look ahead on the trail, not at our feet; the brain
directs torso, limbs, and feet in split-second decisions appropriate to the
terrain. The same applies to meditation: trust direct experience rather than
constantly checking whether you match someone else's path.

A strong habit is wanting some authority to anoint your experience (however
small) with blessing. Practicing trust **in the face of insecurity** deepens
the path over time. Feet and breath both teach watching your step, proceeding
mindfully, being at home in every moment wherever they carry you.

**Try:** When "Am I doing this right?", "Is this what I should be feeling?", or
"Is this what is supposed to happen?" arises — do not answer. Look more deeply
into the present moment; hold the question in awareness with breath and full
context. Trust that "This is it," whatever and wherever this is. Keep continuity
of mindfulness without analyzing, judging, condemning, or doubting — observe,
embrace, open, let be, accept. Right now. Only this step. Only this moment.
