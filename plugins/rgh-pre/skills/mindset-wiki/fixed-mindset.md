---
type: concept
title: Fixed Mindset
description: >
  The belief that a personal quality — intelligence, talent, personality —
  is a carved-in-stone fact about oneself, which turns every situation
  that could reveal that quality into a pass/fail test of self-worth.
sources:
  - title: "Mindset: The New Psychology of Success"
    resource: "Mindset: The New Psychology of Success (Carol S. Dweck), Chapter 1: The Mindsets"
---

A fixed mindset treats qualities like intelligence, talent, and moral
character as fixed quantities a person either has or doesn't — the hand
they were dealt, not something responsive to effort. Because the quality
is believed fixed, every relevant situation stops being a chance to
develop and becomes instead a test of whether the fixed quantity measures
up: will I succeed or fail, look smart or dumb, be accepted or rejected.
The stakes attached to ordinary setbacks are consequently disproportionate
— a single poor outcome isn't information about one performance, it's
evidence about a permanent, defining trait.

This produces a specific pattern under pressure to avoid situations that
might expose inadequacy, to devalue effort (since needing to try hard can
itself look like evidence of insufficient natural ability), and to invert
common growth-oriented wisdom ("if at first you don't succeed, you
probably don't have the ability"). A person's mindset is not necessarily
uniform — it can be fixed in one domain (e.g. intellectual ability) and
more growth-oriented in another (e.g. relationships), and situationally
triggered mindsets can even be temporarily induced. See [growth
mindset](growth-mindset.md) for the contrasting belief, and [mindset
shapes response to setbacks](mindset-shapes-response-to-setbacks.md) for
how this plays out concretely when something goes wrong.
