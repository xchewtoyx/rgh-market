title: Seven Signs of Driven-Doing
description: >
  Seven cognitive-mode markers — from autopilot through harsh self-treatment —
  that characterize the rumination-fueled doing mode MBCT aims to step out of.
sources:
  - title: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition"
    resource: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression (Segal, Williams, Teasdale), ch. 5"
---

Old habits of negative thinking persist in a cognitive mode with these features
(foregrounded session by session across MBCT):

1. Living on **automatic pilot** rather than with awareness and conscious choice
2. Relating to experience through **thought** rather than direct sensing
3. Dwelling on **past and future** rather than being fully in the present
4. Trying to **avoid, escape, or get rid of** unpleasant experience rather than
   approaching with interest
5. **Needing things to be different** rather than allowing them as they are
6. Seeing thoughts as **true and real** rather than as mental events
7. Treating oneself **harshly and unkindly** rather than with kindness

The core skill is to exit and stay out of these self-perpetuating routines —
**be mindful, let go** — relinquishing the need for things to be different that
drives the thinking patterns. The program's aim is freedom, not happiness or
relaxation per se, though those may follow.

Kindness pervading practice teaches that unwanted experience can be approached
with gentle curiosity. Experiential learning requires treating all experience as
"grist for the mill," with participants as experts on themselves.

See [driven-doing mode](driven-doing-mode.md) for the relapse mechanism and
[doing mode versus being mode](doing-mode-being-mode.md) for the alternative.
