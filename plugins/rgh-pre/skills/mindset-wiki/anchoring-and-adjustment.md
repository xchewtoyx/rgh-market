title: Anchoring and Adjustment
description: >
  Estimates start from an initial value and adjust insufficiently, leaving
  final judgments biased toward whatever anchor was offered first — even
  when random.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 12"
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 41 (Tversky & Kahneman, 1974)"

Numerical estimates begin from an initial anchor and adjust — but adjustment
is typically insufficient, biasing answers toward the starting point. The
**anchoring effect**: considering a particular value before estimating an
unknown quantity keeps estimates close to that number — even obviously
random anchors (wheel of fortune: medians 25 vs. 45 for African UN members;
SSN last digits anchoring physician counts).

**Anchoring index** = (difference in estimates) / (difference in anchors).
A redwood-height study: anchors 180 vs. 1,200 feet produced mean estimates
282 vs. 844 — index **55%**. Real-estate agents valuing a house showed
**41%** anchoring despite denying influence; business students showed
**48%**. Charitable willingness-to-pay anchored at $5 vs. $400 moved means
from $20 to $143 (>30% index). German judges rolling loaded dice (3 vs. 9
months) sentenced shoplifters to 5 vs. 8 months on average — **50%** index.
Random anchors work as well as plausible ones.

**Two mechanisms:** (1) **Adjustment (System 2)** — start at anchor, move
until uncertain you should go farther; ends prematurely. Nodding enhances
anchoring; head-shaking reduces it; depletion or mild intoxication increases
it. (2) **Priming/suggestion (System 1)** — anchors evoke compatible
evidence without belief ("Gandhi died at 144?" still activates extreme
old age). Temperature anchors prime summer vs. winter words; price anchors
prime luxury vs. mass-market car brands.

**Counter-anchoring:** focus attention on arguments *against* the anchor,
the opponent's minimum acceptable offer, or their cost of no deal —
activating System 2 can reduce or eliminate the effect. In negotiation,
don't counter an outrageous anchor with an equally outrageous counter —
make clear you won't continue with that number on the table.

**Abuses:** "Limit 12 per person" soup signs doubled average purchases vs.
no limit; first-mover advantage in price negotiation; damage caps can anchor
awards upward for smaller cases. System 2 works on data retrieved by
automatic System 1 and "has no control over the effect and no knowledge of
it."

Self-generated anchors work too: estimating 8×7×6×…×1 in five seconds gave
median 2,250; estimating 1×2×3×…×8 gave median 512 (true answer: 40,320).

When a first number sets the frame, explicitly ask what the estimate would
be with a very different starting point — see [selective skeptic
test](selective-skeptic-test.md).
