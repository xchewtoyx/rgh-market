---
type: concept
title: Innocence Through Past-Action Inquiry
description: >
  Finding release from self-blame over a regretted action by re-entering
  the moment before it and seeing that, given the beliefs held then, no
  other choice was actually available.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 12"
---

For ongoing guilt over something you knew not to do but did anyway, a
standalone exercise:

1. Recall the action and get still.
2. Identify the exact moment just before acting.
3. Re-enter that moment from the perspective of who you were then — place,
   people, emotions, and especially the thoughts/beliefs held in the instant
   before acting.
4. Ask: given what you were believing then, could you have done anything
   different? With the warped or limited understanding you had, did you
   really have a choice?

The sense that something else "could have happened" is "just a thought
you're having now about a then — an imagined past you're comparing with the
real past, which is also imagined." Realizing your thinking caused the
action, and that you had no choice about believing what you believed, is
how you "find your innocence."

**Example — hitting a child:** a man insisted "I knew I could hold back."
Inquiry on the thoughts operating in that moment ("My son should do what I
tell him," "He shouldn't defy me," "Children should be respectful," "The
only way he'll learn is if I hit him") shows there was no conscious
decision — "Either we believe our stressful thoughts, or we question them.
There's no other choice."

The claim is not that harm didn't occur or that amends are unnecessary —
it's that ongoing self-punishment rests on a story of free choice that
doesn't match what was actually believed in the moment. This differs from
[self-forgiveness for past missteps](self-forgiveness-for-past-missteps.md),
which uses a "best I could with what I had" reframe without the inquiry
step into specific beliefs. Pair with [forgiveness through
inquiry](forgiveness-through-inquiry.md) when the regret is tied to harm
done to others.
