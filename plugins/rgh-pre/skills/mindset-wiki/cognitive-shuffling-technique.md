type: concept
title: Cognitive Shuffling Technique
description: >
  Deliberately scrambling attention across unrelated, unconnected words and
  images disrupts the mind's evaluative and planning functions well enough
  to displace pre-sleep rumination, mimicking the loosely associative
  thought pattern of the brain as it naturally drifts toward sleep.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 2, entry 10: Put Your Mind to Sleep"
---

A busy or ruminating mind at bedtime is still running the same
evaluative, planning, problem-solving machinery that's useful during the
day but actively counterproductive when what's needed is to fall asleep.
Cognitive shuffling works by giving that machinery something to do that
it can't turn into logical, evaluative work: deliberately generating a
sequence of unrelated, non-logically-connected words and mental images,
so there's no thread to analyze or worry-loop attach itself to.

One concrete method: pick a short word (e.g., "rain"), then for its first
letter generate a series of unrelated words starting with that letter,
briefly picturing each one before moving to the next; once that letter is
exhausted, move to the word's next letter and repeat; continue through
the whole word. If an intrusive or stressful thought surfaces during the
exercise, the response is simply to return attention to generating the
next unrelated word for the current letter, rather than following the
intrusive thought or fighting it directly.

This is a variant of [single-tasking](single-tasking.md) and [attention
displacement to interrupt a thought](attention-displacement-to-interrupt-thought.md)
purpose-built for the pre-sleep window specifically: instead of anchoring
attention in present-moment sensory input (which requires enough alertness
to sustain), it anchors attention in a low-effort generative task loose
enough that it can't be evaluated or elaborated on the way a real worry
or plan can, which is precisely what allows the mind to drift rather than
engage.
