title: Unconditional Self-Acceptance
description: >
  Self-acceptance removes global self-rating altogether — treating worth as
  intrinsic and stable — whereas boosting self-esteem only raises the
  estimate while leaving you vulnerable to the next setback that knocks it down.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies (Rhena Branch, Rob Willson), ch. 16: Self-Acceptance"
---

**Self-esteem** means estimating your worth. Tie that estimate to changeable
conditions — achievements, appearance, status, approval — and it yo-yos with
them. **Self-acceptance** (Albert Ellis's rational-emotive tradition) stops
rating the whole person: you are too complex, changing, and fallible to
legitimately receive a global verdict. Specific behaviors can be assessed;
the whole "you" cannot.

Core assertions: you are a unique, multifaceted, ever-changing individual;
humans are imperfect by nature; therefore whole-person condemnation is a
[labeling](labels-strip-context.md) error, not a fair summary. One bruised
apple does not make the fruit bowl worthless; one flaw does not cancel a
tapestry. Your "being includes your becoming" — any rating computed today is
wrong tomorrow.

Six practical principles: (1) worth because you are human — intrinsic value
to yourself, not contingent on others' opinions; (2) too complex to measure
globally; (3) let go of permanent labels from single incidents; (4) you are
more than the sum of parts — one characteristic is not the whole "I"; (5)
you are ever-changing, so global ratings are always stale; (6) accept
fallible nature — disappointment and remorse about a blunder are healthy;
self-condemnation drives depression, shame, and avoidance.

Self-acceptance is **not** resignation. Wendy (failed job interview) can
think "I didn't present well; I need IT skills," feel disappointment, enroll
in a course, and keep searching — whereas self-downing ("I'm such a failure")
drives depression and giving up. Acceptance separates acceptable person from
unacceptable behavior, which makes change more likely after moral lapses:
take personal responsibility and name specific problems (addiction vs. stealing)
rather than globally writing yourself off as "a bad person."

Action strategies: desist global labels; be specific ("in what way did I
act stupidly?"); say what you mean ("missing the deadline was a bad move"
not "I'm an idiot"). Apply the [best-friend
argument](best-friend-argument-for-self-acceptance.md) — judge behavior,
not the person. Imperfect self-acceptance is expected; the skill is noticing
self-put-down and returning faster.

Pairs with [self-criticism is not a healthy
motivator](self-criticism-is-not-a-healthy-motivator.md), [high frustration
tolerance](low-frustration-tolerance.md) (willingness to do hard improvement
work without global condemnation), and [friend to yourself](friend-to-yourself.md).
