---
type: concept
title: Anger Requires Past-and-Future Images
description: >
  A flash of anger or jealousy typically depends on brief mental images of
  how things used to be or might go — without those comparisons, the same
  scene can be witnessed without the charge.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 4"
---

No matter how fast temper rises, images of past and future have to be in
place for anger to happen — memories of how someone used to look at you,
contrasted with an imagined future where they never will again, can appear
and vanish in under a second, below everyday awareness. Drop the thought
and witness the room as it is, and inclusion replaces exclusion: "I felt
included in the room, with them."

Violent feeling is a signal you are not in present reality — you are in
comparison (nothing versus something, image of you versus image of them).
Inquiry on the specific images — "Can you absolutely know that's true?"
— often collapses the charge faster than arguing about the other person's
behavior. Pair with [is it true technique](is-it-true-technique.md) rule
that past-and-future claims are unprovable in the present moment, and with
[inquiry on specific incidents](inquiry-on-specific-incidents.md) for
working a single scene rather than a global verdict on someone.

When jealousy compares an image of yourself to an image of a rival, the
comparison is between constructions, not between direct observation of two
people — a specific case of [noticing the story your mind is
telling](noticing-the-story-your-mind-is-telling.md).
