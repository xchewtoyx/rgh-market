---
type: concept
title: Confidence Tracks Coherence, Not Evidence
description: >
  Subjective confidence rises with how well a story hangs together from
  what is currently in mind — not with how complete or reliable the
  underlying evidence is.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 11"
---

Summarizing "In a telephone poll of 300 seniors, 60% support the
president" in three words, almost everyone drops the sample size and says
"elderly support president" — unchanged whether the sample was 150 or
3,000. People focus on the story; finer-grained reliability cues often
fail to register unless blatantly flagged.

Fast thinking suppresses ambiguity and builds the most coherent story it
can. Unless a message is immediately negated, its associations spread as
if true. Sustaining doubt is harder work than sliding into certainty —
effortful thinking can hold incompatible possibilities at once, but that
costs [mental energy](mental-energy-budget.md). This is one face of
[WYSIATI](wysiati.md): confidence reflects internal coherence, not missing
evidence you never thought to ask for.

Practical counters include naming what is unknown, asking how the read
would change with different sample size or source quality, and treating
strong narrative fit as a reason to pause rather than as proof — especially
under [high-stakes bias vigilance](high-stakes-bias-vigilance.md). Pair
with [epistemic versus social
confidence](epistemic-vs-social-confidence.md) when separating "I feel sure"
from "the case is actually strong."
