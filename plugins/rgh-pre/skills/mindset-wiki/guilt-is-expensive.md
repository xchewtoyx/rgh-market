---
type: concept
title: Guilt Is Expensive
description: >
  Keeping someone in a role out of unexamined loyalty guilt costs clarity,
  resentment, and organizational honesty — more than honest reassignment
  or severance would.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 6"
---

An executive kept an underperforming ten-year assistant because "she has
five children" — teaching the rest of the organization that enough children
excuse poor performance, while both parties lived with covert resentment.
When he read his [Judge-Your-Neighbor
Worksheet](judge-your-neighbor-worksheet.md) aloud, she agreed with the
assessment and, asked what she'd do in his position, effectively fired
herself into a better-fitting job near home. He had never investigated the
thoughts behind his "loyalty."

"Guilt is expensive." Honest moves — reassignment, severance, direct
conversation — often serve everyone better than stories that postpone
[inquiry](inquiry-as-thought-testing.md) while resentment compounds. This
pairs with [work and money problems as thinking
problems](work-and-money-problems-as-thinking-problems.md): the numbers
look fine while the beliefs drain the system.
