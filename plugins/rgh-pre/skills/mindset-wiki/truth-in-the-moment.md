---
type: concept
title: Truth in the Moment
description: >
  Integrity means reporting what is true for you right now without
  comparing it to what was true a moment ago — flexibility is honesty, not
  flip-flopping.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 13"
---

Human experience changes constantly, but the place of integrity does not
move: tell the truth as it appears *now*. "Are you thirsty?" — No. Later:
Yes. "That's the truth." Comparing current truth to past truth is where
confusion starts.

A cousin called at 2 a.m. with a loaded pistol to his head, demanding one
good reason to stay alive. After a long wait, finding none, Katie told him
so. He wept that it was the first time he'd heard integrity — what he was
looking for. Had she concocted a reason because she believed he shouldn't
die, "I would have given him less than the only thing I really have to give,
which is my truth in the moment."

People who practice [inquiry](the-four-questions-of-inquiry.md) "get really
clear about the truth as they see it," making it easy to change their minds
when experience shifts — door to firewood to air. "It's simply honest to
report that you've changed your mind when you have." Fear of others'
judgment about that honesty is where suffering begins.

Pair with [speaking your truth as prerequisite for
inquiry](speaking-your-truth-as-prerequisite-for-inquiry.md) and [clarity
enables effective action](clarity-enables-effective-action.md). Distinct
from philosophical debates about whether anything is ultimately true —
this is a day-to-day practice of not arguing with your own present
experience.
