type: concept
title: Sitting Beside the Thought Stream
description: >
  Meditation practice learns to step out of the incessant current of
  thoughts, sit beside it and listen, and use its energy to guide action
  rather than be tyrannized by automatic impulses.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 1: Simple But Not Easy"
---

Most people are thinking virtually all the time. The incessant stream of
thoughts leaves little respite for inner quiet and little room just to be
without constantly doing. Actions are too often driven by ordinary
thoughts and impulses coursing through the mind like a river or waterfall
rather than undertaken in awareness — and a person can get caught in that
torrent, submerged and carried to places they did not choose.

Kabat-Zinn's core meditative image: learn how to get out of this current,
sit by its bank and listen to it, learn from it, and then use its energies
to guide rather than tyrannize. This does not happen by itself; it takes
energy. That deliberate effort to be in the present moment is what is
called practice or meditation practice.

This sits alongside [thought-listening practice](thought-listening-practice.md)
(which exhausts repetitive loops by acknowledging each thought once) and
the general [mindfulness](mindfulness.md) stance of operating from an
observing rather than evaluating mind. Where [rumination](rumination.md)
is involuntary replay that manufactures distress, sitting beside the
stream is voluntary witnessing that keeps thought available as information
without letting it drive behavior unchecked.
