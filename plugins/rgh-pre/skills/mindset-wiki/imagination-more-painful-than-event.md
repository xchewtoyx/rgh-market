---
type: concept
title: Imagination More Painful Than the Event
description: >
  Anticipated harm — especially when the mind projects a vivid future
  scene — often hurts more than the moment itself, and can trigger
  dissociation before anything happens.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 12"
---

In a childhood abuse scene, while still being dragged to the bathroom —
not yet hit — "He's going to beat me" produces terror, panic, and
dissociation ("I leave my body") from an image of the crippled finger
already beating him. Without the thought: "I would be relaxed. I would be
completely free." The anticipated image approached imagined death ("He's
going to kill me!") closely enough that identity split from the body.

At the raised-hand moment: "He's going to hit you again — can you
absolutely know?" No — "just imagination." "Which is more painful?
Imagination or when his hand actually hits you?" "Imagination is worse."
The mind is always remembering or anticipating, never in the actual hit
itself. See [fear lives in memory and
anticipation](fear-lives-in-memory-and-anticipation.md) and [inquiry on
specific incidents](inquiry-on-specific-incidents.md) for working one scene
before arrival, not only after.

Prescription for lingering images: [judge your neighbor
worksheet](judge-your-neighbor-worksheet.md) and question them. "Just
notice that they're not real" — not suppress thoughts, but see the movie.
Without fearful thoughts on the way to the bathroom, even one second after
a hit: "Completely free." The Work "is a practice. It's not a quick fix" —
unquestioned thoughts override until the next situation is questioned too.
