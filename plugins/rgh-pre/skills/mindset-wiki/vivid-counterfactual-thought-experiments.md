title: Vivid Counterfactual Thought Experiments
description: >
  Bias-detection thought experiments (double standard, outsider,
  conformity, selective skeptic, status quo) only work if the alternate
  scenario is actually imagined vividly, and they reveal that a judgment
  is contingent on motive rather than telling you what's actually correct.
sources:
  - title: "The Scout Mindset: Why Some People See Things Clearly and Others Don't"
    resource: "The Scout Mindset (Julia Galef), Chapter 5: Noticing Bias"

A family of related checks — [the double standard
test](double-standard-test.md), [the outsider test](outsider-test.md),
[the conformity test](conformity-test.md), [the selective skeptic
test](selective-skeptic-test.md), and [the status quo bias
test](status-quo-bias-test.md) — all work the same way: they ask what
judgment would be reached in a counterfactual version of the situation
that can't literally be visited, since normally only one branch of the
comparison (the actual, motivated one) is ever experienced. Motivated
reasoning works like a magician's forced choice: whichever conclusion the
mind was already going to reach feels freely and independently chosen,
precisely because the alternate branch was never actually seen.

The test only works if the counterfactual is vividly imagined rather than
verbally asserted — answering "I wouldn't mind" to "how would you feel in
their shoes" without actually picturing the scenario in detail produces a
performance of the right answer, not the diagnostic shift in felt
judgment that a genuinely vivid imagining produces. It's also important
what these tests don't do: they aren't oracles that reveal the correct
answer. Discovering a double standard doesn't say which of the two
standards is the right one; discovering status-quo bias doesn't mean the
status quo must be abandoned. What they reveal is that a judgment is
contingent on motive (protecting image, belonging, or comfort) rather
than being the settled, objective bedrock it feels like from the inside —
which converts a felt certainty into a starting point for further
[cognitive reframing](cognitive-reframing.md), not an immediate verdict.
