---
type: concept
title: Turnaround in One Moment
description: >
  When a harsh judgment list targets someone's character, inquiry can turn
  each label back onto yourself for one specific scene — not as a global
  verdict, but as honest mirror work in that instant.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 4"
---

A wholesale list — "selfish, cowardly, untrustworthy, violent, weak,
irresponsible, cruel, forceful, intimidating" — can skip the four
questions for time and turn to the self, worked one item at a time against
the specific moment: "In that moment with Peter, I was..."

Examples from one Father's Day conversation: *selfish* (demanding an
explanation, wanting promises kept that could no longer be kept, wanting
him responsible for her happiness — not celebrating his new life, not
asking "What is she like?"); *cowardly* (not speaking full truth — with
caveat that silence with someone violent can be wise); *untrustworthy*
(refusing to acknowledge he genuinely couldn't help himself); *violent*
(telling him she didn't accept what he said); *irresponsible* (repeatedly
asking "why" knowing there were no answers); *intimidating/forceful* (two
hours of questioning, tone of voice).

"We're just looking at a moment in time, one situation in time," not a
global character indictment. This pairs with [inquiry on specific
incidents](inquiry-on-specific-incidents.md) and [inquiry
turnarounds](inquiry-turnarounds.md). A deliberate tonal shift helps: speak
the original in a depressed tone, the turnaround energized — "He broke his
word to me!" can equal relief when you'd never have chosen that path.

Keep the "offending" person present in mind rather than trying to be rid of
them: "He's your teacher... That's what every human being is for. They
bring you enlightenment." See [close relationships as
teachers](close-relationships-as-teachers.md).
