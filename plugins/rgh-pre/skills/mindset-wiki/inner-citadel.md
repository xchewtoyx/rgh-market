Nobody is born with a steel backbone or an inner citadel — it must be
forged. The Stoic image is a fortress inside a person that no external
adversity can break down, but only if built and actively reinforced during
calm periods so it can be depended on when difficulty arrives. "We protect
our inner fortress so it may protect us."

Historical parallel: communities without stable homelands rebuilt strength
within minds — a metaphysical temple each believer could draw on wherever
dispersed. Rituals (Passover's bitter herbs, "bread of affliction," viewing
oneself as if having gone out of Egypt) access that sustaining fortitude
across generations.

Theodore Roosevelt, frail with asthma as a child, worked out daily for five
years after his father challenged him — "I'll make my body" — preparing him
for what he called the Strenuous Life: bereavement, political enemies,
defeat, war, assassination attempts. Spiritual strength is crafted through
physical exercise; physical hardiness through mental practice — *mens sana
in corpore sano*.
