title: Addiction as Attachment to Thought
description: >
  There is no addiction to an object — only attachment to an uninvestigated
  concept; inquiry targets the thought before the drink, smoke, or reach for
  relief.

"The Work deals with our thinking, not with the object that we think we're
addicted to. There is no such thing as an addiction to an object; there is
only an attachment to the uninvestigated concept arising in the moment."

Katie chain-smoked for years, quit in 1986, then eleven years later in
Turkey took a offered cigarette without hesitation and hasn't smoked since.
"It's okay if I do smoke... and it's okay if I don't." Addiction is "a
concept arises that says I should or I shouldn't smoke, I believe it, and I
move from the reality of the present moment." Key question: [Who would you
be without your should?](saying-goodbye-to-should.md)

On alcohol: if you think it makes you sick or angry, drinking is "meeting
alcohol where it is" — it does what you know it will. Inquiry isn't to stop
drinking but to end confusion about what alcohol will do. If you keep
drinking, "just notice what it does" — eventually "no fun in it — only a
hangover," without pity or victimhood.

With alcoholics, Katie finds they were "drunk with their thinking before
they were drunk with their drinking." Instruction: don't necessarily do The
Work on drinking itself — go back to the thought **just prior** to "I need a
drink" and inquire on that. Alcohol is framed as honest: it promises to get
you drunk and make things worse, and does — "a great teacher of integrity."

Combine inquiry with twelve-step sharing so you "hear it yourself" — "You
are always the one you're working with." When a parent catastrophizes a
daughter driving drunk, inquiry returns to "woman sitting in chair waiting
for her beloved daughter." Roxann later asked for help, did The Work, joined
AA, and handled problems by writing and questioning rather than drinking.
"When there's peace here, there's peace there."

Consider what you might be addicted to beyond substances — being
appreciated, attention, [being right](being-right-vs-being-free.md).
