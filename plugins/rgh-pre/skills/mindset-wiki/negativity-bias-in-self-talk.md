---
type: concept
title: Negativity Bias in Self-Talk
description: >
  Self-criticism arises faster and more readily than either neutral recall
  or self-praise, showing that the mind's default self-talk pathways are
  asymmetrically tilted toward the negative.
sources:
  - title: That Little Voice in Your Head
    resource: "That Little Voice in Your Head (Mo Gawdat), Chapter 3: Under Attack"
---

A simple response-time test makes the bias visible: asked to answer, in
order, a neutral factual question (what did you eat yesterday), a
self-critical question (name something you dislike about yourself), and a
self-praising question (name something you like about yourself), most
people answer the self-critical question fastest, the self-praising
question next, and the neutral factual question slowest. The ordering
shows that self-criticism is not merely common but is the most readily
accessible pathway in the mind — faster to reach than even simple factual
memory.

This asymmetry matters directly for anyone whose self-talk includes
impostor-style thoughts ("I don't actually deserve this," "I'll be found
out") or a fear of coming across as arrogant when stating a genuine
strength: the speed and readiness of the self-critical response is not
evidence that the self-critical content is more true or more important
than a neutral or positive alternative — it reflects which pathway is most
worn, not which one is most accurate. Which pathway is most accessible is
shaped by repetition over time, not fixed; see the related practice of
strengthening a chosen line of self-talk in [that little voice in your
head (self-talk overview)](self-talk-as-trainable-pattern.md).
