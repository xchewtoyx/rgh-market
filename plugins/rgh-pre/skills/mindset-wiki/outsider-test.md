---
type: concept
title: Outsider Test
description: >
  Asking what an uninvested outsider, dropped into the current situation
  with no history or attachment to it, would decide — used to see past the
  weight of past decisions and identity that cloud a judgment from inside.
sources:
  - title: "The Scout Mindset: Why Some People See Things Clearly and Others Don't"
    resource: "The Scout Mindset (Julia Galef), Chapter 5: Noticing Bias"
---

The test's defining question: "how would I evaluate this if it weren't my
situation — what would a replacement, with no history here, do in my
place?" A useful worked version imagines being personally teleported into
one's own current life and circumstances with no memory of, or attachment
to, the decisions that led there — freed from any need to stay consistent
with past choices or protect a sunk investment. This strips away the
specific bias of feeling obligated to continue a path just because it was
already chosen, which is otherwise very hard to see from inside the
situation.

The classic worked example: a company deeply identified with a failing
business line can ask what a newly-installed outside CEO, with no
personal history in that line, would do — often revealing an obvious
decision (abandon it) that insiders couldn't see past their own
attachment to it. Applied personally, this test is useful for any
situation where continuing feels obligatory mainly because of history
already invested (a career path, a long-running commitment, a belief held
for years) rather than because of its actual current merits — see [the
status quo bias test](status-quo-bias-test.md) for a closely related check
aimed specifically at that kind of inertia.
