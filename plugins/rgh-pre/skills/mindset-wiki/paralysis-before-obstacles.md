type: concept
title: Paralysis Before Obstacles
description: >
  Most people's default response to blocking problems is paralysis — enough
  freedom to feel you could move, enough stuckness to blame yourself for not
  following through.
sources:
  - title: "The Obstacle Is the Way: The Timeless Art of Turning Trials into Triumph, Expanded 10th Anniversary Edition"
    resource: "The Obstacle Is the Way (Ryan Holiday), ch. 3"
---

"Let's be honest: Most of us are paralyzed." Whether the obstacle is
systemic (institutions, costs, disruption) or individual (age, fear,
poverty, confidence), the emotional response is often uniform: fear,
frustration, anxiety, confusion, resentment, depression, anger, despair —
"just enough freedom to feel like you can move, just enough to feel like
it's your fault when you can't seem to follow through or build momentum."

The real culprit is usually attitude and approach, not external
circumstance alone. Countless books teach success; fewer teach how to
overcome failure or treat obstacles — so people stay stuck while others
convert obstacles into launching pads.

The antidote is a method: the [perception action will
framework](perception-action-will-framework.md). Today's obstacles are
often internal — tension, frustration, unmet expectations, learned
helplessness — even amid material abundance ("Great times are great
softeners. Abundance can be its own obstacle.").

Pair with [decision paralysis](decision-paralysis.md) when the freeze is
about choosing among paths.
