title: Welcoming Decentering
description: >
  Decentering is not merely stepping away from thoughts — it is opening to
  the whole mind-body state with a welcoming, allowing, compassionate attitude
  rather than fixing, suppressing, or dissociating from difficulty.
sources:
  - title: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition"
    resource: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression (Segal, Williams, Teasdale), ch. 3"
---

MBCT's decentering extends beyond cognitive therapy's original focus on
thoughts alone. MBSR teaches a different relationship to feelings, body
sensations, and impulses to act — the whole [emotion as entangled
package](emotion-as-entangled-package.md). **Decentering** (also called distancing):
seeing thoughts in wide enough perspective to recognize them as "simply thoughts"
rather than necessarily reflecting reality — without decentering, patients argue
with themselves over whether a thought is true and risk getting caught regardless.
CT's long-term relapse reduction may work less by changing thought *content* than
by changing patients' *relationship* to negative thoughts — repeatedly identifying
and standing back to evaluate accuracy produces a general shift from treating
thoughts as necessarily true or central to the self, to seeing them as passing
mental events. This freed relapse-prevention work to occupy cognitive "space"
otherwise filled by rumination without being restricted to content change.

The critical variable is the **mode of mind** brought to decentering:

- **Welcoming stance:** allow difficult experience, bring kindly awareness,
  adopt invitation rather than "need to solve."
- **Fixing stance:** step away to ignore, suppress, repress, avoid, or
  treat problems as enemies to eliminate — which reinforces rumination-based
  solving attempts that trap people in the state they are trying to escape.

The **fixing stance** is the therapist reflex: collaborate on untying knots,
figure out what's wrong, close the gap. MBSR instructors instead empower
participants to relate mindfully moment-by-moment and let go of the idea
that problems must be fixed. Fighting unwanted thoughts, feelings, impulses,
and sensations often creates more tension; observing the mental struggle from
a calm vantage reduces tension over time.

**Skillful alternative:** recognize the reactive urge to jump in and solve;
let go of active problem-solving; stand back with nonreactivity and kindly
awareness. Body-focused practice (scan, stretches, walking, yoga) is central
— not an extra — because negative thoughts and feelings often express in the
body, giving sensations that can be held in awareness rather than pushed away.
