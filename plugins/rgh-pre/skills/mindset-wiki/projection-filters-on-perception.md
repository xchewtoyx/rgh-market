---
type: concept
title: Projection Filters on Perception
description: >
  Habitual mental spin — dream spouse, dream job, dream colleagues — can
  color perception so strongly that you see your thoughts about people and
  situations rather than what is actually present.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 2: Waking Up"
---

Without waking to our moments, whole days or a whole life can slip past
unnoticed. A practical check: look at other people and ask whether you are
really seeing them or just your thoughts about them — "dream glasses" that
color everything with our own spin (dream children, dream spouse, dream
job, dream colleagues and friends), living in a dream present for a dream
future.

Taking off those glasses may let us see more accurately what is actually
here. Thoreau's Walden experiment was partly about this: "I went to the
woods because I wished to live deliberately, to front only the essential
facts of life... and not, when I came to die, discover that I had not lived."
His deepest conviction: "To affect the quality of the day, that is the
highest of arts.... I have never yet met a man who was quite awake."

This is one face of [habitual automaticity](habitual-automaticity.md):
perception runs through accumulated expectations rather than fresh
attention. [Mindfulness](mindfulness.md) and the question "Am I awake now?"
counter it by checking whether attention is on present experience or on
the internal movie. Related but distinct: [brain fills gaps with
assumptions](brain-fills-gaps-with-assumptions.md) describes cognitive
filling-in; projection filters describe ongoing relational and identity
narratives laid over people and roles.

**Try:** From time to time ask, "Am I awake now?" and whether you are
seeing this person or your story about them.
