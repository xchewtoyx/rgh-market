---
type: concept
title: Law of Least Effort in Thinking
description: >
  People normally avoid working-memory overload by splitting hard problems
  into easy steps and offloading intermediate results to paper or long-term
  memory rather than juggling them live.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 3"
---

Even people who think for a living rarely face tasks as demanding as
holding six digits for immediate recall, let alone rapid serial
addition — in a normal day we **conduct our mental lives by the law of least
effort**.

The usual strategy: break a complex problem into multiple easy steps;
commit intermediate results to long-term memory, notes, or a checklist
instead of an easily overloaded working memory. "We cover long distances by
taking our time."

This is adaptive, not laziness — but it explains why [task sets and
executive control](task-sets-and-executive-control.md) feel rare and costly,
and why adding friction (writing steps out, slowing down) can help when
[engaged monitoring](engaged-vs-lazy-monitoring.md) must catch a [fast
intuitive error](fast-and-slow-thinking-heuristic.md). Pair with
[willpower needs strategy](willpower-needs-strategy.md): do not stack
depleting [mental energy](mental-energy-budget.md) demands when the
environment could carry part of the load.
