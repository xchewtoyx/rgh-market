type: concept
title: The Interrogation Technique for Defusing a Fear
description: >
  A seven-question sequence — from identifying the realistic worst case
  through to weighing the cost of inaction — that converts a vague,
  amplified fear into a concrete, sized-down, actionable picture.
sources:
  - title: Solve for Happy
    resource: "Solve for Happy (Mo Gawdat), Part 2, Chapter 8: Might as Well Jump"
---

A structured sequence for working through a specific fear rather than
either avoiding it or being run by it, closely related to [the safety
reality check](safety-reality-check.md): (1) "What's the worst that can
happen?" — let the mind generate escalating scenarios, then discard the
unrealistic ones and settle on the single realistic worst case, rather
than the vaguest and most catastrophic version. (2) "So what?" — test
whether that realistic worst case is actually survivable, rather than
treating it as unthinkable by default. (3) "How likely is it?" — assess
the real probability based on actual observed experience, not on how
vivid or frightening the scenario feels. (4) "Is there anything I can do
now to prevent this?" — convert the fear into concrete preparatory action,
which both reduces the odds of the bad outcome and directly builds
confidence, since preparation is something rumination alone can't
provide. (5) "Can I recover?" — evaluate the realistic path back even if
the worst case does happen, since most feared outcomes turn out to be
survivable and not permanent. (6) "What will happen if I do nothing?" —
compare the real cost of staying stuck against the cost of confronting the
fear; a fear tends to be amplified specifically to keep this comparison
from being made honestly, and the cost of continued avoidance is often
higher than the cost of acting. (7) "What is the best-case scenario?" —
deliberately shift attention to the plausible upside as a source of
motivating energy to actually act.

This is a more complete, structured escalation than a single reframing
question — it works through likelihood, survivability, preventability,
recoverability, and the cost of avoidance in sequence, rather than
addressing only one of these at a time. It pairs directly with
[catastrophizing](catastrophizing.md) as its primary target: catastrophizing
skips straight to an amplified worst case and treats it as the expected
outcome, and this sequence is the deliberate, step-by-step antidote to
that shortcut.
