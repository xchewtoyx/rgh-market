---
type: concept
title: Drop the I Feel Like Hedge
description: >
  Softening judgment with "I feel like" hides the behavior a belief actually
  produces — inquiry names the action directly to expose cause and effect.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 4"
---

When Marisa says "I feel like I'm prying, I'm demanding," Katie has her
drop the hedge: "I would drop the 'I feel like.' ... You pry and demand."
The belief "Husbands should express what they're feeling" — tested against
reality: "what's the reality on this planet? ... basically they don't" —
produces concrete behavior, not a vague feeling-about-a-feeling.

Question 3 asks how you react *when you believe* the thought; hedges like
"I feel like" blur that link. See [linguistic tells for cognitive
distortions](linguistic-tells-for-cognitive-distortions.md) for similar
language that keeps [noticing the story your mind is
telling](noticing-the-story-your-mind-is-telling.md) at arm's length.

After 17 years, "Give me a stress-free reason to believe the thought" —
from [the four questions of inquiry](the-four-questions-of-inquiry.md) —
can yield none: "your husband is not the problem... You're the one who
believes this lie that hurts so much... How can your husband be the
problem? You're trying to alter [arguing with
reality](arguing-with-reality.md)."
