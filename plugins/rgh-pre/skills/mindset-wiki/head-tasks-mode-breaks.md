title: Head Tasks Mode Breaks
description: >
  Thinking-heavy work resists continuous mindfulness — periodic sensory
  check-ins and breathing-space pauses prevent slipping too far into driven
  doing mode during deadline pressure.
sources:
  - title: "The Mindful Way through Depression: Freeing Yourself from Chronic Unhappiness"
    resource: "The Mindful Way through Depression (Williams, Teasdale, Segal, Kabat-Zinn), ch. 10"
---

Much work is "head" tasks — thinking, planning, writing under deadline — which
resist staying present. Even respected teachers find mindfulness harder when
writing than when binding books by hand. The difficulty is **inherent to the
task type**, not a personal deficiency.

**Countermeasure:** break up head tasks periodically — every half hour or so,
a minute of sensory awareness: mindful walk, body movement, cool air,
birdsong. Widen awareness to the world around you to keep from slipping too
far from [being mode](doing-mode-being-mode.md).

Mode breaks can include standing, gentle stretching, breath focus, sensing
soles and groundedness. Linger longer on Step 1 of the [three-minute breathing
space](three-minute-breathing-space.md) during work — noticing thoughts,
feelings, bodily sensations — rather than rushing to steps 2–3. What surfaces
may shock: discontent, "I don't want this — I want that," tension and
[aversion](aversion.md) — but also the beginnings of compassion for unrecognized
pain.

The [doing mind](driven-doing-mode.md) constantly computes the gap between
current state and desired state; comparing your actual work state to the
peaceful state you long for generates further unhappiness — a form of craving.
See [letting go of happiness as goal](letting-go-of-happiness-as-goal.md).

Larry Rosenberg's daily mindfulness steps apply here: when possible do one
thing; pay full attention; when the mind wanders, bring it back; repeat
billions of times; investigate distractions.
