---
type: concept
title: Learning Orientation vs. Proving Orientation
description: >
  Fixed and growth mindsets define success, failure, and effort
  differently — one frames every situation as a test to pass or fail, the
  other frames the same situation as a chance to develop.
sources:
  - title: "Mindset: The New Psychology of Success"
    resource: "Mindset: The New Psychology of Success (Carol S. Dweck), Chapter 2: Inside the Mindsets"
---

The two mindsets amount to living in two different "worlds" with different
definitions of the same words. Under a [fixed
mindset](fixed-mindset.md): success means proving you're smart or
talented (validating a fixed trait); failure means a setback that reveals
you're not; effort is a bad sign, because needing to try hard implies you
lack the innate ability that would have made it easy. Under a [growth
mindset](growth-mindset.md): success means stretching yourself to learn
something new (developing); failure means not growing or not yet
achieving something, rather than a verdict; effort is what produces
ability, not a confession of its absence — "I don't divide the world into
the weak and the strong ... I divide the world into the learners and
nonlearners."

This shows up concretely in what people choose when given the option:
offered an easy, already-mastered task or a harder unfamiliar one, a
proving orientation sticks with the easy task (nothing to lose), while a
learning orientation seeks out the harder one precisely because it's not
yet mastered. It also shows up in what people pay attention to after
getting something wrong: a proving orientation tunes out information that
would help correct the error once the right/wrong verdict is in, while a
learning orientation keeps attending to it. See [the power of
yet](the-power-of-yet.md) for the specific reframe that operationalizes a
learning orientation when facing something not yet mastered, and [fixed
mindset](fixed-mindset.md) / [growth mindset](growth-mindset.md) for the
underlying beliefs driving each orientation.
