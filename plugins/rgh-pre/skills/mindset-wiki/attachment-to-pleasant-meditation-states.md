---
type: concept
title: Attachment to Pleasant Meditation States
description: >
  Clinging to calm or "another world" feelings during meditation sets up
  frustration when they end — the task is the same regardless: be aware of
  whatever is present, then return to the anchor.
sources:
  - title: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition"
    resource: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression (Segal, Williams, Teasdale), ch. 11"
---

Pleasant settled states during sitting show something is happening — but they
will not always come. Sometimes practice feels bad, boring, or frustrating; that
is still meditation.

Getting hooked on recreating a pleasant state — anger when interrupted "like a
child whose ice cream was snatched" — is **attachment** in the
[feeling tone and reactivity](feeling-tone-and-reactivity.md) sense. The effort
to cling turns the pleasant into a source of frustration.

The task is identical in all conditions: be aware of whatever is present, then
return to the breath. Practice offers something beyond chasing ups and downs.
Notice similar attachment in daily life when pleasant states come and go — what
feelings arise as you try to hold on?

Pairs with [non-striving in meditation](non-striving-in-meditation.md) and
[attachment to comfort](attachment-to-comfort.md) — a different but related
clinging pattern in life choices rather than on the cushion.
