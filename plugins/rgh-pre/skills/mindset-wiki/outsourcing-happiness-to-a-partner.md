---
type: concept
title: Outsourcing Happiness to a Partner
description: >
  Believing a spouse or partner is supposed to make you happy puts you in
  their business, prisons them in a fantasy role, and blocks self-acceptance
  that no external person can supply.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 9 (Charles dialogue)"
---

"I get into some heavy 'shoulds' about what a beloved soulmate should think
and feel about me" — carried for a whole life. When a partner says they
are repulsed, the pain often tracks not only the words but the belief that
wives are supposed to see husbands as not repulsive. That belief
"two-dimensionalizes" her: "Why are you being that way with me? Don't you
see who I am?" — which is itself a prison.

Whose business is repulsion? Yours when you feel it; hers when she feels
it. "What happens when you're mentally in her business? Separation." If
you couldn't stop your own repulsion at something you witnessed, how can
she be required to stop hers at you?

Turnarounds surface the mirror: "I'm angry at myself because I told
Deborah she repulses me — for her willingness to dispose of the
relationship so easily." "There's no way she can't be a mirror image of
your thinking. There is no one out there but your story." The partner you
want to live with may be "someone who doesn't exist, the woman I want her
to be."

"I want her to be grateful for life as it is" → "I want me to be grateful
for life as it is." "How can a person who doesn't know how to be happy
teach someone how to be happy?" Giving money as sole breadwinner to buy
love, approval, and acceptance you couldn't give yourself is "a price tag,"
not a gift — "the receiving is experienced in the moment you give something
away... What comes back is none of your business."

When a partner leaves, the pain may be "the death of your mythology" — a
Donna Reed picture of family togetherness, not the departure itself. "I
need me to love myself as I am, warts and all." Without the story: "A
strong, talented, sexy, powerful man" — "Welcome to the power of
ownership. No one can touch that. Not even you."
