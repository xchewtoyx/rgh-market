---
type: concept
title: Inviting Difficulty In Practice
description: >
  A formal MBCT exercise deliberately brings a moderate difficulty to mind
  and investigates it through body sensations with welcoming awareness —
  aiming for spaciousness around the feeling, not elimination of it.
sources:
  - title: "The Mindful Way through Depression: Freeing Yourself from Chronic Unhappiness"
    resource: "The Mindful Way through Depression (Williams, Teasdale, Segal, Kabat-Zinn), ch. 7"
---

After settling on breath and widening to the whole body, bring to mind a
difficulty you don't mind staying with briefly — a misunderstanding, argument,
or situation involving anger, regret, or guilt (present or past).

Tune into physical sensations the thought evokes. Direct attention to the region
of strongest sensation "in the gesture of an embrace, a welcoming." Breathe into
that region on the in-breath, out from it on the out-breath; explore how
intensity shifts moment to moment. Periodically: "It's okay for me to feel this;
whatever it is, it's already here." "Softening" or "opening" on each out-breath.
Saying it's okay is not endorsing the original situation — it helps awareness stay
open to bodily sensations now. Optionally hold sensations and breath together.
When sensations no longer pull strongly, return to breath as primary object.

**What changes:** tension may become spacious — difficulty "still there" but
smaller, with room to breathe around it (solid rock shrinking to a small stone).
Mindfulness is not about getting rid of difficult states; the intention is relating
so they don't cause getting stuck in unhappiness. Awareness touching a thought or
feeling can make it dissipate like a soap bubble — but deliberately trying to get
rid of it may get you more stuck.

**Pacing:** gaining confidence to let down defenses takes time; severe trauma may
need a protected therapeutic environment. Each person chooses their own pace for
[working the edge](working-the-edge.md).

The path of honesty and openness — treating difficulty like a sick child needing
tender holding — runs counter to self-preservation instincts yet may be the only
path offering genuine healing. Suspension of judgment lets you see fear without
object, aching emptiness, or supposedly constant dull ache ebbing and flowing.
Focusing on physicality helps recognize your distinctive aversion pattern faster:
"Oh, here you are again."

Pairs with [allowing and letting be](allowing-letting-be.md) and [approach
versus avoidance in emotions](approach-versus-avoidance-in-emotions.md).
