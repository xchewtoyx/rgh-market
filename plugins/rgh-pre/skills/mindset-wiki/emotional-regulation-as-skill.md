title: Emotional Regulation as Skill
description: >
  Emotional regulation is recognizing, understanding, and managing your
  reactions — a capacity that varies by neurotype and is shaped by culture,
  not a moral score of how "calm" you appear.
sources:
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Ellie Middleton), ch. 4"
---

Emotional regulation is "the ability to recognize, understand and effectively
manage your emotions and reactions to different situations" — exerting
control over one's emotional state, which may include rethinking a
challenging situation, hiding visible sadness, or focusing on reasons to
feel calm.

Differences can show as rapid mood swings, trouble self-soothing, spiralling
from small setbacks, intense sobbing, difficulty focusing on anything but
the emotion, or reactions others deem "out of proportion." The same
intensity can make positive emotions vividly good — joy from music, pride at
loved ones' achievements, excitement over "small" things.

Apparent absence of expected reaction also counts: not crying at sad events
(may need longer to process), not smiling when happy, not responding to
others' emotions in expected ways, or [alexithymia](alexithymia-as-emotional-blindspot.md)
— difficulty identifying one's own feelings. These are differences, not
deficits to shame — see [no bad feelings](no-bad-feelings.md) and [outward
emotion is valid](outward-emotion-is-valid.md).

Regulation techniques often interrupt automatic spiralling (breathing,
present-moment attention, questioning thoughts) — the goal is manageability,
not eradicating emotion.
