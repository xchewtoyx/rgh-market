---
type: concept
title: Emotion as Decision Input
description: >
  Decisiveness and follow-through depend on felt emotion informing choices,
  not logic alone — impaired access to emotion removes a needed input to
  practical decision-making.
sources:
  - title: "The Unmasking Workbook for Autistic Adults"
    resource: "The Unmasking Workbook for Autistic Adults (Jessica Penot), ch. 9"
---

Antonio Damasio's case of "Elliot" — frontal-lobe damage left IQ, memory,
and reasoning intact but destroyed the capacity to feel and understand
emotion. He could not make basic decisions, complete projects, hold a job,
or sustain marriage. The lesson: capability comes from balance between
reason and emotion, not from logic operating in isolation.

This complements [emotional bias in judgment](emotional-bias-in-judgment.md),
which warns that stated reasons often rationalize prior feelings. Here the
point is the opposite pole: without accessible feeling, decisions stall or
misfire because a necessary signal is missing. [Alexithymia as emotional
blindspot](alexithymia-as-emotional-blindspot.md) may partly explain
executive-function difficulties when people substitute exhausting
reasoning-for-feeling and still get it wrong.

**Practice:** track and acknowledge feelings daily, then deliberately factor
emotion into decisions alongside logic — not to override reasoning, but to
restore the input stream decisions normally draw on. Daily feelings tracking
against your own stressors, burnout, and meltdown phases builds the habit over
time.
