---
type: concept
title: Approach Versus Avoidance in Emotions
description: >
  Sensing bodily tensing and bracing signals the brain's avoidance pattern;
  mindfulness counters with curiosity, compassion, and welcoming awareness —
  bathing difficulty in open acceptance like a parent embracing a suffering child.
sources:
  - title: "The Mindful Way through Depression: Freeing Yourself from Chronic Unhappiness"
    resource: "The Mindful Way through Depression (Williams, Teasdale, Segal, Kabat-Zinn), ch. 7"
---

Unpleasant feelings trigger threat-warning systems that demand priority
attention. Distraction does not shut the alarm; disturbing thoughts return.
The alternative is not fighting the alarm but changing **how** we attend:
turn and face difficulty with open, spacious, affectionate attention to
feelings as they show up in the body.

The automatic sequence: [driven-doing mode](driven-doing-mode.md) dominated
by worry, memory-digging, and [doing-mode rumination](doing-mode-rumination.md)
— which registers as further unpleasantness and triggers another aversive
cycle. Body-located awareness of the **attempt to push away** unpleasant
experience opens breathing room between detecting "unpleasant" and the
near-instantaneous aversive reaction.

**Avoiding** shuts off from experience; **approaching** stays open to it.
Bodily tensing signals avoidance mode switching on; approach qualities —
curiosity, compassion, goodwill — balance avoidance with a welcoming pattern.

This is not striving for an ideal of happiness in difficulty. It is bathing
the situation and even aversion to it in open, compassionate awareness —
"we welcome them in, as they are already here anyway." Unpleasant experiences
are passing mental events — bundles of bodily sensations, feelings, and
thoughts — greeted with interest rather than dread.

See [working the edge](working-the-edge.md) for graded contact with discomfort
and [welcoming decentering](welcoming-decentering.md) for the allowing stance.
