---
type: concept
title: Contingency Plan Trap
description: >
  Trying to outsmart every disaster with a perfect escape plan often
  increases anxiety while ignoring what is actually possible — humility
  plus clear action beats fear-driven control.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 12"
---

"I need a contingency plan" — not knowable as true; "kind of a relief."
"You can't outsmart reality. Where you are, right now, might be the
safest place in the world. We just don't know." Without the thought: lighter,
though grief for what happened can surface — "that's humility. For me,
that's a sweet thing."

Walking a plan to absurdity (evacuate extended family, five jobs for a
helicopter, tunnels closed anyway) shows: "To make a contingency plan
work... you need to be psychic." Fury at family for not helping becomes
fury at yourself — "I'm judging them, and I can't even drive."

Proactive ability mixed with humility "can be clear and helpful." Pen
analogy: in an emergency you reach for what you need without fear —
"Fear isn't so efficient. Fear is blind and deaf." Practical steps (meeting
place if phones fail, learning to drive) can follow from a calm mind rather
than terror. Children learn from you whether they must always be on guard.
See [safety reality check](safety-reality-check.md) and [inner
terrorism](inner-terrorism.md).

Reframe charged images: an ash-covered man walking home may be "doing
better than you were" — mask, briefcase, heading to family — not a zombie
heralding apocalypse. "Reality and the story never match; reality's always
[kinder than your stories](reality-kinder-than-your-stories.md)."
