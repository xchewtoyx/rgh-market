---
type: concept
title: Stop Judging Them for Judging You
description: >
  Complaining that others judge you while you judge them for it keeps the
  same pattern running — inquiry on your side of the exchange has to come
  first before any conversation about their behavior can land.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Loving What Is (Byron Katie), ch. 4"
---

"My parents aren't supposed to judge me" collapses on contact with reality:
"that's their job... Everything's a judgment. 'It's a sky' — that's a
judgment. That's what we do." People should stop judging people? "What
planet do you think you're on?"

The practical rule from inquiry on family conflict: when you stop judging
them for judging you, *then* go talk to them about judgment. "When you stop
doing what you want them to stop doing, then you can talk to them." Turn
the statement: "I'm confused and saddened by me because I judge myself" and
"...because I judge my parents and my family."

This is [staying in your own business](staying-in-your-own-business.md)
applied to meta-judgment: the distress isn't only that they judge — it's
that you believe they shouldn't while you judge them for it. See [being
right versus being free](being-right-vs-being-free.md) when the should-
statement is absorbing years of rebellion and distance.
