---
type: concept
title: Autistic Burnout as Wellbeing Deficit
description: >
  Autistic burnout is the collapse that follows running a personal economy
  of wellbeing in chronic deficit — depleting activities outpacing
  replenishing ones until even ordinary demands exceed capacity.
sources:
  - title: "Knowing Why: Adult-Diagnosed Autistic People on Life and Autism"
    resource: "Knowing Why (ASAN), ch. 2: Erin Human, Emerging From Burnout"
---

All humans have a personal economy of wellbeing: activities that replenish
(sleep, food, hobbies, rest) versus activities that deplete (stress,
illness, poor sleep, hunger, social strain). Autistic people are more
likely than neurotypicals to run on deficit for extended periods because
environments are often ill-suited to their needs — chronic sensory
overload or deprivation, social situations that confound natural abilities,
and executive-functioning demands with too little support amount to living
beyond one's means. Stacking ordinary life pressures (high-stress work or
school, illness, financial strain, dysfunctional relationships) on top
makes burnout almost inevitable.

Burnout can arrive as a slow, imperceptible slide rather than a sudden
crash — permanent stripped-down survival mode that never looks urgent
enough to trigger support, even while capacity erodes. The crystallizing
moment may be something as small as a single load of laundry undoing
someone who has been doing "all the usual things" (doctor visits, diet,
sleep effort, mood trackers, meditation, socializing, exercise).

Recovery requires tipping the economy back toward surplus — not just
adding generic self-care but identifying what actually replenishes for
this person (see [deep focus as restoration](deep-focus-as-restoration.md),
[misapplied self-care advice](misapplied-self-care-advice.md)). The
pattern often traces to [masking as a burnout
mechanism](masking-as-burnout-mechanism.md): years of pushing beyond
neurological boundaries to meet neurotypical demands.
