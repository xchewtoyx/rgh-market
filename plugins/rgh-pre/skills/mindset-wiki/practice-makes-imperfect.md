---
type: concept
title: Practice Makes Imperfect
description: >
  Rigid thinking and unhealthy emotions can recur despite best efforts —
  expecting perfect healthy thinking sets you up to fail; setbacks are
  opportunities to learn, not proof that change failed.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition (Branch, Willson), ch. 20"
---

Regular practice of new healthy thinking minimizes relapse risk but never
produces a perfectly healthy thinker — you are only human. Perfectionism
about your own thinking is itself a trap.

Give yourself permission to make mistakes with new beliefs. When conviction
wanes or old patterns resurface, treat it as normal rather than catastrophic.
A [positive data log](positive-data-log.md) reminds you that setbacks need
only be temporary while new beliefs are bedding down.

Facing doubts directly (via [zigzag technique](zigzag-technique.md)) rather
than suppressing them prevents private reservations from resurfacing under
stress and triggering full relapse to old beliefs and avoidant behavior.
