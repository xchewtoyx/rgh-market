type: concept
title: Reducing Demands Over Trying Harder
description: >
  Recovery from burnout often requires doing less, not pushing harder —
  unlearning the belief that more effort will erase disability and
  treating guilt over rest as internalized ableism.
sources:
  - title: "Knowing Why: Adult-Diagnosed Autistic People on Life and Autism"
    resource: "Knowing Why (ASAN), ch. 2: Erin Human, Emerging From Burnout"
---

A lifelong habit of overcompensation — born from [masking as a burnout
mechanism](masking-as-burnout-mechanism.md) — makes "do less" feel
morally wrong even when it is the only sustainable path. Drive and
perfectionism that others label ambition may be adaptations to an
invisible disability, carrying a push-until-crash-then-push-harder cycle.

The single most important recovery adjustment is often reducing the
number and intensity of self-imposed demands. Guilt over "unproductive"
time is [internalized ableism](internalized-ableism-guilt.md): the
harshness applied to oneself would never be applied to a friend in the
same position. Genuine acceptance — "I am disabled, in fact" — counters
the belief that trying harder could make the disability go away.

Recovery is nonlinear and relapse-prone: regained energy tempts a return
to old achievement-seeking (over-committing, filling days with busyness).
A workable roadmap includes recognizing overdoing-it signs earlier,
deliberately resting after socializing, carrying sensory tools everywhere,
and repeating that disability is real as an ongoing corrective.
