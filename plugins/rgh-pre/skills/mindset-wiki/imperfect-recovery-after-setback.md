type: concept
title: Imperfect Recovery After Setback
description: >
  Missing your own standards is human and inevitable — the move is to get
  back up, celebrate behaving like a human however imperfectly, and continue
  the pursuit without exasperation.
sources:
  - title: "Discipline Is Destiny: The Power of Self-Control"
    resource: "Discipline Is Destiny (Ryan Holiday), ch. 15"
---

"Not all of us can always be Jackie Robinson — not even Jackie Robinson
was always Jackie Robinson." Everyone admired has lost their temper, hit
snooze, fallen into bad habits, failed as spouse, neighbor, or parent.
Witnessing such moments in someone else, we'd reassure rather than berate —
the question is whether we extend that grace to ourselves.

Marcus Aurelius reminded himself not to "feel exasperated, or defeated, or
despondent because your days aren't packed with wise and moral actions,"
but to "get back up when you fail, to celebrate behaving like a human —
however imperfectly — and fully embrace the pursuit." Failure and mistakes
are inevitable; the practice is return, not perfection.

This operationalizes [friend to yourself](friend-to-yourself.md) and
[self-discipline without self-hatred](self-discipline-without-self-
hatred.md). Pairs with [acting despite negative
feelings](acting-despite-negative-feelings.md) and [reducing demands over
trying harder](reducing-demands-over-trying-harder.md) when recovery
requires rest rather than another self-attack.
