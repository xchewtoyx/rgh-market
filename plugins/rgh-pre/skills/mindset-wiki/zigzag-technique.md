---
type: concept
title: Zigzag Technique for Belief Strengthening
description: >
  To deepen conviction in a new healthy belief, deliberately attack it with
  your own doubts, then redefend it — alternating until attacks are exhausted
  and always ending on a defense, never on an attack.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition (Branch, Willson), ch. 20"
---

After formulating a helpful alternative belief, intellectual agreement often
outruns gut conviction — the [head-to-heart problem](head-to-heart-problem.md).
The zigzag technique plays devil's advocate with yourself:

1. Write the belief you want to strengthen; rate conviction 0–100%. Confirm
   it is true, logical, and helpful.
2. Write doubts, reservations, and attacks using every unhealthy argument
   that comes to mind.
3. Dispute the attack and redefend the healthy belief — stay focused on
   defense, not sidetracked by attack specifics.
4. Repeat attack and defense until attacks are exhausted. **Always stop on
   a defense**, never on an attack.
5. Rerate conviction. If little gain, revisit instructions or review with a
   therapist to spot where you "zigged when you should have zagged."

Suppressing doubts ("swept under the rug") lets them resurface in stressful
moments and trigger relapse to old beliefs — facing misgivings directly is
part of [practice makes imperfect](practice-makes-imperfect.md) belief change.

[positive data log](positive-data-log.md).
