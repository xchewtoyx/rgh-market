title: Noticing the Story Your Mind Is Telling
description: >
  Explicitly naming the narrative interpretation the mind has spun around
  an event — and treating it as a constructed story rather than the event
  itself — loosens its grip without needing to argue about whether it's
  true.
sources:

---

After an event (an awkward interaction, a mistake), the mind automatically
generates a narrative interpretation of what it means ("I am socially
awkward," "I always mess this up") that gets experienced as simply
describing what happened rather than as one constructed reading among
several. Explicitly asking "what is the story my mind is currently
weaving?" reframes the narrative as an object being observed rather than
an unquestionable fact — the same effect [thought
defusion](thought-defusion.md) produces for a single thought, applied
here to a whole interpretive storyline built around an event.

A useful extension: imagining the narrative as a movie or book and asking
what its title would be ("The Time I Embarrassed Myself Forever," "Proof
I'm Not Cut Out for This") makes its constructed, dramatized quality more
visible — a title makes clear that a specific narrative shape has been
imposed on the raw event, which is easier to notice as an interpretation
once it has a name. This doesn't require disputing whether any part of the
story is true; naming it as a story is often sufficient to loosen its
automatic grip on how the event is being processed.

In Byron Katie's framing, a "story" is any sequence of thoughts we convince
ourselves is real — about past, present, or future, about what should or
could be, or why things are the way they are. Stories are untested,
uninvestigated theories about what events mean, appearing constantly (someone
leaves a room without a word, a boss calls you in). Operating on them
without awareness is "the dream." Small uninvestigated stories breed bigger
ones; bigger ones breed major theories about life being terrible or
dangerous. The central question when a story is painful: who would you be
without it? Testing stories rather than living inside them is what [inquiry
as thought-testing](inquiry-as-thought-testing.md) is for.
