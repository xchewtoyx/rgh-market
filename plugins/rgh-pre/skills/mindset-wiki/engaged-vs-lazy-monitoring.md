---
type: concept
title: Engaged Versus Lazy Monitoring
description: >
  Many intuitive errors persist not because the person lacks ability but
  because they stop at the first plausible answer — engaged thinking means
  staying skeptical of your own quick impressions long enough to check them.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 4"
---

One job of effortful thinking is to monitor suggestions from automatic
thinking — letting some through, modifying or blocking others. Failures of
that monitoring show up in puzzles with compelling wrong answers: the
bat-and-ball problem ($1.10 total, bat costs $1 more than ball — 10¢ feels
right but is wrong), invalid syllogisms endorsed because the conclusion
feels plausible, or answering "Michigan" murder-rate questions without
retrieving that Detroit is in Michigan.

The shared failure is often insufficient motivation — stopping at a
superficially plausible answer instead of spending a few seconds checking.
People who avoid that trap are more alert, more skeptical of their own
intuitions, and more willing to invest mild effort. High raw intelligence
does not confer immunity: many capable students still give the intuitive
wrong answer on bat-and-ball items.

This is distinct from IQ-style algorithmic skill. What matters for catching
your own distorted thinking is closer to [judgment limited by attitude more
than knowledge](attitude-over-knowledge-in-judgment.md) — a standing
willingness to look — plus the habit of pausing on quick answers long
enough to verify them. Low scores on brief "cognitive reflection" puzzles
correlate with more impulsive choices and present bias, suggesting the same
supervisory laziness shows up outside the lab.

When a judgment feels instant and confident, treat that as a cue to ask
whether you actually answered the hard question or a substituted easier one
(see [heuristic substitution](heuristic-substitution.md)), especially under
[high-stakes bias vigilance](high-stakes-bias-vigilance.md).
