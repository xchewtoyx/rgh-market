The Stoic split — ta eph'hemin, ta ouk eph'hemin: what is up to us, what
is not — maps the playing field. Up to you: emotions (in the sense of how
you meet them), judgments, creativity, attitude, perspective, desires,
decisions, determination. Not up to you: weather, economy, others'
choices, trends, disasters, whether someone funds your company or steals
your idea first.

To complain, argue, or give up about the second category is itself a
choice — usually one that burns energy without moving you. Tommy John's
career turned on asking, again and again, "Is there a chance? Is there
something I can do?" — any provisional yes got full effort; impossible
was parsed from merely unlikely.

The Serenity Prayer and [acceptance versus
resignation](acceptance-vs-resignation.md) share the same structure: accept
the fixed, act on the open. Focusing exclusively on what you can influence
magnifies effective power; every ounce directed at what you cannot change
is self-indulgent waste. Pair with [agency over
interpretation](agency-over-interpretation.md) for the inner half of the
split and [staying in your own business](staying-in-your-own-business.md)
when distress comes from controlling others' business.
