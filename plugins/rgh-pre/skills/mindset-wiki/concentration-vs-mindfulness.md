---
type: concept
title: Concentration Versus Mindfulness
description: >
  One-pointed concentration (samadhi) steadies the mind but is incomplete
  without mindfulness — absorption without inquiry can become withdrawal
  rather than full wakeful engagement with life.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 3"
---

**Concentration** (*samadhi*) is sustaining unwavering attention on one object
— classically the breath — continually returning when the mind wanders. Deep
concentration brings steadfast calm, absorption, and peace; it is the foundation
without which [mindfulness](mindfulness.md) "will not be very strong."

But concentration alone can be closed rather than open — trancelike rather
than fully awake — missing curiosity, inquiry, and engagement with the full
range of experience. Attachment to stillness as refuge from life's messiness
"arrests development and short-circuits wisdom." Mindfulness puts calm,
stable mind in service of understanding interconnectedness.

**Vision** sustains daily practice: a map truly your own — values, first
principles, what you honor — renewed each day because mindfulness requires
awareness of purpose. Practice embodies the vision rather than forcing calm
or kindness. On anger: mindfulness can reveal its toxicity even when you are
"objectively on high ground"; transmuted to forcefulness and wisdom without
self-righteous smoke, its power to transform multiplies — awareness "larger
than the anger" can hold it like a [pot](mindfulness-pot-for-difficult-feelings.md).

Meditation (*bhavana*, "development through mental training") is human maturation
work — facing shadow aspects, repetitive inner labor like "bucketing out the
pond," tempering through heat rather than avoiding darkness.
