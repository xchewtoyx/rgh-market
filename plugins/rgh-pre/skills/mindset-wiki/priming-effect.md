---
type: concept
title: Priming Effect
description: >
  Recent exposure to a word, idea, or symbol temporarily makes related
  thoughts and behaviors easier to evoke — often without conscious awareness.
sources:
  - title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 5"
---

Priming: exposure to a stimulus changes the ease of evoking related material.
Seeing EAT makes SO_P more likely completed as SOUP than SOAP; seeing WASH
reverses it. Priming speeds recognition of blurred or quiet words and
spreads weakly through the associative network (EAT → fork, hungry, diet,
cookie) — "like ripples on a pond."

Priming extends beyond words to actions and emotions without awareness —
the Florida effect: scrambled sentences with elderly-associated words (Florida,
forgetful, bald, gray, wrinkle) led students to walk more slowly afterward,
even though "old" never appeared. Reading about the experiment can slightly
slow your own rising from a chair.

Real-world primes: polling stations in schools increased support for school
funding; money primes increase self-reliance and selfishness (less help,
more distance). Cultures saturated with money, status, or authority symbols
may shape behavior via priming people never notice.

Disbelief is not an option — effects are robust if not always large. See
[ideomotor effect](ideomotor-effect.md) and [illusion of
truth](illusion-of-truth.md) for related automatic influences.
