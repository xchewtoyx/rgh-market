description: >
  Before starting, imagine the project has already failed and name why —
  rehearsal that turns surprise into preparation and probes plan weaknesses
  while you can still fix them.
sources:
  - title: "The Obstacle Is the Way: The Timeless Art of Turning Trials into Triumph, Expanded 10th Anniversary Edition"
    resource: "The Obstacle Is the Way (Ryan Holiday), ch. 29"

Psychologist Gary Klein's **premortem** inverts the postmortem: on the eve
of a major launch, the leader announces "The project has failed
spectacularly. Tell me what went wrong" — *before* work begins. The Stoics
called the same move **premeditatio malorum** (premeditation of evils):
before travel, a speech, or policy, rehearse the plan, then walk through
what could go wrong — storm, ill captain, pirates, booing audience, delays.

Seneca: "Nothing happens to the wise man against his expectation... Nor
do all things turn out for him as he wished but as he reckoned — and above
all he reckoned that something could block his plans." A military rule: it
is unacceptable for a leader to say "I did not think that would happen."
If it can happen, assume it will; be fitted for defeat or victory.

**Why it helps:** "The worst thing that can happen is not something going
wrong but something going wrong and catching you by surprise." Unexpected
failure discourages; rehearsed failure does not. Pleasant surprise beats
unpleasant surprise. When nothing can be done about a "what if," manage
expectations: "It will suck but we'll be okay. We'll endure it."

Run the chain: What if X → Then I will Y / Instead I'll just Y / No problem,
we can always Y. Anticipation does not make things easier but prepares you
for how hard they actually are — like training on hills before a flat race.
"Chimeras are like bandages — they hurt only when torn away"; building
expectations only in imagination sets up disappointment.

Pair with [fear-setting](fear-setting.md), [prepare for none of it to
work](prepare-for-none-of-it-to-work.md), and [contingency plan
trap](contingency-plan-trap.md). Being called a pessimist matters less than
being blindsided.
