title: Safety Reality Check
description: >
  A structured set of questions for testing whether a present fear is
  tracking an actual current threat or an amplified, outdated, or
  statistically unlikely one.
sources:
  - title: That Little Voice in Your Head
    resource: "That Little Voice in Your Head (Mo Gawdat), Chapter 3: Under Attack"
  - title: Solve for Happy
    resource: "Solve for Happy (Mo Gawdat), Part 2, Chapter 8: Might as Well Jump"
---

When [aversion](aversion.md) or [catastrophizing](catastrophizing.md) is
running, a structured self-interrogation can separate real danger from
manufactured fear: Is there a literal physical threat present right now?
How lethal is the feared outcome actually, compared to how it's being
treated? What are the real statistical odds of it happening, versus the
odds implied by how much attention or media coverage it gets? How have
other people who faced this same feared outcome actually fared — did most
survive or even come out ahead? Has this particular long-feared thing ever
actually happened before, despite years of fearing it? Did the fear form
in a different life context that no longer applies (see [conditioning and
context mismatch](conditioning-and-context-mismatch.md))? Do other people
in a similar situation share this level of fear — and if not, why not?
Is a protective resource already in place (savings, support, a fallback
plan) that the fear is ignoring?

The check does not argue that nothing is ever dangerous — it isolates
whether the current fear response is proportionate to a real, present
threat or whether it has been inherited, amplified, or extrapolated well
past what the situation warrants.
