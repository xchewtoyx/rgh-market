---
type: concept
title: Judgment Is Limited by Attitude More Than Knowledge
description: >
  Knowing about biases and reasoning errors does little to improve
  judgment on its own — what actually determines whether someone catches
  their own distorted thinking is their underlying willingness to look for
  it in themselves.
sources:
  - title: "The Scout Mindset: Why Some People See Things Clearly and Others Don't"
    resource: "The Scout Mindset (Julia Galef), Introduction"
---

Knowing that assumptions should be tested, or being able to name a list of
cognitive biases, doesn't automatically translate into better judgment —
in the same way that knowing exercise is healthy doesn't automatically
make someone fit. The missing ingredient is not information but attitude:
a genuine, standing willingness to notice a bias operating in one's own
current reasoning, rather than only recognizing it in hindsight or in
other people's reasoning.

This reframes self-talk work and bias-countering techniques generally:
having a technique like [cognitive reframing](cognitive-reframing.md) or
[testing a limiting belief](testing-a-limiting-belief.md) available is not
the same as being disposed to actually apply it to oneself in the moment
it matters. The deeper target for change is the underlying motivation —
wanting to see a situation accurately even when an inaccurate but more
comfortable read is available — which the specific techniques then serve,
rather than replace.
