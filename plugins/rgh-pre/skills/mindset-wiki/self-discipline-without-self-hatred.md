---
type: concept
title: Self-Discipline Without Self-Hatred
description: >
  High standards need not mean whipping yourself — discipline is strictness
  toward behavior, not permission to talk to yourself in ways you'd reject
  from anyone else.
sources:
  - title: "Discipline Is Destiny: The Power of Self-Control"
    resource: "Discipline Is Destiny (Ryan Holiday), ch. 15"
---

Cleanthes stopped a stranger berating himself in Athens: "Remember, you're
not talking to a bad man." The point of self-discipline is strictness toward
standards, not self-hatred — hold the bar high without accepting excuses,
but do not despise yourself when you miss. We slip into negative self-talk
("You suck. You screwed up. You blew it.") we'd never accept from someone
else and would never say to a friend.

Thoreau: "It is hard to have a Southern overseer, it is worse to have a
Northern one; but worst of all when you are the slave-driver of yourself."
Seneca: no school is kindlier or more loving of humankind — "but of all
people," and that includes you. His measure of philosophical progress:
"I have begun to be a friend to myself."

Treat yourself as you would a friend — reassurance and advice, not
admonishment; never let a friend call themselves worthless or give up. This
is instrumentally useful: it's what lets you pull yourself out of the depths
and back onto the road. "It is an act of self-discipline to be kind to the
self... Don't beat yourself up. Build yourself up."
