  Repetitive self-stimulation that feels pleasant and reduces stress but

  can flag arousal even when emotion is not consciously named.

Stereotypical behaviours — body rocking, hand flapping, leg-shaking,
finger-spelling, dancing alone, and other forms of stimming — feel pleasant
and reduce stress. Stimming spans every sense: movement (twirling,
head-banging, finger-flicking, skin-picking), visual (eye-fluttering,
staring at bright lights), smell, taste, posture, touch, sound (humming),
and more. The common thread is *that* a person stims, not *how*. Late-
identified autistics often deny stimming while visibly doing it.

For state reading, stimming is a **signal channel**, not only a social
concern:

- Increased or changed stimming can mark rising arousal or sensory load
  even when the person does not label anxiety consciously.
- Stimming is regulatory — it can prevent a meltdown or intensify a
  positive feeling; it helps some people "stay in this world" (attention,
  thinking, emotional wellbeing). Neurotypical fidgeting may aid attention
  but does not appear to provide the same emotional-regulation function.
- Stimming often correlates in time with environmental triggers — noise,
  temperature change, crowds — making incident logging useful for root-cause
  tracing alongside [sensory profile mapping](sensory-profile-mapping.md).
- In socially accepting environments, stimming needs may subside over time;
  a drop in stimming can mean lower load, not that the underlying need
  vanished.

**Suppression costs.** Forcing stillness removes a genuinely valuable
regulatory tool. People trained to sit perfectly still may channel the
pent-up need elsewhere — e.g. mistaking nicotine addiction for chemical
dependence when the real need was to stim. Holding the body still "not by
relaxing, but by constant force" can sever hunger/fullness and other
[interoceptive](interoception.md) signals until movement is restored.
Suppressing stims to look acceptable can increase load and pull attention
away from internal state — see [masking blunts signal](masking-blunts-signal.md)
and [unhelpful self-monitoring](unhelpful-self-monitoring.md).

**Verbal stimming** (echolalia, finger-spelling, wordplay) is the same
signal class in the auditory/motor channel — often misread as giftedness
rather than regulation.

Reading stimming as data supports calibration; treating it only as behaviour
to eliminate obscures the arousal signal it often carries. Distinguish from
compulsive repetition in [stim vs. compulsion signal distinction](stim-vs-compulsion-signal-distinction.md).
