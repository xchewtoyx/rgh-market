type: concept
title: Misophonia
description: >
  An extreme emotional reaction to specific everyday sounds that most people
  barely notice, with misperceptions that can make a state reading feel
  certain when it is actually distorted.
sources:
  - title: "Is This Autism? A Guide for Clinicians and Everyone Else"
    resource: "Is This Autism? A Guide for Clinicians and Everyone Else (Donna Henderson, Sarah Wayland, Jamell White), ch. 8"
---

Misophonia is distinct from general auditory
[over-responsivity](sensory-over-responsivity.md): the triggers are usually
eating or breathing noises, and the reaction escalates through disgust and
irritability to anger, rage, aggression, or an urgent need to escape the
sound source.

Three features matter for calibrating confidence in a state reading:

- **Extreme externalizing reactions** — the emotional storm can look like
  interpersonal conflict rather than a sensory event, so the underlying
  trigger is easy to misread.
- **Misperceptions** — unlike typical sensory sensitivity, where the person
  knows they react more strongly than others, some misophonics believe others
  are deliberately making noise to irritate them or are being
  "unreasonably" loud during normal activity. The reading feels accurate
  from inside even when the attribution is wrong.
- **Quiet environments increase risk** — general auditory sensitivity is
  helped by quiet spaces; misophonia is the opposite. Soft, isolated sounds
  become more noticeable and triggering when ambient noise is low, so a
  "calm" environment can still produce a false sense that the person should
  be fine.

When a reaction to sound feels interpersonal rather than sensory, check
whether misophonia might be shaping the reading before acting on it. See
also [distinguishing anxiety from sensory overload](distinguishing-anxiety-from-sensory-overload.md)
for overlapping internal states.
