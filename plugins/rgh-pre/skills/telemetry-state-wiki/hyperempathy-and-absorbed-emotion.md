title: Hyperempathy and Absorbed Emotion
description: >
  A heightened sensitivity to other people's emotional states that can
  register as one's own feeling, making it hard to tell whether an
  emotional reading originates internally or was absorbed from the
  surrounding environment.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"

Empathy, like sensory processing, can run hyper- as well as hypo-: some
people are heightened in their sensitivity to others' pain and to the
emotional atmosphere of a room, rather than having difficulty accessing
empathy at all. The described experience is of "porous skin" — full of
holes letting everything in, with no shield distinguishing ambient emotion
from one's own.

This matters for state reading because a strong emotional signal is not
automatically evidence about the person's own situation — it may instead be
an accurate read of someone else's state, absorbed rather than generated.
When an emotional reaction seems disproportionate to anything happening in
one's own life, it is worth checking whether the surrounding social or
media environment (a distressed friend, a painful news story) is the actual
source. Henderson and colleagues describe **extreme affective empathy** in
many autistic people: shutting down when someone nearby — even a fictional
character — experiences a big emotion, whether or not it is overtly
displayed. Combined sensory and empathic overwhelm (e.g., near a crying
baby) can be highly dysregulating; the resulting withdrawal or meltdown can
**look like a lack of empathy** when the opposite is true. This is a distinct failure mode from
[raw spots](raw-spots-and-triggers.md), which amplify a genuinely
internal reaction rather than importing an external one.
