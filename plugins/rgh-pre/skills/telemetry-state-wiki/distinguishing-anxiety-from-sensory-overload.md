Distress that feels the same from the inside can have two different
sources, and telling them apart is a distinct calibration skill:

- **Sensory overload** — distress tracks external stimuli: bright lights,
  noise, crowding. It rises and falls with the sensory environment.
- **Anxiety** — distress is internally driven, rooted in thoughts about
  potential future events, largely independent of the immediate sensory
  environment.

The diagnostic is straightforward once framed this way: does the distress
track a specific external stimulus, or does it persist and vary regardless
of the surroundings? A related but distinct pattern is **sensory dread** —
anticipation of a *known* future sensory-overload situation (e.g. dread
before a trip to a mall, dentist, or grocery store). Sensory dread is
future-oriented like anxiety, but its content is about anticipated sensory
input rather than an internally generated thought, so it sits between the
two categories.

Getting this distinction right matters because misreading sensory overload
as anxiety (or vice versa) leads to addressing the wrong thing — see
[stress response signals](stress-response-signals.md) for a related
mismatch between what stress looks like and what it is.
