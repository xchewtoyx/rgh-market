title: Walking Meditation as State Anchor
description: >
  Using deliberate walking attention — formal or informal — as an alternative
  body anchor when sitting meditation is impractical due to pain, agitation,
  or anger, keeping mindfulness in motion rather than abandoning state reading.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life (Jon Kabat-Zinn), Part 5"
---

Some people who find sitting very difficult — including those with
significant pain, agitation, or anger — can "walk with it" into deep
practice. Traditional settings interleave sitting and walking as the same
practice; what matters is how the mind is kept.

**Formal walking:** attend to footfall as a whole or in segments (shifting,
moving, placing); optionally couple with breath. Walking goes nowhere —
back and forth in a lane — so "having no place to go makes it easier to be
where you are." Any pace from ultra-slow to brisk; feel sensations in feet,
legs, carriage, and gait — inner watching, not staring at feet. Same
distractions as sitting (perceptions, thoughts, feelings, impulses) worked
with the same way: "walking is stillness in motion, flowing mindfulness."

**Informal walking:** normal sidewalk, corridor, or hiking with intention
to recall "you are here in your body," accepting each moment. Slowing when
rushing helps: "If you miss the here, you are likely also to miss the
there."

For telemetry: when agitation makes body signals hard to read in stillness,
movement can be easier to stay mindful with than sitting — see MBCT's use
for those unable to settle. Pair with [body scan](body-scan.md) lying-down
practice for complementary anchors and [automatic pilot blinds signal](automatic-pilot-blinds-signal.md)
when mind ricochets between past and future during movement.
