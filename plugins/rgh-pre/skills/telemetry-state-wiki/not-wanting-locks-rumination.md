---
type: concept
title: Not-Wanting Locks Rumination
description: >
  The implicit "I don't want to feel like this" that keeps the mind in
  concept-based thinking about feelings rather than reading them directly —
  a locknut holding rumination in place.
sources:
  - title: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition"
    resource: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition (Zindel V. Segal, J. Mark G. Williams, John D. Teasdale), ch. 11"
---

Worry and rumination seem to address a difficulty but move **away** from a
direct sense of it because rumination includes judgment about the
experience — **"I don't want to feel like this."** This "not wanting"
acts like a locknut, holding a mode dominated by thinking *about* feelings
rather than experiencing them.

The loop: ruminative thinking creates more charged feelings and further
negative thoughts (also "not wanted"); over time raw experience and
judgments about it fuse, feeding a sense that intimate parts of self are
"rotten to the core." Avoidance and preoccupation lock together from
wanting things different from how they are; when avoidance fails, the
response is often to redouble the same strategy rather than change it.

For telemetry:

- A tangled reading may be rumination-about-state, not state itself.
- Comparison questions ("Am I still worse than last week?") and "if only"
  thoughts are often not-wanting disguised as assessment.
- First step toward skillful response: being present with unpleasant tone
  without immediately needing it different — see
  [attachment and aversion reactions](attachment-and-aversion-reactions.md).
