title: Demand Avoidance as Arousal Signal
description: >
  Sudden resistance to a task — even one you were about to do — when it
  is framed as a demand, marking nervous-system fight-or-flight activation
  rather than simple laziness.
sources:

Demand avoidance means the brain becomes strongly resistant to anything
perceived as a demand — real or imagined expectation, request, or external
pressure — which can activate the nervous system into fight-or-flight and
drive avoidance at all costs. The community increasingly reframes
"pathological demand avoidance" as **persistent drive for autonomy** —
more accurate to internal experience than an outsider's clinical label.

Illustrative pattern: about to empty the dishwasher, then someone says
"please empty the dishwasher" — sudden strong resistance appears despite
having been about to do it. Henderson and colleagues note the same pattern
for **pervasive drive for autonomy** (PDA): intense negative reaction can
be triggered even by demands the person *wants* to meet — productivity,
kindness to loved ones, learning, work — if the demand is perceived as
someone else's expectation. Loss-of-autonomy fear can dominate willingness.
Hype around a popular film can itself read as a
demand to watch, triggering resistance.

For state reading, sudden task resistance is a **arousal signal**, not a
character flaw. Reducing or reframing demands (flexible posting schedules,
weekly to-do lists instead of fixed calendar slots, meal lists instead of
fixed meal plans) can lower nervous-system triggering and improve accurate
readings of whether the person actually wants or can do the task.

Pair with [stress response signals](stress-response-signals.md) when
avoidance co-occurs with agitation or shutdown — the demand may be
sensory-social load, not the literal task.
