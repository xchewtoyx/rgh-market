type: concept
title: Unhelpful Self-Monitoring
description: >
  Self-monitoring aimed at social performance — eye contact, tone, stim
  suppression — that displaces monitoring of internal state and degrades
  confidence in what is actually happening inside.
sources:
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are (Ellie Middleton), ch. 2"
---

Self-monitoring splits into useful and costly forms. **Self-observation** and
**self-recording** (journalling, mental notes) aimed at "How do I feel?",
"How is this task going?", "Am I on task?" support accurate state reading.

**Unhelpful self-monitoring** tracks social performance instead: amount of
eye contact, suppression of stims, facial expressions, encouraging nods,
"friendly enough" tone, not talking "too much" about special interests.
[Masking](masking-blunts-signal.md) demands this extra layer beyond what
many neurotypical people track — constant checking whether the outward
presentation is acceptable.

The cost: unhelpful monitoring displaces helpful monitoring. Energy spent on
performance leaves little room to ask how one actually feels, what one needs,
or what would help. In safe environments where performance monitoring can
release, space opens for the internal questions that matter for telemetry.

Neurodivergent people are often framed as deficient at self-monitoring when
they are instead **over-monitoring the wrong targets**. Behaviours that
"slip through" (unmasking) are signals that capacity has shifted from
performance back toward internal state — worth reading as information, not
as failure.

Reframe toward **self-knowing**: notice how the brain naturally works and
lean into those patterns rather than treating them as wrong and constantly
fixing them. Compassionate self-awareness supports state reading; neuronormative
"shoulds" distort it.
