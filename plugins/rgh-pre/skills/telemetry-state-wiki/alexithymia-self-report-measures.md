There is no single clinical test for [alexithymia](alexithymia.md) — it is
not diagnosed the way autism is — but validated self-report measures can
give a useful sense of where someone falls on the spectrum, which is itself
useful context for calibrating confidence in that person's own emotional
readings:

1. **Online Alexithymia Test** — covers seven dimensions of alexithymia and
   produces a visual profile across areas rather than a single total score;
   accessible and designed for self-reflection rather than research use.
2. **Toronto Alexithymia Scale (TAS-20)** — historically the most widely
   used measure. Caveat worth carrying into any reading based on it: a 2024
   study found its "difficulty identifying feelings" subscale overlaps
   substantially with general psychological distress — scores can be
   inflated during hard times rather than cleanly reflecting trait
   alexithymia, i.e. it can mistake acute noise for a stable signal.
3. **Perth Alexithymia Questionnaire (PAQ)** — a 24-item measure of
   difficulty identifying feelings, difficulty describing feelings, and
   externally oriented thinking, across both positive and negative
   emotions. Stronger research support for distinguishing trait alexithymia
   from general distress than the TAS-20; validated with Autistic
   participants.

None of these are diagnostic on their own, but they can help a person put
words to their emotional profile and bring something concrete into
conversations with clinicians or therapists.
