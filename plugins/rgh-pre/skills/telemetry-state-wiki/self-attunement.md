Self-attunement is the moment-to-moment practice of pausing to check what is
actually happening internally, rather than proceeding on autopilot or on an
assumed/performed state. Two obstacles make this harder than it sounds:
[masking](masking-blunts-signal.md), which pulls attention away from
authentic experience and obscures a person's own needs and preferences, and
difficulties with [interoception](interoception.md), which complicate
accurate self-assessment even when attention is directed inward. Both
distance a person from the body signals self-attunement is trying to read;
in a severe case this can show up as dissociation from the body as a
protective response to overwhelm.

**In-the-moment technique.** When feeling overwhelmed, pause and ask what
specifically is causing the feeling, rather than accepting a vague global
label like "bad" or "off." When anxious, name the specific story the mind is
spinning, rather than treating the anxiety as free-floating.

**Self-check-in variant.** A short habit of asking "what do I need or want
right now?" once or twice a day, and — in social situations specifically —
reframing the internal question from "are they enjoying this?" to "am I
enjoying this, do I want to continue?" This shifts the attention that
masking habitually directs outward (toward how one is coming across) back
toward the internal signal that self-attunement needs.

For people who are significantly disconnected from body signals (common
after late diagnosis, or after a period of heavy masking), self-attunement
often has to be rebuilt gradually — treated as "rebuilding trust with your
body" rather than expected to work immediately. See
[body scanning](body-scan.md) for a structured technique that supports this.
