Nervous systems that tip into stress states more easily are also more prone
to shutdown or freeze responses rather than obvious fight-or-flight — which
means stress can go unnoticed by the person experiencing it, and by
observers, precisely because it doesn't match the expected signal.
Recognising both signatures matters for an accurate reading:

- **Fight-or-flight**: a surge of energy, agitation, anxiety, or
  restlessness. This is the pattern most readily recognised as "stress."
- **Freeze or fawn**: emotional numbness, fatigue, disconnection,
  dissociation, or immobilization — or, in the fawn variant, over-compliance
  and appeasement of others, often alongside difficulty setting boundaries.
  This pattern is easy to misread as calm from the outside, and sometimes
  from the inside too, because it lacks the obvious agitation of
  fight-or-flight.

Because the freeze/fawn signature can look like ordinary calm, an
apparently calm or compliant presentation should not automatically be read
as evidence of low stress — it's one of the two patterns stress actually
takes, not the absence of it.

A **meltdown** is an uncontrollable physiological response to overwhelm —
not willful, goal-directed behavior like a temper tantrum. Treating it as
volitional misreads the state and degrades calibration. Autistic adults
sometimes use **neuro-crash** for short-term overwhelm when a system is
pushed past its limit, producing fight/flight/freeze (meltdown or shutdown).
Common triggers include: being around agitated people; being rushed or pushed
beyond capability; invasions of personal space; sensory overload; lack of
predictability; unclear expectations; and physiological reactions. Some
people reject the "anxiety" label clinicians apply to neuro-crash — the
felt state may be overwhelm rather than named worry.

**Shutdown signature (autistic):** speech and cognitive processing slow;
seeks quiet, dark, alone; may become nonresponsive or need excessive
prompting; may sleep excessively yet still feel sleepy when rested — often
after burnout or meltdown. Distinguish from meltdown's outward discharge —
see [four autistic stress state signatures](four-autistic-stress-state-signatures.md).
