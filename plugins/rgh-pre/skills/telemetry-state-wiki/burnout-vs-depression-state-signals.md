title: Burnout vs. Depression State Signals
description: >
  Autistic burnout and clinical depression overlap in fatigue and withdrawal
  but differ in pleasure capacity, worthlessness, memory, and masking
  collapse — distinctions that change how much to trust a mood reading.
sources:
  - title: "Is This Autism? A Guide for Clinicians and Everyone Else"
    resource: "Is This Autism? A Guide for Clinicians and Everyone Else (Donna Henderson, Sarah Wayland, Jamell White), ch. 9"
---

Autistic burnout — deep fatigue from long-term exposure to taxing situations
without adequate recovery, often linked to passing as non-autistic — can last
months to years and is frequently first noticed in adolescence. It overlaps
depression in withdrawal and low energy but is not the same state.

Calibration differences useful at the bedside of a state reading:

| Signal | Burnout | Depression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pleasure | Can still feel pleasure in some contexts | Pleasure often broadly diminished |
| Self-worth | Typically lacks depression's worthlessness/guilt core | Hopelessness, worthlessness, guilt common |
| Memory | Short-term memory challenges reported | Concentration problems common |
| Masking | Masking capacity drops sharply; skills may appear to regress | Less energy to mask; similar surface pattern |
| Intervention fit | Does not respond like depression to medication or reframing-heavy therapy; recovery needs **demand relief** | May respond to standard depression treatments |

Burnout can **lead to** depression; both raise suicide risk. Treating burnout
as depression — or reading "I can still enjoy this" as proof depression is
absent — mis-calibrates the state.

When fatigue, withdrawal, and masking collapse coincide, ask which column
the reading fits before naming the state. See
[behavioral shift as emotional signal](behavioral-shift-as-emotional-signal.md)
for how regression shows up before a verbal label, and
[chronic autonomic hyperarousal](chronic-autonomic-hyperarousal.md) when
long-term activation rather than collapse is the dominant signal.
