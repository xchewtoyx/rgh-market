---
type: concept
title: The Emotional Storm
description: >
  The observation that pure, single emotions are rare — multiple emotions
  and physical sensations typically co-occur — which means a state
  reading usually needs to be decomposed rather than reduced to one label.
sources:
  - title: "That Little Voice in Your Head"
    resource: "That Little Voice in Your Head (Mo Gawdat), ch. 7"
---

A single emotional moment is rarely one pure emotion; it is closer to a
mixed paint colour that needs deliberate deconstruction back into its base
pigments. At any given moment several emotions and physical sensations are
typically present together — for example, simultaneously feeling pleased
about progress on one thing, worried about another person, guilty about an
unrelated lapse, and mildly hungry, all at once, none of which cancel the
others out.

Trying to force this mixture into a single label loses information; a more
accurate reading treats the moment as a storm to be scanned in full,
noting the distinct sensations and their likely
[physical signatures](emotional-physical-signature.md) rather than settling
for whichever emotion is loudest or easiest to name. A structured way to do
this scan is a full-body, full-emotional-state pass — physical sensations
first, then the complete blend of concurrent emotions — which is the same
underlying move as a [body scan](body-scan.md), extended from physical
sensation to the layered emotional state sitting on top of it.
