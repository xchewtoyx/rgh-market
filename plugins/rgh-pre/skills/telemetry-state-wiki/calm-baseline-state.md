---
type: concept
title: Establishing a Calm Baseline
description: >
  Deliberately noting what a resting, non-activated body and mind feel
  like, so that later deviations from it are recognisable as signal rather
  than treated as the default.
sources:
  - title: "That Little Voice in Your Head"
    resource: "That Little Voice in Your Head (Mo Gawdat), ch. 7"
---

Reading a deviation requires knowing what "normal" feels like first. A calm
or "control" state — the body and mind at rest, without an activated
emotion running — is worth deliberately noting and remembering, not
assumed. A practical way to build this reference point: scan the body
before and during recalled emotional memories and compare each to how the
same body feels at rest, logging the distinct physical difference each
emotion produces relative to that resting state. See
[the physical signature of an emotion](emotional-physical-signature.md) for
what is being compared against the baseline, and
[body scanning](body-scan.md) for the scanning technique itself.

The same logic applies over longer timescales to a single recurring
emotion or reaction: see
[recurring-emotion tracking](emotion-tracking-journal.md) for building a
baseline of a specific pattern's frequency and typical signature over
days or weeks, rather than a single sitting.
