When [alexithymia](alexithymia.md) is degrading a state reading, it matters
which kind is in play:

- **Primary (trait) alexithymia** — innate, relatively stable across time
  and situations, present as part of a person's baseline emotional
  processing for as long as they can remember. Likely genetic and
  neurodevelopmental in origin.
- **Secondary (state) alexithymia** — more temporary and situational; can
  develop in response to trauma, chronic stress, medical conditions, or
  major life events (PTSD is a common example). Functions as a kind of
  shutdown or protective numbing that wasn't present before, and may ease as
  the underlying circumstances are processed or supported.

A person can have both at once: a longstanding trait tendency significantly
amplified by high stress, burnout, or masking. Long-term suppression of
authentic expression, chronic hypervigilance, and dissociation all further
degrade trust in internal signals — see
[masking blunts emotional signal](masking-blunts-signal.md). The practical
implication for calibrating confidence: a sudden *drop* in emotional
legibility relative to someone's own history is worth treating as a signal
in itself (possible burnout, trauma, or acute stress), even when the person
also has a lifelong trait-level baseline of lower emotional legibility.

**Stability evidence.** A 2025 longitudinal study followed adults over seven
months and found alexithymia scores stayed stable even as depression,
anxiety, and wellbeing scores fluctuated — it did not simply rise and fall
with emotional distress, which is read as evidence for a genuine
trait-level component rather than pure reactivity to circumstances.
