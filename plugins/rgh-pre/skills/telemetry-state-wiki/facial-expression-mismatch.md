---
type: concept
title: Facial Expression Mismatch
description: >
  When outward facial display systematically misrepresents felt emotion,
  degrading both others' readings of you and your confidence that others
  see what you feel.
sources:
  - title: "Knowing Why: Adult-Diagnosed Autistic People on Life and Autism"
    resource: "Knowing Why: Adult-Diagnosed Autistic People on Life and Autism (Autistic Self Advocacy Network), ch. 5"
---

Some people discover via webcam meetings or feedback that their face reads
as far more serious than they feel, and that what feels like smiling barely
registers on camera. The outward channel and the internal channel diverge —
a first realization that facial expressions misrepresent actual emotions to
others.

This is a **calibration problem for state reading in both directions**:

- Others' readings of your mood from your face are unreliable.
- Your assumption that you are signalling warmth or ease may be wrong even
  when the internal reading feels accurate.

It overlaps with [masking personas](masking-personas.md) performed warmth
versus felt state, but can persist even when not deliberately masking.
When social feedback ("you look angry") conflicts with internal experience,
check facial expression mismatch before treating either channel as authoritative.
See also [unhelpful self-monitoring](unhelpful-self-monitoring.md) when
effort goes into performance that still fails to match intent.
