type: concept
title: Second Arrow Suffering
description: >
  The added distress fired onto an initial pain or unpleasant feeling —
  anger, fear, shame, self-criticism — which often hurts more than the
  first arrow and is identifiable as a separate telemetry channel.
sources:
  - title: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition"
    resource: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition (Zindel V. Segal, J. Mark G. Williams, John D. Teasdale), ch. 11"
---

Buddhist teaching uses **two arrows**: the first arrow is physical or
emotional pain from what happened; the second is the suffering added by
reacting to that pain — anger, fear, grief, distress, self-criticism.
The second arrow more often causes the greater unhappiness, and we fire
it at ourselves — so we can learn to free ourselves from it.

Examples in MBCT dialogue:

- Dread and stomach tightness imagining being judged for skipped home
  practice — the interesting signal is the self-critical pattern, not
  only the "failure" fact.
- Getting upset about having negative thoughts ("Why am I so stupid and
  weak?") — aversion to aversion.

For state reading, separate channels:

1. **Primary signal** — sadness, tight chest, dread, fatigue.
2. **Second arrow** — shame about sadness, guilt about dread, anger about
   tightness, meta-judgment about "weakness."
