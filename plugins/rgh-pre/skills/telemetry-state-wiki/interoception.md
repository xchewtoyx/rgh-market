The mind–body split is largely artificial for state reading: major emotional
events disrupt logical thinking and memory; extreme temperature can block both
clear thought and emotional calm. Cognition, emotion, and bodily condition are
intertwined channels, not independent inputs — a reading that treats "mental
state" separately from physical comfort or sensory load is often incomplete.
See [embodied signals precede conscious reading](embodied-signals-precede-conscious-reading.md)
for how fast somatic channels can register state before verbal self-report
catches up.

Emotions are partly interoceptive events: if the underlying internal signals
are fuzzy or confusing, identifying and naming an emotion becomes much
harder regardless of effort or vocabulary. Research links
[alexithymia](alexithymia.md) to interoceptive confusion and lower
interoceptive awareness rather than to a "feelings vocabulary" problem alone
— meaning that when a state reading feels unreliable, the fault often lies
further upstream, at the level of the raw body signal, not at the level of
naming it.

Interoceptive awareness is not simply high or low as a single dial; both
directions distort a reading:

- **Heightened interoception** can mean feeling everything "at max volume"
  but struggling to differentiate one signal from another (e.g. anxiety vs.
  hunger blur together into a vague "something is wrong").
- **Lowered interoception** means signals like thirst, hunger, needing the
  bathroom, or emotion itself may not register at all, or register too late
  to be useful in the moment — e.g. not noticing a rapid heart rate tied to
  anxiety, or low awareness of hunger/satiety until external reminders or
  accidents surface the need ([sensory under-responsivity](sensory-under-responsivity.md)).

Interoception is trainable rather than fixed — see
[body scanning](body-scan.md) and
[micro interoceptive check-ins](micro-interoceptive-check-ins.md) for
methods that build it deliberately. It sits alongside two other
"hidden" senses that follow the same over-/under-responsive pattern:
[proprioception](proprioception.md) and [the vestibular sense](vestibular-sense.md).
Mapping how interoception, proprioception, and the vestibular sense show up
for a specific person is the goal of
[sensory profile mapping](sensory-profile-mapping.md).

Reading interoceptive signals accurately also depends on how much
[masking](masking-blunts-signal.md) is currently suppressing them, and on
deliberate [self-attunement](self-attunement.md) to bring them back into
attention.
