title: Sensory Over-Responsivity
description: >
  When sensory input registers as too strong, producing avoidance, unusual
  reactions, or meltdowns — or, critically, only an internal reaction with
  no outward sign that anything is wrong.
sources:
  - title: "Is This Autism? A Guide for Clinicians and Everyone Else"

Over-responsivity (also called sensory sensitivity or sensory defensiveness)
means a stimulus reads as extreme relative to what most people would
experience. Outward signs can include meltdowns, startling at quiet sounds,
or avoidance — with or without a verbal explanation.

The calibration problem for state reading: **over-responsivity can produce no
observable behavioral manifestation at all.** The only signal may be an
internal reaction ranging from mild discomfort through distraction,
irritation, overwhelm, or rage. A telemetry reading based on outward
behavior or self-report of "I'm fine" can miss a load that is already
building — see [masking blunts signal](masking-blunts-signal.md) for a
related pattern where the visible channel is unreliable.

Because many people — especially girls and women — **internalize** sensory
differences without observable behavioural signs, query directly about
sensory experience rather than relying on observed behaviour alone.

Examples across senses include aversions to specific sounds, light
discomfort, tactile defensiveness (seams, tags, air or water on skin),
discomfort with light human touch, smell sensitivity, hygiene-related sensory
aspects, food texture or restriction reactions, and crowd overwhelm from
combined visual, auditory, and olfactory input.

**Firm versus light touch** often split: repeated first-person reports describe
light touch as aversive, alarming, or anxiety-producing while deep pressure
(weighted blankets, firm hugs) reads as neutral or soothing — the same touch
modality can signal opposite things depending on pressure depth. See
[proprioception](proprioception.md) for how pressure registration fits the
wider sensory map.

Over-responsivity is one of three common response patterns alongside
[sensory under-responsivity](sensory-under-responsivity.md) and
[sensory craving](sensory-craving.md); any of the eight sensory systems can
show any pattern. Mapping which senses and patterns apply to a given person
is part of [sensory profile mapping](sensory-profile-mapping.md).

Misophonia is a distinct auditory challenge — see [misophonia](misophonia.md)
rather than folding it into generic sound sensitivity.
