  criticism, or disappointment — often out of proportion to the cue and
  resistant to slow cognitive reframing in the moment.

Rejection sensitive dysphoria (RSD) is not a formal diagnosis but a widely
used label — especially among ADHDers, some of whom are also autistic — for
a distinctive state pattern: perceived disappointment of self or others, or
feeling rejected, teased, or criticized, triggers a **rapid and dramatic**
emotional response.

For telemetry confidence:

- The surge is **immediate and visceral**, not a slow-building mood — a
  sideways glance, tone shift, or ambiguous comment can register as
  devastating rebuke.
- Autistic people with RSD may **misread neutral social cues** as hostile
  *or* struggle to parse underlying intent, compounding dysregulation.
- Because the response is so fast, behavioral and cognitive interventions
  often land **after** the state has already peaked — the reading in the
  moment may not yield to "consider alternative interpretations."

Treat a sudden flood of shame, rage, or despair following minor social
friction as a possible RSD signature rather than proof the trigger was
objectively catastrophic. Pair with
[heuristic substitution in state reading](heuristic-substitution-in-state-reading.md)
when a single cue substitutes for a full social assessment, and
[eroded perception trust](eroded-perception-trust.md) when chronic
invalidation has trained hyper-vigilance to negative evaluation.
