type: concept
title: Mood-Colored Interpretation
description: >
  Current mood filters ambiguous facts toward the most negative plausible
  story, which then feels like evidence rather than interpretation — a
  calibration hazard when trusting a state reading or its implied cause.
sources:
  - title: "The Mindful Way through Depression: Freeing Yourself from Chronic Unhappiness"
    resource: "The Mindful Way through Depression (Mark Williams, John Teasdale, Zindel Segal, Jon Kabat-Zinn), ch. 1"
  - title: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition"
    resource: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition (Zindel V. Segal, J. Mark G. Williams, John D. Teasdale), ch. 9"
---

Mood does not only change how strong a reaction feels — it changes what
**interpretation** gets selected for the same facts. In a low mood, the mind
picks out and elaborates the most negative reading of ambiguous data,
which further lowers mood, which recruits more "evidence" of
worthlessness, isolation, and inadequacy.

The source describes this as writing commentary on a silent film: the
world supplies images; mood supplies the plot. When depressed, themes of
worthlessness and self-blame permeate automatic thoughts — what looks like
distortion from outside can feel like absolute truth from inside, which is
why the reading feels high-confidence even when B is driving C.

**Calibration checks:**

- Is the event actually ambiguous, or only the story about it?
- Would the same facts support a different B in a better mood?
- Has a glum mood recently triggered self-critical investigation ("why am I
  such a loser?") that deepened the mood rather than clarified the signal?
- **Negative thinking as early warning:** adhesive, depression-shaped
  interpretations often precede escalation — if this B would likely arise
  only when depressed, treat it as a [relapse signature](relapse-signature-state-reading.md)
  signal, not neutral data. "The event itself is neutral; all the action is
  on what you make of it."
