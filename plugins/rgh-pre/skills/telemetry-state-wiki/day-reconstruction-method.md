title: Day Reconstruction Method
description: >
  A structured way to rebuild yesterday as timed episodes and rate feelings
  per episode, producing a duration-weighted picture of experienced state
  rather than a single global judgment.
sources:
  - title: "Thinking, Fast and Slow"
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 38"
---

Continuous experience sampling (random prompts through the day) is accurate
but expensive. The **Day Reconstruction Method (DRM)** is a practical
alternative: in a session, relive the previous day in detail, break it into
episodes like film scenes, and answer experience-sampling-style questions
about each (activity, companions, feeling ratings 0–6), with start and end
times so longer episodes count more in a duration-weighted daily affect
measure.

People who retrieve a past situation in detail can often relive its
accompanying feelings, including earlier physiological indications of
emotion. Several comparisons with true experience sampling have supported
DRM validity. The questionnaire can also include life-satisfaction measures
— interpreted as satisfaction of the [remembering self](experiencing-vs-remembering-self.md),
not necessarily the experiencing self.

The **U-index** classifies an episode as unpleasant if any negative-feeling
rating exceeds all positive ratings; an individual's U-index is the
percentage of waking time in such states. Because it is grounded in time
rather than a single rating scale, a population drop from 20% to 18%
means total time in emotional discomfort fell by a tenth.

DRM is useful when a global "how was your day?" answer feels unreliable —
it separates episodes, surfaces which activities and companions drove
unpleasant time, and reduces [substitution](heuristic-substitution-in-state-reading.md)
of current mood into life evaluation. See also
[emotion tracking journal](emotion-tracking-journal.md) for a lighter
ongoing variant.
