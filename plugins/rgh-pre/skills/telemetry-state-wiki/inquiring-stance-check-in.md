  A brief four-question scan — how things are now, what is in mind, what is
  in the body, what is skillful — that steps out of automatic pilot before
  trusting a reactive state reading.
sources:
  - title: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition"
    resource: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition (Zindel V. Segal, J. Mark G. Williams, John D. Teasdale), ch. 11"

MBCT trains an **inquiring stance** — stepping back to observe rather than
being drawn into "bad feeling" or arguing against negative thoughts. Four
questions recur:

1. **How are things right now?**
2. **What is going through my mind here?**
3. **What is going on in my body?**
4. **What is the most skillful response to make right now?**

This opens wider perspective: thoughts and feelings seen within a larger
field, with more space to work and more self-forgiveness when intentions
go awry. Example distinction: "I am sad, I am tired, but I'm not
depressed" — sadness and tiredness read without collapsing into a
depression label or denying what is present.

Use as a lightweight alternative to formal practice when busy — a momentary
check-in without closing eyes. Pair with the three-minute breathing space
described in [automatic pilot blinds signal](automatic-pilot-blinds-signal.md)
(responsive use when unpleasant feelings or bodily tightening appear).

See [micro interoceptive check-ins](micro-interoceptive-check-ins.md) for
shorter body-first variants and [situation-interpretation-reaction model](situation-interpretation-reaction.md)
when mind content needs separating from facts.
