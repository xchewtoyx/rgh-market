type: concept
title: Attachment and Aversion Reactions
description: >
  Automatic next-moment responses to feeling tone — clinging to pleasant,
  pushing away unpleasant, tuning out neutral — which often run below
  awareness and distort what a state reading is actually about.
sources:
  - title: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition"
    resource: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition (Zindel V. Segal, J. Mark G. Williams, John D. Teasdale), ch. 11"
---

Every experience registers a **feeling tone** — pleasant, unpleasant, or
neutral — usually subtle and often unnoticed. Immediately after, the mind-
body tends to react:

- **Pleasant** → **attachment**: need to hang on or get more (e.g. anger
  when a settled meditation is interrupted, "like ice cream snatched away").
- **Unpleasant** → **aversion**: need to get rid of the experience and
  prevent recurrence — at the root of relapse-related mind states in
  depression.
- **Neutral** → boredom, restlessness, tuning out.

Evolutionarily, aversion served escaping external threats; applied to
**internal** experiences it co-opts problem-solving into rumination —
counterproductive attempts to eliminate unhappiness.

For telemetry:

- The readout may be attachment/aversion to a tone, not the underlying
  event — clinging to calm can produce frustration when calm ends.
- Recognition precedes skillful response; Session 4 MBCT focuses on seeing
  what "takes us away" (doing, rumination, worry) before changing it.
- Central theme across attachment and aversion: **needing things to be
  different from how they are right now**.
