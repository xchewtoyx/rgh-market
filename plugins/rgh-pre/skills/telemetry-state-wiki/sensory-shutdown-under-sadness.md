type: concept
title: Sensory Shutdown Under Sadness
description: >
  During sad mood, the brain's default-mode rumination can actively suppress
  sensory processing, blunting interoceptive signal and raising depression
  risk when the shutdown is intense.
sources:
  - title: "The Mindful Way through Depression: Freeing Yourself from Chronic Unhappiness"
    resource: "The Mindful Way through Depression (Mark Williams, John Teasdale, Zindel Segal, Jon Kabat-Zinn), ch. 6"
---

When depressed, effort often goes into ruminative brooding as the default
mode network's (DMN) daydreaming turns negative. Brain-imaging studies of
people watching sad films show sadness activates the DMN at the expense of
sensory processing — in some people the DMN actively shuts down sensation
processing.

Farb et al. followed participants who watched sad films in an fMRI scanner
for two years. Sadness shut down sensory processing; higher-than-average
intensity of that shutdown made a person **twenty-five times more likely**
to become depressed than someone with less intense shutdown. Relying on the
DMN to respond to sadness with fixing, thinking, and planning made relapse
more likely; reducing its influence through mindfulness or curiosity was
associated with resilience. DMN activity tended to quiet following eight
weeks of mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (MBCT).

For state reading: during sad or ruminative periods, interoceptive and
sensory channels may be deliberately muted — a real-time body-based reading
can under-report load. Pair with [delayed emotional processing](delayed-emotional-processing.md)
and [body scan](body-scan.md) practice that re-engages sensation rather than
feeding rumination.
