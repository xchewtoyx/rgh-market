title: Graded Awareness Model
description: >
  Awareness of the present moment runs along a graded scale rather than
  being simply on or off, which matters for judging how much of a current
  state reading is actually being registered versus missed.
sources:
  - title: "Solve for Happy"
    resource: "Solve for Happy (Mo Gawdat), ch. 10"
---

Attention to the present moment is not a binary switch but a dimmer,
running through distinguishable stages:

1. **Reception** — a sound or stimulus reaches you with no attention at
   all.
2. **Rejection** — the stimulus is actively blocked out.
3. **Attention** — interest engages and the stimulus becomes a genuine
   perception.
4. **Awareness** — full immersion in the moment.
5. **Connection** — a hyper-attuned state that picks up subtle signals
   others miss (e.g. noticing disagreement in someone's body language
   before it's said aloud).

A supporting large-scale study (Killingsworth, via trackyourhappiness.org;
15,000+ participants, 650,000+ momentary reports) found people were happier
when fully present regardless of what they were doing, and this held even
when the wandering thought itself was neutral or positive — full presence
correlated with a better state reading independent of the moment's actual
content.

The practical use of this model is diagnostic: a "reading" taken while
sitting somewhere low on the scale (reception or rejection) is not
comparable in reliability to one taken from genuine awareness or
connection, even if both nominally involve "paying attention." See
[the four corners of awareness](four-corners-of-awareness.md) for a
checklist of what to scan once attention has actually reached the
awareness stage.
