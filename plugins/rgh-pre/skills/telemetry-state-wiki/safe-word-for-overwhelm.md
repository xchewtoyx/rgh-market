title: Safe Word for Overwhelm
description: >
  A pre-agreed phrase, gesture, or code with a trusted person to signal
  need to leave or get help when meltdown, shutdown, or panic has degraded
  normal communication.
sources:

During autistic meltdowns, shutdowns, anxiety attacks, or general overwhelm,
communication with others becomes difficult — full verbal shutdown, or
difficulty forming coherent sentences even when words are attempted. Better
[self-monitoring](micro-interoceptive-check-ins.md) can make these states
more noticeable; unmasking can make them more frequent because feelings are
less constantly covered.

A **safe word** (or code phrase, gesture, communication card, or AAC device)
pre-arranged with a trusted person communicates need without explaining in
the moment. Examples: a phrase about parking to signal leaving a social
event; a phrase about the printer to signal leaving a loud meeting; three
taps on a partner's arm.

The specific signal can be nonsensical to onlookers — what matters is prior
agreement with a safe person so nothing must be justified when capacity is
low. Mapping stressful situations, who the safe person is in each, and
which signal to use turns overwhelm from an unreadable crisis into a
state that can still be **acted on** even when it cannot be fully described.
