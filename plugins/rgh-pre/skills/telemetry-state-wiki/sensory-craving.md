type: concept
title: Sensory Craving
description: >
  A strong pull toward specific sensory input — visual inspection, smell,
  touch, pressure, or repetitive movement — that signals what the nervous
  system is seeking, not merely what the person "likes."
sources:
  - title: "Is This Autism? A Guide for Clinicians and Everyone Else"
    resource: "Is This Autism? A Guide for Clinicians and Everyone Else (Donna Henderson, Sarah Wayland, Jamell White), ch. 8"
---

Sensory craving (related terms: sensory seeking) is the third common
response pattern alongside [over-responsivity](sensory-over-responsivity.md)
and [under-responsivity](sensory-under-responsivity.md). It shows up as
unusual interest in sensory aspects of the environment — excessive smelling
or touching of objects, fascination with spinning lights or objects, close
visual inspection, looking from the corner of the eye or through squinted
eyes, unusual sensory exploration (licking objects or people), chewing or
sucking on clothing, strong attraction to particular textures, or craving
deep pressure and weighted-blanket-type input.

For state reading, craving is a **signal channel**: what someone is drawn
to repeat or seek often reflects which inputs their system currently reads as
insufficient or rewarding. Tracking craving patterns over time — which
textures, movements, or pressures show up when stressed versus calm — adds
detail to [sensory profile mapping](sensory-profile-mapping.md) and can
surface shifts before overload or shutdown become obvious.

Craving deep pressure overlaps with [proprioception](proprioception.md)
under-responsivity; craving spinning or rocking overlaps with
[vestibular](vestibular-sense.md) under-responsivity. The craving label
describes the behavioral pull; the hidden-sense notes describe the underlying
registration pattern.
