---
type: concept
title: Emotion as a Decision Signal
description: >
  Emotion is not optional input to good decisions — without felt emotional
  feedback, even intact reasoning and memory cannot reliably choose or
  follow through on everyday actions.
sources:
  - title: "The Unmasking Workbook for Autistic Adults"
    resource: "The Unmasking Workbook for Autistic Adults (Jessica Penot), ch. 9"
---

Antonio Damasio's case of "Elliot" (from *Descartes' Error*) illustrates
why state reading matters beyond self-understanding. After frontal-lobe
damage, Elliot kept his IQ, memory, and intelligence but lost his capacity
to feel and understand emotion — and consequently could not make basic
decisions, complete projects, hold a job, or sustain a marriage. Decisiveness
and capability come not from logic alone but from balance between reason
and emotion.

For an agent helping someone read state: impaired access to emotion removes
a needed input to decision-making. This may partly explain why
[alexithymia](alexithymia.md) co-occurs with executive-functioning
difficulty — the person is deciding without a channel that normally weights
options and motivates follow-through.

**Practical implication:** track and acknowledge feelings, then deliberately
factor emotion (not just logic) into decisions. Daily feelings tracking —
see [recurring-emotion tracking](emotion-tracking-journal.md) and
[affect labeling](affect-labeling.md) — builds the signal quality needed for
state-informed choices about stressors, burnout, and meltdown risk.
