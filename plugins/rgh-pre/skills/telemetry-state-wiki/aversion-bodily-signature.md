title: Aversion Bodily Signature
description: >
  Recurring somatic patterns — contraction, pushing-away, sudden fatigue —
  that signal the mind-body has registered something it wants gone, often
  before the mind names an emotion or admits dislike.
sources:
  - title: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition"
    resource: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition (Zindel V. Segal, J. Mark G. Williams, John D. Teasdale), ch. 11"
---

**Aversion** is the mind's habitual reaction to unpleasant feeling tone —
driven by not wanting the experience. The body often signals it first:

- Contraction or tightness in face, shoulders, torso.
- A felt sense of "pushing away" or "not wanting."
- Sudden fatigue or exhaustion ("giving up") when the mind encounters
  something it dislikes — e.g. blank mind during meditation triggering
  "doing it wrong," then chest clutching and feeling lost.
- Tunnel vision around the triggering thought or feeling.

The body-mind can treat one's own thoughts and memories as enemies to
eradicate or flee — but "none of us can run fast enough to get away from
our own thoughts," leaving stuck, lost, helpless feelings that reactivate
old patterns "as if happening all over again."

For telemetry confidence:

- Learn personal contraction patterns as early alerts — often before
  conscious dislike is admitted.
- When scattered or drawn into mental drama, check where it shows in the
  body before trusting the narrative.
- Identifying the earliest moment in the sequence creates choice over
  automatic reactivity; aversion will still occur, but need not own
  attention.

Pair with [internal barometer](internal-barometer.md),
[embodied signals precede conscious reading](embodied-signals-precede-conscious-reading.md),
and [attachment and aversion reactions](attachment-and-aversion-reactions.md).
Spacious attention — holding breath, body, sounds, and thoughts in a wider
field — can counter tunnel vision when aversion narrows focus.
