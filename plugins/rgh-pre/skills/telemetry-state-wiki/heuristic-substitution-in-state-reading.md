---
type: concept
title: Heuristic Substitution in State Reading
description: >
  When a hard question about overall state is unanswered quickly, the mind
  often substitutes an easier one — usually current mood — and treats the
  answer as complete.
sources:
  - title: "Thinking, Fast and Slow"
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 10"
---

When a satisfactory answer to a hard question is not found quickly, System 1
finds a related easier question and answers it instead. The **target
question** is what you intend to assess; the **heuristic question** is what
you actually answer.

Examples that distort state and well-being readings:

- "How happy are you with your life?" → "What is my mood right now?"
- "How much would you contribute to save a species?" → "How much emotion
  do I feel when I think of dying dolphins?"

In student surveys, asking about dating before happiness made the
correlation between dates and happiness soar; asking happiness first left
correlation near zero. Priming a specific domain (parents, finances,
romance) before a general happiness question lets that domain's emotional
answer substitute for the harder overall assessment — the present state of
mind looms very large.

The **affect heuristic** extends this: likes and dislikes determine beliefs
about risks and benefits without separate evidence. Political preference
shapes which arguments feel compelling; disliking irradiated food predicts
believing its risks are high.

For telemetry confidence: a ready intuitive answer to "how are you?" or
"how was your week?" may be a heuristic substitute, not a full inventory.
Slow the question, separate domains, or use structured methods like
[day reconstruction](day-reconstruction-method.md) or scheduled
[micro interoceptive check-ins](micro-interoceptive-check-ins.md) to reduce
substitution. See [eroded perception trust](eroded-perception-trust.md) when
the substituted answer has been trained by others' invalidation.
