---
type: concept
title: Eroded Perception Trust
description: >
  When repeated invalidation of felt sensations and needs trains a person to
  stop trusting their own readings, making even accurate signals hard to
  believe.
sources:
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are (Ellie Middleton), ch. 2"
---

Growing up undiagnosed and unsupported, many neurodivergent people are
repeatedly told their perceptions, pain, or needs are wrong or invalid — music
"isn't too loud" when it feels painful, hunger cues dismissed, emotional
reactions labelled silly. Over time this erodes trust in one's own
judgement: feelings get suppressed, and eventually the person stops
recognizing them altogether.

Combined with [interoception](interoception.md) differences, this compounds
difficulty identifying emotions and sensations. The calibration problem is
not only weak signal — it is **distrust of signal that does arrive**. A
reading may be accurate but feel unbelievable, or be overridden by what
others have said should be true.

Rebuilding perception trust is described as a muscle trained over time, not
an instant fix. Practices that help:

- Finding ways of listening to the body without immediately judging the answer.
- Spending time alone or with other neurodivergent people where
  [unhelpful self-monitoring](unhelpful-self-monitoring.md) can drop.
- Scheduling regular [micro interoceptive check-ins](micro-interoceptive-check-ins.md)
  or [emotion tracking journal](emotion-tracking-journal.md) entries to
  practise naming what is there before someone else contradicts it.
- Using tools like emotion-sensation wheels to map body sensations to
  feelings when vocabulary is thin — see [emotional vocabulary building](emotional-vocabulary-building.md).

The danger of eroded trust extends beyond daily state reading: if a person
learns their perceptions are "wrong," they may be more likely to accept an
abuser's claim that their perceptions are wrong. Calibrating confidence in
a reading includes noticing whether invalidation history is pulling the
confidence dial down even when the signal is clear.
