title: Affect Labeling
description: >
  Naming an emotion in real time, in specific rather than reflexive terms,
  which both improves the accuracy of the state reading and measurably
  reduces distress.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"

  - title: "That Little Voice in Your Head"

---

Labeling emotions in real time reduces distress: in a cited spider-phobia
study, the group instructed to state their emotion aloud showed the most
improvement. The proposed mechanism is that labeling activates the
prefrontal cortex, which downregulates amygdala and limbic reactivity —
naming is not just descriptive, it changes the state being described. This
makes affect labeling especially valuable where
[alexithymia](alexithymia.md) is present, since the practice directly
targets the "difficulty identifying feelings" core feature.

Building the underlying skill draws on several supports together:
developing awareness of the physical sensations, thoughts, and behaviours
tied to a given emotion (pairs with [body scanning](body-scan.md) and
[the physical signature of an emotion](emotional-physical-signature.md));
visual aids such as an emotion matrix or feeling wheel; tracking mood via an
app or worksheet; and journaling.

**The reflexive-"I'm fine" counter-practice.** A specific, concrete version
of affect labeling: several times a day, when asked how you are, resist the
reflexive "I'm fine" and instead name the actual current physical and
emotional state in specific terms — for example "I feel anxious about the
report I need to submit tomorrow, but excited about how my date went last
night." Practiced deliberately and repeatedly, this counters the drift
described in [masking blunts the signal](masking-blunts-signal.md), where
habitual "I'm fine" answers stop the person noticing their own state, not
just hiding it from others.
