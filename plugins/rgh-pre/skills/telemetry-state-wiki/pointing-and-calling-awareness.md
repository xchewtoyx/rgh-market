Japanese railway **Pointing-and-Calling** requires operators to point at each
object (signal, speedometer, timetable) and call its status aloud ("Signal is
green"). Platform staff point along the edge and declare "All clear!" before
departure. Using eyes, hands, mouth, and ears together raises awareness from
nonconscious habit to a more conscious level — cited reductions of errors
up to 85% and accidents by 30%. A "point-only" MTA variant still cut
incorrectly berthed subways 57% in two years.

Personal analogue: verbally checking items before a trip ("I've got my
keys. I've got my wallet.") or saying the intended action and its
consequence aloud — "I'm about to eat this cookie, but I don't need it.
Eating it will cause me to gain weight" — makes consequences more real and
interrupts mindless routine.

For telemetry:

- The more automatic behavior becomes, the less consciously we monitor it;
  repeated success breeds assuming "next time will be just like the last."
- Behavior change starts with awareness — you must notice habits before
  changing them.
- Saying a needed action aloud ("Tomorrow I need to go to the post office
  after lunch") forces acknowledgment and improves follow-through.
