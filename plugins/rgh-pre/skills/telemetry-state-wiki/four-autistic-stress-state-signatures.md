title: Four Autistic Stress State Signatures
description: >
  Distinct readable states — burnout, inertia, meltdown, shutdown — with
  different somatic and behavioral signatures, so telemetry can identify
  which stress response is forming rather than collapsing them into "stress."
sources:
  - title: "The Unmasking Workbook for Autistic Adults"
    resource: "The Unmasking Workbook for Autistic Adults (Jessica Penot), ch. 6"
---

Autistic stress responses are not one phenomenon. Four community-named
states differ in **what to read**:

| State | Signature cues |
| --- | --- |
| **Burnout** | Extended exhaustion from pushing too hard or overwhelm; loss of skills/function; increased sensory aversions. |
| **Inertia** | Hard to stop once engaged, hard to start if not — "unending train" of same activity; often misread as laziness. |
| **Meltdown** | Capacity exceeded; regulation impossible; increased stimming, crying, echolalia, irrational thoughts, erratic behavior; often preceded by ignored burnout. |
| **Shutdown** | After burnout/meltdown or deep overload; speech and cognition slow; seeks quiet; nonresponsive or needs prompting; may sleep excessively yet still feel sleepy. |

Hyperconnectivity means more brain activity more of the time — exhausting;
[masking blunts signal](masking-blunts-signal.md) intensifies drain. What
exhausts each person varies.

**Alexithymia compounds risk:** difficulty identifying in-the-moment
feelings; hours or days to understand upset; rarely noticing burnout until
near meltdown — see [alexithymia](alexithymia.md) and
[delayed emotional processing](delayed-emotional-processing.md).

During meltdown/shutdown, rational self-assessment is unreliable — plan
assumes the person cannot think clearly. See [stress response signals](stress-response-signals.md)
for fight/flight/freeze framing; breakdown pattern catalogues live in
failure-modes — here the task is **which signature is forming now**.

Track with [stimuli-response journal](stimuli-response-journal.md) to learn
personal proneness (e.g. shutdown-prone vs meltdown-prone).
