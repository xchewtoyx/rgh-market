---
type: concept
title: Present-Moment Actionability Check
description: >
  Using physical location and time ("where are my feet right now?") to test
  whether an anxious state reading corresponds to a problem actionable in
  this moment, separating present threat from future worry.
sources:
  - title: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery"
    resource: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery (Sol Smith), ch. 8"
---

The brain does not reliably distinguish a problem happening now from one
that may happen later — a big assignment can trigger the same response as
being chased by a tiger. **Look at your feet** is a calibration pause: your
feet tell you where and when you are actually able to address a problem.

- Feet in bed at 3 a.m. → not the time to solve money, work, or school
  problems; no one expects you to.
- Worried about eviction → are your feet currently being forced out by a
  sheriff? If not, the worry targets something not currently happening.
- Feet at your desk during work hours → work; feet in the car → not work
  time for the thing you're anxious about.

This works best after mindfulness practice has widened the gap between self
and intrusive thoughts — see [masking blunts signal](masking-blunts-signal.md)
for how mask-driven anxiety replays others' voices. The check does not deny
real future risks; it calibrates whether the current arousal level matches
present actionability. Worrying weeks ahead rarely reduces the sting when a
crisis arrives, and coping in the actual moment differs from anything
imagined beforehand.

Pair with [micro interoceptive check-ins](micro-interoceptive-check-ins.md)
and operating from where you actually are rather than where you wish you
were — the real world does not care what you think it "should" be.
