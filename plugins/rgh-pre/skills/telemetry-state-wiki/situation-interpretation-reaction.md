When something happens, people usually notice two things: what happened and
how they reacted. The **A–B–C model** names a hidden middle step:

- **A (situation)** — the facts a video camera would record, without
  commentary.
- **B (interpretation)** — the running story, often just below awareness,
  that assigns meaning to A. Often taken as fact rather than hypothesis.
- **C (reaction)** — emotions, body sensations, and behavior.

Typical error: attributing C to A ("that situation made me angry") when B
actually drove C. Ambiguous events illustrate this sharply — waving to
someone who walks past without responding. In a good mood, B may be benign
("they didn't notice me"); in a low mood, B may be hostile ("they ignored
me on purpose"), cueing rumination and a very different C. Same A, different
B, different C. MBCT's walking-down-the-street exercise lists parallel
interpretations of one trigger — "He didn't acknowledge me" (upset), "What
did I do?" (worried), "Nobody likes me" (isolated), "Fine, do what you want"
(angry), "She was preoccupied" (concern) — showing **no right interpretation**;
variability makes it easier to see thoughts are not facts.

The inner monologue can trigger an entire story without realizing it — one
moment thinking about the friend, the next simply feeling alone. Learning to
see automatic thoughts as thoughts (not facts) gives freedom not to be swept
by the emotional cascade. Stance: curiosity, not immediate problem-solving.

For telemetry confidence:

- When a reaction feels "obvious" and automatic, pause for B — what story
  was added between fact and feeling?
- Compare B against A as literally as possible; vignettes in the source
  show bosses and families misread because Gaia and Jim treated harsh B
  stories as confirmed facts.
- A low mood systematically recruits the most negative B and then treats
  past "evidence" as proof — see
  [mood-colored interpretation](mood-colored-interpretation.md).

Pair with [heuristic substitution in state reading](heuristic-substitution-in-state-reading.md)
when the "situation" being assessed is actually a mood-colored substitute,
and with [rejection-sensitive dysphoria as state signal](rejection-sensitive-dysphoria-as-state-signal.md)
when ambiguous social data reliably triggers a hostile B.
