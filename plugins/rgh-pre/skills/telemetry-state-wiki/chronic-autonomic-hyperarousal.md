---
type: concept
title: Chronic Autonomic Hyperarousal
description: >
  A persistently elevated sympathetic or reduced parasympathetic baseline
  that can make "normal" days feel keyed-up and distort what counts as calm.
sources:
  - title: "Textbook of Autism Spectrum Disorders"
    resource: "Textbook of Autism Spectrum Disorders (Eric Hollander, Randi Hagerman, Casara Ferretti, Eds.), ch. 6"
---

Some people show chronic autonomic nervous system hyperarousal relative to
peers — for example lower respiratory sinus arrhythmia and higher resting
heart rate, reflecting a chronic biological threat response rather than a
momentary stress spike.

For establishing a personal [calm baseline state](calm-baseline-state.md),
this matters: what reads as "a bit anxious" may actually be someone's
habitual baseline, and what reads as calm to an observer may still sit above
a neurotypical resting level. Heart rate alone is a weak signal; heart rate
variability and reactivity patterns carry more information about autonomic
balance.

Chronic hyperarousal overlaps with high anxiety-disorder comorbidity in
autism, so a keyed-up reading may reflect trait-level arousal, situational
stress, or both — see [distinguishing anxiety from sensory overload](distinguishing-anxiety-from-sensory-overload.md)
before treating every arousal spike as one cause.

External measures such as electrodermal activity (skin conductance
reflecting sympathetic sweat-gland activity) and peripheral temperature
(nose temperature often decreases under stress-eliciting stimuli) can
sometimes corroborate or challenge a subjective reading, though device
noise and individual variation limit everyday use. When available, pairing
subjective check-ins with such markers can improve confidence calibration.
