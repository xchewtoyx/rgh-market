**Habits Scorecard** exercise:

1. List daily habits in sequence (wake, alarm, phone, bathroom, shower,
   teeth, dress, tea, …).
2. For each, mark **+** (good), **–** (bad), or **=** (neutral) for your
   current goal — context-dependent (daily bagel bad for weight loss, good
   for bulking).
3. At first, **only notice** — no changing yet. Observe without judgment:
   "Oh, how interesting that they would do such a thing," as if watching
   someone else.

Tie-breaker when hard to categorize: "Does this behavior help me become the
type of person I wish to be? Does this habit cast a vote for or against my
desired identity?" There are no absolute good/bad habits — only effective
ones that solve problems in the moment (smoking reduces stress now even when
net-negative later).

For telemetry: automatic routines often carry hidden state signals (phone
check, skipped meals, isolation) that go unread until inventoried. Pair
with [pointing-and-calling awareness](pointing-and-calling-awareness.md) to
lift specific items from the scorecard into conscious attention. See
[behavioral shift as emotional signal](behavioral-shift-as-emotional-signal.md)
when habit changes reflect mood rather than drive it.
