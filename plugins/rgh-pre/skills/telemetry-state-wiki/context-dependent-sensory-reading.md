title: Context-Dependent Sensory Reading
description: >
  The same sensory stimulus can read as pleasant or unbearable depending on
  social load, interest, and environment — so stimulus type alone is an
  incomplete state input.
sources:
  - title: "Knowing Why: Adult-Diagnosed Autistic People on Life and Autism"
    resource: "Knowing Why: Adult-Diagnosed Autistic People on Life and Autism (Autistic Self Advocacy Network), ch. 4"

---

Sensory response is not fixed per stimulus. The same noise or music can be
pleasant when alone and "at one with" it — crows cawing, industrial
machinery, diverse music — but intolerable when layered onto social demands
in a crowd. Baklava may trigger illness at a high-sensory wedding while
kataifi with the same ingredients is fine at home.

Sensory experiences also change day to day with situation and state of mind
— exquisite noise sensitivity during a test but enjoyment of loud video
game volume later. An individual can be over-responsive to some inputs and
under-responsive to others, and profiles differ person to person even under
the same diagnostic label.

For calibration, always pair **stimulus + context** when reading overload:

- Is social [calorie](social-calories.md) depletion active?
- Is the environment deliberately disorienting (malls, airports, Gruen-effect
  retail design)?
- Is interest high enough to absorb some load (museum vs noisy gathering)?

Misreading context-dependent sensory distress as generic anxiety or bad
mood leads to wrong responses — see
[distinguishing anxiety from sensory overload](distinguishing-anxiety-from-sensory-overload.md).

Advance warning of a sensory experience can convert it from unpleasant to
bearable or pleasurable; surprise contact or noise without warning reads worse.
