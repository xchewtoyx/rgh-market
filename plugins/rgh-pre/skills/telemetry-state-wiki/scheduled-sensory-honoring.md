Sensory needs vary by day and channel — after a busy office with overlapping
sound and bright light, quiet darkness or white noise may be required; on
another day, visual stims or crunchy textures may be sought. Hoping to
honor those needs spontaneously often fails when [unhelpful self-monitoring](unhelpful-self-monitoring.md)
and masking fill the available attention.

**Scheduled sensory honoring** treats sensory check-in as a form of
self-monitoring: a regular diary or calendar slot to ask which senses need
avoiding or seeking right now. Both sensory-avoiding behaviours (dark room,
noise-cancelling headphones, scent-free products) and sensory-seeking
behaviours (weighted blanket, crunchy foods, fairy lights) belong in the
plan — not only avoidance.

Autism is often framed as social disability, but many autistic people report
sensory processing as the bigger lived difference, with over- and
under-sensitivity across light, sound, smell, texture, and touch/pressure.
Mapping those patterns feeds [sensory profile mapping](sensory-profile-mapping.md);
scheduling honoring time makes the map actionable before burnout cycles repeat.
