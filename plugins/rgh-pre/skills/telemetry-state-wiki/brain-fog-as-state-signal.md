title: Brain Fog as a State Signal
description: >
  Cognitive dulling during burnout or overload — lost train of thought,
  word-finding failure, weakened imagery — that marks a state shift even
  when emotion is not clearly named.
sources:
  - title: "Knowing Why: Adult-Diagnosed Autistic People on Life and Autism"
    resource: "Knowing Why: Adult-Diagnosed Autistic People on Life and Autism (Autistic Self Advocacy Network), ch. 2"
---

Brain fog during autistic burnout can include inability to hold a train of
thought, memory loss, inability to access words (spoken or typed), and loss
of visual thinking or imagery. For people who rely on visual cognition,
losing that faculty is especially distressing and is itself a readable
state marker — not laziness or inattention.

Brain fog often co-occurs with "stripped down survival mode" that does not
look like acute crisis from the outside, which makes it easy to under-read
severity. The person may report only tiredness while cognitive channel
capacity has already collapsed.

Treat emerging brain fog as a **late or overload signal** — similar to
[delayed interoception](interoception.md) registering hunger or cold only
at the brink of breakdown. Recovery strategies that restore readable state
include [deep focus as restorative state](deep-focus-restorative-state.md)
and reducing self-imposed demands rather than pushing harder through fog.
