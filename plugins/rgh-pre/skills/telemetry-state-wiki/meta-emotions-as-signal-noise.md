title: Meta-Emotions as Signal Noise
description: >
  Feelings about feelings — guilt about depression, shame about anger —
  that layer a second emotion on top of a primary one and can block accurate
  reading of the underlying state.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition (Rhena Branch, Rob Willson), ch. 7"
---

**Meta-emotions** are feelings about your feelings — a "second helping" of
emotion from rigid demands about which feelings are acceptable. Common
example: guilt about depression, arising from self-imposed demands ("I
mustn't let others down," "I mustn't put strain on loved ones"). Typical
guilt-producing thoughts in depressed people:

- "I should be contributing more to the running of the home."
- "I must demonstrate love and care to my children."
- "My partner and children are worried about me, and I'm making them suffer."
- "I shouldn't be neglecting my friends."

For state reading, meta-emotions are **noise on the primary channel** —
you may read "guilt" when the underlying signal is sadness, depression, or
anger. Recognising meta-emotions matters because they can block work on the
primary emotional problem; resolving guilt about being depressed frees
attention to read the depression itself.

**Checklist when a reading feels tangled:**

- Have you named how you feel inside?
- Can you identify the theme of the emotion?
- How does the emotion lead you to behave — helpful or unhelpful urges?
- Is another emotion getting in the way (guilt or shame about anger,
  depression, or another primary emotion)?

See [affect labeling](affect-labeling.md) for naming practice and
[alexithymia](alexithymia.md) when the primary emotion itself is hard to
access.
