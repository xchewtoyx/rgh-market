---
type: concept
title: Recurring-Emotion Tracking
description: >
  Journaling a single recurring emotion over days or weeks — its physical
  signature, frequency, and context — purely to observe it and surface
  trigger patterns, without taking corrective action during the tracking
  period itself.
sources:
  - title: "That Little Voice in Your Head"
    resource: "That Little Voice in Your Head (Mo Gawdat), ch. 7"
---

Where a single [body scan](body-scan.md) or moment of
[affect labeling](affect-labeling.md) captures state at one point in time,
recurring-emotion tracking builds a baseline for a specific pattern over
a longer window. Method: pick one recurring emotion (e.g. anger, shyness,
boredom) and track it in a journal for days or weeks. For each occurrence,
note the physical signature felt in the body — see
[the physical signature of an emotion](emotional-physical-signature.md) —
compared against a [calm baseline](calm-baseline-state.md), and tally when
it happens. Over time, patterns in what precedes each occurrence become
visible (for example, noticing that a specific kind of comment reliably
precedes an anger response).

The observation period is explicitly just that — observation, with no
corrective action taken during it. Mixing observation with an attempt to
immediately fix or suppress the pattern makes both the tracking and the
correction less reliable; the tracking phase exists to establish an
accurate baseline first.
