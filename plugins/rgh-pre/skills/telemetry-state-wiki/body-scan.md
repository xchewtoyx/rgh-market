[Interoception](interoception.md) is trainable, and the body scan is the
core exercise for training it. Method: find a distraction-free space, start
at the toes, note sensations using neutral descriptors ("tingling,"
"tight," "relaxed") without judgment, and scan upward to the head. Over
time this builds links between bodily sensations and emotions (e.g.
noticing that anger reliably shows up as tense shoulders) — see
[the physical signature of an emotion](emotional-physical-signature.md).
The goal is noticing, not judging or fixing what is found.

If standard emotion or sensation words fail to fit what's noticed, invented
descriptors work too — for example describing a sensation by colour ("it
feels purple") rather than forcing it into a word that doesn't match. One
client related to emotions about other people via colours (safe = green,
dangerous = red, sensory-overload-inducing = purple); charting in that
personal vocabulary enabled workable recognition strategies. The point is to
build a personal sensation vocabulary, not to use a standardised one — see
[emotional vocabulary building](emotional-vocabulary-building.md).

**Grounding variant (top-down off).** Sit comfortably, let go of reason and
thought, focus on breathing, then scan region by region — feet, hands,
legs, arms, back, face — noting temperature, tension, softness, soreness;
then breathing quality, heartbeat, overall body state. Attach any word to
the resulting mood, including non-standard ones ("purple," "blank,"
"stupid"). Difficulty completing the exercise, or shutting down at the
mood-naming step, is itself informative about
[interoceptive](interoception.md) access and
[alexithymia](alexithymia.md) severity.

A full scan also functions as a way to establish what a calm, resting body
feels like, which is the reference point that makes later deviations
detectable — see [establishing a calm baseline](calm-baseline-state.md).
For a lighter-weight alternative when a full scan feels too intrusive or
effortful, see
[micro interoceptive check-ins](micro-interoceptive-check-ins.md).

**Variant: scanning for a specific emotion's signature.** The same
head-to-toe attention can be applied deliberately while recalling an
emotional memory, rather than only in a neutral resting state: notice
physical changes before and during the recollection, compared to the calm
baseline. Repeated across different remembered emotions, this builds a
personal catalogue of [physical signatures](emotional-physical-signature.md)
— useful when an emotion is present but hard to name, since the felt
pattern can be matched against the catalogue instead.

**A concrete scan sequence**, useful as a default ordering when none of the
above variants is specified: forehead tension, eyes, sinuses, throat, jaw
(noticing any teeth grinding), stomach, lung capacity (via one deep
breath), neck mobility, shoulders, back, legs, feet — noting sensations at
each stop without trying to fix anything, then repeating the full pass a
second time immediately after the first.

**Not relaxation training.** The intention is not to feel relaxed or calm —
that may or may not happen. The point is awareness of the body as it is.
Treating the scan as relaxation training revives striving: chasing pleasant
feelings makes them slip away. When peace arises, notice it experientially
knowing it comes and goes; when unpleasant sensation arises, breathe into it
and let it release on the outbreath without fixing. There is no "good" or
"bad" meditation as long as experience is met mindfully — restlessness,
itchiness, boredom, and self-judgment become data, not failure.

**Mind wandering.** Minds wander from breath and body — normal. Notice
where attention went, acknowledge without fighting, gently return. Over time,
smiling at wandering and redirecting replaces self-berating.

**MBCT spotlight sequence.** Move awareness methodically through legs, pelvis,
back, abdomen, chest, fingers, hands, arms, shoulders, neck, head, face —
then whole body and free breath. If falling asleep, prop head up, open eyes,
or sit rather than lie down.

**Kabat-Zinn lying-down scan.** Forty-five-minute systematic attention
through regions (any order — head-to-feet, feet-to-head, side-to-side):
breathe into a region on the inbreath, let it dissolve on the outbreath,
then move on. Non-systematic variant: focus whichever region dominates
(pain, tension) with openness — "profoundly healing" for cells, psyche, and
whole body. Reclaims the body as locus of being, not only "resident of the
head." See [emotional body regions](emotional-body-regions.md) for heart,
solar plexus, and throat metaphorical attention. Practice deliberately at
different times — floor, fields, under trees — not only at bedtime; attend
to body while falling asleep and waking.

**Walking anchor.** When sitting is impractical, see
[walking meditation as state anchor](walking-meditation-as-state-anchor.md).

**Informal practice.** Choose one routine daily activity and bring moment-to-
moment awareness each time. Forgetting to practice, and your reaction to
realizing you forgot, is itself practice data.
