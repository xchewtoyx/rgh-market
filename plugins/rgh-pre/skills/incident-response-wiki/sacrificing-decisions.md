title: Sacrificing Decisions
description: >
  Crisis decisions that foreclose ambitious options to establish a damage
  floor when time, information, and resources cannot support a fully
  optimal outcome.
sources:
  - title: "Resilience Engineering in Practice: A Guidebook"
    resource: "Resilience Engineering in Practice (Hollnagel et al., Eds.), Chapter 2 (Pariès, Hudson Ditching)"
---

Under crisis, Simon's satisficing degrades into **sacrificing**: bounded
rationality collapses under lack of time, knowledge, and resources. No
error-free course exists; even hindsight cannot crown a single "right"
answer given what was knowable in the moment.

A sacrificing decision **minimises disaster odds by deliberately
foreclosing** the most ambitious branch whose failure mode is intolerant —
establishing a bottom line damage class. Flight 1549 illustrates the
pattern twice:

1. **Ditching versus runway attempt.** Altitude and speed looked too low
   for La Guardia or Teterboro. Post-event simulation hours later suggested
   a runway might have been reachable — information unavailable in real time.
   The trade-off: Hudson ditching (certainly bad, possibly survivable)
   versus airport attempt (possible happy ending, near-certain catastrophe
   on failure). The crew chose the bottom line.

2. **Engine relight after ditching line secured.** Relight procedure could
   recover thrust but requires stopping an engine first, risking failure to
   restart and loss of envelope protection. With ditching already accepted,
   the crew attempted relight to reopen a better outcome — this time
   without the catastrophic-failure penalty of the first decision already
   locked in.

Sacrificing decisions pair with [defence in depth tactical
retreat](defence-in-depth-tactical-retreat.md): each line of defence
breached lowers sights and sacrifices higher ambitions to save what can be
saved. They require [acute emotional regulation](acute-emotional-regulation.md)
to execute under fundamental surprise and single-shot constraints — no
"try again" button.

In personal acute incidents, sacrificing may mean cancelling the ambitious
plan, accepting partial completion, or choosing the survivable bad option
over the glamorous risky one when information cannot catch up to pressure.
