title: Rumination Interrupt Runbook
description: >
  Short-term procedures to break an acute rumination feedback loop before it
  escalates into meltdown, shutdown, self-harm, or multi-day functional loss.
sources:
  - title: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery"
    resource: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery (Sol Smith), ch. 9"
---

Rumination is not problem-solving. It is an acute feedback loop: amygdala
signals danger → intellectualising and obsessing → body feels anxiety about
the thoughts → amygdala re-signals danger, escalating like electrical
impulses until shut off. Awareness alone will not stop it; continuing to
"think it through" strengthens the loop. Interrupt the brain rather than
reason further.

**Detection threshold:** if repeated thinking about something removes
enjoyment of life for a day or two, interferes with normal activities,
disrupts sleep, or causes crying, meltdown, or shutdown — that is rumination,
not useful processing. Revisiting a resolved misunderstanding with someone
and upsetting them again is another signal.

**Interrupt method 1 — max cognitive load:** Tetris, sudoku, complex math,
jigsaw puzzles, card tricks — timed pressure maxes load faster than untimed
options. The **Stroop effect** (naming ink colour of mismatched colour words
aloud) fights strong pattern-seeking pathways and is effective at the verge
of meltdown or self-harm.

**Interrupt method 2 — flood with endorphins:** running, jumping jacks, or
similar until breaking a sweat makes sustained rumination unlikely. Walking
is optimal for thinking, not calming, unless in greenery or as walking
meditation; sprinting may punch the nervous system harder for those used to
distance running.

**Interrupt method 3 — dopamine fix:** deliberately pleasurable activity,
avoiding anything resembling a previously overcome addiction (food, smoking,
alcohol if those were the person's disorders). Guilty-pleasure activities
without actual guilt: a treat, a favourite show, rearranging furniture —
anything that pulls attention out of the head.

Long-term tools (mindfulness built in calm periods, journaling to "complete
the circuit" by putting thoughts on paper) support recovery but do not work
as first-line interrupts when rumination is new or acute. See [post-incident
recovery](post-incident-recovery.md) for integration after the loop breaks.
When rumination persists with self-harm risk, follow [incident escalation
path](incident-escalation-path.md).
