Meltdown and shutdown are acute incidents in the [emergency operating
region](operational-mode-envelopes.md): loss of agency over how emotion
is processed, often after ignored [autistic burnout detection
thresholds](autistic-burnout-detection-thresholds.md). They are not medical
emergencies in the cardiac or panic-attack sense — they are explosions of
pent-up emotion — but they require runbook response, not reasoning or
shaming.

**Pre-planned response reduces frequency.** Andrew's case: severe meltdowns
with suicidal ideation during the episode — wanting to die to stop the
sensation, not because death was appealing. Once meltdown began he was
irrational and unreachable by reasoning. Tracking revealed that a pre-
planned closet retreat where his partner held him while he rocked ended
meltdowns sooner and resolved the suicidal ideation; consistent use reduced
meltdown frequency because having a coping plan lowered anxiety around the
sensations. He eventually went to his partner proactively before full
meltdown, and episodes became rare under profound stress only.

**Coping skills assume irrational state.** Design responses for a person
who cannot think rationally during the episode — most describe meltdown as
irrational, overwhelming, and inescapable with no universal rational exit.
Options that have helped clients include being held; going somewhere quiet,
dark, and alone; pre-agreed medication with a psychiatrist; sleep when alone
(if reachable). Pre-write the plan in calm periods via [autistic stress
state tracking](autistic-stress-state-tracking.md); execute it without
negotiating logic mid-episode.

**Operational fact:** once triggered, meltdowns and shutdowns **cannot be
stopped**. They must run their course until energy is spent. Do not attempt
to talk someone out of one, argue logic, or treat it as a tantrum to
extinguish. [Acute emotional regulation](acute-emotional-regulation.md)
techniques apply to bystanders and pre-incident rehearsal, not to halting
an episode already underway.

**Meltdown presentation:** loss of agency over emotional processing — emotion
overflows when the neocortex can no longer route it. Triggers include
feeling the reaction is irrational, chronic misunderstanding, bureaucracy
frustration, powerlessness spinning cause and effect, and isolation. Physical
signs may include limb movement, head-hitting, screaming, inability to
talk, falling to the floor, crying — intensely physical for many.

**Shutdown presentation:** an inward version common in people shamed for
childhood meltdowns — inability to talk or move, dissociation ("blackness
covering their eyes, ears filled with moss"), not fainting but similar,
with panic and sometimes internal screaming. Like meltdowns, shutdowns
cannot be stopped once started, though certain inputs can comfort toward
the end.

**Stabilisation during the episode:**

- Move somewhere **safe and private** — away from too many eyes. Public
  episodes draw shame and bystander anger; people of color face elevated
  risk during public meltdowns. Privacy is almost always the better option.
- Apply **individual regulation aids** — there is no universal fix. For
  some, deep pressure or being held helps; for others, darkness, silence,
  pain, or softness. Discover and pre-plan what works before the next
  incident. [Distress tolerance stabilisation](distress-tolerance-stabilisation.md)
  can halt escalation before full meltdown when safety is confirmed.
- A caring loved one present helps when available; do not force interaction
  if the person cannot respond.

**Aftermath:** relief and often a clear head, but also possible absolute
exhaustion and prolonged "cocooning." Sudden post-episode clarity can look
alarming to witnesses and tip into a fawning rebound — suppressing negative
emotion and over-committing to please others — so it is **not** a good time
to make decisions. See [post-incident recovery](post-incident-recovery.md).

When meltdown feelings include wanting to die to make the sensation stop —
not because death is appealing but because the episode is unbearable — treat
that as [incident escalation path](incident-escalation-path.md) territory,
not something to resolve in-wiki.
