title: Dangerous Coping Escalation Trigger
description: >
  When hidden addictive or self-destructive coping mechanisms surface during
  acute load or unmasking, treat identification and disclosure as an escalation
  step rather than solo management.
sources:
  - title: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery"
    resource: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery (Sol Smith), ch. 9"
---

During acute self-examination or unmasking, unhealthy coping mechanisms
developed to blot out rumination and feeling — substance use, disordered
eating, self-harm, gambling — may surface. These are not willpower failures;
they are veils separating a person from an inner life that has become
hostile. Autistics face elevated rates: roughly half deal with substance
abuse; roughly seventy percent with disordered eating; three times the
self-harm rate of non-autistic people. Suicide rates are higher across every
age group and gender.

**Detection during unmasking:** review family history before deep self-
examination — patterns develop suddenly and subtly and may be hidden even
from the person ("in your best interests"). Mask and addiction are like two
rooms in one house — the tidy room for guests and the room where mess from
cleaning the first gets stashed. Secrecy eventually becomes unsustainable;
common immovable-obstacle triggers include DUI, overdose, suicide watch after
cutting, financial ruin, or a textbook paragraph triggering decades of
flashbacks.

**Runbook response — escalate, do not solo:**

- Be sceptical of self-assessment that says the mechanism is under control.
- If an unhealthy coping mechanism is identified, **do not keep it secret**.
  "You cannot take care of it on your own; otherwise, you would have by now."
- Get help: therapist, social worker for safety-net resources, or call
  sympathetic contacts even when it seems unlikely they can help.
- Safety of self, family, and friends is the top priority during unmasking.

Stopping the mechanism may surface everything under the mask — stimming,
anxiety, shame, lack of friendships — which is incident aftermath, not
failure. See [post-incident recovery](post-incident-recovery.md) and [incident
escalation path](incident-escalation-path.md). Rumination driving the cycle
may need [rumination interrupt runbook](rumination-interrupt-runbook.md) once
the mechanism is addressed.
