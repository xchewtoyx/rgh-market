title: Error-Likely Climate Signals
description: >
  Local workplace conditions that correlate with acute incidents and near
  misses, detectable before a specific failure occurs, warranting referral
  or outside risk support.
sources:
  - title: "Resilience Engineering in Practice: A Guidebook"
    resource: "Resilience Engineering in Practice (Hollnagel et al., Eds.), Chapter 7 (Siemens Turbine Maintenance Case Study)"
---

An error-likely **climate** is a local, rapidly changeable set of workplace
conditions — shaped by upstream culture but sensitive to today's leadership,
resources, and crew — that raises the probability of significant loss even
before a particular error happens. Reason distinguishes climate from culture:
climates can shift quickly when management or circumstances change.

Field-service case studies correlated these climates with significant losses
and near misses:

- Leaders with a top-down or intimidating style
- Leaders closed to concerns, not encouraging questions
- Leaders disengaged from the actual work (not present where work happens)
- Unclear roles and responsibilities for the outage or incident structure
- Day-versus-night shift competition instead of coordination
- Crew not working as a team
- External party directing or overly involved in field scope
- Leaders unfamiliar with current practices or contract-crew culture
- Leaders not open to help

Workers trained to recognise these climates can refer the situation to
off-site management (anonymously if needed) or request a risk-management
specialist visit. Training only off-site professionals to ping failed in
one rollout: they either lacked full situational comprehension or, on
noticing trouble, jumped into helping within their own role instead of
referring up. Organisation-wide noticing training worked better — clerks,
front-line workers, project managers, and directors all scanning for
climate and [incident detection thresholds](incident-detection-thresholds.md).

When climate signals appear during an acute episode, treat them as
stabilisation inputs: reorganise communication, clarify roles, or
[escalate](incident-escalation-path.md) before the climate compounds an
already-critical situation.
