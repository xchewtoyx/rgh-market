title: Distress Tolerance Stabilisation
description: >
  A structured pause procedure to tolerate acute discomfort without escalating
  into meltdown, flight, or dissociation when basic safety is confirmed.
sources:
  - title: "The Unmasking Workbook for Autistic Adults"
    resource: "The Unmasking Workbook for Autistic Adults (Jessica Penot), ch. 9"
---

When anxious or uncomfortable and **basic safety is confirmed**, use this
stabilisation sequence before the situation escalates to [meltdown and
shutdown runbook](meltdown-shutdown-runbook.md) territory. Autistic people
may over-react to minor discomfort (bright mall lighting) and under-react to
major danger — reasoning cannot substitute for autonomic threat perception,
so the runbook focuses on tolerating and routing distress rather than
analysing it away.

**Step-by-step:**

1. Take five natural breaths, counting only — do not alter the breath.
2. Take three breaths with exhale longer than inhale (e.g. inhale 4 seconds,
   exhale 8 seconds).
3. Look around and label what you see; observe the uncomfortable emotion
   without acting on it or fleeing it.
4. Ask: "Am I having a meltdown?" — if yes, follow your **pre-written
   meltdown plan** from [meltdown and shutdown
   runbook](meltdown-shutdown-runbook.md).
5. Ask: "Am I reacting to a life event or a situation I can control?" — if
   controllable, change the situation (thermostat, drink of water); if not,
   use the meltdown plan and focus on what can be controlled. Anxiety and
   sadness can be natural signals, like physical pain — not every discomfort
   requires escape.
6. Allow the emotion to be felt; ask what it suggests might help; use your
   personal self-soothing list (call a friend, walk, sensory bag, pet an
   animal).

Pre-write the meltdown plan, controllable-things list, and self-soothing
list in calm periods. When trauma plus autism is in play, a workbook is not
enough — see [incident escalation path](incident-escalation-path.md) for
neuro-affirmative trauma therapy. Learning to respond to discomfort with
boundaries (not overriding danger signals trained away in childhood) prevents
some incidents from starting; that boundary work overlaps controls-design,
but mid-incident stabilisation belongs here.
