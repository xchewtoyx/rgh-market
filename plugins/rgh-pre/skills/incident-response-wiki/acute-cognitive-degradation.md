---
type: concept
title: Acute Cognitive Degradation
description: >
  Temporary loss of skills and executive function during an acute burnout or
  overload incident, distinct from permanent decline and requiring safe-mode
  operation rather than working harder.
sources:
  - title: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery"
    resource: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery (Sol Smith), ch. 7"
---

During an acute incident, capacity can drop sharply — parallel to childhood
"autistic regression" but occurring in adult burnout. This is [incident
detection threshold](incident-detection-thresholds.md) confirmation, not
evidence of permanent failure.

**Observable degradation** includes impaired long-, short-, and working
memory: names and numbers won't come, nouns stall, objects and cars are lost,
multi-character stories become unfollowable, books won't hold attention,
audiobooks become "a jumbled series of sounds" while zoning out repeatedly.
A person who prided themselves on tracking complex work from memory may fall
behind without realising it mid-task and assume termination is imminent.

**Runbook response:** do not respond by working harder — that often deepens
the incident. Shift to safe-mode operation: reduce load, accept temporary
skill loss as incident state rather than identity, and address underlying
depletion (often dopamine deficit rather than insufficient executive effort)
through lateral restoration — an unrelated pleasurable pursuit that
"turns down the volume" on panic and restores sense of capacity for less-
desired tasks. The head-on push fails when the real problem is depletion,
not effort shortage.

Recovery of work skills can follow without medical leave when the acute
phase is handled sideways rather than by doubling down. See [post-incident
recovery](post-incident-recovery.md) and [meltdown and shutdown
runbook](meltdown-shutdown-runbook.md) when degradation clusters with
episodic loss of agency.
