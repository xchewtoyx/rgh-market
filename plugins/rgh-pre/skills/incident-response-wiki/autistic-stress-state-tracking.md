  Deliberate self-monitoring to detect which acute stress state is approaching
  before meltdown, shutdown, or burnout fully engages.

[Incident detection thresholds](incident-detection-thresholds.md) fail when
internal-state monitoring is impaired. Alexithymia — difficulty identifying
and describing emotions — means many autistic adults do not realise how
upset or burnt out they are until already near [meltdown and shutdown
runbook](meltdown-shutdown-runbook.md) territory. Because meltdown,
shutdown, burnout, and inertia are distinct acute states with different
runbooks, deliberate tracking is a detection procedure, not steady-state
prevention.

**Four stress states to distinguish:**

- **Burnout** — extended exhaustion from pushing too hard or being
  overwhelmed, with skill loss and increased sensory aversion. See [autistic
  burnout detection thresholds](autistic-burnout-detection-thresholds.md).
- **Inertia** — inability to transition between activities; often misread as
  laziness. Can manifest as an unending train of the same activity.
- **Meltdown** — capacity exceeded; regulation impossible; may include
  stimming increase, crying, echolalia, irrational thoughts, erratic
  behaviour. Often preceded by ignored burnout periods.
- **Shutdown** — capacity pushed past coping, often after burnout or
  meltdown; speech and cognition slow; seeks quiet; may become nonresponsive
  or sleep excessively while still feeling tired.

**Stimuli/response journal:** track stimuli (loud noise, social interaction,
work hours, masking) and the response they produced — fatigue, irritability,
decreased mood, inertia when changing tasks. Goal: identify which stimuli
are most exhausting and which state you are most prone to enter.

**Mood/environment tracker:** log daily state, what happened, and other
factors (pain, social contact, hours worked, pleasurable activities).
Patterns emerge — e.g. more work hours move you toward burnout; a daily hug
is therapeutic — letting you add mood-improving activities when fatigue,
pain, or irritability accumulate, reducing meltdown and burnout frequency.

Tracking reveals when to lighten load before an incident; it does not
replace runbook response once an episode is underway.
