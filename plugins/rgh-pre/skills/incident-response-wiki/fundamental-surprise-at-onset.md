---
type: concept
title: Fundamental Surprise at Onset
description: >
  The initial disbelief and cognitive conflict when an acute incident
  arrives, even when the event type was anticipated at system level, and
  how to move through it into focused response.
sources:
  - title: "Resilience Engineering in Practice: A Guidebook"
    resource: "Resilience Engineering in Practice (Hollnagel et al., Eds.), Chapter 2 (Pariès, Hudson Ditching)"
---

Even well-anticipated system-level risks can arrive as **fundamental
surprise** at the sharp end within seconds — a major cognitive conflict
between mental models and present experience. First response is often shock
and disbelief, not immediate competent action.

Capt. Sullenberger on dual engine loss: "the worst sickening pit of your
stomach... My initial reaction was one of disbelief." ATC Patrick Harten:
"I simply could not wrap my mind around those words... I felt like I'd been
hit by a bus." Bird strikes were frequent, studied, and mitigated at
system level; simultaneous dual failure still felt impossible in the
cockpit.

The runbook implication: **expect denial at onset** and design transitions
that do not require instant full comprehension. Successful outcomes still
showed very fast operational comprehension after the initial beat —
option assessment globally right because there was no second chance.

Factors that bridge surprise to response:

- [Crisis rehearsal](crisis-rehearsal.md) and experience converting panic
  into focus ("I was not calm, but very focused")
- [Sacrificing decisions](sacrificing-decisions.md) once the situation is
  minimally parsed
- [Acute emotional regulation](acute-emotional-regulation.md) to prevent
  disbelief from freezing action

Fundamental surprise is a detection sub-phase: the incident has started,
but recognition lag is human and normal. Minimise wall-clock time in
disbelief; do not mistake initial denial for evidence the situation is not
serious.
