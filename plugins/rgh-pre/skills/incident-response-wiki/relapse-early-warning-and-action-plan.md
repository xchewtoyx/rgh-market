Post-incident and between-incident periods still need runbooks. A relapse is
not "back to square one" — it is a weed resurging in ground you already
know. Write the plan while cognition is clear; execute it when [incident
detection thresholds](incident-detection-thresholds.md) for your problem type
fire.

**Identify personal warning signs in advance.** Depression relapse examples:
pessimistic thinking about future coping; ruminating on past failures;
losing interest in family and friends; difficulty getting out of bed, wanting
to sleep more instead of chores or exercise. Compile high-risk triggers:
seasonal change, sleep deprivation, lack of exercise, hassles piling up,
reduced positive social contact; interpersonal strain with partner, children,
boss, or work conditions. OCD, anxiety, bipolar, and eating disorders may
need tenacious vigilance even when symptom-free.

**Three-step action plan structure (MBCT):**

1. **First step:** always pause — a breathing space acknowledging mind and
   body, bringing attention to breath, expanding to body. Depression demands
   specificity about what to do next; do not drift.
2. **Second step:** choose a gathered practice that helped before — a
   mindfulness track, class reminder, letter to self, or the statement that
   intense feelings now need the same response practiced then.
3. **Third step:** take action — especially pleasure or mastery activities
   that helped before, even when they feel futile now — broken into smaller
   parts or time-limited slices.

Some write and seal a letter listing actions to open only when depression
starts — pre-loading wisdom for a state where it may not otherwise be
accessible. **Waking vulnerability:** mornings are a high-risk window even
without depression history; for those with history, waking can mimic episode
symptoms and evoke relapse fear. Self-statement: "Just because I am depressed
now does not mean I have to stay depressed." Ask: "How can I best be kind to
myself right now?" and "I do not know how long this mood will last; how can I
best look after myself until it passes?"

**Weed-killing countermeasures (CBT):** challenge pessimistic thinking as
symptom not fact; interrupt rumination via [rumination interrupt
runbook](rumination-interrupt-runbook.md); keep meeting people despite
decreased interest; force out of bed and maintain an activity schedule.
Avoidance fertilises relapse — check whether skipped activities reflect
disinterest or fear. Crisis plan elements: consider GP/psychiatrist for
medication; tell a trusted person; reuse effective exercises; keep lifestyle
active. See [incident escalation path](incident-escalation-path.md) when
warning signs include self-harm planning.

Family and friends can hold a parallel plan phrased to anticipate resistance
— e.g. "I know you probably will not be keen on this idea but..." — with
agreed steps when they notice your early warning signs: mindful walking or
body scan; reminders of what helped in class or therapy; breathing space
leading into bodily focus, thought review, or considered action in simple
steps; reading something that reconnects with wiser mind. Review these plans
with supporters while cognition is clear.

When a sign of life anchor appears during dormancy — see [signs of life
detection](signs-of-life-detection.md) — fold it into the action plan as
something to return to when mood threatens relapse.
