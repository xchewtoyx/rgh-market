Training is authority — a release valve for uncertainty and fear.
Unfamiliarity with sights, sounds, and sequences is fixable; fixing it
raises tolerance for stress when the real incident arrives.

**Graded exposure (NASA).** Astronauts rehearsed launch day hundreds of
times — breakfast through airfield ride — in escalating exposures to every
cue short of actual liftoff, solving variables and removing uncertainty until
the sequence felt as natural as breathing. The question for high-reliability
roles shifts from "how skilled are you?" to "can you keep an even strain?"

**Simulator and edge-case training (aviation ACCOMPLI).** Young pilots
reported confidence in normal flight and rehearsed abnormal procedures but
not in surprises at the edges of standard operating procedures, non-stabilised
approaches under traffic pressure, interpreting procedures to context, or
managing interruptions. Suggested remedies: unbriefed simulator sessions,
exposure to realistic emotional examples from incidents, instructors who
teach judgment in unexpected situations, and solo-flight experience that
forces self-knowledge under surprise. Current syllabi often pre-brief each
emergency once, practise without surprise, debrief, and move on — producing
Mintzberg's **fallacy of predetermination**: so much emphasis on anticipated
events that outside-the-envelope situations feel impossible, though they
happen constantly.

**Mental simulation elsewhere.** Paediatric cardiac surgeons with the best
fatality and near-miss scores regularly practised "what if" mental
simulations of complications. Counter-factual thinking and situation-awareness
training showed measurable benefits in other domains.

**Rehearsed catechism (Epictetus).** Before facing a powerful threat,
rehearse while calm: exile, imprisonment, dishonour, and death were named
indifferent in school — do they change character now? No. What are the real
goods? Right exercise of choice and right use of impressions. The goal is
that acute confrontation feels smaller than preparation promised.

Rehearsal is preventive design that belongs upstream in steady-state
planning, but its incident-response payoff is immediate: when [incident
detection thresholds](incident-detection-thresholds.md) fire, rehearsed
procedures compete successfully against panic. Pair rehearsal with [acute
emotional regulation](acute-emotional-regulation.md) for the in-moment
stance once the script ends or the surprise exceeds it.
