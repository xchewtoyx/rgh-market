  Observable cues that autistic burnout has crossed from ordinary strain into
  an acute incident requiring load reduction, safe-mode operation, or escalation.

Autistic burnout is not a bad week. It is an acute incident that can last
three months to several years once fully engaged. [Incident detection
thresholds](incident-detection-thresholds.md) generalise to personal load;
these are the autistic-specific cues that the situation has crossed from
manageable masking load into something requiring a runbook response.

**Restraint collapse** is an early threshold signal — typically at end of
workday but sometimes extending for days: no energy for anything beyond
phone-scrolling, passive TV, minimal interaction, takeout instead of
cooking, staying up late in "revenge" for a stolen day without using the
reclaimed time meaningfully, and disbelief that others sustain hobbies or
social life after similar work. The mechanism: restraining and editing
thoughts and feelings for surroundings costs more cognitive energy than
expressing them; constant self-inhibition erodes the habit of connecting
with internal state at all — "a tide receding before a tsunami."

**Sensory intensification** is a second sign: light, noise, touch, crowds,
and smells become overwhelming; irritability rises with a short fuse before
losing temper or crying. Treat this as a signal to slow down and lighten
load before the full incident lands.

**Cognitive-emotional spiral** marks deeper threshold crossing: a feeling
of being "found out" as a fraud among "the healthy and normal," escalating
from "not good enough" through "unworthy" to catastrophic self-judgment.
Hyperbolic self-talk during acute lows is a reality-check cue that often
gets missed anyway — treat extreme self-assessment as a detection signal,
not accurate self-knowledge.

Burnout is frequently misdiagnosed as depression; depression treatment may
address symptoms without the underlying overload causes. When these
thresholds cluster, shift to [acute stabilisation
procedures](acute-stabilisation-procedures.md) and consider whether
[incident escalation path](incident-escalation-path.md) applies — over
two-thirds of autistic people report suicidal ideation during burnout-scale
load, and intellectualised "exit door" thinking still warrants erring on
the side of safety with self or a professional.
