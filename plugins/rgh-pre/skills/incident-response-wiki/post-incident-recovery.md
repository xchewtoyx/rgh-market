The incident is not over when the acute threat stops. [Resilience state
transitions](resilience-state-transitions.md) require detecting that the
abnormal condition has ended, reverting or redefining normal, and
absorbing resources released from emergency mode.

**Post-event can be harder than the event.** ATC Patrick Harten reported
the lowest point came after learning everyone survived — intrusive images
of tragedy persisted for weeks; regaining confidence took paid leave and
time. Cockpit crew were less affected than the controller, consistent with
models emphasising involvement in action, altruism, and group solidarity
as protective factors. Plan recovery time even when the operational outcome
was good.

**Return to normal** need not mean returning to the identical prior state —
the world may have changed — but durable performance at least at prior
quality. Organisations that handle recurring mass-casualty events may find
formal lessons-learned debriefs **lose value over time** as handling grows
routine; debrief is a tool, not a mandatory tail on every incident.

**Personal recovery framing (kintsugi).** Courage here is not pretending
to be unbreakable but **getting back up after being broken** — repairing
with visible seams rather than hiding damage, becoming stronger at broken
places, and including requesting help, changing course, and preparing
differently among legitimate recovery moves. Hemingway's line: those who
will not admit breakage are destroyed; many grow strong where they were
broken.

**Post-meltdown and post-shutdown recovery.** After [meltdown and shutdown
runbook](meltdown-shutdown-runbook.md) episodes, relief and clarity often
arrive together with absolute exhaustion and a need for prolonged
"cocooning." Sudden post-episode clarity can look alarming to witnesses and
tip into a **fawning rebound** — suppressing negative emotions and over-
committing to please others again. That rebound is not recovery; it is a
relapse pattern. Do not make commitments or major decisions in the clarity
window immediately after an acute episode. Public episodes compound recovery
because shame and lost dignity feed back into burnout — plan privacy during
episodes partly to protect the recovery phase.

Recovery completes the Westrum time axis (foresee, cope, **repair**) from
[Westrum threat situation types](westrum-threat-situation-types.md). It is
not steady-state prevention; it begins once stabilisation and escalation
have done their work.
