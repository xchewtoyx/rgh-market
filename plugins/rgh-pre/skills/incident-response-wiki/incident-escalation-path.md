Escalation is a runbook step, not an admission of defeat. It should be
stated plainly rather than implied.

**Organisational pattern (field service).** On observed risk-profile change,
the first recommended action is contacting the district service manager —
the person to whom on-site leadership reports — triggering a conversation
among district manager, regional director, and site leadership about what
help exists and what to deploy. The person closest to the situation,
customer, and workers makes the final action decision; a central support
group adds cross-site learning, pattern spotting, and mitigating-action
design. Escalation options in the menu include commercial support (freeing
the lead for logistics), logistics help (parts, people, tools), heightened
coordination (daily calls with helpers), and buffering specialists who
travel to high-complexity jobs.

**Clinical pattern (anaesthesia).** Calling a colleague is not automatic
when a protocol fails; it is a **pivotal decision** weighing availability,
time of day, and what function the call would serve. In unthought-of
situations — events outside the practitioner's preoperative envelope —
mobilising additional expertise is often the observable sign that the
system has left the prepared region. See [envelope trespass escalation
trigger](envelope-trespass-escalation-trigger.md).

**Personal and professional care.** Wiki runbook guidance is not a
substitute for professional or emergency medical care. When symptoms
include self-harm risk, inability to meet basic needs, psychosis, or
medical emergency, escalation means contacting appropriate professionals or
emergency services — stated as an explicit step, not left implicit.

**Depression and suicidal ideation.** Suicidal thoughts are common in
depression and do not necessarily mean acting on them — but hopelessness
about the future is a **symptom**, not a fact. When hopeless or defeated
feelings progress into **making plans** to end life, escalation is immediate:
regular doctor, or **988 or 911 in an emergency** — stated plainly, not
implied. Interim runbook steps while arranging professional help: recognise
hopelessness as symptom; commit to a defined treatment trial (e.g. six weeks)
as an experiment; tell a friend or family member; apply problem-solving to
problems currently viewed as hopeless. Complex depression (bipolar,
cyclothymic, dysthymic/persistent depressive disorder) requires appropriate
medication and specialist assessment — CBT techniques supplement but do not
replace that care.

**Autistic burnout and meltdown context.** Over two-thirds of autistic
people report suicidal ideation; during [autistic burnout detection
thresholds](autistic-burnout-detection-thresholds.md) some experience ending
things as an intellectualised "thought puzzle" — a hypothetical exit door —
rather than felt despair. That distinction does not reduce risk: be honest
with self or a professional about whether impulse tends toward self-harm or
toward locating locus of control, and **err on the side of safety either
way**. Acute reassurance that lows tend to pass does not replace escalation
when harm risk is present. During [meltdown and shutdown
runbook](meltdown-shutdown-runbook.md) episodes, wanting to die to stop
the sensation is a meltdown symptom, not a reason to skip professional help
if attempts or persistent ideation are in play.

Escalation pairs with [acute stabilisation
procedures](acute-stabilisation-procedures.md): stabilise what you can
locally, then escalate with a clear picture of what help is needed.
