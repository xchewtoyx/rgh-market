---
type: concept
title: Interconnectedness
description: >
  The claim that nothing exists in isolation — everything is embedded in
  complex webs of cause, condition, and flux — and that seeing this directly
  deepens appreciation and corrects the illusion of separateness.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 7"
---

We know from childhood that everything is connected — this happens because
that happened. No sunlight, no life. No water, no life. No parents, no you.
These relationships are not simple and linear but embedded in a complex web of
finely balanced interconnections — life, health, the biosphere as systems with
no absolute starting or end point. The futility and danger of letting thinking
make anything into an absolutely separate existence without mindfulness of
interconnectedness and flux: everything simultaneously contains, and is
contained by, everything else, and everything is in flux.

Awareness of impermanence can deepen appreciation — of life, people, food,
opinions, moments — because everything we contact connects us to the whole
world in each moment, and things are only here temporarily. "It makes now so
much more interesting. In fact, it makes now everything."

Mindfulness practice is the ongoing discovery of interconnectedness — not
reconnecting what was never isolated, but seeing that our way of seeing creates
and maintains separation. Eventually you may see it is not quite right to say
we are doing the threading; we become conscious of a connectedness that has
been here all the time. Steinbeck and Ricketts: man is related inextricably to
all reality, known and unknowable — all things are one thing and that one thing
is all things, bound by the elastic string of time. Pair with [karma as
accumulated tendency](karma-as-accumulated-tendency.md), [wholeness and
eachness](wholeness-and-eachness.md), and [spirituality as
wholeness](spirituality-as-wholeness.md).
