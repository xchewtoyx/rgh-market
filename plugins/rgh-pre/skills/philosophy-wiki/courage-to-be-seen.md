---
type: concept
title: Courage to Be Seen
description: >
  The claim that fear of embarrassment and public judgment — not only fear
  of physical danger — is a deficiency of courage that blocks destiny,
  original work, and self-actualization.
sources:
  - title: "Courage Is Calling: Fortune Favors the Brave"
    resource: "Ryan Holiday, ch. 7"
---

Jerry Weintraub and James Caan, classmates at the Neighborhood Playhouse, were
sent to buy tights for a Martha Graham dance class. Caan put them on; Weintraub
couldn't let himself be seen that way. Both succeeded, but only Caan realized
the shared dream of performing — his name as Sonny Corleone in *The Godfather*,
Weintraub's as producer. "This was the dividing line, the moment of truth."
The fear of being seen — embarrassment, self-consciousness, what other people
think — is not the fear that keeps a soldier from battle, but it is still a
deficiency of courage that costs us our destiny.

"There is no change, no attempt, no reach that does not look strange to
someone... To gamble on yourself is to risk failure. To do it in public is to
risk humiliation." Jerry Seinfeld: people rank public speaking above death —
at a funeral, most would rather be in the casket than giving the eulogy. The
Roman orator Crassus, even at the height of fame, felt "a tremor through my
whole thoughts, as it were, and limbs" before speaking, and was once so
"disheartened and incapacitated with fear" that a judge adjourned the hearing
to spare him — yet history remembers him because he pushed through. "It doesn't
care about our rathers." Prescription: "You put on the tights. You push through
the stage fright... You just learn to stop thinking about what they think.
You'll never do original work if you can't."

Epictetus: "If you wish to improve, be content to appear clueless or stupid."
Fleeing discomfort, standing in the back instead of the front, is fleeing
opportunity and actualization, not just success — "Who might we be if we
didn't care about blushing? What could we accomplish if we didn't mind the
spotlight?" See [courage to be yourself](courage-to-be-yourself.md) and
[parrhesia speaking truth to power](parrhesia-speaking-truth-to-power.md).
