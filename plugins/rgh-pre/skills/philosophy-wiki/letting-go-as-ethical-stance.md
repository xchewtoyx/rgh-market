type: concept
title: Letting Go as Ethical Stance
description: >
  The Buddhist-influenced claim that ceasing to cling — to outcomes, views,
  likes and dislikes — is a conscious choice to release into present moments
  as they unfold, prerequisite to clarity and wise action.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 3"
---

Letting go means just what it says: an invitation to cease clinging to
anything — an idea, a thing, an event, a particular time, view, or desire. A
conscious decision to release with full acceptance into the stream of present
moments as they unfold. Giving up coercing, resisting, or struggling, in
exchange for allowing things to be as they are without getting caught in
attraction or rejection — the intrinsic stickiness of wanting, liking, and
disliking. Like opening your palm to unhand what you have been holding.

This applies not only to outer events but to holding on with the mind — clinging
to narrow views and self-serving hopes. Becoming transparent to the strong pull
of likes and dislikes requires allowing fears and insecurities to play out in
full awareness — recognizing the lenses slipped unconsciously between observer
and observed. Stillness, insight, and wisdom arise only when you settle into
being complete in this moment, without seeking or holding on to or rejecting
anything.

Non-judging does not mean ceasing responsible action or that anything goes — it
means acting with greater clarity, balance, effectiveness, and ethics once you
recognize immersion in unconscious liking and disliking. Trust — confidence that
things can unfold within a dependable framework — stabilizes practice when not
based on naivete. Generosity, at the deepest level, is inward giving: sharing
the fullness of your being with no giver, gift, or recipient separable — only
the universe rearranging itself. These attitudes support [patience as
discipline](patience-as-discipline.md), [ahimsa (non-harming)](ahimsa-non-harming.md),
and [impermanence of self and non-attachment](impermanence-of-self-and-non-attachment.md).
