---
type: concept
title: Awareness Beyond Thinking
description: >
  The claim that awareness is not the same as thought — it lies beyond
  thinking yet makes use of it — and that sustained awareness reveals our
  fundamental nature as integrated and whole rather than fragmented like the
  thinking mind.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 4"
---

Thinking differentiates humans from other species and is miraculous, but
unchecked, thinking crowds out other precious facets of being — wakefulness is
often the first casualty. Awareness is not the same as thought: it lies beyond
thinking, though it makes use of it. Awareness is more like a vessel that can
hold and contain our thinking, helping us see and know our thoughts as thoughts
rather than getting caught up in them as reality.

The thinking mind is almost always fragmented — that is thought's nature. But
awareness, sustained moment to moment, reveals that our fundamental nature is
already integrated and whole, unlimited by the potpourri of the thinking mind.
Awareness is like a magical soup pot that cooks the chopped-up fragments of
experience — thoughts, feelings — into a whole without needing to actively do
anything. You let the fragments stir while holding them in awareness; whatever
comes up in mind or body goes into the pot and becomes part of the soup.

Meditation is not trying to change your thinking by thinking more — it is
watching thought itself; the watching is the holding. By watching thoughts
without being drawn into them, you learn something liberating about thinking,
becoming less a prisoner of thought patterns that are narrow, inaccurate,
self-involved, habitual to the point of imprisoning, and just plain wrong.

Thinking is a continual cascading waterfall; cultivating mindfulness means
finding a vantage point behind or beyond the waterfall — you still see and hear
the water, but you are out of the torrent. Practicing this way, thought patterns
change by themselves toward integration, understanding, and compassion — not
because you try to replace one thought with a purer one, but because you
understand the nature of thoughts and your relationship to them, so they serve
you rather than the reverse. This is distinct from [positive thinking is not
meditation](positive-thinking-is-not-meditation.md). See also [habitual
automaticity](habitual-automaticity.md) on the thought-stream's tyranny and
[ordinary consciousness as sleep](ordinary-consciousness-as-sleep.md) on
waking from automatic identification with thought.
