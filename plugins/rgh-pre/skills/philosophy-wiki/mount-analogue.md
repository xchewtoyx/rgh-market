  The metaphor that life itself is the mountain — the climb is the teacher,
  not standing at the top — and that teaching is sharing a progress report,
  not absolute truth, for those still on the path.

An Everest climb leader, asked whether an older veteran would get a summit
chance: "He may. But in the end, it's the mountain that will decide who will
climb it." There are outer mountains and inner mountains; you carry the whole
mountain inside yourself, the outer one as well as the inner. Sometimes you
search without finding it until sufficiently motivated and prepared to find a
way through — first to the base, then the summit.

The mountain climb is a metaphor for the life quest, spiritual journey, path of
growth: arduous difficulties along the way are the challenges needed to stretch
and expand boundaries. In the end, it is life itself which is the mountain, the
teacher. The climb itself is the adventure, not standing at the top — you
cannot stay at the summit; the journey up is not complete without descent and
stepping back to see the whole again from afar.

Rene Daumal's rule in Mount Analogue: before moving to your next encampment,
replenish the camp you leave for those coming after, and go back down to share
what you learned from farther up. That is all any of us do when we teach — a
progress report, a map of experiences, by no means absolute truth. We are all
on Mount Analogue together and need each other's help. Pair with [job on the
planet](job-on-the-planet.md) and [you cannot escape yourself](you-cannot-escape-yourself.md)
(work where you are, not only on imagined peaks).
