---
type: concept
title: Defiance Under Coercion
description: >
  The Stoic and practical claim that even under threat one retains the power
  to refuse wrong, resist tyranny, and withhold consent — because no one can
  make a person do wrong without their own participation.
sources:
  - title: "Courage Is Calling: Fortune Favors the Brave"
    resource: "Ryan Holiday, ch. 12"
---

"It has been said that a Stoic is someone who says 'Fuck you' to fate. That's
right. They resist. They fight." Commander Jeremiah Denton, forced into a
propaganda broadcast ten months into captivity, blinked Morse code throughout
the filmed interrogation, spelling T-O-R-T-U-R-E for the world to see — turning
his captors' own exercise into humiliation on the international stage.

The pattern generalizes to everyday coercion carrying an implicit "or else":
the corporate attorney invoking an NDA, the competitor threatening to bury your
business, the politician demanding submission, the official demanding you back
off. King Philip threatened a besieged Spartan garrison that if he breached the
walls he'd kill every one of them; the Spartans' reply was the single word
"If" — raw defiance, refusing to concede before being forced to. Frederick
Douglass and "Nelly" mustered this same defiance inside chattel slavery.
Ben Franklin's proposed motto for the new republic extended the principle:
"Rebellion to Tyrants is Obedience to God" — applied not only to tyrants but to
"bullies, liars, abusers, assholes, frauds, demagogues, cheaters, and bad
actors."

Courage's internal voice is a series of refusals: "Over my dead body," "Not if
I can help it," "I'm doing things my way, according to my own code, no matter
what you say." People are not powerless even under threat: "They can hurt you.
They can yell at you. They can do horrible things. But you are not powerless.
In fact, you have more power than you know." Charles de Gaulle told his British
allies, "I am too poor to bow" — refusing submission to enemies or friends
alike. Seneca's Hercules, in one of his plays: "If they can force you, then
you've forgotten how to die." Core claim: "No one can make a person do wrong.
We hold that power. It's just a question of how far we're willing to take it."
This connects [refusal to surrender](refusal-to-surrender.md), [parrhesia
(speaking truth to power)](parrhesia-speaking-truth-to-power.md), and the Stoic
[power of choice](stoic-inner-citadel.md) under conditions that seem to remove
all options except compliance.
