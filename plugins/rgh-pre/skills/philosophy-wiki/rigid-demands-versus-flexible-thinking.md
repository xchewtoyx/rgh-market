---
type: concept
title: Rigid Demands Versus Flexible Thinking
description: >
  The claim that demanding the world, yourself, or others "must" be a
  particular way blocks adaptation to reality and fuels emotional disturbance,
  while flexible preferences preserve energy for creative response.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "Rhena Branch and Rob Willson, ch. 25"
---

Core philosophies held about yourself, other people, and the world greatly
affect your ability to respond to negative events. People with flexible,
healthy philosophies are less prone to emotional disturbance and better able
to solve problems.

Making demands — "must," "should," "have to" — about yourself, others, and the
world is a fundamental problem because it limits adaptation to reality. Humans
are fallible and the world stays imperfect; insisting "It shouldn't be this way!"
leaves you irate, depressed, or anxious and less able to cope. Circumstances
can be desirable, preferable, even better if different, without having to be a
particular way. Accepting reality while striving to improve it where wise and
achievable saves energy for creative thought and action.

The same flexibility applies to approval: prefer being liked without believing
you need it, so self-opinion stays stable when criticized. Prefer romantic
relationship without believing they are essential to survival or lovability.
Prefer partners while believing you can survive singleness — linked to less
jealous possessiveness. This parallels [arguing with reality causes
suffering](arguing-with-reality-causes-suffering.md) and the [dichotomy of
control](dichotomy-of-control.md): stake peace of mind on what you can
influence, not on rigid demands that reality conform.
