---
type: concept
title: Accepting Unfairness and Uncertainty
description: >
  The claim that insisting life should be fair or certain blocks resilience
  and wise risk-taking, while accepting injustice and unknowability as
  features of existence frees energy for what you can still influence.
sources:
  - title: "CBT For Dummies, 4th Edition"
    resource: "Rhena Branch and Rob Willson, ch. 25"
---

People are treated unjustly with no rebalancing; bad things happen to nice
people and undeserved good fortune happens too. Life is unpredictable and
uncertain much of the time — that is just how life is. Insisting the world
should be fair and should give certainty will not get you fairness or
certainty. Accepting the reality of injustice and uncertainty increases
resilience and reduces anxiety about decisions and risk-taking. You can still
strive to play fair yourself while being less outraged when justice does not
prevail.

Healthy, productive people tolerate risk and uncertainty. Demanding certainty
or guarantees in an uncertain world reliably produces worry and inactivity.
The illusion of complete safety costs rewards, excitement, and new experiences.
Not knowing the future is grounds for calculated risks and experiments, not
avoidance or excessive reassurance-seeking. Making educated decisions while
accepting that one hundred percent certainty is rare and unnecessary reduces
undue anxiety. Risk is inherent to existence; living in an uncertain world
means learning to embrace uncertainty at the periphery of consciousness
rather than let it dominate.

This complements [fate thinking versus probabilistic thinking](fate-thinking-versus-probabilistic-thinking.md)
(scientific "how" over metaphysical "why") and [karma as accumulated
tendency](karma-as-accumulated-tendency.md) (consequences unfold from
conditions, not from a guaranteed just order).
