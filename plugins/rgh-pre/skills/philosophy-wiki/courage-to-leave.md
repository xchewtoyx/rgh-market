---
type: concept
title: Courage to Leave
description: >
  The claim that walking away from a dead end — emigration, a failed project,
  a bad marriage — can be as courageous as staying, and that enduring in the
  wrong thing is not wisdom but eventually cowardice.
sources:
  - title: "Courage Is Calling: Fortune Favors the Brave"
    resource: "Ryan Holiday, ch. 14"
---

Maria Giovanna, Frank Serpico's mother, emigrated from Italy alone at twenty-
seven, seven months pregnant, gave birth prematurely mid-crossing, arrived in
winter hemorrhaging and knowing no English, lost her baby, and was rescued a week
later by distant relatives. She did backbreaking factory work in Brooklyn for a
year awaiting her husband. Holiday frames leaving home — crossing oceans and
deserts, braving gunfire, prejudice, walls, uncertainty, for a dismal, naïve,
projected hope of a better life — as possibly "the most courageous thing a human
being can do." Goebbels called refugees "cadavers on leave" — mere bodies
fleeing, "not sending their best." Holiday's rebuttal: the boldness, gamble,
tenacity, and determination of immigrants is by definition a virtue we admire;
they are "indefatigable warriors," descendants of pioneers and explorers.

Getting up and leaving isn't limited to emigration: quitting a dead-end job,
calling it on a project one's sunk a life into, walking away from a political
party, deciding to divorce after unhappy years. Some people use bad
circumstances as an excuse to despair; others do something about it. In Plato's
dialogue with Laches, Socrates elicits the definition "Courage is a sort of
endurance of the soul" — but notes that enduring in the wrong thing, staying in
a foolhardy or impossible endeavor, isn't wisdom. Leaving is scary — the end of
something can feel like a kind of dying, and no one can promise the next place
or try will go better — but continuing the same thing in the same place over
and over is not just insanity but eventually a form of cowardice. What counts
is that someone is doing something, controlling what happens to them rather than
the reverse. No situation is hopeless, we're never without agency, and we can
always bravely pack up and move. This pairs with [courage to be yourself](courage-to-be-yourself.md)
when leaving is the price of integrity, and with the [dichotomy of control](dichotomy-of-control.md)
— you cannot control where you started, but you can choose whether to stay.
