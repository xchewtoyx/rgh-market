type: concept
title: Trust Your Own Experience
description: >
  The claim that meditative and philosophical paths deepen when you trust your
  own direct experience rather than deferring to external authority to anoint
  it with blessing — especially in the face of insecurity about doing it right.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 5"
---

There is really and truly no one right way to practice meditation, though there
are pitfalls to watch for. The counsel is to encounter each moment with
freshness, look deeply into it, then let go into the next moment not holding to
the last — each breath a new beginning, a new letting go, a new letting be.
Just as sunsets and moonrises speak for themselves, meditative experience
should not be forced or narrated — best not to speak at all in moments such as
these.

Core theme: trust your own direct experience rather than deferring to external
authority to validate it — the strong habit of wanting some authority to anoint
your experience (however minuscule, and it usually is) with his or her
blessing. Practicing this trust in the face of insecurity deepens the path over
time. Feet and breath both teach us to watch our step, to proceed mindfully, to
truly be at home in every moment, wherever our feet carry us.

Notice whenever the thought arises in meditation — Am I doing this right? Is
this what I should be feeling? Is this what is supposed to happen? Instead of
answering these questions, look more deeply into the present moment; hold the
question in awareness along with the breath and the full context of the moment.
Trust that This is it, whatever and wherever this is — keep a continuity of
mindfulness without analyzing, discoursing, judging, condemning, or doubting;
simply observe, embrace, open, let be, accept. Right now. Only this step. Only
this moment.

Seneca's [an eclectic approach to wisdom](eclectic-approach-to-wisdom.md) —
truth lies open; stand on your own feet; great thinkers left questions, not
answers — and [Buddha as awakened one](buddha-as-awakened-one.md) on being
yourself rather than adopting a belief system, extend the same stance. Pair with
[what is my way](what-is-my-way.md) on inquiry without condemning not-knowing,
and [courage to be yourself](courage-to-be-yourself.md).
