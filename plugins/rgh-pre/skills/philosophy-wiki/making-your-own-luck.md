---
type: concept
title: Making Your Own Luck
description: >
  The claim that moral opportunity is not merely handed out by circumstance
  — heroes actively seek the moments that test them, and true good fortune
  consists in character, intentions, and actions rather than external luck.
sources:
  - title: "Courage Is Calling: Fortune Favors the Brave"
    resource: "Ryan Holiday, ch. 17"
---

Sociologists speak of "moral luck": not everyone finds themselves positioned
to reveal a government secret, save a drowning person, or stand with the
suffragettes. Churchill described the Earl of Rosebery as living in "an age
of great men and small events" — but the Victorian period was full of massive
injustices: slavery, horrific factory conditions, colonial abuses, frequent
wars, mass starvation. "Where were these 'great' men?" Even Churchill was
late on the Bengal Famine and misheard Gandhi's moral call.

"A hero is a person who does what needs to be done, not just for themselves
but for others. That is, a hero makes their own luck — events don't just
happen to them." Shakespeare says we meet the time as it seeks us; Holiday
adds: "we have to seek the time and the moments too. We can't be passive. We
can't wait. We must reach out." Marcus Aurelius: "True good fortune is what
you make for yourself. Good fortune: good character, good intentions, and
good actions."

Most people won't face stakes as high as de Gaulle's or Frederick Douglass's
— and that's probably for the best — but it doesn't exempt anyone. "Just
because we don't hear a voice like Nightingale, doesn't mean we aren't called
to something, locally or globally. Curse the darkness or light a candle?
Bemoan the calm seas or build a motor? We will our purpose into existence.
We choose to be heroes. And if we don't, it's on us." See [agency as
effective truth](agency-as-effective-truth.md), [refusal of the
call](refusal-of-the-call.md), and [virtue as the sole
good](virtue-as-the-sole-good.md) for the Stoic grounding that externals are
not where happiness lives.
