title: Slavery to Passions and Craving
description: >
  The Stoic diagnosis that voluntary dependency on pleasure, ambition, or
  comfort is a deeper slavery than legal bondage — and that self-mastery
  begins with refusing anything that masters you.
sources:
  - title: "Discipline Is Destiny: The Power of Self-Control"
    resource: "Ryan Holiday, ch. 3"
  - title: "Letters on Ethics: To Lucilius"
    resource: "Seneca, Letters on Ethics (tr. Graver and Long), Letter 47"
---

Seneca asks: "Show me a man who isn't a slave... One is a slave to sex,
another to money, another to ambition; all are slaves to hope or fear." The
prescribed first step is to pull yourself out of ignorance of your dependency,
then "get clean" — from mistress, work-addiction, lust for power, or whatever
binds you. Legal slavery is shameful; voluntary slavery to passion is more
shameful still, because it is chosen. This is the psychological complement to
[Stoic arguments about the dignity of enslaved persons](stoic-slavery-and-human-dignity.md):
the same writer who insists a slave is "your brother, born of the same seed"
also insists that the master who serves lust or greed is the more truly enslaved.

Self-mastery is "an instinctive reaction against anything that masters us."
The specific object matters less than the structure: cigarettes, social-media
likes, cable news, phones — or sex, drink, promotion — what Buddhists call
*tanha*, thirst or craving. The problem is not the pleasure but the *need*;
need is the source of suffering. Addiction specialists describe the loss of
"the freedom to abstain" — once dependency forms, abstaining is no longer a live
option without struggle.

A diagnostic test: if this habit were invented today, would you start it,
knowing what it costs? Not knowing then does not excuse continuing to choose it
now. Eisenhower quit a decades-long cigarette habit cold at fifty-eight — "The
only way to stop is to stop, and I stopped." Richard Feynman quit an ordinary
drinking pull before it became dependency: "Nothing... should have that kind of
power over me." Talent destroyed by an internal parasite that must be "fed and
fed but was never full" is a collective loss, not only a personal one — works
never made, potential unfulfilled.

Breaking dependency is always available in principle: "it's never too late to
come back and beat it." What counts is what you do today. This theme belongs
under [temperance as self-mastery](temperance-as-self-mastery.md) and connects
to [freedom through frugality](freedom-through-frugality.md): strip need, and
autonomy returns.
