---
type: concept
title: Happiness Through Self-Knowledge
description: >
  The claim that outer sources of happiness — God, environment, other people —
  can help, but happiness will be no deeper than one's capacity to know oneself
  inwardly and meet the outer world from being at home in one's own skin.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 4"
---

"Inside" and "outside" are limited distinctions. In formal practice's stillness,
turning energy inward reveals that we contain the entire world in our own mind
and body. Extended inward dwelling teaches the poverty of always looking outside
ourselves for happiness, understanding, and wisdom. God, the environment, other
people can help us find happiness and satisfaction — but that happiness will
be no deeper than our capacity to know ourselves inwardly, to meet the outer
world from the comfort of being at home in one's own skin, from intimate
familiarity with one's own mind and body.

Dwelling in stillness touches what is most real and reliable in ourselves and
most easily overlooked. Being centered in ourselves, even briefly, against the
pull of the outer world, lets us be at home wherever we are, at peace with
things as they are, moment by moment. Kabir: "Don't go outside your house to see
the flowers... Inside your body there are flowers." Lao-tzu: "The heavy is the
root of the light. The unmoved is the source of all movement." Thoreau: "Direct
your eye right inward, and you'll find / A thousand regions in your mind / Yet
undiscovered."

Next time dissatisfaction arises or something seems missing, turning inward as
an experiment instead of reaching for distraction — sitting and entering
breathing for a few minutes without looking for anything — is a test of this
claim. Reside at the center of the world; let things be as they are. This
complements [spirituality as wholeness](spirituality-as-wholeness.md) on direct
experience of wholeness brought to whatever you are already doing, and [soul
versus spirit](soul-versus-spirit.md) on the downward journey through darkness
and eachness, not only upward transcendence.
