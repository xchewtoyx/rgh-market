---
type: concept
title: Obstacles Never End
description: >
  The Stoic acceptance that life offers no permanent obstacle-free state —
  each breakthrough only qualifies you for the next test, and the proper
  response is preparation to start again rather than hoping for an end.
sources:
  - title: "The Obstacle Is the Way"
    resource: "Ryan Holiday, ch. 35"
---

Virgil: "Live on in your blessings, your destiny's been won. But ours calls
us on from one ordeal to the next." "The great law of nature is that it
never stops. There is no end." Successfully navigating one obstacle only
produces the next — not bad news but the source of life's interest and
opportunity. Life is "a process of breaking through these impediments — a
series of fortified lines that we must break through." Each breakthrough
teaches something and builds strength, wisdom, and perspective; each time,
"a little more of the competition falls away," until what remains is "the
best version of you."

A Haitian proverb: "Behind mountains are more mountains." "Elysium is a myth.
One does not overcome an obstacle to enter the land of no obstacles." The
inverse of what people expect: "the more you accomplish, the more things will
stand in your way." The condition is permanent — "always fighting uphill.
Always against the wind" — and the prescribed response is acceptance and
preparation: "Get used to it and train accordingly."

Life is "a marathon and not a sprint": conserve energy, treat each battle as
one of many, and use each to make the next easier, while keeping perspective
that passing one obstacle only proves you're "worthy of more" — the world
keeps escalating challenges once it knows you can handle them. The behavioral
ideal: "Never rattled. Never frantic. Always hustling and acting with
creativity. Never anything but deliberate. Never attempting to do the
impossible — but everything up to that line." The obstacle is flipped by
"improving in spite of them, because of them." End state: "no longer afraid.
But excited, cheerful, and eagerly anticipating the next round." See
[discipline of the will](discipline-of-the-will.md) for beginning again after
each trial, [adversity as training ground](adversity-as-training-ground.md)
for why hardship is material rather than mere misfortune, and [amor
fati](amor-fati.md) for willing the succession rather than resenting it.
