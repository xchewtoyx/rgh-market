---
type: concept
title: Concentration Without Mindfulness Is Incomplete
description: >
  The Buddhist-influenced claim that one-pointed concentration (samadhi)
  stabilizes the mind but is incomplete without mindfulness — and that
  attachment to the pleasant stillness of concentration can arrest development
  and short-circuit wisdom.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 3"
---

Concentration is a cornerstone of mindfulness practice. Mindfulness is only as
robust as the mind's capacity to be calm and stable: without calmness, the
mirror of mindfulness will have an agitated and choppy surface and will not
reflect things with any accuracy. Concentration is the capacity of the mind to
sustain unwavering attention on one object of observation — in Sanskrit,
*samadhi*, one-pointedness, developed by continually bringing attention back
whenever it wanders.

The calmness from intensive concentration practice is steadfast, profound, hard
to disturb. Cultivating samadhi over an extended period is a great gift to
oneself. This stability forms the foundation for cultivating mindfulness:
without some degree of samadhi, mindfulness will not be very strong; the deeper
the concentration, the deeper the potential for mindfulness.

Deep samadhi is very pleasant — absorption in stillness and undisturbed
peacefulness, attractive even intoxicating — naturally leading one to seek this
peacefulness. But concentration practice, however strong and satisfying, is
incomplete without mindfulness to complement and deepen it. By itself it
resembles withdrawal from the world — closed rather than open, absorbed rather
than available, trancelike rather than fully awake — missing the energy of
curiosity, inquiry, investigation, openness, availability, engagement with the
full range of phenomena experienced by human beings. Mindfulness puts
one-pointedness and calm mind in the service of looking deeply into and
understanding [interconnectedness](interconnectedness.md).

Concentration can also be seriously limiting if one is seduced by its pleasant
quality and comes to see it as a refuge from life in an unpleasant and
unsatisfactory world — tempted to avoid the messiness of daily living for
tranquility. This is attachment to stillness which, like any strong attachment,
leads to delusion — it arrests development and short-circuits the cultivation of
wisdom. Pair with [everyday life as the path](everyday-life-as-the-path.md)
on not escaping chores for a separate practice, and [spirit of
inquiry](spirit-of-inquiry.md) on the curiosity mindfulness adds beyond stillness.
