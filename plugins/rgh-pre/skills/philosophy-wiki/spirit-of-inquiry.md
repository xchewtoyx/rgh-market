title: Spirit of Inquiry
description: >
  The claim that inquiry is fundamental to living mindfully — asking "What is
  this?" without expecting quick answers, carrying wonder through daily life,
  with no timetable for acknowledging what we most need to face.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 7"
---

The spirit of inquiry is fundamental to living mindfully. Inquiry is not just
problem-solving; it is a way to stay in touch with the basic mystery of life
and our presence here — questions like Who am I? Where am I going? What does it
mean to be? What does it mean to be a parent, worker, boss, or homeless person?
What is my karma? What is my way? What is my [job on the planet](job-on-the-planet.md)
with a capital J?

Inquiry does not mean looking for answers, especially quick ones from
superficial thinking. It means asking without expecting answers, just pondering
the question, carrying the wondering, letting it percolate, bubble, cook, ripen
— coming in and out of awareness like everything else. You do not need to be
still to inquire — inquiry and mindfulness can occur simultaneously in daily
life and are described as one and the same thing, come to from different
directions. You can hold What am I? or What is this? while fixing a car,
walking to work, doing dishes, or listening to your daughter sing.

Problems of all sizes arise constantly; the challenge is to meet them with
inquiry in the spirit of mindfulness — What is this thought, this feeling,
this dilemma? How am I going to deal with it? or even Am I willing to deal with
it or even acknowledge it? The first step is acknowledging there is a problem.
It might take forty or fifty years to even approach acknowledging some big
demons we carry — but maybe that is okay too. There is no timetable for inquiry.

Inquiry is like a pot sitting on your shelf, ready to do the cooking whenever
you are ready to take it down, put something in it, and heat it on the stove.
It means asking over and over: Do we have the courage to look at something,
whatever it is, and inquire, what is this? What is going on? — looking deeply
for a sustained period, questioning what is at the root, what is the evidence,
what are the connections, what would a happy solution look like. It is not so
much thinking about answers, although the questioning will produce a lot of
thoughts that look like answers. It really involves listening to the thinking
the questioning evokes, as if sitting by the side of the stream of your own
thoughts — the same posture described in [awareness beyond
thinking](awareness-beyond-thinking.md).

Seneca's [nightly self-examination](self-examination-as-daily-practice.md) and
[what is my way](what-is-my-way.md) on resting in not-knowing extend inquiry
into structured daily practice. Pair with [admitting you do not know](admitting-you-do-not-know.md)
and [philosophy as therapy, not theory](philosophy-as-therapy-not-theory.md).
