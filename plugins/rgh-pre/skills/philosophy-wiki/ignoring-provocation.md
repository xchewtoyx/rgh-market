type: concept
title: Ignoring Provocation
description: >
  The Stoic discipline of refusing to supply the second half of a conflict —
  enduring slights and insults when engagement would trade self-control for
  self-indulgence and abandon the task at hand.
sources:
  - title: "Discipline Is Destiny: The Power of Self-Control"
    resource: "Ryan Holiday, ch. 10"
  - title: "Discourses, Fragments, Handbook"
    resource: "Epictetus, Discourses, Fragments, Handbook (tr. Robin Hard)"
---

Arthur Ashe Sr. drove his employer William Thalhimer to a 1950s Richmond real-
estate negotiation where the seller insulted and bullied Thalhimer throughout.
Thalhimer quietly endured and completed the purchase. Asked why, he said: "I
came out here to purchase that piece of land. I got the piece of land. It
belongs to me now, not to him. That man can go on cursing me as long as he
likes. I have that land." The episode shaped Ashe Sr.'s pragmatism raising sons
in the segregated South — and, through him, Arthur Ashe Jr.'s capacity to
channel provocation productively.

Cato the Younger, struck in a Roman bath scuffle, refused the offender's
apology afterward — not from anger, but claiming: "I don't even remember being
hit." He declined to carry a grudge. James Peck, a white Freedom Rider, found
that refusing to retaliate stunned attackers into a lull — perhaps prompting:
"Why isn't this person consumed with hatred like I am? ... Are they actually
better than me?"

Core Stoic claim: "It takes two for a real conflict to happen... when we are
offended, when we fight, we are complicit. We have chosen to engage. We have
traded [temperance as self-mastery](temperance-as-self-mastery.md) for self-
indulgence." Aesop's lion harassed by a gnat — the discipline needed is the
ability to ignore, endure, and forget, applied not only to cruel provocations
but to unintentional slights from people we love. Ruth Bader Ginsburg's
mother-in-law: "It helps to be a little deaf" — guidance through fifty-six
years of marriage and twenty-seven on the Supreme Court alongside ideological
opposites like Antonin Scalia, also her close friend.

Practically: "They want you to get upset. Because if you're going to stop and
reply to every attack, as Lincoln said, you might as well admit defeat right
now. You'll never get anything done." Someone else's lack of self-control is
not justification for abandoning your own — it is "not a good look or a recipe
for success and achievement." Pair with [strict with yourself, tolerant with
others](strict-with-yourself-tolerant-with-others.md): inward rigor includes
refusing to be jerked into every battle another person invites.
