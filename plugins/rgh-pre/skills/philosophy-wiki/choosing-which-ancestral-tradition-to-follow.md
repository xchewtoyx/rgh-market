---
type: concept
title: Choosing Which Ancestral Tradition to Follow
description: >
  The claim that everyone inherits both courage and cowardice from their
  forebears — and that living well means consciously aligning with the
  lineage of those who endured and acted rather than those who shrank.
sources:
  - title: "Courage Is Calling: Fortune Favors the Brave"
    resource: "Ryan Holiday, ch. 7"
---

Everyone descends from people who endured terror, uncertainty, and disaster and
kept going — those who crossed land bridges, rebuilt after fires, fought in
armor, demanded rights, faced down mobs, escaped slavery, pushed science's
frontiers. "Their blood surges through your veins." James Baldwin to his
nephew: "You come from sturdy peasant stock... men who picked cotton and dammed
rivers and built railroads and in the teeth of the most terrifying odds
achieved an unassailable and monumental dignity."

Ancestors were also afraid — but it is a choice which tradition to follow:
courage or cowardice. "There were cowards too, but we can write them out of
the family tree." Xenophon rallying his stranded army in Persia: "I shall
remind you of the dangers which our fathers have also been through, so that
you realize that it is right for you to be brave and that with the help of
the gods, the brave find safety even from the worst difficulties." History is
"flesh and blood," not fairy tale — real, unexceptional people who fought
fate and survived long enough to set today's events in motion, literally or
figuratively our parents, having "passed us a baton." Florence Nightingale's
imagined legacy: "When I am no longer even a memory, just a name, I hope that
my voice may perpetuate the great work of my life." See [making your own
luck](making-your-own-luck.md) for taking up the baton actively rather than
passively inheriting it.
