type: concept
title: Three Domains of Concern
description: >
  Byron Katie's partition of all mental business into what is mine, what is
  yours, and what belongs to God — anything outside everyone's control — as
  an ethical map for where suffering begins when we live outside our own lane.
sources:
  - title: "Loving What Is, Revised Edition: Four Questions That Can Change Your Life"
    resource: "Byron Katie, ch. 1"
---

Katie identifies exactly three kinds of business in the universe: mine, yours,
and God's. God's business is anything outside your control, my control, and
everyone's control — earthquakes, floods, war, when I will die. Much stress comes
from mentally living out of one's own business — thoughts like "You need to get
a job," "I want you to be happy," "You should be on time" put you in someone
else's business; worry about uncontrollable events puts you in God's business.

Being mentally in someone else's business produces separation: when she thought
"My mother should understand me," she felt lonely, and traced every episode of
hurt and loneliness in her life to being in someone else's business. "If you are
living your life and I am mentally living your life, who is here living mine?
We're both over there." Believing you know what's best for someone else — "even
in the name of love" — is "pure arrogance," producing tension, anxiety, and
fear. Practical test: when stressed, ask whose business you're mentally in;
this can be clarifying to the point of laughter, and repeated practice can
reveal "you don't have any business, either, and that your life runs perfectly
well on its own."

The partition parallels the Stoic [dichotomy of control](dichotomy-of-control.md)
— what is up to us versus what is not — but adds an explicit middle category for
other people's choices and lives. Staying in your own business is not
indifference; it is the precondition for acting without the extra suffering
that comes from mentally governing what was never yours to govern. See also
[arguing with reality causes suffering](arguing-with-reality-causes-suffering.md)
for why crossing these lines usually means fighting what is.
