---
type: concept
title: Selfing
description: >
  The ingrained tendency to construct an "I," "me," and "mine" out of almost
  every experience and operate from that limited, mostly fantasy perspective —
  recognition of which loosens suffering without denying functional personhood.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 7"
---

"I," "me," and "mine" are products of thinking — an inevitable tendency to
construct out of almost everything an "I," a "me," and a "mine," then operate
from that limited perspective, mostly fantasy and defense. It happens almost
constantly but goes unnoticed, like the fish in water. Out of virtually any
moment, thinking constructs "my" moment, "my" child, "my" opinion, "my"
problem.

With sustained attention, the self turns out to be a construct of mind, hardly
permanent. Looking for a stable core "you," you find only more thinking —
name, age, gender, opinions are labels, not fundamentals. Ask "Who is the I who
is asking who am I?" and you arrive at "I don't know." The "I" is known only
by attributes, none constituting the whole person; it dissolves and reconstructs
moment by moment, feeling diminished and insecure because its existence is
tenuous — worsening the tyranny of being caught in "I, me, mine." Buddhist
view: there is no absolute separate self, only the ongoing process of selfing.

No-self does not mean being a nobody — everything is interdependent; you are
only you in relationship to all other forces and events. You are already a
somebody, but not reducible to name, age, beliefs, or fears. Practice is not
becoming a contemplative zombie but seeing things as they are, including
[interconnectedness](interconnectedness.md) and the non-solidity of conventional
selfhood. Take things a little less personally — maybe it just happened, not
aimed at you. The self is a chaos-theory strange attractor: patterned yet
unpredictably disordered, never repeating — hopeful, because you can stop
taking yourself so seriously. Recognizing selfing lets the universe make room;
excessive self-centered activity makes the dream world of self-oriented
thinking feel all too real. Pair with [impermanence of self and
non-attachment](impermanence-of-self-and-non-attachment.md) and [keep identity
small](keep-identity-small.md).
