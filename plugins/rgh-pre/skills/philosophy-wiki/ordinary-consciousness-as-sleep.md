---
type: concept
title: Ordinary Consciousness as Sleep
description: >
  The Buddhist-influenced claim that everyday waking awareness is severely
  limited — more like an extended dream than true wakefulness — and that
  systematic self-observation is how a person wakes from automaticity.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 1"
---

From the Buddhist perspective, our ordinary waking state of consciousness is
"severely limited and limiting," resembling an extended dream rather than
wakefulness. Meditation is framed not as relaxation but as waking from "this
sleep of automaticity and unconsciousness," giving access to a fuller spectrum
of conscious and unconscious possibilities that sages, yogis, and Zen masters
have explored systematically for millennia.

Diminished [present-moment intimacy](present-moment-intimacy.md) has
consequences: unconscious, automatic actions — often driven by deep-seated
fears and insecurities — accumulate over time and can leave a person feeling
stuck, out of touch, and unable to redirect energies toward satisfaction,
happiness, and health. The philosophical diagnosis parallels Stoic and other
traditions that locate disturbance in uninvestigated mental activity — see
[world as self-projection](world-as-self-projection.md) and [judgments, not
events, disturb us](judgments-not-events-disturb-us.md) — but Kabat-Zinn
emphasizes a broader claim about the quality of awareness itself: most of us
are not fully awake to our own lives.

The West's cultural orientation toward controlling and subduing nature rather
than honoring that we are an intimate part of it may need counterbalance from
views that systematic self-observation of our own nature and the nature of our
minds can lead to greater satisfaction, harmony, and wisdom — a complement to
reductionist and materialist frames, neither particularly "Eastern" nor
mystical. Thoreau in New England in 1846 saw the same problem with the ordinary
mind state. Waking up is the shared project across [Buddha as awakened
one](buddha-as-awakened-one.md), [mindfulness as the art of living](mindfulness-as-art-of-living.md),
and the ongoing work of [spirit of inquiry](spirit-of-inquiry.md).
