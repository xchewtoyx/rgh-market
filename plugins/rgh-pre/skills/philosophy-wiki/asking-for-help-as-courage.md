title: Asking for Help as Courage
description: >
  The Stoic claim that needing and requesting aid is not weakness but a
  soldier's duty mid-mission — and that refusing to ask isolates people and
  blocks the help structures designed for exactly their situation.
sources:
  - title: "Courage Is Calling: Fortune Favors the Brave"
    resource: "Ryan Holiday, ch. 7"
  - title: "Meditations"
    resource: "Marcus Aurelius, Meditations (tr. Gregory Hays)"
---

Frontline responders run toward danger, parents subordinate their needs to
their children's, and the cheerful hold up others — but even these people
need to be able to ask for help themselves. The Stoics, though strong and
dutiful, were not superhuman and had to ask for help too, and doing so was
itself an act of courage. Marcus Aurelius: "Don't be ashamed to need help.
Like a soldier storming a wall, you have a mission to accomplish. And if
you've been wounded and you need a comrade to pull you up? So what?"

Asking for help is not seeking exemption or unfair advantage: "You're looking
for a hand, not a handout... You're taking advantage of the opportunities and
the protections that were designed for precisely the situation you're in."
Examples of people afraid to ask: the addict afraid to admit powerlessness,
the executive with imposter syndrome, the depressed mother afraid to demand
support, the veteran hiding the true cost of heroism — "We are afraid to open
up... and so all of us feel more alone." Asking is generative: the student who
asks a question learns; the friend who reveals vulnerability strengthens the
friendship; the employee who admits an overloaded workload triggers a hire;
the person who speaks about shame done to them can propel society into action.

"We are as sick as our secrets. We are at the mercy of fears we dare not
articulate, paralyzed by assumptions we refuse to put to the test." "So ask
now, right now, while you have the courage. Before it's too late... Ask for
help. It's not just brave, it's the right thing to do." See [unity over
self](unity-over-self.md) for how connection diminishes the grip of private
fear, and [stoic cardinal virtues](stoic-cardinal-virtues.md) for courage as
one facet of integrated excellence rather than lone self-sufficiency.
