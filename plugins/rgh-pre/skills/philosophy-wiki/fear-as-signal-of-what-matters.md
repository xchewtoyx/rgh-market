---
type: concept
title: Fear as Signal of What Matters
description: >
  The counterintuitive claim that fear often points toward the morally
  necessary action — not as counsel to obey, but as a compass indicating
  where conscience and growth lie.
sources:
  - title: "Courage Is Calling: Fortune Favors the Brave"
    resource: "Ryan Holiday, ch. 6"
---

Theodore Roosevelt hesitated before inviting Booker T. Washington to dine at
the White House in 1901 — the first time a Black man dined as a sitting
president's guest — fearing backlash from southern relatives, the press, and
voters. He went ahead precisely because of the fear: "The very fact that I
felt a moment's qualm on inviting him because of his color made me ashamed
of myself, and made me hasten to send the invitation... I am very glad that I
asked him, for the clamor aroused by the act makes me feel as if the act was
necessary." Holiday's rule: "Our fears point us, like a self-indicting arrow,
in the direction of the right thing to do."

This is not the same as letting [involuntary scare versus chosen
fear](involuntary-scare-versus-chosen-fear.md) rule the day. Fear alerts us
to danger but also to opportunity — "that tinge of self-preservation is the
pinging of the metal detector going off. We may have found something. Will
we ignore it? Or will we dig?" Fear always has a reason for inaction, so
absent occasional hesitation, we're probably not pushing ourselves enough.
Washington's own risk accepting the invitation was severe — Senator Benjamin
Tillman's response was "We'll have to kill a thousand niggers to teach them
their place again."

Eleanor Roosevelt's formulation — do the thing you cannot do — captures the
pattern: it is almost always the thing you should do when something tells you
you're not allowed. The proverb says not to take counsel of your fears;
perhaps the better move is to listen closely and then do the opposite. See
[courage against despair and nihilism](courage-against-despair.md) for how
Roosevelt's caring was the wellspring of such acts, and [refusal of the
call](refusal-of-the-call.md) for when fear blocks the summons entirely.
