---
type: concept
title: Spirituality as Wholeness
description: >
  The claim that spirituality is direct experience of wholeness and
  interconnectedness — not a separate domain of life but the inner quality
  brought to whatever you are already doing.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 8"
---

"Spirit" derives from the Latin spirare, to breathe — inbreath as inspiration,
outbreath as expiration. In the deepest sense, the breath itself is the
ultimate gift of spirit, though its virtues stay unknown while attention is
absorbed elsewhere. The work of mindfulness is waking up to vitality in every
moment; in wakefulness, everything inspires and nothing is excluded.

Kabat-Zinn avoids the word "spiritual" in clinical and secular settings not
because meditation is not spiritual practice, but because the word's connotations
are inaccurate, incomplete, and frequently misguided — entangled in belief
systems and unconscious expectations that block people from hearing that genuine
growth is possible. He prefers "consciousness discipline." When patients call
their stress-clinic experience the most spiritual they ever had, he hopes the
insight grows rather than becoming an endpoint — practice is not about getting
anywhere else, not even to pleasant spiritual experiences.

A working sense of spiritual: experiencing wholeness and interconnectedness
directly — seeing individuality and totality as interwoven, nothing separate
or extraneous. Doing science is spiritual; so is washing dishes. It is the
inner experience that counts, and you have to be there for it; all else is mere
thinking. Watch for self-deception, grandiosity, and impulses toward
exploitation — much historical harm came from people attached to one view of
spiritual truth, or hiding behind spirituality to harm others. Narrow,
literalist views place spirit above the "gross" domain of body, mind, and
matter, letting people use spirit-ideas to run from life. This complements
[soul versus spirit](soul-versus-spirit.md): wholeness without tyrannical
flattening of difference, and descent through darkness as well as ascent toward
unity.
