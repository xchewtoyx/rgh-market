title: Courage as Gateway to Every Good
description: >
  The claim that fear-driven conformity, if universalized, would freeze all
  progress — and that what we want in life and what the world needs lies on
  the far side of courage, which remains a choice we can wield or refuse.
sources:
  - title: "Courage Is Calling: Fortune Favors the Brave"
    resource: "Ryan Holiday, ch. 7"
---

A 1944 Army Life handbook: fear before battle is normal, "the last step of
preparation," and the first fight is with yourself before you can fight the
enemy. Fear has physiological logic — self-preservation keeps you alive — but
the real question isn't whether fear has a benefit, it's what the world would
look like if everyone acted purely from fear all the time: "It would be hell."
George Bernard Shaw: "the reasonable man adapts himself to the world... progress
depends on the courage of the unreasonable man."

Fear-driven maxims ("the tall poppy gets cut down," "go with the grain," "you
can't beat City Hall") are self-serving but safe — yet if universally followed,
good would never triumph over evil and nothing would ever improve. Courage is
a matter of choice: "We choose what voice we will listen to. We choose whether
we'll play it safe, think small, be afraid, conform, hide, or be cynical...
whether we look down over the side of the narrow bridge and turn back — or keep
going." Courage is optional, but "we can't escape the fact that it is the thing
upon which every other good thing depends. What we want in life, what the world
needs — all of it is on the other side of fear. All of it is accessed through
courage, should we choose to wield it."

See [stoic cardinal virtues](stoic-cardinal-virtues.md) for courage as the
form every virtue takes at the testing point, [courage against despair and
nihilism](courage-against-despair.md) for the worldview that makes wielding it
worthwhile, and [involuntary scare versus chosen
fear](involuntary-scare-versus-chosen-fear.md) for distinguishing useful alarm
from fear as a way of living.
