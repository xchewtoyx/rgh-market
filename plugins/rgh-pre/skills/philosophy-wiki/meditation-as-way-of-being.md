title: Meditation as a Way of Being
description: >
  The claim that meditation is better understood as a Way of living in harmony
  with things as they are — not a technique — including the ability to know
  something about where you are now even when you do not know where you are
  going.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 4"
---

Meditation is better understood as a Way of being, living, listening, and
walking the path of life in harmony with things as they are — not a technique
applied from outside ordinary life. Part of this is acknowledging that at
crucial times you genuinely do not know where you are going or even where the
path lies, while still being able to know something about where you are now —
even if that knowing is lost, confused, enraged, or without hope.

Conversely, we can become trapped believing too strongly that we know where we
are going, especially when driven by self-serving ambition — a blindness from
self-furthering agendas that leaves us thinking we know when we do not know as
much as we think. The Grimm tale of the water of life, retold in [admitting you
do not know](admitting-you-do-not-know.md), dramatizes arrogant brothers who
presume they know the way and become stuck, versus the youngest who openly
admits he has no idea and receives help.

The journey is uniquely yours — you cannot imitate someone else's and stay true
to yourself. Can meditation practice be an intimate part of this way of being?
Can you commit to lighting your path with [mindfulness as the art of
living](mindfulness-as-art-of-living.md) and awareness? Pair with [everyday
life as the path](everyday-life-as-the-path.md) — Snyder's claim that daily
chores are not distractions from practice but the path itself — and [what is
my way](what-is-my-way.md) on sustained inquiry into direction.
