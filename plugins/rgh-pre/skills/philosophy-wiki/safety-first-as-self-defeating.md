---
type: concept
title: Safety-First as Self-Defeating
description: >
  The ethical claim that pursuing personal safety above principle is not
  only cowardly but strategically futile — it forfeits credibility and
  often fails to secure even the survival it promises.
sources:
  - title: "Courage Is Calling: Fortune Favors the Brave"
    resource: "Ryan Holiday, ch. 6"
---

Cicero's operating principle during Caesar's rise was to "choose the stronger
side and reckon the safe course the better." He praised Caesar once victory
was clear — even censoring his own eulogy for fallen friends so as not to
offend the dictator — then after Caesar's assassination again waited to see
how things sorted rather than acting rightly. The irony: Mark Antony killed
him anyway, and even survival would have cost all credibility — he lost both
life and any chance at being a hero. Theodore Roosevelt's blunt verdict:
"Never yet was worthy adventure worthily carried through by the man who put
his personal safety first."

The contrasting case is Vernon Johns, civil rights pioneer and pastor, hauled
before a white judge over a controversial sermon. The safe (Ciceronian) choice
was to apologize and self-censor. Johns refused: "Everywhere I go in the
South the negro is forced to choose between his hide and his soul. Mostly,
he chooses his hide. I'm going to tell him that his hide is not worth it."
Self-interest's promised safety is a lie — "you're a fool if you think
staying on the good side of bad people is a safe bet." Most people die in
bed; "getting up and getting active is much safer." Peter Drucker: it is
risky to try to make the future, but riskier not to try, because someone
else eventually will and you'll be left behind. Dylan Thomas: each day is
"always touch-and-go." No amount of ass-covering changes that we are
"fugitives from the law of averages... marked for death from birth."

Putting safety first endangers you — of being forgotten, of never coming
close, of being complicit. Courage here is not certainty about outcomes but
the ability to say: "I'm not sure, but I will get through it with my soul
intact. I will make the best of it. I will not be afraid." See [living
consistently with your values](living-consistently-with-your-values.md) and
[parrhesia speaking truth to power](parrhesia-speaking-truth-to-power.md)
for the public-virtue side of the same refusal.
