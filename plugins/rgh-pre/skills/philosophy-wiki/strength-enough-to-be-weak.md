---
type: concept
title: Strength Enough to Be Weak
description: >
  The claim that performed invulnerability is often weakness masking fear,
  while appropriate openness about vulnerability is where genuine strength lies.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 3"
---

Strong-willed, accomplished people often seem invulnerable to inadequacy,
insecurity, or hurt — isolating and ultimately painful. Others collude in the
"Rock of Gibraltar" persona that does not allow real feelings; one can get out
of touch with true feelings behind image and aura. This happens often to
fathers and people in positions of power.

Thinking of yourself as "getting stronger" through practice creates a similar
trap: believing in the part of the supremely invulnerable, correct meditator
who has everything under control can arrest development without knowing it. We
all have an emotional life; walling ourselves off at our own peril. When you
notice building an image of invincibility, special knowledge, or wisdom from
meditative experience, ask whether you are running from vulnerability, grief,
or fear. If you are truly strong, there is little need to emphasize it. Better
to direct attention where you fear most to look — allowing yourself to feel,
even cry, not having opinions about everything, not appearing invincible but
appropriately open. What looks like weakness is actually where strength lies;
what looks like strength is often weakness, an attempt to cover fear.

Experiment with being soft when impulse is to be hard, generous when impulse
is withholding, open when impulse is to shut down. When grief or sadness
arises, let it be; notice labels attached to crying or vulnerability. Trust
your deepest strength: to be present, to be wakeful. Pair with [megalopsychia
(greatness of soul)](megalopsychia-greatness-of-soul.md) and [strict with
yourself, tolerant with others](strict-with-yourself-tolerant-with-others.md).
