title: Mindfulness as the Art of Living
description: >
  The claim that mindfulness is waking up and living in harmony with oneself
  and the world — paying attention on purpose, in the present moment, and
  nonjudgmentally — as a practical route to the art of conscious living rather
  than a belief system or religion.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 1"
---

Mindfulness is described as an ancient Buddhist practice with profound
relevance for present-day life — not because one must become a Buddhist, but
because it concerns waking up, examining who we are, questioning our view of
the world and our place in it, and cultivating appreciation for the fullness of
each moment. "Most of all, it has to do with being in touch."

Core definition: mindfulness means paying attention in a particular way — on
purpose, in the present moment, and nonjudgmentally. This nurtures greater
awareness, clarity, and acceptance of present-moment reality, waking us to the
fact that our lives unfold only in moments. Missing too many of those moments
risks missing what is most valuable and the depth of our possibilities for
growth and transformation.

Kabat-Zinn frames mindfulness as the art of conscious living and the heart of
Buddhist meditation, yet insists it has little to do with religion except in
the most fundamental sense — an attempt to appreciate the deep mystery of being
alive and to acknowledge being vitally connected to all that exists. Committing
to paying attention openly, without falling prey to likes and dislikes,
opinions and prejudices, projections and expectations, opens new possibilities
and frees us from the straitjacket of [habitual automaticity](habitual-automaticity.md).

The practice is a systematic process of self-observation, self-inquiry, and
mindful action — not cold, analytical, or unfeeling. Its tenor is gentle,
appreciative, and nurturing; an alternative term offered is "heartfulness."
Mindfulness offers a simple but powerful route for getting unstuck,
reconnecting with one's own wisdom and vitality, and taking charge of the
direction and quality of life — relationships within family, work, the larger
world, and most fundamentally one's relationship with oneself as a person. Pair
with [present-moment intimacy](present-moment-intimacy.md) on the cross-tradition
root of this orientation and [spirituality as wholeness](spirituality-as-wholeness.md)
on waking to vitality in every moment.
