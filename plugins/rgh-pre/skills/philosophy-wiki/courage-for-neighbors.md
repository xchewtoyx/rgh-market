---
type: concept
title: Courage for Neighbors
description: >
  The claim that moral bravery often matters most in ordinary reciprocity with
  those nearest us — showing up, trying, and refusing indifference even when
  dramatic rescue fails.
sources:
  - title: "Courage Is Calling: Fortune Favors the Brave"
    resource: "Ryan Holiday, ch. 13"
---

The Kitty Genovese case became "the touchstone of indifference" — neighbors
reportedly ignoring cries — but the chapter reframes the story around Sophia
Farrar, who dressed and rushed out without a thought for her own safety, found
Kitty dying, stayed with her, comforted her, and shouted for paramedics until
Kitty bled out en route to the hospital. Sophia's words: "I only hope that she
knew it was me, that she wasn't alone." She was never named in the famous
story and never sought credit. Kitty and Sophia were real friends — ordinary
reciprocal neighborliness, not abstract heroism.

George Eliot (Middlemarch): "People glorify all sorts of bravery except the
bravery they might show on behalf of their nearest neighbors." Courage is not
only the single dramatic moment — it is trying even when the outcome is
failure. Sophia did not save Kitty, but she showed up, called for help, and
comforted her — "and that's what heroes do." Miep Gies, hiding and supplying
Anne Frank's family for months: "Any attempt at action is better than
inaction. An attempt can go wrong, but inaction inevitably results in failure."
Varlam Shalamov refused, under gulag pressure, to become a foreman whose
freedom would come at other prisoners' expense — pride in never having sold
anyone out.

Personal success and modern "freedom" is not freedom from caring about others —
not permission to be indifferent to the people nearest you. This is neighborly
[megalopsychia (greatness of soul)](megalopsychia-greatness-of-soul.md) at
domestic scale: love overriding self-preservation for someone you know, not
a stranger on a pedestal. Pair with [focus scrutiny on your own courage](focus-scrutiny-on-your-own-courage.md)
inward and [making others better by example](making-others-better-by-example.md)
outward — the question is not only who runs into burning buildings but who
crosses the hall when someone might be in trouble.
