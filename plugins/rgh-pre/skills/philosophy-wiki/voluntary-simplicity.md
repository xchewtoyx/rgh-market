title: Voluntary Simplicity
description: >
  The intentional choice to do less, acquire less, and go fewer places so
  you can be more present — countering the impulse to fill time and miss
  nourishment at a deep level.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 3"
  - title: "Discipline Is Destiny: The Power of Self-Control"
    resource: "Ryan Holiday, ch. 3"
---

A recurring impulse squeezes another task into the moment — just this phone
call, just stopping off here — regardless of direction. It does not care what
it feeds on, as long as it is feeding: scavenging to fill time, conspiring with
mind to keep you unconscious, lulled in fog, missing breakfast while the belly
is full, unavailable to others and to the play of light, smells, energies in
the room.

Voluntary simplicity counters such impulses: intentionally doing only one thing
at a time and being fully present — a walk, time with the dog in which you are
really with the dog. Going fewer places in one day rather than more, seeing
less so you can see more, doing less so you can do more, acquiring less so you
can have more. Within family life and work, ample opportunity to choose
simplicity in small ways: slowing down, not answering the phone, not acquiring
on impulse, sitting and doing nothing for an evening, going to sleep early.

Saying no to keep life simple is an arduous discipline, well worth the effort
but tricky — real needs and opportunities must still be met. A commitment to
simplicity in the midst of the world is a delicate balancing act, always needing
retuning. The notion keeps you mindful of what is important — an ecology of
mind, body, and world in which everything is [interconnected](interconnectedness.md)
and every choice has far-reaching consequences. Choosing simplicity adds deepest
freedom. Thoreau: "Simplify, simplify." Complements [freedom through
frugality](freedom-through-frugality.md) and [non-doing and wu wei](non-doing-and-wu-wei.md).
