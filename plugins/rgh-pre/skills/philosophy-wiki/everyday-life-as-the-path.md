title: Everyday Life as the Path
description: >
  Gary Snyder's claim that ordinary daily chores are not distractions from a
  separate formal practice but our path — apprenticed to reality itself, we
  must master the twenty-four hours.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 6"
---

Gary Snyder, in *The Practice of the Wild*: "All of us are apprenticed to the
same teacher that the religious institutions originally worked with: reality.
Reality-insight says... master the twenty-four hours." Ordinary daily chores —
herding children into the car pool, changing the filter, wiping noses, going to
meetings, washing dishes, checking the dipstick — are not distractions from more
serious pursuits, not a set of difficulties to escape so one can get to a
separate formal practice on a path: it is our path.

This reframes [meditation as a way of being](meditation-as-way-of-being.md) and
[spirituality as wholeness](spirituality-as-wholeness.md): doing science is
spiritual; so is washing dishes. The inner experience counts, and you have to
be there for it. [Non-doing and wu wei](non-doing-and-wu-wei.md) is not
withdrawal from ordinary life but full presence within it. Zen's coming full
circle to the ordinary — being free and easy in the marketplace — pairs with
[soul versus spirit](soul-versus-spirit.md) on not using spirit-ideas to run
from life's messiness.

Pair with [present-moment intimacy](present-moment-intimacy.md) and [job on the
planet](job-on-the-planet.md): the place to start is right here; the best time
is now.
