---
type: concept
title: What Is My Way
description: >
  The sustained inquiry into one's Way with a capital W — who am I, where am I
  going, what do I truly love — without seeking fixed answers, resting in
  not-knowing until the question winds up doing you rather than you doing it.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 5"
---

The hypocrisy of telling children they cannot always have their own way while
adults want exactly the same thing, just less honestly. Fairy tales warn about
being granted three wishes. A Maine backwoods joke — "You can't get there from
here" — reframed: you can only get there if you are fully here.

The central inquiry is What exactly is my way? — my Way with a capital W —
alongside related questions rarely lingered on: Who am I? Where am I going?
What path am I on? Is this the right direction for me? What is my yearning, my
path? What do I truly love? The instruction is explicitly not to seek an
answer: we do not have to come up with answers, nor think that there has to be
one particular answer. Better not to think at all. Instead, persist in asking
the question, letting any answers formulate and go of themselves — watch,
listen, note, let be, let go, and keep regenerating the question.

The intention is to remain open to not-knowing, including allowing I don't know
and relaxing into that rather than condemning oneself for it. Sustained inquiry
eventually takes on a life of its own: it will wind up doing you rather than you
doing it, and permeates the bland, the humdrum, the routine with new vitality.
The human life span is a mythic hero's journey — the fairy tale, the Arthurian
quest — the trajectory between birth and death that no one escapes; we only
work with it differently. Take on the challenges we meet to find our own way
and ultimately not only have it but, more importantly, live it.

planet](job-on-the-planet.md) on What is my job with a capital J?, and
