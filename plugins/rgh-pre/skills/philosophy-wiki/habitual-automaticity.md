title: Habitual Automaticity
description: >
  The claim that habitual unawareness and automaticity are exceedingly
  tenacious forces working against wakefulness — requiring sustained inner
  commitment to keep capturing moments in awareness, yet yielding enlightening
  and liberating work when met.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 1"
---

While [mindfulness as the art of living](mindfulness-as-art-of-living.md) may
be simple to practice, it is not necessarily easy. The forces working against
mindfulness — our habitual unawareness and automaticity — are exceedingly
tenacious, strong and unconscious enough that sustained inner commitment and a
certain kind of work are needed just to keep capturing moments in awareness.

Yet this work is intrinsically satisfying because it reconnects us with
habitually overlooked aspects of our lives. It is enlightening: seeing more
clearly areas of life we were out of touch with or unwilling to look at —
including grief, sadness, woundedness, anger, and fear, and also joy,
peacefulness, and happiness that often pass fleetingly and unacknowledged. It
is liberating: leading to new ways of being in one's own skin and in the
world, freeing us from habitual ruts. It is empowering: opening channels to
deep reservoirs of creativity, intelligence, imagination, clarity,
determination, choice, and wisdom.

We tend to be particularly unaware that we are thinking virtually all the time.
The incessant stream of thoughts leaves very little respite for inner quiet; we
leave little room just to be. Our actions are too often driven by thoughts and
impulses running through the mind like a coursing river or waterfall rather
than undertaken in awareness — we get caught in the torrent, which can submerge
our lives. Meditation means learning how to get out of this current, sit by its
bank and listen, learn from it, and use its energies to guide us rather than
tyrannize us. This does not happen by itself; it takes energy. See also
[awareness beyond thinking](awareness-beyond-thinking.md) on the distinction
between awareness and the thought-stream, and [ordinary consciousness as
sleep](ordinary-consciousness-as-sleep.md) on waking from automaticity.
