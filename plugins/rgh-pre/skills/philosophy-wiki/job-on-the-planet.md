title: Job on the Planet
description: >
  Buckminster Fuller's reframing of vocation as asking what the planet needs
  done that you know something about and probably will not happen unless you
  take responsibility — living as if your life were a gift to be spent in
  service of the whole.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 7"
---

At thirty-two, after business failures, Buckminster Fuller contemplated suicide
at Lake Michigan. Instead he decided to live from then on as if he had already
died that night — no longer worrying personally how things worked out, free to
devote himself as a representative of the universe. The rest of his life would
be a gift. He continually asked: "What is it on this planet that needs doing
that I know something about, that probably won't happen unless I take
responsibility for it?" — following where that question led.

Kabat-Zinn frames this as working for humanity as an employee of the universe
at large: you still modify your locale through who you are and what you do, but
it is no longer merely personal — part of the totality of the universe
expressing itself. The personal inquiry: "What is my job on the planet with a
capital J?" or "What do I care about so much that I would pay to do it?" If the
honest answer is "I don't know," keep asking — across decades it can lead
places conventions and self-limiting beliefs would not. It may not change what
you do but can change how you see and hold it. The place to start is right
here; the best time is now.

Fuller's maxim: what seems to be happening at the moment is never the full
story. The honeybee seeks honey but is simultaneously nature's vehicle for
cross-pollination — [interconnectedness](interconnectedness.md) as fundamental
principle; nothing is isolated. Emerson: insist on yourself; do that which is
assigned to you. Pair with [mount analogue](mount-analogue.md) on the life
quest as mountain, not summit possession.
