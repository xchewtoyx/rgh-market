---
type: concept
title: Soul Versus Spirit
description: >
  The archetypal distinction between spirit's upward quest for unity and
  transcendence and soul's downward journey through multiplicity, darkness,
  and eachness — both needed for a fully developed human life.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 8"
---

Drawing on archetypal psychology, spirit has an upwardly rising quality —
ascent above earthbound qualities toward the non-material, light, oneness,
nirvana, heaven, cosmic unity. Soul moves downward: the symbolic descent
underground, into rock, water, darkness, complexity, ambiguity, eachness and
suchness. Fairy tales across cultures are mostly soul stories — quests through
shadow, grief, and metamorphosis — not spirit stories of pure transcendence.

Unity is a real and rare human experience, but the quest for spiritual unity,
especially in youth, is often nine parts wishful thinking and only one part
direct experience — transcendence can be a great escape, high-octane fuel for
delusion. Zen emphasizes coming full circle to the ordinary and everyday:
"being free and easy in the marketplace," grounded anywhere, neither above nor
below, simply and fully present. "If you meet the Buddha, kill him" — any
conceptual attachment to Buddha or enlightenment misses the mark.

The mountain meditation is not only peak loftiness but groundedness at the base,
rooted in rock, willing to sit with fog, rain, depression, angst, confusion,
pain. Rock symbolizes soul; water pools downward, dark, mysterious, receptive.
The fully developed human embodies the unity of soul and spirit, up and down,
material and non-material. No truly spiritual work lacks soul; no truly soulful
work lacks spirit. Perhaps the most spiritual thing any of us can do is look
through our own eyes, see with eyes of [wholeness and eachness](wholeness-and-eachness.md),
and act with integrity and kindness. Pair with [spirituality as direct
experience](spirituality-as-wholeness.md), which warns against narrow categories
that exclude science, parenting, the body, or ordinary washing of dishes.
