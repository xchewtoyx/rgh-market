type: concept
title: Karma as Accumulated Tendency
description: >
  The Buddhist-influenced claim that every effect has antecedent causes and
  every action seeds further tendencies — not fixed destiny, but a momentum
  that can imprison or be refashioned only in the present moment.
sources:
  - title: "Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life"
    resource: "Jon Kabat-Zinn, Part 7"
---

Karma means "this happens because that happened" — B is connected to A;
every effect has an antecedent cause and every cause an effect that is its
measure and consequence. A person's karma is the sum total of direction in
life and the tenor of things that occur around them, shaped by antecedent
conditions, actions, thoughts, feelings, sense impressions, and desires.

Karma is often confused with fixed destiny. Better understood, it is an
accumulation of tendencies that lock you into particular behavior patterns,
which themselves generate further similar accumulations. It is easy to become
imprisoned by karma and to locate the cause always elsewhere — other people,
conditions beyond control — rather than within yourself. But it is not
necessary to remain a prisoner of old karma; you can make new karma, and
there is only one time to do it: now.

Practice anger and isolation in a relationship for forty years and you wind up
imprisoned in anger and isolation. Apportioning blame is unsatisfactory;
ultimately it is mindlessness that imprisons — getting better at being out of
touch, more stuck in lifelong habits of not-seeing, reacting, and blaming.
Everything has consequences whether police catch you or not: "We are always
caught. Caught in the karma of it. We build our own prisons every day."
Many who made destructive choices "never knew choices were there" — an instance
of unawareness (ignorance): not recognizing how unexamined impulses, especially
colored by greed or hatred however justified, can warp a mind and a life.

Changing karma means stopping what clouds mind and body and colors every
action — not merely "doing good deeds" but knowing who you are and that you
are not your karma at this moment, aligning with how things actually are,
seeing clearly. The instrument is your own mind, through which all thoughts,
feelings, impulses, and perceptions translate into action. Only what happens
now happens later; if there is no clarity or compassion now, it will not
magically appear later under stress. Kabir: "The idea that the soul will join
with the ecstatic just because the body is rotten — that is all fantasy. What
is found now is found then." This pairs with [impermanence of self and
non-attachment](impermanence-of-self-and-non-attachment.md) and [living
consistently with your values](living-consistently-with-your-values.md) as
the ethical demand that present orientation shapes every future moment.
