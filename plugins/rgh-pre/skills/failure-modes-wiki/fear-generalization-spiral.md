## Recognizing it

- **Trigger**: an identified core fear that the mind treats as needing
  comprehensive protection against.
- **Mechanism/marker**: rather than addressing the one fear, the mind
  scans outward — through memory for past pain, through imagination for
  possible future scenarios — and adds each plausible connection as a new
  flagged threat. The scope of what feels dangerous widens over successive
  passes, even though nothing new has actually happened.
- **Progression if unaddressed**: each additional layer of flagged threat
  is meant to increase safety but instead increases the number of things
  that can trigger a felt sense of danger, so the person ends up feeling
  more threatened, not less, the more thoroughly they've tried to protect
  themselves. The compounding structure of flagged fears becomes an
  unreachable standard of "safety" that ordinary reality is chronically
  measured against and found wanting, producing persistent background
  unease regardless of what is actually happening.
- **What it is commonly confused with**: this looks from the inside (and
  to others) like appropriate thoroughness or caution — "considering all
  the risks," "not being caught off guard." The distinguishing marker is
  the outcome: genuine risk assessment narrows toward specific, actionable
  concerns; this pattern instead keeps expanding the set of things flagged
  as dangerous without producing any corresponding protective action, and
  measurably increases felt vulnerability as it runs.

## Distinguishing marker from a rumination spiral

Both patterns are self-reinforcing, but their shape differs. A [rumination
spiral](rumination-spiral.md) deepens: the same thought gets replayed with
increasing embellishment or catastrophizing. This pattern widens: a single
root fear spawns new, only loosely related fears across memory and
imagination, whether or not any one of them is dwelt on repeatedly. The two
can co-occur — a widening set of fears each capable of triggering its own
deepening replay — but recognizing which shape is active clarifies whether
the more useful question is "why does this keep coming back" (depth) or
"why does this keep spreading to new things" (breadth).
