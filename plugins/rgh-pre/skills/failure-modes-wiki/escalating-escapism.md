## Recognizing it

- **Trigger**: an unresolved negative thought or source of distress that
  hasn't been addressed at its root — the escape activity targets the
  discomfort of thinking about it, not the thing itself.
- **Mechanism**: the escape works by suspending thought altogether (fun,
  distraction, stimulation), not by producing a thought that actually
  resolves the distress. Relief is genuine but temporary — it lasts only as
  long as the activity holds attention, and the underlying thought resumes
  once it ends.
- **Progression if unaddressed**: because the root cause is never touched,
  the same distress returns and needs silencing again — and, like drug
  tolerance, the same dose of escape produces less relief over time,
  pushing toward larger or more intense forms of distraction to get the
  same effect. The escalation path runs from mild diversion toward
  progressively more extreme or risky pursuits, at the far end including
  substance use, as the person searches for something strong enough to
  quiet the thought stream.
- **What it is commonly confused with**: genuine happiness or contentment,
  because both can look and feel pleasurable in the moment. The
  distinguishing marker is durability after the activity stops: contentment
  reached by actually resolving or reframing the underlying thought
  persists once the activity ends; relief from escape evaporates as soon as
  attention returns to the original thought, which is why it must be
  repeated — and escalated — indefinitely rather than being needed once.

## Distinguishing marker from occasional healthy pleasure

Not all use of pleasurable distraction is this pattern. A single deliberate
use of a healthy pleasure to interrupt a spiraling negative thought and
create space to think more clearly is a different, bounded use — the
difference is whether the activity is a one-off circuit-breaker used
occasionally, or a repeated, escalating substitute for ever addressing the
source of the distress. The same underlying dynamic appears in [emotional
avoidance](emotional-avoidance.md), applied specifically to the use of
pleasure/stimulation as the avoidance mechanism.
