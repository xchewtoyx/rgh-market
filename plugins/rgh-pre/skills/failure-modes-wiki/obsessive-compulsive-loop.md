## Recognizing it

- **Obsession**: a persistent, unwanted thought, image, doubt, or urge that
  intrudes and triggers distress — reaches clinical level when it causes
  significant distress, interferes with life, and is present more than an
  hour a day.
- **Compulsion/ritual**: observable actions (checking, washing) or mental acts
  (repeating phrases, counting) performed in response to obsessions that do
  not actually help — attempts to eliminate the thought, reduce danger, or
  reduce discomfort. Give immediate but non-lasting relief, requiring
  repetition.
- **Avoidance**: actions to avoid triggering the obsession (e.g. avoiding
  sharp objects near others, avoiding discarding empty cleaning products).
- **Maintenance mechanism**: avoidance, checking, washing, reassurance-seeking,
  comparing, readjusting, and repeating are short-term distress reduction that
  maintains the problem long-term — the "solution becomes the problem." Doubling
  a checking ritual often *increases* worry frequency, demonstrating the ritual
  is part of the problem, not protection.
- **Stress flare pattern**: OCD described as a stress response — "mental
  eczema" — flaring with stress, illness, fatigue, hormonal change, or even
  positive change (moving house, holiday). Diagnostic threshold hinges on how
  much *choice* one feels to stop a ritual without debilitating distress and
  how much day-to-day interference occurs.

## What it is commonly confused with

- **[Stim versus compulsion](stim-vs-compulsion.md)** — repetitive behavior
  driven by sensory pleasure or self-soothing versus driven by intrusive
  thoughts the person tries to neutralize.
- Ordinary carefulness or cleanliness — the distinguishing tell is
  time-consuming distress (>1 hour/day or clinically significant impairment),
  rituals not realistically connected to what they claim to prevent, and
  escalation when rituals are increased.

## Progression if unaddressed

Severe cases: housebound or bedridden; relationship and job loss; secondary
[depression vicious cycle](depression-vicious-cycle.md). Mild-to-moderate
cases: functioning under considerable extra strain.
