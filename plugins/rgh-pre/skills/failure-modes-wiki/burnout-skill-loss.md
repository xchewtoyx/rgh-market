## Recognizing it

- **Trigger**: [autistic burnout](autistic-burnout.md) in progress — the
  same demand/support mismatch that drives burnout, not a separate primary
  deficit.
- **Marker**: impaired long-, short-, and working memory during burnout:
  can't recall names or numbers; words (even nouns) don't come quickly;
  losing objects or one's car in a parking lot; trouble following
  multi-character stories; inability to concentrate on books; audiobooks
  becoming "a jumbled series of sounds" while zoning out and losing the plot
  repeatedly. Parallels childhood "autistic regression" (loss of reached
  developmental milestones) but in adults — a person who prided themselves
  on tracking complex information may begin losing track mid-task without
  realizing it and assume they are about to be fired.
- **Progression if unaddressed**: work performance collapse and panic that
  the loss is permanent — when the underlying driver is often dopamine
  depletion and burnout load rather than a head-on executive-function
  failure that more effort would fix.

## What it is commonly confused with

Permanent cognitive decline, ADHD executive dysfunction as a standalone
diagnosis, or laziness — the reliable tell is that skills were reliably
present before burnout and can return when burnout load eases (sometimes
without medical leave or working harder on the failing task itself).

## Relationship to autistic burnout

Listed in [autistic burnout](autistic-burnout.md)'s symptom cluster as
decline in skills/functioning; this note isolates the *regression* shape —
skills that were mastered and dependable suddenly unreliable — which is
especially frightening at work and easy to misread as fraud exposure rather
than a burnout marker.
