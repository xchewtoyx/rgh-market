## Recognizing it

- **Trigger**: any experience — sound, sight, body sensation, or thought —
  automatically registered on an internal barometer as unpleasant. Because
  unpleasantness underlies all negative emotions (sadness, anger, disgust,
  anxiety), tuning into this early registration gives a generic early-warning
  system before the specific emotion is fully formed.
- **Marker**: the fast, seemingly automatic next-moment reaction of needing
  to get rid of the experience and prevent it recurring — distinct from the
  initial unpleasant registration itself. At first the unpleasant perception
  and the aversion reaction are often experienced as fused; with practice they
  can be distinguished: unpleasantness registered first, "get me out of here"
  aversion registered second (and itself unpleasant).
- **Bodily signatures**: contraction in shoulders or lower back, tightening
  of the forehead, clenching of the jaw, tightening of the belly — fight-or-
  flight reactions whether escaping an external threat or one's own feelings.
  When the "tiger is inside us" and has lingered a long time, these reactions
  often stop being noticed. During meditation or reflection, aversion may
  also show as sudden fatigue or exhaustion ("giving up") alongside tension.
- **Cognitive consequence**: aversion co-opts the mind's problem-solving
  capacity in an attempt to eliminate unhappiness — producing
  [discrepancy-monitor rumination](discrepancy-monitor-rumination.md) and
  [rumination spiral](rumination-spiral.md) when applied to internal
  experience. The mind treats one's own thoughts and memories as enemies to
  eradicate or flee, but "none of us can run fast enough to get away from our
  own thoughts" — leaving stuck, lost, helpless feelings that reactivate old
  mental patterns.
- **Progression if unaddressed**: tunnel vision around the triggering
  thought or feeling; vicious cycles of getting upset about having negative
  thoughts ("I should not be feeling like this"); [experiential
  avoidance](experiential-avoidance.md) deepens as aversion drives further
  walling-off; in depression history, aversion at the root of relapse-related
  states of mind.

## Session-recognition example

A participant asked to focus on thoughts finds the mind goes blank, triggering
"I must be doing it wrong," then a distant memory of feeling less-than (missing
school, left behind), then a clutched chest, tiredness, and a sense of being
lost. The sequence: something happens → judgment thought → old memory of
inadequacy → bodily reactivity. The body treats what the mind does not like as
an enemy, reactivating old patterns "as if it was happening all over again" and
creating more tension — more enemies.

## What it is commonly confused with

The unpleasant feeling tone itself — aversion is the *reaction* to
unpleasantness, not the initial registration. Productive problem-solving
about an external situation differs from aversion applied to internal
states; the latter narrows focus and perpetuates suffering. [Attachment
reaction](attachment-reaction.md) is the mirror pattern for pleasant
experiences (clinging rather than pushing away).

## Avoidance-mindset linger

Even a purely symbolic avoidance trigger (e.g. helping a cartoon mouse escape
an owl rather than reach cheese) can activate the avoidance system strongly
enough to reduce creativity, close down options, and weaken flexibility on
a subsequent unrelated task — scoring 50 percent lower than approach-oriented
participants on a follow-up test. The same action has different downstream
consequences depending on whether it was done to move toward something
welcomed versus to avoid something negative: avoidance-elicited mind-states
produce lingering caution, vigilance for things going wrong, and narrowed
focus of life even after the symbolic threat passes.

## Early recognition value

Identifying the earliest moment in the sequence — before aversion fully
engages automatic reactivity — is the recognition target. The promise is not
that aversion won't occur, but that one can notice it nonjudgmentally when
it does, weakening its power to destroy the next moment.
