title: Cross-Employer Performance-Capping Cycle
description: >
  A recurring pattern in which a competent employee's authentic traits get
  penalized as a "fit" or "personality" problem despite strong outcomes,
  forcing masking that ends in burnout or departure, then repeats unchanged
  at the next employer because the cause is structural rather than
  individual.

## Recognizing it

The pattern is distinguishable from ordinary career friction by its shape
across repeated cycles, not any single incident:

- **Trigger**: performance evaluation is framed around vague "meeting
  behaviors," presentation, or relationship-building rather than
  job-relevant outcomes — even when outcome-based metrics (technical
  quality, delivery, results) are already strong or exceptional. The
  feedback effectively asks the person to change *how they are*, not *what
  they do*.
- **Early indicators**: ratings capped below top tiers despite acknowledged
  excellence ("achieved" but never "exceeds"); being routed into
  interpersonal-skills coaching aimed at passing as neurotypical (small
  talk, eye contact, tone-softening) rather than skill development;
  disclosure of a diagnosis met with suspicion or a formal fitness
  reassessment instead of support.
- **Progression if unaddressed**: coached self-consciousness about one's own
  natural behavior compounds into depression or burnout; the person either
  resigns or is quietly not renewed/promoted; because the underlying cause
  travels with the industry's evaluation culture rather than with one bad
  manager, the same shape recurs at the next employer — new context,
  identical cycle.
- **What it is commonly confused with**: read by the person experiencing it
  (and often by the employer) as a personal skill deficit or "not the right
  culture fit," when the actual driver is a mismatch between outcome-based
  competence and a personality-based evaluation standard applied unevenly.
  Treating each occurrence as an isolated, employer-specific problem instead
  of recognizing the repeating shape is what allows the cycle to continue
  unaddressed across job changes.

## Distinguishing marker

The reliable tell that this is the cross-employer cycle rather than a
one-off bad fit: outcome performance stays consistently strong across every
occurrence, while the capping reason changes cosmetically (banter,
"political" savvy, demeanor, assertiveness) but always targets identity-tied
behavior rather than deliverables.
