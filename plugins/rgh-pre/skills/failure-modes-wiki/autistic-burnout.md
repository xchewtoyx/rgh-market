## Recognizing it

- **Trigger**: sustained effort to meet neurotypical-paced demands (work,
  social, sensory) without adequate support or rest — the mismatch between
  ongoing demand and available support, not any single event. In autistic
  people's own accounts, trying to pass as non-autistic ([masking](masking.md))
  is the most commonly cited cause. Masking-built lives require keeping many
  "spinning plates" in the air; some autistics hear "give 100 percent"
  literally, framing work as binary — absolutely succeeding versus on the
  verge of being fired.
- **Early warning**: [restraint collapse](restraint-collapse.md) — end-of-day
  or multi-day passive collapse after sustained inhibition — often precedes
  full burnout; intensified sensory issues and irritability with a short fuse
  are the next signal to slow down and lighten load.
- **Duration**: can last months to years; often first experienced in
  adolescence — not a short post-stress recovery window. Recovery itself is
  nonlinear and relapse-prone: regained energy can pull the person back into
  old achievement-seeking patterns before overdoing-it signs are recognized.
- **Onset shape**: can be a slow, imperceptible slide rather than a sudden
  crash — permanent "stripped down survival mode" without ever feeling in
  acute crisis, which paradoxically makes it harder to get support because
  it never looks urgent enough from the outside.
- **Symptom cluster** (the combination, not any one item alone, is the
  marker):
  - unrelenting fatigue that rest and sleep do not resolve
  - heightened sensory sensitivities — stimuli that were previously
    manageable become overwhelming, with more frequent
    [meltdowns](meltdown.md) or [shutdowns](shutdown.md)
  - decline in skills/functioning: focus, organizing, problem-solving,
    even speech — see [burnout skill loss](burnout-skill-loss.md) for the
    regression shape when previously reliable skills suddenly fail
  - **brain fog**: inability to hold a train of thought, memory loss,
    inability to access words (spoken or typed), and loss of visual
    thinking or imagery — a defining symptom cluster for some people
  - emotional dysregulation: either intensified emotion or numbness,
    increased anxiety and irritability
  - diminished tolerance for change — an increased need for sameness and
    predictability
  - social withdrawal and loss of motivation for previously enjoyed
    activities
  - [masking](masking.md) capacity breaking down — often the first
    externally visible sign, and why many adults are not diagnosed
    autistic until burnout strips their masking away
- **Progression if unaddressed**: can develop into depression, and is a
  known contributor to suicidality in autistic people if the underlying
  demand/support mismatch is not addressed. Emotional core for some: a
  feeling of finally being "found out" as a fraud — "not good enough"
  escalating to "unworthy" to hyperbolic self-condemnation. Some autistics
  "think their feelings" rather than feel them, so suicidal ideation can
  present as an intellectualized "thought puzzle" — a hypothetical exit
  door — rather than acute emotional crisis; either framing warrants honest
  safety assessment. If recovery is followed by a return to the same
  unaddressed conditions rather than a change in them, the same collapse
  tends to recur — see the [work-collapse-repeat
  cycle](work-collapse-repeat-cycle.md).

## Distinguishing it from depression

Autistic burnout is a distinct phenomenon from depression, even though
unaddressed burnout can lead into depression. The reliable markers
separating the two:

- Burnout sufferers can still feel pleasure and typically lack the
  feelings of worthlessness typical of depression.
- Burnout's symptom cluster is tied to a specific demand/support mismatch
  and includes autistic-burnout-specific features (masking breakdown,
  spiking sensory sensitivity, increased need for sameness) that plain
  depression does not typically produce.
- Burnout may produce inability to speak or self-care and memory
  challenges (especially short-term) — overlapping with depression
  presentation but arising from the demand overload rather than a primary
  mood disorder.
- Burnout does not respond to medication or to therapeutic interventions
  that help depressed people (e.g. reframing or considering alternative
  interpretations); recovery requires relief from the demands taxing the
  person's system.

Treating burnout as if it were only depression — without addressing the
demand mismatch — leaves the actual driver in place. Both conditions can
raise suicide risk if unaddressed.

## What it is commonly confused with

Apparent "rest" does not reliably resolve it: time off spent on activities
that don't actually rejuvenate (versus activities that do) can look like
recovery from the outside while the underlying depletion continues,
producing confusion about why symptoms persist despite downtime.

## Related presentation: ADHD burnout

The same underlying pattern — sustained, unsupported effort against an
unaccommodating environment collapsing into debilitating exhaustion — shows
up with a different symptom emphasis (task-size distortion, generalized
mental/physical exhaustion, rather than sensory sensitivity and masking
breakdown specifically) in [ADHD burnout](adhd-burnout.md).
