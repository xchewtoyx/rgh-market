## Recognizing it

- **Phase 1 (masked overload)**: disturbances or demands grow faster than
  responses can be deployed, but compensatory control partially succeeds —
  masking the developing problem. The person (or subsystem) works harder
  and harder to hold the line while nearing capacity limits. Outwardly,
  things may still look functional.
- **Phase 2 (decompensation event)**: compensatory capacity is exhausted
  and the controlled parameter suddenly collapses — [shutdown](shutdown.md),
  [meltdown](meltdown.md), [autistic burnout](autistic-burnout.md), or acute
  health/decision-making failure.
- **Critical diagnostic cue**: not abnormal symptoms alone but the
  increasing force required to resist them relative to base capability —
  "how hard the base control system is working" without yet having failed.

## Personal-scale examples

- Sustained [masking](masking.md) holding performance until masking
  capacity breaks down abruptly.
- Automation-style silent compensation in daily life (pushing through
  sensory overload, skipping meals, overriding fatigue) until a minor
  trigger produces disproportionate collapse.
- Falling behind the tempo of operations — demand surges faster than
  recovery or adaptation can match.

## What it is commonly confused with

A sudden breakdown misread as a reaction only to the immediate trigger
(the laundry, the one comment, the minor inconvenience) — when phase 1
compensatory overload was building for weeks or months and the trigger
only arrived at the moment capacity ran out.

## Distinguishing marker from gradual decline

The collapse is abrupt relative to the preceding masked period. Recognizing
phase 1 requires attending to effort cost and margin, not waiting for
phase-2 failure — the same discipline as maintaining "tactical reserves"
before definitive need is obvious.
