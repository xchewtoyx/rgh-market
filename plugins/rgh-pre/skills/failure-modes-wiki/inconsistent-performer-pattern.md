## Recognizing it

- **Trigger**: overcommitment (difficulty saying no, adding tasks on top of
  full-time work out of novelty excitement) combined with sensory or
  executive overload at work.
- **Marker**: alternating between periods of [hyperfocus](hyperfocus.md)
  overdelivery and periods of shutting down to bare minimum under overwhelm
  — labeled an "inconsistent performer" from the outside. Strong work
  ethic without boundary-reading can also produce being taken advantage of
  and overworked, deepening the cycle.
- **Progression if unaddressed**: panic, overwhelm, and executive dysfunction
  (unable to figure out where or how to start), followed by anger at self
  and fear of letting people down. Job-hopping or quitting when cumulative
  exhaustion from wrong-fit environments exceeds tolerance — sometimes
  misread as flightiness rather than protective response to overload.
- **What it is commonly confused with**: lack of ego, lack of discipline, or
  unreliable character — when the actual driver is capacity cycling between
  hyperfocus absorption and overload shutdown, not variable commitment.

## Distinguishing marker from ordinary performance variation

The alternation tracks overload and stimulation profile, not mood or
interest alone: hyperfocus when tasks are novel, urgent, or absorbing;
shutdown when demands stack past capacity or sensory environment is
wrong (fluorescent lighting, temperature swings, perfume, unpredictable
management). Consistent performance requires conditions that fit how the
person actually functions — not more effort to "try harder."
