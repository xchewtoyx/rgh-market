## Recognizing it

- **Mechanism**: when depressed, effort tends to go into ruminative brooding
  as the default mode network's (DMN) daydreaming turns negative. Brain-
  scanner studies of people watching sad films show sadness activates the
  DMN at the expense of sensory processing — in some people the DMN actively
  shuts down sensation processing.
- **Marker**: during sad mood, reduced registration of body sensations and
  sensory input — not merely distraction but an active trade-off where
  thinking/fixing/planning displaces interoceptive awareness. Relying on the
  DMN to respond to sadness with fixing, thinking, and planning made relapse
  more likely in longitudinal follow-up.
- **Risk gradient**: higher-than-average intensity of sensory shutdown
  during sadness made a person **twenty-five times more likely** to become
  depressed over two years than someone with less intense shutdown (Farb et
  al.).
- **Progression if unaddressed**: compounds with [experiential
  avoidance](experiential-avoidance.md) and [aversion
  reaction](aversion-reaction.md) — the person loses the body-based early-
  warning system just when it is most needed, so mood and unregistered
  bodily states drive judgments and behavior unnoticed until escalation.

## What it is commonly confused with

Ordinary sadness with intact body awareness — sensory shutdown is the
specific pattern of DMN dominance actively muting sensation processing, not
sadness alone. [Alexithymia](alexithymia.md) as a trait difficulty
labeling emotions differs from this mood-activated sensory muting, though
both obscure interoceptive signals.

## Relationship to other patterns

Overlaps the interoceptive blindness described in [experiential
avoidance](experiential-avoidance.md) but names a distinct neural mechanism:
ruminative DMN activity actively suppresses sensory channels rather than
avoidance alone dampening awareness. Reducing DMN influence through
mindfulness or curiosity was associated with resilience in the same
research program.
