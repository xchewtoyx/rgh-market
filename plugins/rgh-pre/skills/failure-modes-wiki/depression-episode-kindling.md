## Recognizing it

- **Trigger pattern shift**: early depressive episodes are typically preceded
  by significant negative life events, but as more episodes accrue, life
  events play a progressively smaller role in triggering the next episode.
  Risk of further depression rises with each episode while the *relative
  contribution* of life events to that risk declines — though even after many
  prior episodes, a stressful event still somewhat raises the odds.
- **Marker**: episodes that seem to arise spontaneously, independent of
  circumstances, in someone with a history of multiple prior episodes — the
  psychological processes of episode onset become more autonomous over time,
  parallel to neurobiological "kindling" or sensitization accounts where each
  episode produces small threshold changes.
- **Progression if unaddressed**: relapse pathways ([cognitive
  reactivity](cognitive-reactivity.md), [rumination
  spiral](rumination-spiral.md), [depression vicious
  cycle](depression-vicious-cycle.md)) become easier to activate with less
  external provocation.

## What it is commonly confused with

A single bad week causing a first or rare depressive episode — kindling
describes a *history-dependent* shift in how much external stress is required
to trigger recurrence, not the ordinary acute-stress → low-mood link in
someone without a multi-episode pattern.
