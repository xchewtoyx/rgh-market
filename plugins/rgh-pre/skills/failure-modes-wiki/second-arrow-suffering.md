  unhappiness than the original pain itself.

## Recognizing it

- **Structure**: the first "arrow" is the initial pain — physical injury,
  loss, disappointment, or unpleasant feeling tone. The second arrow is the
  added suffering of the reactive response fired at oneself: anger about
  being hurt, fear of recurrence, grief layered with self-blame, or
  self-critical thoughts about having the reaction at all.
- **Marker**: vicious cycles of getting upset about having negative
  thoughts or feelings — "I should not be feeling like this. Why am I so
  stupid and weak?" The secondary reaction often causes greater unhappiness
  than the first arrow alone.
- **Progression if unaddressed**: the second arrow is self-fired and
  therefore learnable to recognize; without recognition it compounds
  [aversion reaction](aversion-reaction.md) and [rumination
  spiral](rumination-spiral.md), turning a manageable first-arrow event
  into prolonged suffering.

## What it is commonly confused with

The first arrow itself — appropriate grief, sadness, or pain about a real
event. The distinguishing marker is whether the distress has escalated
beyond what the original situation warrants through reactive self-
judgment, rumination about the reaction, or fighting the feeling rather
than the external cause.

## Relationship to other patterns

A specific instance of [aversion reaction](aversion-reaction.md) applied
to one's own already-unpleasant inner state — rejecting the first arrow
rather than allowing it to be present.
