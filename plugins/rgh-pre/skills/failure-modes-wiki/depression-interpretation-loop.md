## Recognizing it

**A–B–C model of emotions:**

- **A** = facts of the situation (what a video camera would record)
- **B** = interpretation — the "running story" often just below awareness,
  often taken as fact
- **C** = reaction — emotions, body sensations, behavior

People typically see A and C but are unaware of B, so they believe the
situation itself caused the reaction when it was really the interpretation.

Example: waving to someone who walks past without responding — good mood →
benign interpretation (didn't notice you), little reaction. Low mood →
deliberately ignored, rumination about what you did wrong. Same facts,
different feelings depending on B.

## The feedback loop

A low mood makes us pick out and elaborate the most negative interpretation,
which deteriorates mood further, which recruits more "evidence" of
unlikability ("this happened before," "no one likes me"), settling into
themes of worthlessness, isolation, and inadequacy. A glum mood can trigger
self-criticism ("Why am I such a loser?"); investigating the "why" plunges
mood further and primes negative thoughts for future recruitment.

**Unhappiness itself is not the problem** — harshly negative self-views that
unhappy moods switch on transform passing sadness into persistent depression.
This is the cognitive entry point for [cognitive
reactivity](cognitive-reactivity.md) and the [depression four-dimension
spiral](depression-four-dimension-spiral.md).

## What it is commonly confused with

Accurate perception of social rejection — the distinguishing tell is
ambiguous data read catastrophically under low mood, with interpretations
later contradicted when mood lifts (boss worried about support, family
desperate to help but person too ashamed to notice).
