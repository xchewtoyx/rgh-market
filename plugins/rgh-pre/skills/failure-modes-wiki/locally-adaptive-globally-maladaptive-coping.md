  A breakdown pattern in which coping that succeeds locally — for one task,
  role, or moment — undermines broader goals, relationships, or long-term
  wellbeing when groups or life domains fail to coordinate.
sources:
  - title: Resilience Engineering in Practice

## Recognizing it

- **Trigger**: conflicting goals across roles, echelons, or life domains —
  each part of the system working hard toward its local objective without
  knowledge of others' difficulties or shared long-term constraints.
- **Marker**: locally rational, effortful adaptation that makes it harder
  for other parts (or future self) to meet their responsibilities or
  undermines shared global goals — the tragedy-of-the-commons shape at
  personal scale.
- **Examples**:
  - pushing through work demands locally while eroding health globally
  - one coping strategy (venting frustration, correcting others, taking on
    every task asked) that solves a local problem but damages relationships
    or team coordination
  - production-pressure trade-offs that gradually erode safety margins —
  the organizational pattern of sacrificing rest, recovery, or boundaries
  for short-term output

## What it is commonly confused with

Good teamwork, dedication, or problem-solving — because each local action
is rational and effortful. The failure is coordination and global goal
alignment, not lack of effort.

## Relationship to other patterns

Overlaps with [indiscipline of overwork](indiscipline-of-overwork.md) when local
overwork undermines long-term capacity, and with [cross-employer performance
capping cycle](cross-employer-performance-capping-cycle.md) when masked local
performance hides global unsustainability.

## Why recognition matters

Without perspective contrast across roles and time horizons, locally
adaptive behavior looks successful until global failure emerges elsewhere —
hard to diagnose from inside the local frame alone.
