## Recognizing it

Two feedback loops run in parallel, both driven by the same initial drop in
activity:

- **Withdrawal loop**: decreased activity reduces motivation and increases
  lethargy, which leads to dropping previously enjoyed activities, which
  removes access to pleasant/positive experience, which worsens mood, which
  further decreases activity.
- **Neglect/overwhelm loop**: reduced activity lets tasks and
  responsibilities pile up; the growing backlog itself becomes a source of
  feeling overwhelmed, which produces guilt and a sense of ineffectiveness
  or failure, which worsens mood and further suppresses activity.

Both loops close back on the same starting point (reduced activity), which
is what makes this a self-sustaining cycle rather than a single low patch
that resolves on its own.

## Distinguishing marker: not ordinary tiredness

The counterintuitive tell that separates this from ordinary physical
tiredness is the effect of rest. Ordinary tiredness responds to rest —
sleeping and sitting still restores capacity. In this cycle, resting and
doing nothing tends to *increase* lethargy and low mood instead, partly
because idle time gives more room for rumination on distressing thoughts.
Treating the lethargy as if it were ordinary tiredness — responding with
more rest — reinforces the withdrawal loop rather than interrupting it.

## What it is commonly confused with

The neglect/overwhelm loop's backlog-driven guilt is easy to misread as
straightforward procrastination or poor discipline, obscuring that the
backlog is itself a downstream effect of the mood-driven activity drop, not
its original cause.

## Additional maintaining factors (CBT depression model)

Beyond the two activity loops above, depression traps people in thinking and
behavior patterns that lower mood further — and some attempts to alleviate
depressed feelings can worsen symptoms:

- **Negative thinking** — helpless and worthless beliefs; beliefs the world
  is unsafe or undesirable.
- **Social withdrawal** — avoiding people and interaction.
- **Procrastination** — avoiding bills, appointments, phone calls because
  they seem too difficult or scary.
- **Shame** — ashamed of the depression itself, imagining harsh judgment from
  others.
- **Guilt** — overestimating how much one's low mood inconveniences loved
  ones.
- **Hopelessness** — believing one will never feel better.
- **[Rumination spiral](rumination-spiral.md)** — repetitive cyclical
  negative thinking that deepens depression by focusing attention on how
  depressed one feels.

Doing only what depression makes you feel like doing tends to maintain
symptoms; the counter-pattern is doing the opposite of what depression
directs — get up, dressed, answer the phone, meet friends — which limits
rumination by forcing attention onto external things. Depression dulls
capacity for enjoyment, which returns with patience; "follow your plan, not
how you feel" is the operational marker.
