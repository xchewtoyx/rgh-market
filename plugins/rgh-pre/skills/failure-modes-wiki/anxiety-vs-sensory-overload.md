## The distinction

- **Anxiety**: internally driven — fear of a potential future event, fed by
  thoughts rather than by present stimuli.
- **Sensory overload**: externally driven — distress caused by present
  sensory input (bright lights, noise, crowding) exceeding what can
  currently be processed.
- **Sensory dread**: a hybrid — anticipatory distress about a *known,
  upcoming* sensory-overload situation (e.g. dread before a trip to a mall
  or dentist). The anticipation is internally generated like anxiety, but
  its object is a specific external sensory trigger, and it resolves the
  same way sensory overload does once the trigger is avoided, reduced, or
  passed.

## Diagnostic test

Track whether the distress rises and falls with proximity to the external
stimulus, or persists regardless of the sensory environment and instead
tracks the content of one's thoughts. Distress that intensifies specifically
as sensory input increases (and eases when it's reduced or removed) points
to sensory overload or sensory dread; distress that continues regardless of
the sensory environment, and is entangled with "what if" thinking about the
future, points to anxiety.

## Why the distinction matters

The two states can feel identical from the inside — both can present as
racing thoughts, physical tension, urge to escape — but they call for
different levers to actually resolve (environmental versus cognitive).
Treating a sensory-overload state as if it were anxiety (or vice versa)
means addressing the wrong lever and getting little relief even though the
diagnosis felt intuitively right.
