## Recognizing it

- **Marker**: the person often does not register hunger, tiredness, cold, or
  heat until they are at the very brink of a breakdown — not merely late,
  but absent as a felt signal until capacity is nearly gone. Temperature
  dysregulation can produce panic-attack presentation (heart rate spike,
  sweating, dizziness) before the person registers they are overheating —
  unrecognized as a body-state signal rather than primary anxiety.
- **Progression if unaddressed**: unmet body needs accumulate silently until
  they precipitate [shutdown](shutdown.md), [meltdown](meltdown.md), or
  [autistic burnout](autistic-burnout.md) from what looks like a minor
  trigger — when the actual driver is a long-unregistered basic need.
- **What it is commonly confused with**: poor self-care habits, laziness, or
  disorganization — when the underlying issue is that interoceptive signals
  arrive too late to guide action, not that the person chooses to ignore
  them.

## Relationship to alexithymia

[Alexithymia](alexithymia.md) covers difficulty labeling emotions from
bodily sensations; delayed interoception is the related pattern where basic
physical needs themselves (not just emotions) fail to register until
extreme. Both obscure distress from observers and can compound each other.

## Why recognition matters

Managing delayed interoception requires deliberate, periodic self-check-ins
for unmet body needs rather than relying on felt signals — a recognition
pattern, not a treatment prescription. Without recognizing the pattern,
others may escalate demands on someone who is already depleted but not yet
aware they are hungry, cold, or exhausted.
