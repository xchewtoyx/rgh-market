## Recognizing it

- **Why it matters**: with repeated episodes, depression can escalate with
  decreasing external trigger ([depression episode
  kindling](depression-episode-kindling.md)) — making advance identification
  of personal warning signs critical.
- **Marker**: a unique combination of changes, identified while mood is
  stable, that signal depression developing again. Common examples (each
  person's mix differs):
  - negative thoughts/feelings taking hold, feeling adhesive, difficult to
    dismiss — often the natural bridge from "thoughts are mental events" to
    relapse monitoring
  - irritability with self and others
  - withdrawal from social participation
  - changing sleep or eating habits
  - getting easily exhausted
  - giving up on exercise
  - not wanting to deal with mail, bills, deadlines
- **Progression if unaddressed**: hopelessness (part of depression's
  territory) makes heeding warnings feel pointless ("back to square one");
  [cognitive reactivity](cognitive-reactivity.md) and [rumination
  spiral](rumination-spiral.md) engage before the person notices the
  signature.

## What it is commonly confused with

Ordinary bad weeks or single symptoms in isolation — a relapse signature is
the *pattern* of several co-occurring shifts, unique to the individual.
Hypervigilant scanning for every listed sign is itself a failure mode; the
aim is balanced awareness without treating normal variation as catastrophe.

## What prevents noticing

Past barriers to heeding warnings: pushing away, denial, distraction,
self-medication with alcohol, arguments, blaming family or colleagues —
worth listing alongside the signature itself when planning relapse response.
