## Recognizing it

- **Trigger**: an intensely focused attention style fixating on minor
  details without an internal signal for whether those details matter to
  the bigger picture — the fixation is on granularity, not necessarily on
  the highest-value part of the task.
- **Marker**: distress, fatigue, and time cost concentrated in
  disproportionately minor aspects of a task, alongside a subjective sense
  that the work isn't done until every detail is addressed, regardless of
  whether the audience or outcome would ever register most of them.
- **What it is commonly confused with**: reads from the outside as high
  standards or conscientiousness (and is often praised as such), which
  obscures that the actual driver is difficulty calibrating *where* effort
  should go rather than a deliberate choice to prioritize quality. It is
  also distinct from [hyperfocus](hyperfocus.md)'s monopolization risk:
  hyperfocus overruns on *duration* within an absorbing task, while this
  pattern overspends effort on the wrong *level of detail* within a task,
  and can occur even in short tasks.
- **External calibration as the missing signal**: because the difficulty is
  in judging relative importance internally, an external read on which
  details actually matter (rather than more effort or more time) is what
  is missing — without it, the same disproportionate-effort pattern repeats
  on the next task.
