  others' emotions strongly — overloads the nervous system and produces
  shutdown or meltdown behavior that is easily misread as lack of empathy.

## Recognizing it

- **Trigger**: proximity to someone experiencing a strong emotion — even a
  fictional character on screen — whether or not that emotion is overtly
  displayed. Combined sensory and empathic input (e.g. being near a crying
  baby) can be especially dysregulating.
- **Marker**: shutdown or other dysregulated behavior in response to others'
  emotional states, driven by *too much* felt empathy rather than too little.
  The person may shut down when someone nearby experiences a big emotion
  because the empathic input itself exceeds what the nervous system can
  currently hold.
- **What it is commonly confused with**: the resulting withdrawal or
  dysregulated behavior can look like a lack of empathy or emotional
  disconnection from the outside — when the underlying mechanism is extreme
  affective empathy producing [shutdown](shutdown.md) or
  [meltdown](meltdown.md), not indifference. This is the opposite of what
  is actually happening.

## Distinguishing marker

The reliable tell is the *direction* of the empathic response: the person
is overwhelmed by feeling others' emotions, not failing to register them.
Outward shutdown in this pattern is a capacity-exceeded response to empathic
input, not evidence that empathy is absent.
