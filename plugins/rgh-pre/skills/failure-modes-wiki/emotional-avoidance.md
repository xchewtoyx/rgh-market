## Recognizing it

- **Trigger**: a felt sense of "too-muchness" — emotional or sensory input
  that feels unmanageable to fully process — leading to minimizing,
  numbing, or simply not engaging with the emotion at all rather than
  experiencing and processing it.
- **Marker**: a habitual tendency to disengage from or minimize emotional
  content specifically (as opposed to a one-off moment of not having
  bandwidth), often self-identifiable as a longstanding coping style rather
  than a situational response.
- **Progression if unaddressed**: increased vulnerability to substance use,
  self-harm, and general emotional numbing; heightened background stress,
  anxiety, and disconnection from self and others; possible contribution to
  chronic pain; impaired problem-solving, since problems tied to avoided
  emotions don't get engaged with well enough to resolve. Sustained
  suppression does not make the emotion disappear — it can instead build
  until it surfaces more disruptively than if it had been processed
  gradually, showing up as [burnout](autistic-burnout.md) or as a
  [meltdown](meltdown.md)-scale reaction to a comparatively minor trigger.
  Hiding emotion from others is frequently downstream of first suppressing
  it from oneself, and a habitual reflexive "I'm fine" in response to being
  asked is a common surface indicator that this suppression is already
  underway.
- **What it is commonly confused with**: temporary grounding or
  distress-tolerance techniques used to return to a processing-capable
  state look similar to avoidance from the outside, but are not the same
  pattern — grounding is a short-term bridge back to engaging with the
  emotion, while avoidance is the emotion never getting engaged with at
  all. Relying on grounding techniques indefinitely, past the point of
  regaining capacity to process, converts the tool into a form of chronic
  avoidance. When the specific avoidance mechanism is pleasure or
  stimulation rather than numbing, the same dynamic takes the more
  specific shape of [escalating escapism](escalating-escapism.md).
  The broader clinical pattern covering thoughts and body sensations
  as well as emotions is [experiential
  avoidance](experiential-avoidance.md).
