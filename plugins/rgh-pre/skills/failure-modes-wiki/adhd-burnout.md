  enormous.

## Recognizing it

- **Trigger**: sustained overexertion compensating for a mismatch between
  how the person's attention and energy naturally work and what a
  standard, unaccommodated environment demands — chronic pressure to sit
  still, sustain uninteresting attention, or otherwise perform outside
  one's natural style. The overexertion frequently isn't recognized as
  such while it's happening — the marathon-runner framing is apt
  specifically because the person often doesn't realize they've been
  running one until they hit the wall.
- **Marker**: tasks that are normally easy or routine start to feel
  enormous, alongside general mental and physical exhaustion — the
  functional cost is disproportionate to the objective size of the task,
  which is what distinguishes this from ordinary tiredness after a hard
  stretch of work.
- **Progression if unaddressed**: sustained chronic pressure to fit an
  unaccommodating environment, met with ongoing criticism or
  misunderstanding when it doesn't succeed, can accumulate into lasting
  emotional trauma on top of the exhaustion itself, not just fatigue that
  resolves with a weekend off.
- **What it is commonly confused with**: read as a sudden drop in
  competence, motivation, or work ethic, when the exhaustion is the
  cumulative cost of demands the person had been meeting all along —
  described as the brain signaling "I need a break" rather than a personal
  failing.

## Relationship to autistic burnout

This is the ADHD-specific presentation of the same underlying pattern
described in [autistic burnout](autistic-burnout.md): sustained,
unsupported effort to meet demands the environment does not accommodate,
producing a debilitating collapse rather than ordinary tiredness. The two
notes differ mainly in symptom emphasis (task-size distortion and
exhaustion here; sensory sensitivity, need for sameness, and masking
breakdown there) because they are grounded in different source material,
not because the underlying mechanism differs — the same person can
recognize either description depending on which traits are more salient
for them.
