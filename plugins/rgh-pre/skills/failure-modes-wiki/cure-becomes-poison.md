## Recognizing it

Core framing: "A cure, overused, becomes a poison." Something designed to
protect — pesticide on a tree, drink after a hard day, a bet to feel alive —
applied beyond dosage limits with good intentions becomes what kills.

- **Early phase**: the drink/bet/relationship/shopping spree "feels like the
  solution. Like oxygen." — genuine attempt at relief, survival, making life
  possible (e.g. drinking as reason to tolerate social events, celebrate
  surviving the day, not feel completely lost alone at night).
- **Shift**: slowly "hollows you out" — what felt like a shield was "a knife
  in the ribs." The poison's lie is that you need it to survive; it numbs
  without nourishing.
- **Marker**: reaching for the habit at stress/loneliness/fear is non-random;
  net effect is now more damaging than healing to body, mind, relationships,
  future — money, mornings lost, relationships eroded.

## What it is commonly confused with

Moderate use of a still-helpful coping tool — the distinguishing tell is
crossing from medicine to poison: tracking cost shows the habit takes more
than it gives. Related to [problem-maintaining
solution](problem-maintaining-solution.md) and [escalating
escapism](escalating-escapism.md) but emphasizes the *was once genuinely
helpful* arc rather than escape from a specific unresolved thought.

## Recognition steps (spotting the poison)

Notice what you reach for when hurting; ask whether it still helps or hurts;
track cost in black and white; name the poison out loud (refusing denial's
power); repeatedly choose life over numbness — sometimes hourly.

Reaching for a poison is evidence you were trying to survive, not weakness —
but once recognized, stopping is the brave act.
