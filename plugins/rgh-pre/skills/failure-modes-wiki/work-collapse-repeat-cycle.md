## Recognizing it

- **Trigger**: professional life pursued without integrating one's actual
  needs and identity into how the work is structured — most visibly, work
  sustained primarily through [masking](masking.md) rather than through
  conditions that fit how the person actually functions.
- **Shape of the cycle**: sustained masked effort → [autistic
  burnout](autistic-burnout.md) or collapse → enough recovery to return to
  work → the same unsupported conditions resume, unchanged → the cycle
  repeats. What makes this a distinct pattern from a single burnout episode
  is the repetition itself: each collapse is treated as an isolated event
  to recover from and return past, rather than as evidence that the
  underlying conditions need to change.
- **What it is commonly confused with**: each individual collapse tends to
  get diagnosed and addressed on its own terms (as a bout of burnout, a
  rough patch, a health issue) without anyone connecting it to the same
  collapse the cycle before. The recurrence is the actual signal that the
  conditions — not the person's resilience or resourcefulness — are the
  problem, but recurrence is exactly what a per-incident view is unable to
  see.

## Distinguishing marker

A single burnout episode followed by a genuine change in conditions (role,
workload, support) that prevents recurrence is not this pattern. The marker
specific to the work-collapse-repeat cycle is that conditions after
recovery are the same conditions that produced the collapse — the person's
capacity resets, but the environment does not.
