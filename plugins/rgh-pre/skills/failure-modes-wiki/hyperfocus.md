## Recognizing it

- **Condition-dependence**: the marker is not simply "works hard" — it is a
  sharp contrast in output speed and quality between conditions. One
  documented case completed work roughly 3x faster than peers, but the
  effect only appeared in silence; the same person's daytime open-office
  hours (meetings, ambient noise, interruptions) produced comparatively
  little. The enabling condition is not always environmental quiet —
  for some people (particularly with ADHD) the gating condition is
  genuine personal interest in the task itself, producing the same sharp
  contrast between an absorbing task and an equally important but less
  engaging one. ADHD framing: not a deficit of attention but a
  dysregulated attention system — unstimulating tasks (admin, housework)
  are genuinely hard to sustain focus on, while highly stimulating tasks
  can produce focus exceeding neurotypical levels; stopping is what's
  difficult, not starting.
- **Trigger for the displacement pattern**: when the environment that
  enables hyperfocus (e.g. quiet) is unavailable during standard working
  hours, a person may compensate by shifting hyperfocus-productive work to
  off-hours (nights, early mornings) rather than getting the condition
  changed — accumulating unpaid overtime while standard hours are spent
  fighting distraction for comparatively little output.
- **What it is commonly confused with**: observers who only see the
  after-hours output tend to read the person as an admirable "workaholic,"
  when the actual driver is that standard hours were largely unusable for
  the work in question. Conversely, observers who only see the
  standard-hours struggle may read the same person as underperforming or
  unfocused. Both readings miss that the person's total productive capacity
  was never the issue — access to the enabling condition was.

## Why this matters for recognition

Because hyperfocus output is condition-gated rather than effort-gated,
treating the off-hours output as the baseline to demand more of (rather
than identifying and protecting the enabling condition) misreads the
pattern and tends to push toward unsustainable hours rather than toward the
actual fix.

## Progression if unmanaged

Hyperfocus is double-edged even when the enabling condition is available.
Once engaged, it can be as hard to exit as it is productive to be in:
sessions can run past the point of benefit, displacing sleep, other
responsibilities, and relationships, without the person noticing the
overrun in the moment. Sustained unmanaged hyperfocus is itself a route
into [autistic burnout](autistic-burnout.md) — the same absorption that
produces exceptional output on the task at hand can quietly cut into the
recovery time and life maintenance that prevent burnout.
