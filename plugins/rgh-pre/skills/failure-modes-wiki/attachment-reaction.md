## Recognizing it

- **Trigger**: any experience automatically registered on the internal
  barometer as pleasant — the fast, seemingly automatic next-moment reaction
  of needing to hang on to or get more of the experience.
- **Marker**: frustration, anger, or sense of loss when a pleasant state ends
  or is interrupted — "like a child that has had his ice cream snatched out
  of his hand." Getting hooked on recreating a pleasant meditation state or
  mood sets up a life of chasing ups and downs; the pleasant experience
  itself becomes a source of suffering through the effort to cling to it.
- **Neutral-tone variant**: when feeling tone registers as neutral, the
  common reaction is loss of interest, tuning out, boredom, or restlessness
  — a milder form of the same "needing things to be different" dynamic.
- **Progression if unaddressed**: attachment to pleasant states alternates
  with [aversion reaction](aversion-reaction.md) to unpleasant ones,
  locking the person into reactive swings rather than stable awareness.
  Pleasant settled states during practice or daily life become benchmarks
  that make ordinary or difficult periods feel like failure.

## What it is commonly confused with

Genuine enjoyment without clinging — enjoyment that passes without distress
when the experience ends. [Aversion reaction](aversion-reaction.md) is the
mirror pattern (pushing away unpleasantness rather than clinging to
pleasantness); both share the root of needing things to be different from
how they are right now.

## Relationship to other patterns

Attachment and aversion lock together with avoidance and preoccupation,
arising from the natural desire for things to be different from how they
actually are. Together they fuel [experiential
avoidance](experiential-avoidance.md) and [discrepancy-monitor
rumination](discrepancy-monitor-rumination.md).
