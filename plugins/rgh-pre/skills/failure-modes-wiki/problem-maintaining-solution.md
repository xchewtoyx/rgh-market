## Recognizing it

The organizing CBT idea: strategies driven by reducing distress often
reinforce the beliefs and behaviors underpinning the problem. Turning a
counterproductive strategy on its head — doing the opposite of the
established coping pattern — is often the route to lasting change.

Common categories:

- **Avoidance** of feared situations — erodes confidence; never confronts
  the fear ([emotional avoidance](emotional-avoidance.md)).
- **Concealing shame** — chronic insecurity about being "found out."
- **Putting off tasks until "in the mood"** — undone tasks weigh on the mind.
- **Depression activity withdrawal** — feeds [depression vicious
  cycle](depression-vicious-cycle.md).
- **Rumination** to "pull yourself together" or mentally escape via "if only"
  — feeds [rumination spiral](rumination-spiral.md).
- **Daytime napping for fatigue** — disrupts sleep, more fatigue.
- **Excessive control** of thoughts, sensations, or food — trying harder to
  stop anxiety/blushing/disturbing thoughts typically produces more of what
  you're trying to control; feeds [obsessive-compulsive
  loop](obsessive-compulsive-loop.md).
- **Reassurance-seeking and repeated checking** — temporary relief, growing
  uncertainty.
- **Safety behaviors** in anxiety — prevent discovering feared catastrophes
  won't happen or that you could cope.
- **Thought suppression** — suppressed thoughts intrude more often (pink
  elephant effect).

## What it is commonly confused with

Healthy self-care or prudent caution — the distinguishing tell is that the
strategy provides immediate relief while the underlying problem grows or
the same relief requires escalation ([escalating
escapism](escalating-escapism.md), [cure becomes
poison](cure-becomes-poison.md)).

## Diagnostic experiment

Temporarily *increase* the unhelpful strategy (double checking, trying
harder to suppress a thought) often demonstrates faster than reducing it
that the "solution" is part of the problem.
