## Recognizing it

- **Driver**: masking is adopted for safety, not preference — it is a
  protective response to environments where authentic behavior has been
  penalized or is expected to be. It is exhausting to sustain because it
  requires continuous self-monitoring and suppression. A common lifelong
  trajectory: undiagnosed autistic people dismiss their differences as things
  to overcome or hide, interpret others' relative ease as evidence something
  is wrong with *them* rather than with environmental fit, and develop a
  habit of working against their own neurology with a belief that trying
  harder will eventually make them "normal."
- **Compounding factor**: for people who also code-switch along other axes
  (e.g. race, culture), masking layers on top of that existing labor rather
  than replacing it.
- **Cost of sustained masking**: linked to higher anxiety and depression,
  and to erosion of a clear sense of one's own preferences and needs —
  masking can go on long enough that the person loses track of what they
  actually want versus what reads as acceptable to others. Heavy masking
  increases mental illness risk chiefly through chronic anxiety — fear of not
  fitting in, rejection, being "found out" — keeping the body in persistent
  fight/flight/freeze ([chronic stress
  activation](chronic-stress-activation.md)), pumping cortisol and damaging
  joints and immunity. Roughly 80–94% of autistics experience diagnosable
  mental illness affecting daily life versus ~20% general population — not
  because autism causes illness but because masking and social disablement
  compound risk.
- **Masking breakdown as a diagnostic signal**: sustained masking has a
  ceiling. When capacity to keep masking erodes, it is frequently the first
  externally visible sign that something is wrong — many autistic adults
  are not diagnosed until [autistic burnout](autistic-burnout.md) strips
  away enough masking capacity to make underlying differences visible.
  Masking breakdown should be read as a capacity signal, not as the person
  suddenly "getting worse" at coping.

## What it is commonly confused with

Increased masking effort (working harder to pass) can look like improved
adjustment from the outside, when it is actually consuming more of the
person's available capacity for the same external result — the visible
behavior improves while the sustainable cost rises. Conversely, the visible
breakdown of masking is easy to misread as a new or worsening problem,
when it is often the exposure of a preexisting one that had been hidden by
the effort to mask it.

## Mask as partial self

Unmasking reframe: the mask was also you — not your full or chosen self, but
a genuine aspect selected from a spectrum of selfhood. Others' liking and
choosing you while masked was real; unmasking grants access to more of the
self rather than retroactively invalidating who you were. Being called "too
much" reflects environment fit, not an intrinsic flaw. Recovery from
[autistic burnout](autistic-burnout.md) and [burnout skill
loss](burnout-skill-loss.md) comes from embracing the survivor who carried
you this far, not from damning who you were while masked.
