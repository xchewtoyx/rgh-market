## The distinction

A person has a working range ("window of tolerance") within which they can
handle stress while staying balanced, clear-thinking, and socially engaged.
Exceeding that range pushes the nervous system in one of two directions,
not a single generic "overwhelmed" state:

- **Hyperarousal** (exceeding the upper threshold): anxiety, agitation,
  panic — an activated, escalating state. Feeds [meltdown](meltdown.md)
  when it spikes acutely, or [chronic stress
  activation](chronic-stress-activation.md) when it fails to stand down
  between triggers.
- **Hypoarousal** (dropping below the lower threshold): dissociation,
  fatigue, numbness, detachment — a collapsed, disengaged state. Feeds
  [shutdown](shutdown.md).

Neurodivergent people tend to have a narrower window than average, meaning
less stress is required to cross either threshold — but the *direction* a
given overload pushes someone in is not universal; the same triggering load
can produce hyperarousal in one person or situation and hypoarousal in
another.

## Why the distinction matters

Both directions stem from the same root cause — demand exceeding current
capacity — but they look and are experienced as opposites, and are easy to
mistake for unrelated problems: hyperarousal for a purely emotional/anxiety
issue, hypoarousal for laziness, sulking, or disinterest. Recognizing both
as the same underlying mechanism, just pushed in opposite directions,
prevents treating one person's shutdown and another's meltdown (or the same
person's shutdown on one day and meltdown on another) as unrelated
incidents rather than the same capacity-exceeded event manifesting
differently.
