  A pattern in which continuous "urgent" demands are layered onto a
  person's core committed work without ever relieving them of it, until the
  original work dies from neglect and the person disengages.

## Recognizing it

- **Trigger**: a person is invested in a specific, meaningful piece of work,
  and managers (or a broader reactive culture) repeatedly add unrelated
  "urgent" requests on top of it rather than reprioritizing it away or
  resourcing it properly. Offers to protect the core project (e.g. training
  someone else to absorb the new requests) are declined or ignored.
- **Progression if unaddressed**: the original project stalls and is
  eventually reassigned or shelved; the person is left cycling through an
  ever-shifting portfolio of disconnected "urgent" work, with some of that
  work also later shelved for newer requests. The sustained loss of
  investment (having cared about work that was allowed to die from neglect)
  produces distress and depression, which develops into disengagement —
  reduced effort and investment in anything, since investment was
  repeatedly punished rather than protected — and commonly ends in the
  person leaving.
- **What it is commonly confused with**: from the outside this can look
  like the person simply being reassigned as business needs shift, or like
  ordinary attrition — obscuring that the actual mechanism was a sustained
  failure to ever relieve the person of prior commitments before adding new
  ones, which is what converted normal reprioritization into a breakdown
  pattern.

## Distinguishing marker

The reliable tell is the *accumulation without subtraction*: each new
"urgent" item adds to the load rather than replacing something removed.
Healthy reprioritization swaps commitments; this pattern only stacks them.
