## Recognizing it

- **Mechanism**: during a depressive episode the brain associates depressed
  mood with negative thinking; thereafter, sad mood alone can reactivate that
  thinking pattern. Never-depressed people's dysfunctional beliefs change
  little when sad; formerly depressed people endorse dysfunctional attitudes
  much more strongly when sad than when euthymic — as if depressive thinking
  "had not really been wiped from the hard disk during recovery; small shifts
  in mood could reinstall it."
- **Marker**: **cognitive reactivity** — the degree of mood-activated
  reinstatement of negative thinking. Vulnerable people show a much larger
  swing into global negative self-judgment ("I am worthless," "I am stupid")
  from a small mood dip than never-depressed people do. The reactivated
  negative thinking feeds back to intensify mood — a vicious cycle raising
  risk that mild sadness escalates into major depressive episodes.
- **Progression if unaddressed**: patients with the greatest mood-triggered
  increase in dysfunctional beliefs are more likely to relapse over months;
  the [rumination spiral](rumination-spiral.md) and this reactivity often
  form one **mode of mind** — mood, thoughts, images, body sensations wheeled
  into place together with feedback loops between emotion and body.

## What it is commonly confused with

Persistent dysfunctional attitudes as a stable trait measured in normal mood
— research found recovered patients' scores indistinguishable from
never-depressed people's when euthymic; the vulnerability is *reactivity*
under mood challenge, not permanently elevated baseline beliefs.

## Relationship to depression vicious cycle

Feeds the [depression vicious cycle](depression-vicious-cycle.md) at the
cognitive entry point: a small external or internal mood dip becomes the
trigger for thought–behavior spirals (e.g. believing "nobody likes me" →
social withdrawal → worsened mood) that CT originally targeted.
