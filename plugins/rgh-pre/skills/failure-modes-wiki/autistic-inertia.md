## Recognizing it

- **Marker**: the physics-metaphor pattern — objects at rest tend to stay
  at rest, objects in motion tend to stay in motion. Difficulty is
  concentrated specifically at the transition point: starting a task from
  standstill, or stopping/switching away from a task already in progress.
  Once a task is underway, continuing it is often comparatively easy; the
  friction is at the boundary, not throughout. Can manifest as an "unending
  train" of the same activity the person seems unable to stop.
- **What it is commonly confused with**: from the outside — and often from
  the inside too — this reads as laziness, procrastination, lack of
  motivation, or lack of interest in the task. The distinguishing tell is
  that the same person can be highly capable and engaged *once past the
  transition*, and the difficulty recurs consistently at transitions even
  for tasks the person values or wants to do, including stopping a
  genuinely enjoyable activity. Motivation-based accounts predict low
  effort throughout the task; inertia predicts a specific, disproportionate
  spike of difficulty right at the start or stop boundary, followed by
  normal or even elevated capability.
- **Relationship to hyperfocus**: the stopping side of inertia and
  unmanaged [hyperfocus](hyperfocus.md) can look similar (both involve
  difficulty disengaging from a task), but their trigger differs — inertia
  is a generic transition-boundary friction that applies to any task,
  while hyperfocus's stopping difficulty is specific to a state of
  elevated absorption in that particular task.
