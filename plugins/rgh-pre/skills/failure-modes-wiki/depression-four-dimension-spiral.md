## Recognizing it

Depression is more than sad mood: appetite and sleep disturbance, poor
concentration, agitation or profound slowing, loss of interest/motivation,
and in severe cases suicidal ideation. Sadness gives way to depression when
it turns into persistent harsh negative thoughts and feelings, which generate
tension, aches, fatigue, and turmoil that feed more negative thinking.

Four dimensions interact:

- **Feelings** — sad, miserable, hopeless, often clustered with anxiety,
  anger, irritability (especially common in young people, sometimes more
  prominent than sadness). Prior depression history makes sad mood also bring
  low self-esteem and self-blame — feeling like a failure, useless,
  unlovable.
- **Thoughts** — self-critical running commentary interprets ambiguous facts;
  see [depression interpretation loop](depression-interpretation-loop.md).
  Automatic depressed thoughts cluster around worthlessness and self-blame;
  when depressed they "can seem like the absolute truth" — "a war we wage
  against ourselves."
- **Body** — dysregulated rhythms (eating, sleep), unexplained aches and pains
  (80% of depressed people consult physicians for this), fight/flight/freeze
  activation that does not distinguish external threat from internal worry.
  Body contraction feeds back "we are threatened" to the mind; most people
  are too busy thinking to notice body signals; depressed people may avoid
  bodily awareness, producing more unconscious contraction.
- **Behavior** — giving up nourishing activities, working harder on
  "essential" demands, [depression exhaustion funnel](depression-exhaustion-funnel.md),
  choices from a depressive state of mind that keep the person stuck.

## Progression if unaddressed

With repeated episodes, connections strengthen until **any one element alone
can trigger the whole depression** — a fleeting thought of failure triggers
fatigue; a small family comment triggers guilt and inadequacy cascades.
Spirals triggered by small events feel like they "come out of nowhere." Sad
mood can reactivate suicidal ideas across episodes — if someone felt suicidal
in one episode, very likely in the next, especially with large suicidality
swings from mild mood changes ([cognitive
reactivity](cognitive-reactivity.md)).

## What it is commonly confused with

A single bad week of sadness after a loss — the distinguishing tell is the
self-reinforcing cross-dimension loop and persistence beyond the original
upheaval, feeding the [depression vicious cycle](depression-vicious-cycle.md)
and [rumination spiral](rumination-spiral.md).
