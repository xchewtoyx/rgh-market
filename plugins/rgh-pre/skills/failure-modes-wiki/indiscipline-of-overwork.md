## Recognizing it

- **Trigger**: sustained pressure to perform, achieve, or prove capacity —
  often framed internally as discipline or duty — without integrating rest
  and recovery into the load calculation.
- **Marker**: the person keeps working or training past visible deterioration
  in health, judgment, or performance, unable to stop even when rest is
  available and obviously needed. The short-term bargain (more output now)
  is paid for with interest later: career-ending injuries, rushed poor
  decisions, preventable illness, and [autistic burnout](autistic-burnout.md)
  or generic burnout collapse.
- **Progression if unaddressed**: health and relationships erode while work
  continues; decision-making impairment and depression can follow; when the
  role or external structure ends, the person may still be unable to stop —
  the overwork pattern has become the only familiar state ("home to what?").
- **What it is commonly confused with**: admirable discipline, dedication,
  or high standards — when the actual driver is an inability to rest that
  makes the person fragile rather than strong. Skipping workouts and
  overtraining both arrive at the same place; so do skipping rest and
  overworking.

## Distinguishing marker from genuine high capacity

Genuine sustainable high output includes deliberate load management — resting
star players on a grueling road trip, pulling oneself mid-game when
performance drops, taking off-seasons. The indiscipline of overwork is the
*false economy*: treating rest as lost time rather than as part of what makes
long-term performance possible.

## Relationship to neurodivergent patterns

The same shape appears when [masking](masking.md) or internalized ableism
drives a push-until-crash cycle — overcompensation mistaken for discipline.
Recognizing indiscipline of overwork as a breakdown *pattern* (not a virtue
to praise) helps distinguish sustainable effort from a trajectory toward
collapse.
