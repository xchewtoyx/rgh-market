## Recognizing it

- **Trigger example**: an acute relational threat — repeated public
  humiliation or bullying (e.g. being sharply belittled in front of
  coworkers) — can precipitate shutdown immediately, described by one
  affected person as feeling like "a karate chop to the gut."
- **Marker**: a sudden, involuntary drop in the ability to respond, speak,
  or act, following the triggering event — not a gradual disengagement and
  not a decision. The person is not choosing to withdraw; the capacity to
  respond has gone offline.
- **What it is commonly confused with**: from the outside, shutdown can look
  like sulking, stonewalling, or willful non-cooperation, prompting others
  to escalate pressure — which tends to deepen the shutdown rather than
  resolve it, since the person cannot simply choose to respond differently
  in the moment.

Shutdown is the [hypoarousal-direction](hyperarousal-vs-hypoarousal.md)
collapse response; its outward, escalating counterpart is
[meltdown](meltdown.md).

## Cumulative autistic shutdown

A second common form is reached when capacity has been pushed well past
coping ability — often after [autistic burnout](autistic-burnout.md) or
[meltdown](meltdown.md) has already occurred, rather than from one sharp
incident alone. Loos and Miller (2004) described autistic children under
ongoing social stress entering states resembling catatonia — "dazed, sleepy,
and nonresponsive," lasting from ten minutes to two hours.

- **Marker**: speech and cognitive processing slow; the person seeks quiet
  places, may become nonresponsive or need excessive prompting to respond,
  and may sleep excessively while still feeling sleepy when well rested.
  An inward, shame-trained form — common in those shamed for meltdowns as
  children — adds inability to talk or move, dissociation ("as if blackness
  were covering their eyes and their ears were filled with moss"), not
  fainting but similar, with panic and sometimes internal screaming. Like
  meltdowns, shutdowns cannot be stopped once started, though certain inputs
  can comfort toward the end.
- **Progression if unaddressed**: relationship loss, job loss, and friction
  with environments that pathologize the state and treat the person as
  "broken" or "crazy" for entering it — the same social misread as acute
  shutdown, but often with a longer runway of missed warning signs because
  the collapse follows a period of already-depleted functioning.
