  A breakdown pattern in which strategies that previously worked continue
  rigidly even as evidence accumulates that conditions have changed and the
  old approach is no longer adaptive.

## Recognizing it

- **Trigger**: environmental change, new demands, or shifted capacity —
  while the person or organization continues executing past-success
  behaviors without revising the underlying plan or assessment.
- **Marker**: rote continuation of standard procedures assuming conditions
  that are no longer met — trying harder with the same strategy when the
  strategy's prerequisites have failed. Sub-patterns include:
  - oversimplifying the current situation to fit an old model
  - failing to revise assessment as new evidence arrives
  - discounting discrepant evidence that the old approach isn't working
  - literal rule-following when experienced conditions fall outside what the
    plan was designed for
- **Progression if unaddressed**: the mismatch between strategy and reality
  widens until a dramatic failure forces attention — but minor stretch
  signals beforehand were available if investigated.

## Personal-scale examples

- Continuing to [mask](masking.md) and push harder after diagnosis or
  burnout revealed the old cope-no-more pattern.
- Applying neurotypical burnout advice (more socializing, salon visits,
  meditation) when autistic overload is the actual driver.
- Executing a memorized social script in environments that require
  adaptation the script cannot provide.

## What it is commonly confused with

Discipline, consistency, or "not giving up" — when the actual issue is
failure to re-plan. The effort is real; the fit is wrong.

## Distinguishing marker

New evidence or changed conditions are present, but behavior has not
updated — not because the person lacks insight in general, but because
re-planning costs coordination, identity, or energy that feels unavailable
mid-crisis. Recognizing the trap is the first step toward revising rather
than intensifying the outdated strategy.
