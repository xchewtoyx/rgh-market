## Recognizing it

At the heart of depressive [rumination spiral](rumination-spiral.md) is a
**discrepancy monitor**: a process continually comparing self and current
situation against a model of what is desired, required, expected, or feared.
Once switched on, it finds mismatches (that is its job), and registering
mismatches motivates further attempts to reduce the discrepancy.

Crucially, dwelling on the mismatch itself generates further negative mood
— so attempts to solve the "problem" of feeling sad by thinking about it
keep the person locked in the state they are trying to escape. Remaining
upset for days after a minor slight often continues because the mind goes
round and round trying to work out why one got so upset.

## Relationship to relapse

Relapse reactivates automatic negative thinking patterns at lowering mood —
a whole package of thoughts, feelings, and body sensations, not isolated
thoughts ([cognitive reactivity](cognitive-reactivity.md), [depression
four-dimension spiral](depression-four-dimension-spiral.md)). People who
have experienced major depression are invested in avoiding recurrence and
persist in trying to get rid of depression even when repeated efforts fail —
the discrepancy monitor applied to depression itself.

## What it is commonly confused with

Productive problem-solving — genuine problem-solving reaches conclusions
and stops; discrepancy-monitor rumination cycles on the gap between how
things are and how they should be without closing it. The shared root of
both [attachment reaction](attachment-reaction.md) and [aversion
reaction](aversion-reaction.md) is needing things to be different from how
they are right now — effort goes into changing "how it should be" to avoid
facing unpleasantness, and when that strategy fails the response is often
to redouble the same avoidance and rumination rather than change strategy.
