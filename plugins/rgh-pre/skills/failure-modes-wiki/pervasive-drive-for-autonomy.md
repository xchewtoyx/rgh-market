## Recognizing it

- **Trigger**: any perceived external demand or expectation — even trivial
  ones (one glass in the dishwasher, one math problem, showering). The
  demand is experienced as impinging on freedom and autonomy, not merely as
  an unpleasant task.
- **Marker**: intense negative reaction to demands, including demands the
  person *wants* to meet — being productive, being nice to loved ones,
  learning, working. They cannot force themselves if they perceive it as
  someone else's expectation, because loss-of-autonomy fear is so intense.
- **Progression if unaddressed**: high risk of school or work avoidance,
  difficulty launching into adulthood, alienation of relationships that
  buffer chronic stress, and accumulation of trauma, misinterpretation, and
  further anxiety or depression on top of the pattern itself.
- **Hallmark inconsistency**: day-to-day performance varies sharply
  ("Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde" in family reports) — easily misread as purely
  volitional when it reflects an anxiety-driven autonomy need, not choice.
  Some people mask successfully at work or school and fall apart at home,
  or vice versa.
- **What it is commonly confused with**: oppositional defiant disorder,
  bipolar disorder, or intermittent explosive disorder — because reactions
  can be extreme and externally visible. Superficially functional social
  skills can also make observers doubt an underlying autism diagnosis.

Also known in clinical literature as pathological demand avoidance (PDA);
**pervasive drive for autonomy** reframes the pattern as an anxiety-driven
need for self-determination rather than pathology in wanting autonomy.
Demand avoidance is a nervous-system/hormonal reaction, not a rational or
stubborn choice — a demand triggers alarm that giving up autonomy in the
moment is dangerous and cannot simply be overridden by will. Context: autistics
face large pay gaps and unemployment; constant externally imposed obligations
in a "Kafkaesque" work culture compounds the trigger.

## Intervention recognition note

Circumvention via incremental substitution rather than "tricking the brain" —
e.g. coffee → juice → flavored water until a feared substance (water linked
to childhood monitoring) no longer registers as the dreaded demand. Even
wanted tasks (cleaning kitchen) can become impossible once framed as someone
else's request ("Great, you've ruined it. Now I'm not going to.").

## Unmasking context

A major driver of autistic unhappiness is felt lack of autonomy — "white-
knuckling" daily life. Unmasking can feel like dissolving identity ("larval
soup") before stability returns; demand sensitivity often surfaces during
unmasking crisis.

## Distinguishing marker from ordinary avoidance

Ordinary [emotional avoidance](emotional-avoidance.md) avoids distressing
content or situations. Pervasive drive for autonomy is specifically triggered
by the *demand frame* — the sense that someone else expects or requires the
action — even when the action itself would be welcome without that frame.
