---
type: concept
title: Likability as a Wrong Objective
description: >
  Treating universal likability as a life objective trades away boundaries,
  authenticity, and sustainable relationships — accepting that some people
  will dislike your true self is often the prerequisite for protecting what
  actually matters.
sources:
  - title: "The Unmasking Workbook for Autistic Adults"
    resource: "The Unmasking Workbook for Autistic Adults (Jessica Penot), ch. 8"
---

Traditional social-skills training teaches changing innate behavior to please
others. When likability becomes the objective, the chronic costs can dwarf
any acute social win: abuse in youth, relationships collapsing once masking
cannot be sustained, chronic anxiety and self-hatred — despite "following the
rules."

Reverse social skills training reframes the target: sometimes the best outcome
is being **disliked** by people who needed a masked version of you. That
acceptance underlies assertiveness, self-advocacy, boundary-setting, and
protection from exploitation. Letting go of likability can push some people
away while deepening bonds with those who value your authentic self — and
makes it possible to say no, remove harmful people, and protect needed alone
time without guilt.

Evaluate interventions and social commitments with the same question this
bundle applies elsewhere: are you optimizing for visible social approval
(acute) or for [blooming as life outcome](blooming-as-life-outcome.md)
(chronic)? Reflection prompts when auditing a social pattern: What has
pursuing likability cost you? What could you gain if you let yourself be
disliked? Pair with
[minimum-masking trade-off](minimum-masking-trade-off.md) for contexts where
some performance remains necessary, and
[autonomy-preservation heuristic](autonomy-preservation-heuristic.md) to
check whether "helpful" social structures still preserve self-direction.
