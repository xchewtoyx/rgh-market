---
type: concept
title: Downward vs. Upward Resilience
description: >
  Macro-level explicit goal rules and communicated intent (downward) versus
  micro-level sacrifice judgements and professional buffering (upward) —
  both needed to balance conflicting objectives, with over-reliance on
  upward resilience when downward is weak as a structural vulnerability.
sources:
  - title: "Resilience Engineering in Practice: A Guidebook"
    resource: "Resilience Engineering in Practice (Hollnagel, Pariès, Woods, Wreathall, eds.), ch. 12"
---

Balancing [acute vs. chronic objectives](acute-vs-chronic-objectives.md)
requires structure at two scales:

**Downward resilience** — strategic/macro choices that prepare the system:
clear goal rules (not vague double-edged visions), infrastructure, procedures
for safety–efficiency trade-offs, and communicated intent behind plans so
front-line actors know what to optimize when rules don't fit the moment.
Without distinct goal rules at the top, "safe and community-serving" (or
"healthy and high-performing") collapses into political negotiation every
time pressure rises — and sacrifice judgements lack authority.

**Upward resilience** — micro-level commitment when goals conflict:
[sacrifice judgements](sacrifice-judgements.md), experience-based bridging
between written procedure and what circumstances require, craft buffers
(e.g., technicians creating "technical delay" space to preserve airworthiness).
Operators resolve hidden grey zones between design and real work through
professionalism — but that is fragile if management signals efficiency over
safety ("economy gets precedence," weakened trust in central management).

A system that depends on upward resilience to **compensate for absent
downward resilience** is vulnerable: front-line heroism becomes the only
margin. Conversely, downward rules without upward judgement become brittle —
repeated deviation from prescribed design becomes the de facto rule (Snook,
Vaughan), obscuring whether trade-offs are genuine sacrifices or slow drift.

For personal PRE, downward resilience means explicit written priorities —
which objectives are chronic and non-negotiable, what "good enough" acute
performance looks like this season, and intent behind plans so future-you
can make sacrifice calls without re-litigating values under stress. Upward
resilience is the in-the-moment call to slow down, rest, or decline despite
no rule explicitly forbidding the push. Strengthen both: macro clarity so
micro calls aren't lonely, and micro vigilance so small variances register
before drift normalizes — without overconfidence that individual judgment
alone replaces structure.
