description: >
  A five-part checklist for noticing when chronic life objectives are
  actually being met — including unplanned small moments, quiet baselines,
  and slow seasonal payoffs — so evaluation does not chase milestones while
  missing bloom already present.

After pruning and soil work, bloom often arrives quietly and can be missed if
evaluation only watches for big external wins. Five heuristics for judging
whether [blooming as life outcome](blooming-as-life-outcome.md) is happening:

1. **Look for surprise flowers.** Bloom often takes you off guard — unplanned
   moments of joy, connection, pride, or peace along the way. Stay open while
   pursuing set goals; small moments often mean more than the achievement
   itself (guards against
   [peak-end rule and duration neglect](peak-end-rule-and-duration-neglect.md)
   over-weighting memorable peaks).

2. **Redefine success at evaluation time.** Ask whether you feel safe, loved,
   and at home in your own skin — not only whether money, targets, or titles
   moved. A bloom may look like a quiet evening, not fireworks.

3. **Be patient with seasons.** After corrective work, there is usually a
   waiting season before full effect shows — weeks, months, or years in a new
   environment. Do not declare an intervention failed because chronic
   objectives lack leading indicators yet; see
   [chronic-objective measurement asymmetry](chronic-objective-measurement-asymmetry.md).

4. **Savour it when it comes.** People often chase happiness and miss it when
   it is present — pause, notice, and absorb bloom instead of immediately
   pivoting to the next deficit.

5. **Keep tending.** Bloom is not the end of the story; it opens a new season
   requiring continued care of roots, relationships, and environment so the
   baseline does not silently erode under the next acute push.

Use this checklist when asking whether the PRE system as a whole is working —
not only whether a single project shipped.
