type: concept
title: Sacrifice Judgements
description: >
  Deliberately relaxing acute production pressure to stay clear of chronic
  safety margins — a trade-off decision that often looks wasteful in
  hindsight and only works if the culture still rewards it when "nothing
  happened."
sources:
  - title: "Resilience Engineering: Concepts and Precepts"
    resource: "Resilience Engineering: Concepts and Precepts (Hollnagel, Woods, Leveson, eds.), ch. 2"
---

A **sacrifice judgement** temporarily trades acute goals (speed, efficiency,
visible output) for margin against chronic risk (safety, sustainability,
relationship or health boundaries). Examples: converting to a slower surgical
approach when complications build; breaking off an approach in marginal
weather; a personal production slowdown when early warning signs stack.

Two versions of the "courageous voice" story:

1. Stop production; investigation proves the concern was right — easy to
   admire.
2. Stop production; follow-up shows everything was fine — the sacrifice
   looks unnecessary. **This is the real test:** do peers and management still
   treat the voice as courageous and valuable?

If only successful prophecies are rewarded, people balancing multiple goals
will act **riskier than they consciously intend** — the production-over-safety
shift is often implicit and unrecognized. Sacrifice judgements are hardest
because hindsight frequently says "nothing happened," making peer and
supervisor reaction decisive.

For personal PRE, sacrifice judgements include: declining the extra
commitment when sleep debt is rising; taking a restorative weekend despite
a backlog; saying no to a sprint when
[acute vs. chronic objectives](acute-vs-chronic-objectives.md) are out of
balance. The intervention that "cost" visible output may have bought distance
from a boundary that never visibly broke — which is success, not waste.

Ironically, investment in resilience sources is most needed when acute tempo
is highest — the moment extra margin feels least affordable. Tools that
improve discrimination (noticing sliding trade-offs) and criterion placement
(where to set the threshold given current risk) help; so does treating chronic
protection as a **basic value** rather than a negotiable line item. Pair with
[sustainable effort ceiling](sustainable-effort-ceiling.md) (slack as
deliberate margin) and [demand triage by droppability](demand-triage-by-droppability.md)
when capacity is already reduced.
