---
type: concept
title: Chosen Relationship Objectives
description: >
  Once societal default social obligations are stripped away, explicitly
  decide which human connections you actually want — a partner, one friend,
  solitude, family trust — instead of maintaining relationships because
  they look normal.
sources:
  - title: "The Unmasking Workbook for Autistic Adults"
    resource: "The Unmasking Workbook for Autistic Adults (Jessica Penot), ch. 8"
---

A common failure mode is maintaining a friend group, social calendar, or
interaction level because it **seems normal**, not because it serves
sustainable quality of life. After removing the drive to appear normal, many
autistic adults discover they never enjoyed group socializing — and that their
actual objective set is smaller and different: a pair-bonded partner, trusted
family, solitude, perhaps one outing friend — not the default neurotypical
social portfolio.

Naming chosen objectives before investing capacity prevents
[reward-trap vs. genuine duty](reward-trap-vs-genuine-duty.md) confusion in
relationships ("I have to keep this up" when the honest answer is "I thought
I was supposed to want this"). Operative "social skill" then shifts from
performance (eye contact, small talk) toward thoughtful care for people you
** chose** to keep available: asking how to help, listening, learning wants
and needs through clear verbal communication.

This is [personal priority over social default](personal-priority-over-social-default.md)
applied to relationship inventory: what is worth keeping reliably available
*for this person*, not what others expect. Pair with
[minimum-masking trade-off](minimum-masking-trade-off.md) to spend masking
budget only in contexts tied to chosen objectives, and
[self-care domains as protection targets](self-care-domains-as-protection-targets.md)
to audit whether the social domain is protecting chosen connections or
performing obligation.
