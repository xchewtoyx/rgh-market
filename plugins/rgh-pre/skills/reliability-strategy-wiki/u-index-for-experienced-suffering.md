---
type: concept
title: U-Index for Experienced Suffering
description: >
  The percentage of waking time spent in unpleasant affect — a duration-
  grounded metric of the experiencing self's burden, distinct from global
  life-satisfaction scores.
sources:
  - title: "Thinking, Fast and Slow"
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 38"
---

Global life-satisfaction questions address the remembering self; the
**U-index** measures the **experiencing self**: the proportion of time in
states where any negative feeling rating exceeds all positive ratings. Four
unpleasant hours in a sixteen-hour waking day = U-index 25%. Because it is
grounded in clock time, a population drop from 20% to 18% means total
emotional discomfort fell by a tenth — a concrete suffering-reduction target.

Day Reconstruction Method (DRM) reconstructs yesterday as episodes with
duration-weighted affect ratings; it aligns with experience sampling and
can be paired with life-satisfaction measures on the same day for comparison.

Findings illustrate what moves experienced burden: commute, work, and child
care score high; socializing and sex low; weekends beat weekdays mostly via
less disliked activity and less work tension. Mood is driven primarily by
**current situation and attention**, not stable job status — time pressure,
noise, boss presence, and whether eating is the focal activity matter more
than benefits on paper. "Happiness is the experience of spending time with
people you love and who love you."

Kahneman's policy gloss: reduce human suffering; prioritize depression and
extreme poverty; **control use of time** — one of the few domains people can
arrange. For PRE, use U-index thinking when judging interventions: did this
change the *integral* of unpleasant hours, or only how the week feels in
retrospect? Pair with
[life evaluation vs. experienced well-being](life-evaluation-vs-experienced-well-being.md),
[duration-weighted well-being](duration-weighted-well-being.md), and
[peak-end rule and duration neglect](peak-end-rule-and-duration-neglect.md)
to avoid optimizing the wrong verdict.
