type: concept
title: Vital Few vs. Trivial Many
description: >
  A prioritization heuristic for competing demands under fixed capacity —
  identify the small subset of tasks/objectives that actually matter and
  give them the lion's share of time and energy, rather than spreading
  effort evenly across everything that shows up.
sources:
  - title: "Neurodiversity For Dummies"
    resource: "Neurodiversity For Dummies (John Marble, Khushboo Chabria, Ranga Jayaraman), Chapter 12"
---

A common failure mode when objectives compete for the same limited capacity
is overcommitting: taking on (or trying to advance) everything that lands on
the plate, on the assumption that capacity will stretch to cover it.
Capacity doesn't stretch that way, and the result is a pile of half-finished
projects rather than a smaller set of finished, high-value ones.

The corrective move is to explicitly triage: separate the "vital few" —
tasks/objectives whose completion actually matters to the goals worth
protecting — from the "trivial many" that consume time without
proportionate payoff, then deliberately direct the bulk of available time
and energy toward the vital few rather than distributing effort evenly.

This is a general prioritization tool for whenever multiple objectives
compete for the same capacity (see also
[demand triage by droppability](demand-triage-by-droppability.md) for a
related pre-commitment approach used specifically under reduced capacity).
The key discipline is resisting the default of treating everything that
demands attention as equally deserving of it.
