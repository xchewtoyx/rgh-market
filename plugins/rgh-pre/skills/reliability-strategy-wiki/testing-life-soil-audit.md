  work, where many adults spend half their waking hours — supplies enough
  support and return on energy to let chronic life objectives thrive, or
  is quietly burning you out.

Just as a cherry tree cannot bloom in acidic, nutrient-starved soil, a person
cannot reliably hit [blooming as life outcome](blooming-as-life-outcome.md)
in an environment set up to make life hard. "Soil" is whatever surrounds and
feeds you — for most adults, work is the largest patch: nearly half of waking
hours. Unhappiness there often means unhappiness overall; security-and-
community jobs can be enough for some, but a persistent pull toward "something
more" is also a valid objective signal worth naming.

Five audit steps:

1. **Check the pH (toxicity).** Is the environment somewhere you can grow, or
   somewhere quietly burning you out? Do you like colleagues, feel supported,
   look forward to showing up? Intuition often knows before the audit finishes.

2. **Look at the nutrients (exchange).** What do you genuinely get back —
   money, meaning, community, creativity — for the energy you put in? A hollow
   exchange (giving more than you receive) means the soil is starving what you
   need to thrive. This is a [sacrifice judgement](sacrifice-judgements.md)
   in reverse: chronic depletion for acute pay that no longer balances.

3. **Notice your symptoms (leading indicators).** Physical exhaustion, constant
   anxiety, and deep resentment are not "normal work stress" — they are
   warning signs the soil is wrong, especially when your body reacts before
   every shift. See
   [chronic-objective measurement asymmetry](chronic-objective-measurement-asymmetry.md):
   by the time you rationalize symptoms away, chronic objectives may already
   be eroding.

4. **Feed the ground you stand in.** Sometimes soil improves without replanting:
   a new challenge, boundaries, clearer priorities, or asking for support.
   Small shifts can restore nutrients when the environment is fixable.

5. **Dare to replant.** When no amount of compost fixes fundamentally wrong
   ground, leaving — side hustle toward a dream, promotion push, year abroad,
   career change — is not recklessness but creating conditions where realistic
   options can build. Financial constraint may delay the move; under the right
   conditions, options accumulate until risk becomes manageable.

Use this audit when judging whether the PRE system should invest in in-place
controls or treat the environment itself as the failed objective. Pair with
[thriving vs. surviving as the target objective](thriving-vs-surviving.md)
(blaming the person for bad soil misses the diagnosis) and
[success criteria before commitment](success-criteria-before-commitment.md)
(ask whether you are moving toward or away from stated dreams right now).
