title: Self-Care Domains as a Protection-Target Taxonomy
description: >
  One way to decompose "what needs protecting" into concrete life domains —
  physical, emotional, mental, social, and professional — usable as a
  checklist for whether an intervention or life setup is covering every
  front rather than only the one currently making noise.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Preface/Introduction, Chapter 1"
---

A recurring failure mode in self-care is treating whichever domain is
currently loudest (usually physical exhaustion or acute emotional distress)
as the whole picture, and neglecting domains that degrade more quietly. One
concrete taxonomy for auditing coverage:

- **Physical** — the body: sleep, movement, hygiene, nutrition.
- **Emotional** — awareness and regulation of one's own emotional states.
- **Mental** — identity, values-alignment, cognitive/mental-health resilience.
- **Social** — relationships and navigating social environments.
- **Professional** — sustaining work/career without it consuming the other
  domains.

A domain-by-domain check ("which of these am I actually protecting right
now, and which have I quietly let slide?") surfaces neglect that a single
global sense of "I'm managing okay" can hide — a person can be fine on the
domain they're paying attention to while a different domain silently erodes
toward crisis. This taxonomy is one concrete instantiation of the more
general question of which life areas are worth keeping reliably available;
other framings (e.g. safety, basic care, relationships, meaningful work,
autonomy, recovery capacity) carve the same territory differently, and the
choice of taxonomy matters less than actually using one to check for blind
spots rather than triaging by whatever is loudest.
