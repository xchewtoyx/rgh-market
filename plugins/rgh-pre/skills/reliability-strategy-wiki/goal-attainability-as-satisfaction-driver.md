---
type: concept
title: Goal Attainability as a Satisfaction Driver
description: >
  Life-evaluation satisfaction depends heavily on whether stated goals were
  attained — especially for goals the person marked as important — so
  choosing objectives that are difficult to reach is a structural recipe for
  chronic dissatisfaction even when circumstances are objectively good.
sources:
  - title: "Thinking, Fast and Slow"
    resource: "Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman), ch. 39"
---

Global life satisfaction is not just a readout of circumstances; it is
filtered through what the person wanted and whether they got it. In
longitudinal data, the importance placed on financial success at age 18
predicted both later income and later *satisfaction with income*: among
high earners, those who had called wealth essential were far more satisfied
than those who hadn't; among people who wanted wealth and didn't achieve it,
dissatisfaction ran higher than average. The gap between high and low income
on a satisfaction scale was ~5× larger for wealth-focused respondents than
for those who said money wasn't important.

The general principle: **setting goals that are especially difficult to
attain is one recipe for a dissatisfied adulthood.** The least promising
goal measured in one study was "becoming accomplished in a performing art" —
high aspiration, low attainment rate, persistent evaluative gap. Low
correlations between circumstances (e.g. marriage) and satisfaction also
arise from balancing effects: the same circumstance helps some people and
hurts others, washing out in the aggregate.

For PRE objective-setting, this means objectives should be chosen with
attainability and personal fit in mind, not only intrinsic worthiness.
Pursuing a vital objective that is structurally unreachable for this person
in this season trades chronic life-evaluation dissatisfaction for symbolic
commitment. Pair goal choice with
[life evaluation vs. experienced well-being](life-evaluation-vs-experienced-well-being.md):
goals drive evaluation; daily experience has its own logic (and can diverge
when evaluation is mood- or salience-driven). When objectives compete under
fixed capacity, see also
[vital few vs. trivial many](vital-few-vs-trivial-many.md) — not every
ambitious goal deserves protection if it predictably produces a permanent
evaluative shortfall.
