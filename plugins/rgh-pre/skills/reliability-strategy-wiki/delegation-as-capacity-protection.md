---
type: concept
title: Delegation as Capacity Protection
description: >
  Refusing to hand off work you could do — even from good motives — is a
  form of gluttony that erodes the time and reflective space needed to serve
  chronic objectives and the main thing.
sources:
  - title: "Discipline Is Destiny: The Power of Self-Control"
    resource: "Discipline Is Destiny (Ryan Holiday), ch. 12"
---

"It takes discipline *not* to insist on doing everything yourself" — especially
when you hold high standards, enjoy the tasks, or feel guilty spending money
or asking for help. The outcome matches gluttony for control, punishment, or
work: you wear down, the cause suffers, and the world loses comparative
advantage from others' strengths.

Leaders must know how to do anything but cannot do everything. **Share the
load**: delegate to capable people, automate or reassign legacy tasks, and
when possible hire a buffer who says no for you — willpower alone is not
enough. Know what an hour of your time is worth; refusing known
inefficiencies is procrastination dressed as virtue.

The payoff is not only time but **space** — freedom to think, connect, and
reflect on meaning rather than mechanics (King's wished-for week of reading
and meditation). Belafonte paying for household staff for the Kings was
strategy: buying peace of mind so the movement got a stronger leader, not
indulgence.

For PRE, delegation protects
[main thing as priority anchor](main-thing-as-priority-anchor.md) and
[non-negotiable protection targets](non-negotiable-protection-targets.md).
It is upward resilience supported by downward permission — macro clarity that
help is allowed. Without it, [acute vs. chronic objectives](acute-vs-chronic-objectives.md)
collapse into solo heroism until burnout. Privilege limits who can outsource,
but everyone can identify droppable or reassignable tasks per
[demand triage by droppability](demand-triage-by-droppability.md).
