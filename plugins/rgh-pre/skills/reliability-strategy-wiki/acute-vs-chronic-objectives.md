---
type: concept
title: Acute vs. Chronic Objectives
description: >
  Competing objective families — short-term output (acute) versus emergent
  long-horizon system properties like safety and sustainability (chronic) —
  that cannot all be maximized together and default toward sacrificing the
  chronic set unless it is explicitly prioritized.
sources:
  - title: "Resilience Engineering: Concepts and Precepts"
    resource: "Resilience Engineering: Concepts and Precepts (Hollnagel, Woods, Leveson, eds.), ch. 2"
---

When multiple objectives press on the same finite person or organization,
they fall into two families with different time horizons and measurability:

**Acute objectives** — timely delivery, efficiency, narrow "better"
(capability gains, throughput, schedule). Assessed with short-term, pointed
metrics (counts, delays, this week's output). In personal PRE: hitting
deadlines, clearing the inbox, visible productivity, cost/time savings.

**Chronic objectives** — safety, sustainability, centeredness on the actual
person served (not a proxy), equitable access to one's own care and
relationships. These are *emergent* properties of how parts interact over
longer horizons; they are harder to measure and easier to defer. In personal
PRE: recovery capacity, relationship availability, basic care, autonomy,
protection from burnout — the things that keep the system from going brittle.

The tension is structural, not a failure of will. "Faster, better, cheaper"
together, or "advance all six goals at once," is a silver-bullet mirage; the
real task is **balancing inherent trade-offs**, not finding a technique that
eliminates them. Under production pressure, advances in acute performance get
consumed to "do more, faster, in more complex ways" (the law of stretched
systems) rather than banked as margin — and the visible crisis point shifts
while the underlying trade-off remains (fix access, safety erodes; fix
safety, efficiency screams).

Default organizational and personal behavior values what is immediate,
direct, and easy to measure — so acute goals win **unless chronic goals are
put first** as basic cultural values, not one line item among many. Hollnagel:
"If anything is unreasonable, it is the requirement to be both efficient and
thorough at the same time — or rather to be thorough when with hindsight it
was wrong to be efficient." Thoroughness on chronic protection is most needed
precisely when acute pressure is highest.

For PRE, chronic objectives map to
[self-care domains as protection targets](self-care-domains-as-protection-targets.md)
and [thriving vs. surviving](thriving-vs-surviving.md). Acute objectives map
to the metrics that tempt
[outcomes vs. activity](outcomes-vs-activity-signal.md) confusion when
activity substitutes for protecting the long game. When acute and chronic
conflict in the moment, see
[sacrifice judgements](sacrifice-judgements.md) and
[resilience erosion under production pressure](resilience-erosion-under-production-pressure.md).
