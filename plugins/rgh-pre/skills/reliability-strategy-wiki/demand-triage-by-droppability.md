title: Demand Triage by Droppability
description: >
  A pre-prepared three-tier (green/yellow/red) classification of which
  demands can be dropped without real consequence, which are droppable
  depending on circumstances, and which cannot be dropped — decided in
  advance so the choice doesn't have to be made under duress.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Megan Anna Neff), Chapter 3 (Emotional Self-Care)"
---

When capacity is low, competing demands (work tasks, social obligations,
household responsibilities) all feel equally urgent — a failure to
distinguish essential from nonessential demands that piles on pressure and
drives burnout. The traffic-light system addresses this by sorting demands
into three tiers **before** a low-capacity period hits, rather than trying to
triage them in the moment:

- **Green**: can be dropped without real consequence (e.g. a family dinner
  that isn't load-bearing to the relationship).
- **Yellow**: droppable depending on circumstances — judgment call in the
  moment.
- **Red**: hard to drop, carries real consequences if missed.

The value of doing this triage in advance, rather than live, is that
low-capacity states (burnout, overwhelm) themselves impair the kind of clear
judgment triage requires — deciding what's droppable is exactly the
executive-function task that's hardest to do well when it's most needed. A
pre-made list turns a judgment call into a lookup.

This is a general-purpose prioritization move whenever objectives compete for
scarce capacity: rank commitments by actual consequence of dropping them,
not by how loud or immediate they feel, and do that ranking while capacity is
available rather than while it's depleted.
