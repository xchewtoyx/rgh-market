---
type: concept
title: Outcomes vs. Activity as the Evaluation Signal
description: >
  Judging an intervention or a stretch of effort by visible activity
  (hours logged, busyness, being seen to work hard) rather than by the
  actual outcome delivered is a systematic way to reward the wrong thing
  and mistake motion for progress.
sources:
  - title: "The Canary Code: A Guide to Neurodiversity, Dignity, and Intersectional Belonging at Work"
    resource: "The Canary Code (Ludmila N. Praslova), Chapter 7"
---

"Productivity theater" — rewarding hours worked, visible busyness, meetings
attended, and being seen to respond quickly — is a poor proxy for whether
anything of value actually got produced. It's possible to score very high
on activity while quality output stagnates or declines, and possible to
produce excellent outcomes with far less visible activity (e.g. via
uninterrupted focus time that looks, from outside, like doing nothing).

This is the general form of the question this whole bundle exists to keep
asking about any given intervention: not "did it increase how busy/occupied
I looked" but "did it move the actual objective." The same trap that
afflicts organizations that reward hours over outcomes afflicts a personal
PRE system that judges itself by how much it did rather than by whether
what it did actually served
[the vital few objectives that matter](vital-few-vs-trivial-many.md) or
protected [sustainable capacity](sustainable-effort-ceiling.md). Activity is
cheap to measure and easy to fake (including faking it to oneself); outcome
is the only signal that actually answers whether an intervention was worth
the resources it consumed.
