---
type: concept
title: Thriving vs. Surviving as the Target Objective
description: >
  Frames "merely getting by" and "thriving" as different destinations
  entirely, not points on the same scale — a reminder that a PRE system
  optimizing only for avoiding collapse can succeed at that and still fail
  the actual goal.
sources:
  - title: "Neurodiversity For Dummies"
    resource: "Neurodiversity For Dummies (John Marble, Khushboo Chabria, Ranga Jayaraman), Chapter 13"
  - title: "The Cherry Tree Theory: A Simple Guide to Stop F*cking Up Your Life"
    resource: "The Cherry Tree Theory (Rox Pink, Rich Pink), Step 7: The blossoming"
---

A plant left in the wrong conditions can survive there indefinitely —
staying alive, technically "fine" — without ever blooming. Moved to the
conditions it actually needs, the same plant becomes vibrant. The plant
didn't change; the bar for what counts as success did.

The same distinction applies to a person's overall system: "surviving" (not
falling into crisis, keeping obligations met, avoiding visible failure) is a
much lower bar than "thriving" (a life that's actually worth sustaining, not
just one that hasn't broken down). A PRE-style evaluation that only checks
"did anything catastrophic happen" will rate a merely-surviving life as
healthy, because absence-of-incident is not the same target as quality of
life. Thriving requires more than avoiding failure: it requires the right
conditions matched to the actual person (self-understanding of what those
conditions are, per
[self-care domains as a protection-target taxonomy](self-care-domains-as-protection-targets.md)),
plus treating the pursuit of those conditions as ongoing practice rather
than a one-time fix — thriving is closer to a daily exercised capability
than a state reached once and then left alone. Concrete bloom indicators —
ordinary days you do not dread, relationships you do not need to escape —
are spelled out in
[blooming as life outcome](blooming-as-life-outcome.md).

This distinction matters directly for judging interventions: an
intervention that keeps someone surviving (prevents visible breakdown) is
not automatically a good trade against sustainable quality of life if it
does so by permanently capping them at "getting by" rather than addressing
what would let them thrive.
