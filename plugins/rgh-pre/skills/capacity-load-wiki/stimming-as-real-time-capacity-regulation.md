title: Stimming as Real-Time Capacity Regulation
description: >
  Self-stimulatory movement, sound, or touch can offload sensory and
  emotional load in the moment — restoring attention and preventing
  escalation — so suppressing stims raises true demand on top of the task.
sources:
  - title: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery"
    resource: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery (Smith), Chapter 4 (Sensory Issues and Stimming)"
---

**Stimming** — rocking, fidgeting, humming, visual stims, verbal play with
words, and many other forms — is regulatory, not mere habit. It can
prevent meltdown, intensify positive feeling, and help a person "stay in
this world" by tuning arousal and attention. Neurotypical fidgeting may aid
focus; autistic stimming often also carries **emotional regulation**
function the observer cannot see.

For capacity accounting, stimming is **real-time load shedding**: it
offloads sensory and emotional pressure while the person is still in the
demanding context. Forcing stillness removes that valve, so the same
visible task costs more and strain accumulates — see [masking as a hidden
capacity drain](masking-as-hidden-capacity-drain.md) and [movement
suppression as hidden capacity drain](movement-suppression-as-hidden-capacity-drain.md).

A person who sat still all day "not by relaxing, but by constant force"
may seek substitute regulation (food, nicotine, substances) when the
primary stim channel is blocked — mistaking a **capacity need** for an
addiction or discipline problem. Restoring allowed movement (dance breaks,
rocking stool, discreet fidget) can reduce downstream overload.

Stimming also sits in the [optimal stimulation
range](optimal-stimulation-range.md): when ambient input is too low or too
high, a chosen stim can tune arousal back into a workable band. Budget
stims as tools in the pacing kit, not distractions to eliminate.
