Deliberate thought, self-control, inhibition, planning, and other
voluntary effort are not independent budgets. Kahneman's walking analogy
makes the interference concrete: at a stroll pace, light mental work and
walking coexist easily; at maximum sustainable pace, resisting the urge to
slow down is itself a mental effort — self-control and deliberate thought
draw on the same limited pool. The same pattern shows up when maintaining
a coherent train of thought requires constant discipline against the urge to
check email or wander off-task.

Baumeister's **ego depletion** research extends this beyond simultaneous
interference to successive spending: an act of will in one domain — resisting
tempting food, suppressing an emotional reaction to a film, making a series
of conflict-laden choices, trying to impress others — measurably reduces
willingness and ability to exert self-control shortly afterward, even on a
different kind of task (shorter grip endurance, giving up sooner on a hard
cognitive problem). The shared pool spans cognitive, emotional, and
physical voluntary effort, not just "thinking hard."

Depletion is not always a hard ceiling. Unlike raw short-term memory load,
which cannot be overcome by incentive alone, ego-depletion effects can
sometimes be resisted when the stakes are high enough — suggesting at
least part of what looks like "running out" is also reduced motivation to
keep spending, not only an empty tank. Still, for pacing purposes the
practical implication is the same: a schedule that stacks multiple
self-control-heavy demands back-to-back treats them as if they were free
of each other, and will underestimate true load.

Situations that reliably draw down the pool include cognitive busyness,
sleep loss, alcohol, illness, and circadian mismatch (morning people's
self-control is worse at night and vice versa). A real-world illustration:
Israeli parole judges' approval rates spike right after food breaks and
decline steadily until the next meal — fatigue and hunger both implicated
in falling back on the easier default decision.

Flow is the main exception within this bundle's scope: in a well-calibrated
absorbed state (see [challenge-skill balance](challenge-skill-balance.md)),
concentration on the task does not require the same ongoing exertion of
self-control, because the resources normally spent on self-monitoring are
freed for the work itself. For recovery after depletion, see [factors that
replenish the self-regulatory pool](self-regulatory-resource-replenishment.md)
and [glucose and effortful cognition](glucose-and-effortful-cognition.md).
