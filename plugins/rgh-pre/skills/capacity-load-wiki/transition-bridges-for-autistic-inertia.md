---
type: concept
title: Transition Bridges for Autistic Inertia
description: >
  Keeping one sensory or attention constant across a task change softens
  the cost of moving from rest to action or from one activity to another
  when inertia makes both starts and stops expensive.
sources:
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Middleton), Chapter 5 (Flexibility — Tip 24)"
---

**Autistic inertia** — wanting to remain in the current state — makes
starting tasks hard and stopping or switching mid-flow harder still
("just one minute" when interrupted). It applies at macro scale too:
winding down after a busy period, or restarting after a holiday break,
can feel as costly as micro switches.

TimorousAlice's **transition bridge**: carry something constant across the
change so the brain experiences less of a hard cut — continue the same
audiobook, song, podcast, fidget, or phone call from task A into task B.
The mechanism is lowering perceived [task-switching
cost](task-switching-cost.md) by preserving one thread of continuity
while the primary activity changes.

This differs from [routine as decision-capacity
preservation](routine-as-decision-capacity-preservation.md), which removes
choices; bridges ease the physics of moving between states when removal
is not possible. Pair with external alarms when stopping is safety-critical
(hyperfocus + inertia can overrun leave-times).
