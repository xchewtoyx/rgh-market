title: Scheduled Work Boundaries for Guilt-Free Rest
description: >
  When work and personal time share the same space, explicitly scheduling
  stressful obligations and separating work channels from personal ones
  frees off-hours rest from the feeling that relaxation is slacking.
sources:
  - title: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery"
    resource: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery (Smith), Chapter 8 (Beating Burnout — Maintain, Recover, Reload)"
---

Blurred work/home boundaries — common after sustained remote work — can
make any downtime feel like stolen productivity: a phone vibration triggers
"I'm slacking," even during intentional rest. That guilt taxes recovery;
time that looks like rest on the calendar may not restore headroom if the
person is mentally on call.

The capacity fix is structural scheduling, not willpower to "relax harder":

- Schedule the stressful items explicitly (not every hour — just what
  creates background dread), so a blocked movie or walk is clearly not
  work time.
- Separate work channels from personal life: no work email on the phone,
  separate computer logins, no work texts after hours — so cues do not
  re-activate the work role during recovery windows.

This pairs with [proactive recovery scheduling](proactive-recovery-scheduling.md)
(put recovery on the calendar before saturation) and [restorative rest
versus cheap rest](restorative-rest-vs-cheap-rest.md): guilt-laden
"downtime" often behaves like cheap rest — relief in the moment without
net capacity return. Boundaries that license rest make restorative
activities more likely to actually refill the pool.
