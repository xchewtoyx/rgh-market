---
type: concept
title: Energy-Rated Task Lists
description: >
  Colour-coding tasks by energy cost and matching them to the day's
  available energy — not just available time — prevents planning that
  assumes full headroom every morning.
sources:
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Middleton), Chapter 5 (Flexibility — Tip 21)"
---

Traditional to-do lists sort by urgency or deadline; they do not ask how
much [shared pool of voluntary effort](shared-pool-of-voluntary-effort.md)
each item will draw. Charlie Rewilding's adaptation labels tasks green
(minimal energy), yellow (moderate), or red (high), then matches the list
to how much energy is actually available that day — green-only after a
last-minute disruption drained her, red-first when she wakes with unexpected
headroom.

The shift is from "what can fit in the calendar" to "what this body-mind
can afford to spend," which is especially important when duration is hard
to predict intuitively and breaks do not come naturally. It pairs with
[schedule around energy patterns](schedule-around-energy-patterns.md) and
[capacity-load triage menu](capacity-load-triage-menu.md) for deciding what
to defer when the day's budget is already spoken for.
