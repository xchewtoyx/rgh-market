type: concept
title: Movement Suppression as Hidden Capacity Drain
description: >
  Holding the body still through sustained muscular effort — not relaxed
  calm — consumes capacity continuously, so "sitting quietly" can be more
  demanding than visible movement.
sources:
  - title: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery"
    resource: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery (Smith), Chapter 4 (Sensory Issues and Stimming)"
---

"Sit still" instructions often assume stillness is low-cost. For some
people, **stillness is active work**: bracing, clenching, and suppressing
natural movement all day leaves exhaustion that looks disproportionate to
the visible task list.

This is a specific channel of [masking as a hidden capacity
drain](masking-as-hidden-capacity-drain.md): the person appears calm while
running continuous motor inhibition. Reconnecting to movement — rocking,
dancing while cooking, a pivot stool, permitted fidget — can restore
interoceptive signals (hunger, fullness) and lower total load, not only
improve comfort.

When movement is blocked, people may adopt substitute regulators (smoking,
vaping, binge eating) that address the arousal need stimming would have
handled. Treating that as purely chemical addiction can miss the underlying
**demand**: the body still needs a regulation outlet.

Pacing implication: count enforced stillness as ongoing demand in sensory
and physical dimensions; allow [stimming as real-time capacity
regulation](stimming-as-real-time-capacity-regulation.md) where possible
rather than budgeting only for "the meeting content."
