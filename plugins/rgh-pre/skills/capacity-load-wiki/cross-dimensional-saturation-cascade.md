title: Cross-Dimensional Saturation Cascade
description: >
  Unaddressed saturation in one dimension of capacity does not stay
  contained there — it degrades headroom in other dimensions even though
  the demand on those other dimensions hasn't changed.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Neff), Chapter 1 (Cultivating Sensory Safety), Chapter 2 (Physical Self-Care)"
---

A [dimension of capacity](dimensions-of-personal-capacity.md) running hot
tends not to stay sealed to that dimension. Two examples from the source:

- Sensory load behaves like a foundation the other dimensions sit on: when
  sensory input goes unmanaged, the strain shows up as anxiety,
  irritability, and loss of focus — the sensory dimension running hot
  degrades headroom in the cognitive and emotional dimensions.
- Physical discomfort behaves the same way from a different starting
  point: unaddressed physical discomfort intensifies the need for routine
  and predictability, and separately erodes executive functioning and
  social tolerance — a physical-dimension deficit surfacing as reduced
  cognitive and social headroom.

The practical implication is ordering, not just budgeting: because a
saturated dimension's strain leaks into others, the dimension that's
actually driving the trouble is often not the one where the symptoms show
up (cognitive struggle can be a downstream symptom of sensory or physical
saturation, not a cognitive-capacity or cognitive-capability problem in its
own right). Attending to the dimension that's actually saturated — rather
than treating all five as independent and equally weighted — tends to
protect headroom across the others too.

Left unaddressed, cascading saturation can compound into a named breakdown
pattern (meltdown or shutdown) — the mechanics of that breakdown itself are
out of scope for this bundle; this note only covers the cross-dimensional
propagation that precedes it. Once triggering input stops, recovery still
depends on [completing the stress-response
cycle](completing-the-stress-response-cycle.md) rather than happening
automatically.
