type: concept
title: Rumination as Continuous Cognitive Demand
description: >
  Repetitive dwelling on guilt, sadness, or self-directed anger consumes
  capacity continuously in the background — not only during focused
  tasks — so headroom can shrink even when nothing new is being done.
sources:
  - title: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery"
    resource: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery (Smith), Chapter 3 (Mirror Neurons, Pruning, and Energy Cost)"
---

Rumination — looping on guilt, sadness, anger at oneself, or replaying
perceived failures — behaves like **ongoing cognitive demand** rather than
a discrete event. It can run while the person appears idle or between
tasks, drawing from the same [shared pool of voluntary
effort](shared-pool-of-voluntary-effort.md) that deliberate work uses.

Elevated rumination rates mean more capacity spent on self-referential
loops, leaving less for new decisions, social parsing, or recovery
activities. The load is easy to miss in pacing plans that only count
visible commitments: someone with an empty calendar can still be saturated
if rumination is consuming baseline headroom. This interacts with
[elevated baseline cognitive idle cost](elevated-baseline-cognitive-idle-cost.md)
when both background neural activity and repetitive thought are high.

For demand estimation, treat persistent rumination as a standing line item
in the cognitive dimension — not a moral failing to push through. Reducing
it may require dimension-matched intervention beyond "try harder";
cataloguing when rumination becomes a distinct breakdown pattern belongs
elsewhere, but the **mechanic** here is continuous load on capacity.
