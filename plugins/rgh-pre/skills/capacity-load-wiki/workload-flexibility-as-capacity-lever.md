title: Workload Flexibility as a Capacity Lever
description: >
  Adjusting schedule, workload intensity, and continuity of employment —
  not just where work happens — lets people align demand with their
  actual capacity curve instead of forcing a single pattern on everyone.
sources:
  - title: "The Canary Code: A Guide to Neurodiversity, Dignity, and Intersectional Belonging at Work"
    resource: "The Canary Code (Praslova), Chapter 7 (Work Organization — Forms of Flexibility)"
---

Flexibility is often reduced to remote versus office, but Kossek's
framework names three additional levers that directly affect capacity:

- **Schedule flexibility** — shifting start/end times, compressed weeks, or
  shorter paid weeks so work happens when capacity is naturally higher (see
  [schedule around energy patterns, not just clock
  time](schedule-around-energy-patterns.md)). Forcing creative work into
  someone else's "ideal" pattern can break people whose hyperfocus or
  circadian rhythm needs quiet hours, nights, or later starts.
- **Workload flexibility** — full-time, part-time, or job-shared
  arrangements that let people tailor total demand to energy patterns and
  life circumstances rather than assuming one standard load fits all.
- **Continuity flexibility** — leave without job loss (family, sick, mental
  health, sabbaticals), used proactively to prevent saturation rather than
  only reactively after breakdown (see [proactive recovery
  scheduling](proactive-recovery-scheduling.md)).

The recurring pattern behind many "overachiever" profiles is compensating
for an environment where focus is impossible during standard hours by
working nights and weekends to buy quiet — logging more hours while
fighting the schedule instead of fixing the mismatch. When schedule and
workload can be adjusted, the same output often requires fewer total
hours because capacity is spent on production rather than on compensating
for a bad fit.

One-size-fits-all uniformity sacrifices productivity for its own sake;
matching work organization to individual capacity curves is a demand-side
design choice, not a special accommodation.
