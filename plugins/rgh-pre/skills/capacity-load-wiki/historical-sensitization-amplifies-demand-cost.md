---
type: concept
title: Historical Sensitization Amplifies Demand Cost
description: >
  A demand's capacity cost is not fixed by its surface-level content
  alone — a stressor that lands on an unresolved past wound draws
  substantially more capacity than the same stressor would otherwise,
  because present distress compounds with old, unprocessed distress.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Neff), Chapter 3 (Emotional Self-Care)"
  - title: "Neurodiversity For Dummies"
    resource: "Neurodiversity For Dummies (Marble, Chabria, Jayaraman), Chapter 7"
---

The source's term for this is a "raw spot" — a sensitive area shaped by a
past wound that intensifies emotional reactions when a present-moment
stressor touches it. Two objectively similar demands can carry very
different capacity costs for the same person depending on whether one of
them happens to land on a raw spot: the cost isn't just the present
stressor's own weight, it's that weight plus however much of the old,
unresolved pain gets reactivated alongside it.

This matters for demand estimation because it breaks the assumption that a
given type of task or interaction has a roughly stable cost across
occasions. A capacity budget built from "this kind of thing usually costs
about X" will underestimate the actual draw whenever the specific instance
happens to intersect a raw spot — the same category of demand, at a
different amplification factor.

The amplifier doesn't have to be acquired from a specific past wound to
produce the same effect on a demand budget: a standing trait-level
amplification of one demand category (e.g. rejection sensitivity,
sometimes described as an emotional-pain "volume" turned up specifically
for rejection-related triggers) inflates the cost of that whole category
of demand consistently, not just on the occasions that happen to touch a
particular past event. Either mechanism produces the same practical
consequence for demand estimation: a flat per-category cost estimate will
be wrong for whichever categories carry an amplifier, historical or
structural.
