---
type: concept
title: Activation and Recovery Have Asymmetric Rates
description: >
  Entering a stress-activated state is fast and spreads system-wide,
  while recovering from it is slower and propagates more selectively, so
  recovery time is not the mirror image of activation time.
sources:
  - title: "That Little Voice in Your Head"
    resource: "That Little Voice in Your Head (Gawdat), Chapter 8 (Alchemy)"
---

The two branches of stress regulation don't operate at matched speeds. The
activating branch broadcasts to many organs and systems near-simultaneously
— stress is typically felt "everywhere in the body" at once. The
recovery/repair branch signals more selectively, organ by organ, so
settling back down takes measurably longer to spread and complete than the
activation did.

This asymmetry matters for capacity planning because it breaks a natural
but wrong assumption: that if a spike of load took N minutes to trigger,
recovery from it will also take roughly N minutes once the trigger is
gone. Recovery reliably takes longer than the activation that caused it,
and a schedule that budgets equal time for both will consistently
underestimate the recovery side. See [completing the stress-response
cycle](completing-the-stress-response-cycle.md) for the related point that
recovery also doesn't start automatically just because the trigger has
stopped.
