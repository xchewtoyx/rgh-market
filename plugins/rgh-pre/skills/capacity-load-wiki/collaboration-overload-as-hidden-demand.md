---
type: concept
title: Collaboration Overload as Hidden Demand
description: >
  Poorly managed teamwork can consume most of the working day in meetings
  and interruptions, leaving no blocks large enough for deep work and
  distributing collaborative load unevenly onto a few people.
sources:
  - title: "The Canary Code: A Guide to Neurodiversity, Dignity, and Intersectional Belonging at Work"
    resource: "The Canary Code (Praslova), Chapter 7 (Productivity Outcomes and Collaboration)"
---

Collaboration is treated as universally good, but when it is poorly
organized it becomes a major hidden demand: knowledge workers may spend
80–85% of time in meetings or answering help requests, leaving no room for
the [deep-work minimum block size](deep-work-minimum-block-size.md) deep
work requires. The average employee is interrupted 50–60 times per day,
most interruptions unimportant — the same fragmentation pattern as
[task-switching cost](task-switching-cost.md) at organizational scale.

Load is unevenly distributed: a small fraction of people ("super-
collaborators") carry 20–35% of value-added collaborations and burn out
first. Reactive priority-shifting — constant urgent requests displacing
important work — consumes capacity on anxiety and context-switching
without producing outcomes; clear goals and proactive pacing free energy
otherwise lost to "the tyranny of the urgent."

Design responses align with capacity levers: say no to misaligned projects,
staff teams minimally, monitor and redistribute workload, honor do-not-
disturb and async norms, match tasks to what energizes each person, and
treat async individual contribution as real teamwork rather than forcing
simultaneous co-presence. Outcomes focus and [sustainable effort
margin](sustainable-effort-margin.md) both require protecting capacity from
collaboration that papers over chronic under-resourcing — "teamwork" cannot
substitute for enough headcount or time.

For neurodivergent people who need extended focus, collaboration overload
is especially costly; for everyone it is a schedule design failure, not a
personal time-management failure.
