title: Working Memory as Cognitive Capacity Buffer
description: >
  Working memory holds and manipulates information for immediate use, and
  its effective capacity shrinks when other demands — masking, sensory
  processing, anxiety — already occupy slots in the same buffer.
sources:

Working memory is the short-term buffer for information currently in use
— a phone number held through a conversation, directions for a journey,
steps of a task in progress. Capacity is small (classically on the order
of a handful of chunks, fewer for longer material) and everyone loses
trains of thought sometimes; for many neurodivergent people the lapses are
far more frequent.

The crucial capacity-load point: the buffer is not reserved for "the
task." Auditory processing difficulty, [masking](masking-as-hidden-capacity-drain.md),
forced eye contact, stim suppression, and anxiety about fitting in each
consume slots before the actual work begins — "balloons you can't hold
down" in Middleton's metaphor. Reducing those drains (written instead of
verbal instructions, environments where unmasking is safe, inclusion that
lowers anxiety) frees buffer for the job itself without changing underlying
memory skill.

External tools — dictaphone, notes app, written confirmations, alarms,
repeating instructions back — are not cheating; they offload what the
buffer cannot reliably hold. Individualist expectations that everyone
should remember and execute independently without support treat a capacity
limit as a moral failure. The design goal is identifying what actually
needs holding in memory versus what can live outside the head, and
reducing unnecessary pressure to fill the buffer for appearances.

When several items must be held _and_ manipulated under time pressure, the
buffer behaves like a juggler who cannot slow down: information decays
unless continuously refreshed, so the pace is partly imposed by memory
itself. Kahneman's formulation captures the paradox: "the most effortful
forms of slow thinking are those that require you to think fast." Most
knowledge work avoids this ceiling by breaking problems into easy steps
and committing intermediate results to paper or long-term memory rather
than keeping them all in the buffer at once — an everyday application of
the [law of least effort](law-of-least-effort.md).

broader attention-allocation frame, and [designing around capacity
limits](designing-around-capacity-limits.md) for externalizing at the
