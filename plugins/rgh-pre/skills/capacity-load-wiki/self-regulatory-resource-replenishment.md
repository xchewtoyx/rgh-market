The [shared pool of voluntary effort](shared-pool-of-voluntary-effort.md)
is not only depleted by use — several inputs are shown to replenish it
faster than passive waiting. Factors from the ego-depletion literature
summarized in Barkley include:

- Routine physical exercise (also associated with increasing the pool's
  baseline capacity over weeks of practice)
- Ten-minute breaks during self-regulation-heavy stretches
- Relaxing or meditating for at least three minutes after exertion
- Visualizing rewards or outcomes during demanding tasks
- Periodic small rewards during self-regulation-heavy work
- Self-affirming statements of self-efficacy before or during tasks
- Generating positive emotions
- Consuming glucose-rich beverages during the task (see [glucose and
  effortful cognition](glucose-and-effortful-cognition.md))

Depletion is worsened by stress, alcohol, illness, and low blood glucose —
so recovery planning should treat those as compounding deficits, not
background noise.

For pacing: stacking back-to-back self-control demands without any of
these replenishment inputs assumes a pool that refills instantly; a
schedule with brief structured breaks, snack timing, or movement between
heavy blocks is a capacity design choice, not indulgence. This is
distinct from [dimension-matched recovery](dimension-matched-recovery.md),
which targets which *dimension* ran hot; this note targets the shared
voluntary-effort budget that cuts across dimensions.
