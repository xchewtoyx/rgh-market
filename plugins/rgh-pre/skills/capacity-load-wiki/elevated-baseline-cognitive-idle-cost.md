---
type: concept
title: Elevated Baseline Cognitive Idle Cost
description: >
  Some brains run a higher resting cognitive "noise" level and maintain
  more neural connections than typical, so capacity is spent on baseline
  upkeep before any task demand is added — the same energy pool that
  fuels deliberate thinking also funds that idle overhead.
sources:
  - title: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery"
    resource: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery (Smith), Chapter 3 (Mirror Neurons, Pruning, and Energy Cost)"
---

Brains prune unused synapses through childhood and adolescence to save
cognitive energy and tune the person to their environment. When pruning
follows a less typical path, more connections may remain active than a
plan built on "typical" baseline assumes — commonly cited on the order of
~11% more synapses, with some comparisons higher.

Separately, **default mode network** activity at rest — the brain's
background "noise" when not focused on a task — can run substantially
higher than in comparison groups (one cited figure: ~42% higher). Human
brains already spend disproportionate energy relative to other mammals and
are tuned to minimize unnecessary thinking; elevated idle activity means
capacity is being consumed before conscious work begins.

Hyperconnectivity is not framed as purely good or bad, but as a **net
liability in environments not tuned for it** — especially when spare
capacity gets spent parsing social threat and genuineness instead of the
task at hand. This is one structural reason [nervous-system-on-the-outside
load model](nervous-system-load-model.md) depletion rates overshoot shared
schedules: the conversion from "doing nothing visible" to "less headroom"
is faster. Recovery and pacing plans that assume a low-cost idle state will
under-budget baseline draw.

Cognitive effort draws from the same resource pool as physical energy in
this framing — baseline neural upkeep is not free capacity waiting to be
allocated; it is already partially spent. See [shared pool of voluntary
effort](shared-pool-of-voluntary-effort.md) for the broader model of one
pool funding multiple demand types.
