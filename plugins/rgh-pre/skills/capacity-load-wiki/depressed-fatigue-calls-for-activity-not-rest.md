---
type: concept
title: Depressed Fatigue Calls for Activity Not Rest
description: >
  Fatigue from low mood is not ordinary tiredness — resting often deepens
  it — so the capacity move when mood is flat is short, scheduled activity,
  not more downtime.
sources:
  - title: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition"
    resource: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition (Segal, Williams, Teasdale), Chapter 16 (Session 7 — Taking Care of Yourself)"
---

Ordinary tiredness signals a need to rest, and rest refreshes. **Depressed
fatigue** behaves differently: resting can increase lethargy rather than
restore headroom. The instruction is not to wait until motivation returns,
but to do something anyway — briefly, as an experiment — and observe
whether capacity shifts.

This is a capability-versus-capacity distinction in practice: the person
may know *how* to do nourishing activities but lack the felt want to start.
[Action before motivation in low mood](action-before-motivation-in-low-mood.md)
names the pacing rule; [nourishing versus depleting activity
balance](nourishing-versus-depleting-activity-balance.md) helps choose what
to schedule. Pair with [dimension-matched recovery](dimension-matched-recovery.md)
carefully — generic "rest" is the wrong prescription when depressed fatigue
is the bottleneck; a short walk, a small mastery task, or a previously
pleasurable micro-activity may reload faster than lying down.

"Stay in the game" — keep minimal participation even when thoughts insist
there is no point — prevents the [exhaustion funnel from dropping
nourishing activities](exhaustion-funnel-from-dropping-nourishing-activities.md)
from narrowing life to stressors only.
