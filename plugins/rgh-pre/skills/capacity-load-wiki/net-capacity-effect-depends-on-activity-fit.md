type: concept
title: Net Capacity Effect Depends on Activity Fit
description: >
  The same category of activity can be net-depleting or net-restorative
  depending on whether it fits the person's sensory, social, and physical
  preferences, not on the activity category itself.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Neff), Chapter 2 (Physical Self-Care); Chapter 5 (Social Self-Care)"
  - title: "The Canary Code: A Guide to Neurodiversity, Dignity, and Intersectional Belonging at Work"
    resource: "The Canary Code (Praslova), Chapter 7 (Work Organization — Productivity and Purpose)"
---

"Exercise" is not a single entry in a capacity ledger with one fixed
sign. Conventional exercise settings (gyms, team sports) can carry heavy
sensory load (noise, crowding, unpredictable contact) and social load
(performance anxiety, coordination comparison) that make the same nominal
activity — movement — a net drain for one person while being net
restorative, even regulating, for another who does it in a form that fits
their sensory and social preferences (e.g. swimming, walking, stim
dancing, movement paired with a preferred song).

The implication for capacity planning: don't budget activities by category
("exercise costs X, rest costs 0") — budget by fit. A poorly-fitting
version of a nominally restorative activity can cost more than it returns,
while a well-fitting version of a nominally effortful one can be a net
gain. This is a special case of [dimensions of personal
capacity](dimensions-of-personal-capacity.md): the same activity loads
different dimensions differently depending on how it's done, not just
whether it's done. The same "does it actually net-restore, or only look
like it should" test also applies within the single category of
rest — see [restorative rest versus cheap
rest](restorative-rest-vs-cheap-rest.md).

The same fit logic applies to activities pursued for a specific outcome,
not just to recovery: "parallel play" — doing a solo activity alongside
someone rather than interacting with them directly — is one way to reach a
social-connection outcome through a format with much lower sensory and
social cost than direct interaction would carry, without giving up the
outcome itself.

Communication medium is another variable that shifts cost independent of
content: the same meeting held over video rather than audio or in person
adds cost mechanisms that aren't present in the underlying conversation
itself — intensified eye-gaze exposure, added cognitive load from
processing cues through a degraded channel, self-monitoring from seeing
one's own video feed, and constrained physical movement. "The same
meeting" is not one fixed-cost entry either; its cost depends on the
medium it's held in as much as on what it's about.
