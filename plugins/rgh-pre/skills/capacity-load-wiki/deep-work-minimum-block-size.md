type: concept
title: Deep-Work Minimum Block Size
description: >
  Cognitively demanding work typically needs several consecutive
  uninterrupted hours before the effort pays off, so schedules fragmented
  below that threshold systematically waste capacity on restart cost.
sources:
  - title: "The Canary Code: A Guide to Neurodiversity, Dignity, and Intersectional Belonging at Work"
    resource: "The Canary Code (Praslova), Chapter 7 (Work Organization — Forms of Flexibility)"
---

Deep work — focused effort on a cognitively demanding task without
distraction — usually needs at least three to four uninterrupted hours
before the work reaches the depth where the best output happens. Blocks
shorter than that often never amortize the [task-switching
cost](task-switching-cost.md) of entering the state, especially when
interruptions collapse a warmed-up absorbed state rather than merely
pausing it.

**Timeboxing** puts this minimum on the calendar deliberately: reserving
e.g. three morning hours for deep work and informing colleagues not to
interrupt. **Collective timeboxing** — whole teams designating
distraction-free periods — multiplies the benefit but needs culture and
policy support, not only individual calendar discipline; workplaces that
reward productivity theater tend to punish timeboxing despite its output
benefits. No-meeting days and explicit "do not disturb" blocks (no
messages, calls, or drop-bys) are organizational versions of the same
principle.

The capacity implication is structural: a calendar packed with meetings
and reactive interruptions cannot produce deep-work output no matter how
many hours are logged, because the blocks never reach the minimum size
where demanding work becomes efficient. Protecting block size is a
capacity lever distinct from reducing total task count.
