type: concept
title: Vagal Tone as a Determinant of Recovery Capacity
description: >
  Baseline vagal tone governs how flexibly the nervous system adapts to
  and recovers from stress, and functions as a trainable parameter of the
  recovery rate itself rather than a fixed trait.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Neff), Chapter 2 (Physical Self-Care)"
---

Vagal tone — how readily the vagus nerve shifts the body toward its
relaxation response — sets a baseline for how flexibly someone adapts to
stressors and how quickly they recover afterward. Low vagal tone is not a
one-off state to fix in the moment; it's closer to a standing parameter
that makes every stress episode both easier to enter and slower to recover
from, independent of how demanding any single episode actually was.

This distinguishes two different levers in a recovery model: recovering
from *this* saturation event (removing the trigger, discharging the
accumulated response — see [completing the stress-response
cycle](completing-the-stress-response-cycle.md)) versus raising the
underlying recovery-rate parameter itself, which changes how every future
episode plays out. The source describes vagal tone as trainable — regular
activities that engage the vagus nerve shift the baseline over time, not
just during the activity — so it belongs in a pacing plan as a
longer-horizon investment distinct from any single day's recovery budget.
Raising vagal tone is one lever for widening the [window of
tolerance](window-of-tolerance.md) — the band of stress load a person can
absorb while staying regulated.
