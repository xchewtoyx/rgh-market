title: Schedule Around Energy Patterns, Not Just Clock Time
description: >
  Capacity availability varies predictably across the day for a given
  person, so scheduling demanding work against that individual pattern
  uses fewer total hours to produce more usable output than scheduling
  by uniform clock time.
sources:
  - title: "The Canary Code: A Guide to Neurodiversity, Dignity, and Intersectional Belonging at Work"

Time-based scheduling treats every hour of the workday as an
interchangeable unit of capacity; energy-based scheduling treats capacity
as a curve that rises and falls across the day for a given person, and
places demanding work where the curve is highest for them specifically.
The source's illustrative case: a person forced to work standard daytime
hours produced far less per hour than the same person working her natural
quiet hours, because the mismatch between when demanding work was
scheduled and when her capacity was actually available meant most
daytime hours were spent fighting distraction rather than producing —
logging long hours without those hours being genuinely productive ones.
Once schedule matched her actual capacity curve, the same total output no
longer required the inflated hour count.

The general principle: matching a demand's timing to when capacity is
naturally higher is a capacity lever independent of how much total demand
there is — the same task list can cost far more or far less depending
purely on placement against the person's own pattern, which is a
different lever from reducing the task list itself or improving
[activity fit](net-capacity-effect-depends-on-activity-fit.md).

A structural instance of the same mismatch: shifted chronotypes (peak
energy and concentration later in the day, reported as more common in
some populations) forced onto a standard early schedule don't just lose
some efficiency, they can run a chronic, cumulative sleep deficit — the
mismatch cost compounds day over day rather than resetting, which is a
sharper version of the same energy/clock-time mismatch this note
describes for daytime focus.
