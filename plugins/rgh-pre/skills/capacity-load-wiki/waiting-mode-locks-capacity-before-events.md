---
type: concept
title: Waiting Mode Locks Capacity Before Events
description: >
  A later appointment can consume the whole day in anxious limbo when
  working memory, hyperfocus risk, time blindness, and decision paralysis
  combine — leaving no productive headroom before the event even starts.
sources:
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Middleton), Chapter 5 (Flexibility — Tip 23)"
---

"Waiting mode" (an ADHD-community term Middleton adopts) is stuck
unproductivity on days with a single scheduled anchor — e.g., a 2 p.m.
appointment — because four mechanisms overlap:

1. **Working memory** — letting go of the appointment risks forgetting it
   amid everything else in the buffer.
2. **Hyperfocus risk** — shifting fully into another task could miss the
   event entirely (see [hyperfocus bypasses interoception and
   time](hyperfocus-bypasses-interoception-and-time.md)).
3. **Time blindness** — difficulty judging how long tasks take makes "what
   can I fit before 2?" unanswerable without overwhelm.
4. **Decision paralysis** — too many possible sequences (shower, eat, gym,
   petrol…) produce shutdown and default to doing nothing while waiting.

Countermeasures: schedule anchors early so the rest of the day is free;
track how long recurring tasks actually take; use alarms before leave-time
so hyperfocus cannot erase the event. Treat waiting mode as real load on
the calendar — not laziness — when budgeting a day that contains a fixed
anchor.
