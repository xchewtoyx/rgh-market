---
type: concept
title: Precommitted Demand-Shedding
description: >
  Deciding in advance, while headroom is available, which demands can be
  dropped and under what circumstances avoids having to spend scarce
  evaluation capacity on that decision during a period of low headroom.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Neff), Chapter 3 (Emotional Self-Care)"
---

Deciding what to drop when capacity is already low is itself a
capacity-consuming task — evaluating each demand's importance takes
cognitive effort exactly when that effort is scarcest, and all-or-nothing
thinking under strain tends to blur which demands are actually essential.
The source's answer is a **traffic-light system**, prepared ahead of time
while headroom is available: green (droppable without real consequence),
yellow (droppable depending on circumstances), red (hard to drop, real
consequences). When a low-headroom period arrives, the categorization work
is already done — the only decision left is "drop the greens," which costs
far less capacity than evaluating each demand from scratch.

This is a distinct pacing mechanism from [fallback to known-cost
options](fallback-to-known-cost-options-under-load.md): that pattern
substitutes a cheaper option for a recurring need, while this one removes a
demand from the ledger entirely, and both work by moving the evaluation
cost to a moment when capacity can afford it rather than paying it at the
point of use. The mirror-image case is precommitting a *limit* rather than
a *drop list* — see [absorbing activities crowd out other
commitments](absorbing-activities-crowd-out-other-commitments.md) for when
the risk is an activity that overruns rather than a pile of demands that
needs triaging.
