---
type: concept
title: Fallback to Known-Cost Options Under Load
description: >
  Deliberately keeping a stock of low-variance, already-evaluated choices
  (a routine, a "safe" option) for use when headroom is low avoids
  spending scarce capacity on evaluating novelty, at the cost of variety.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Neff), Chapter 2 (Physical Self-Care)"
---

Novel choices (a new food, an unfamiliar routine) carry an evaluation cost
on top of whatever the choice itself costs: sensory uncertainty, decision
effort, risk of a bad outcome. Keeping a stock of "safe" options — foods,
routines, or activities already known to be tolerable and low-effort — and
deliberately reaching for them during periods of reduced headroom trades
away variety in exchange for cutting that evaluation cost entirely. The
source frames this explicitly as legitimate self-care during stress or
transitions, not a lapse in effort or a failure to "eat properly" or "do
things properly."

This is a pacing strategy distinct from reducing the amount of activity:
it reduces the *cost per unit* of a recurring need by substituting a
known-cost option for an unknown-cost one when capacity is already
constrained, keeping the higher-variety or higher-effort version of the
same need for times when headroom allows evaluating it.
