title: Self-Maintenance as Preventive Capacity Work
description: >
  Baseline routines that fund physical and cognitive capacity are
  preventive maintenance — done before the day starts so breakdown is
  less likely — not luxury "self-care" reserved for after everything else
  is finished.
sources:
  - title: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery"
    resource: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery (Smith), Chapter 8 (Beating Burnout — Maintain, Recover, Reload)"
---

"Self-care" often reads as leisure or reward — a bubble bath after bills
and reports are caught up. **Self-maintenance** reframes the same
activities as preventive repair: greasing the wheels *before* the drive so
the fan belt does not fail en route. The goal is not a treat after
catching up; it is building enough headroom to catch up at all.

This is a pacing stance, not a moral one. Compare against your own
current baseline and make just-enough improvements; overhauling everything
at once often produces a burst of change, burnout on the change itself,
and abandonment — so the bar should be low enough that accidental
over-performance is likely. Walking, adequate sleep, and simple food
patterns are capacity inputs to [body budget as cognitive
baseline](body-budget-as-cognitive-baseline.md), not indulgences to earn.

The [maintain-recover-reload cycle](maintain-recover-reload-cycle.md)
names three coordinated moves; maintenance is the first — funding the
system before demand peaks rather than repairing only after saturation.
