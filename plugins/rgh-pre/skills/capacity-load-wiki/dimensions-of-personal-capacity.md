---
type: concept
title: Dimensions of Personal Capacity
description: >
  Personal capacity is better modelled as several independently-saturating
  dimensions — sensory, physical, emotional, cognitive, and social — than as
  one undifferentiated pool of energy.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Neff), Preface/Introduction"
---

Treating "capacity" as a single number obscures which dimension is actually
depleted. A demand can be light in one dimension and heavy in another — a
quiet, solitary task might be sensorially and socially cheap but cognitively
expensive; a lively social gathering might be socially nourishing but
sensorially and physically draining. Without separating dimensions, a
person (or an agent reasoning on their behalf) can only observe "I'm
depleted" without being able to say depleted *how*, which makes it hard to
plan recovery or choose the next activity well.

One workable partition — sensory, physical, emotional, cognitive, and
social — treats sensory load as foundational: unaddressed
[saturation in one dimension cascades into the others](cross-dimensional-saturation-cascade.md)
rather than staying contained. Each dimension can also deplete at a
different baseline rate for different people; see the
[nervous-system load model](nervous-system-load-model.md) for one account of
why that rate can be structurally faster for some people than the
"typical" pace a shared plan or schedule assumes.

Because dimensions saturate and recover somewhat independently, pacing
decisions should budget per-dimension rather than against one global energy
total.
