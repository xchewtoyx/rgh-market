title: Nourishing Versus Depleting Activity Balance
description: >
  Daily activities differ in net capacity effect — some refill headroom,
  others drain it — and pacing requires auditing the mix, not only total
  hours busy or free.
sources:
  - title: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition"
    resource: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition (Segal, Williams, Teasdale), Chapter 16 (Session 7 — Taking Care of Yourself)"
---

A useful audit labels typical activities **N** (nourishing) or **D**
(depleting/draining). The same activity can flip categories by mood or
context — rushing kids to school may feel depleting most mornings but
nourishing when there is time to talk. The question "what does it depend
on?" matters as much as the label.

Two subtypes often lift low mood but get skipped first under load:

- **Pleasure activities** — enjoyable for their own sake (meal with a
  friend, bath, dessert).
- **Mastery activities** — sense of accomplishment or "taking care of
  business" (filing, groceries, clearing one desk corner) even when not
  inherently fun.

People often notice depleting activities more easily than nourishing ones;
the default audit skews toward seeing only drain. Scheduling small steps
from the N and P/M lists — not waiting to feel like it — is how behavioral
activation rebuilds capacity when mood is low (see [depressed fatigue
calls for activity not rest](depressed-fatigue-calls-for-activity-not-rest.md)).

This extends [net capacity effect depends on activity
fit](net-capacity-effect-depends-on-activity-fit.md) and [restorative rest
versus cheap rest](restorative-rest-vs-cheap-rest.md): category labels
("exercise," "rest," "work") are poor proxies; the signed effect on
headroom is what belongs in a pacing plan.
