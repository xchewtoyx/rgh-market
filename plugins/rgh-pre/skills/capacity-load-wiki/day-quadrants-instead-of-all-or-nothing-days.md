---
type: concept
title: Day Quadrants Instead of All-or-Nothing Days
description: >
  Splitting the day into bounded segments gives multiple fresh starts
  when one block goes poorly, countering the trap of writing off an
  entire day after a bad morning.
sources:
  - title: "How to Be You: Simple Ways to Love Who You Are"
    resource: "How to Be You (Middleton), Chapter 5 (Flexibility — Tip 22)"
---

All-or-nothing thinking treats a day as one unit: an unproductive morning
or a missed 9:00 start can feel like the whole day is lost. Middleton's
four-quadrant split (e.g., morning, late morning/lunch, afternoon, evening
— timings adjustable) assigns tasks to a specific quadrant when planning.
If one quadrant fails, three remain — it was a bad quadrant, not a bad
day.

Clustering one activity type per quadrant where possible reduces
[task-switching cost](task-switching-cost.md) for busy brains that struggle
to jump between admin, creative work, and self-care in the same block. The
pattern is a pacing boundary tool: it limits how far a single saturation
episode propagates forward without requiring perfection in any one segment.

See [graded task decomposition for low
headroom](graded-task-decomposition-for-low-headroom.md) for breaking
work below the day level when even a quadrant feels too large.
