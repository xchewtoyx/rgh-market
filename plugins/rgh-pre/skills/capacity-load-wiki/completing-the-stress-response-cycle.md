title: Completing the Stress-Response Cycle
description: >
  Recovering from acute stress load requires physiologically discharging
  the accumulated stress-response energy, not merely removing the trigger
  that caused it.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"

Removing the source of overload stops new load from arriving, but it does
not by itself restore capacity. The body can still be holding the stress
response it built up while the trigger was active — outwardly calm, even
dissociated, while internally still activated. Recovery in this state
requires actively discharging that pent-up energy (e.g. movement, release,
time in a genuinely safe environment) rather than assuming that capacity
refills automatically once the demand stops.

This is a condition on recovery rate, not just recovery occurring at all:
two people who stop being exposed to the same stressor at the same moment
can still recover at very different rates depending on whether the
accumulated response actually gets discharged afterward. Planning a
recovery window around "time since the trigger ended" alone will
underestimate how long full recovery takes if the discharge step is
skipped or interrupted. See [activation and recovery have asymmetric
rates](activation-recovery-rate-asymmetry.md) for why the recovery side
takes longer to begin with, independent of the discharge question.

Left unaddressed, this response duration decouples from the trigger's own
duration: the activation response doesn't distinguish a brief, trivial
trigger from a genuine emergency, so a string of minor, unresolved
triggers across a day can accumulate into a sustained activated state that
outlasts any one of them, even though no single trigger was severe enough
to explain it on its own. Discharge doesn't have to wait passively for
the trigger to fully resolve, either — deliberately inducing the
physical signature of the recovery state (relaxed posture, slowed
breathing) can actively engage recovery before the external situation is
settled, rather than recovery only ever following as a downstream effect
of the situation resolving.
