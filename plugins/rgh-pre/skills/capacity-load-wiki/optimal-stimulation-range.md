---
type: concept
title: Optimal Stimulation Range
description: >
  Focused output is maximized within a personal range of ambient
  stimulation rather than simply below a ceiling — both under- and
  over-stimulation degrade functioning, so headroom management has to
  guard a floor as well as a cap.
sources:
  - title: "Self-Care for Autistic People: 100+ Ways to Recharge, De-Stress, and Unmask"
    resource: "Self-Care for Autistic People (Neff), Chapter 6 (Professional Self-Care)"
  - title: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery"
    resource: "The Autistic's Guide to Self-Discovery (Smith), Chapter 4 (Sensory Issues and Stimming)"
---

The source's term is the "Goldilocks zone": the band of ambient stimulus
that is neither too little nor too much for a given person to function
well, particularly called out for people who are simultaneously
stimulation-seeking and overload-prone (e.g. co-occurring ADHD). This
adds a floor to the [window of tolerance](window-of-tolerance.md)'s
upper-bound framing: it isn't only that too much input saturates capacity,
it's that too little input can also degrade output — an environment tuned
purely to minimize stimulation can undershoot the range and cost
performance just as overstimulation does.

The practical consequence is that a plan built only to avoid saturation
(strip out sensory load, minimize demand) can overshoot into
understimulation and still fail to produce good output, even though it
successfully avoided overload. Managing capacity well means tuning ambient
input into the range, not just capping it — a sensory tool kit or
quiet-space accommodation should be paired with a way to add stimulation
back in (a stim song, a fidget tool) when the environment undershoots the
range rather than only ever removing input. Permitted [stimming as real-time
capacity regulation](stimming-as-real-time-capacity-regulation.md) and
fidget tools can raise arousal into the band; cited research links
fidgeting to improved sustained attention, with larger effects for people
with higher ADHD trait scores. A related optimal band applies
to task difficulty relative to skill rather than ambient stimulus — see
[challenge-skill balance](challenge-skill-balance.md).
