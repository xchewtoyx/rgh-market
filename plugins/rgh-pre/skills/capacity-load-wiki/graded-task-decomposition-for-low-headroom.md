title: Graded Task Decomposition for Low Headroom
description: >
  When available capacity is low, success depends on decomposing demands
  into steps small enough to complete at the current level — including
  time-boxed goals — rather than matching neurotypical task size.
sources:
  - title: "Back from the Bluez"
    resource: "Back from the Bluez (Nathan, Rees, Lim, Correia), Module 2 (Behavioural Activation — Fun and Achievement)"
---

When headroom is severely reduced — as in depression's withdrawal and
overwhelm loops — the common error is trying to do too much too soon.
Graded task assignment applies the same logic as physical training: start
within present capability and build gradually, not from the size of task
that would be reasonable at full capacity.

Any task can be decomposed until something achievable appears: not "clean
the kitchen" but "wash five plates"; not "get out of bed for the day" but
"get out of bed for ten minutes." **Time-based goals** often work better
than amount-based ones at this stage — read for five minutes rather than
a chapter, weed for ten minutes rather than a defined area — because
open-ended targets fail more often when initiation itself is costly.

The priority at low headroom is not what or how much gets done but that
something gets done: action can precede motivation rather than waiting
for motivation to arrive first. Activity planning should mix channels —
not only pleasure but achievement-type tasks that rebuild a sense of
control — because depletion is not only anhedonia but also hopelessness
and ineffectiveness.

This is a pacing rule for the bottom of the capacity curve, distinct from
[sustainable effort margin](sustainable-effort-margin.md) at the top: the
same person may need margin when healthy and microscopic steps when
saturated. See [capacity load triage
menu](capacity-load-triage-menu.md) for removing load when even graded
steps are too large.
