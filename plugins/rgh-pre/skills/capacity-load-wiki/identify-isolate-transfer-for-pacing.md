type: concept
title: Identify-Isolate-Transfer for Pacing
description: >
  A coping skill developed in one context can be repurposed for capacity
  pacing elsewhere by stripping it to its active ingredient and reapplying
  that mechanism across dimensions or disabilities.
sources:
  - title: "Knowing Why: Adult-Diagnosed Autistic People on Life and Autism"
    resource: "Knowing Why (ASAN), Chapter 7 (Samantha Hack — Intersecting Identities and Coping Skills)"
---

**Identify → isolate → transfer** is a meta-technique for reusing pacing
tools across contexts:

1. **Identify** — notice broadly what behavior helps and why.
2. **Isolate** — ask what specific benefit you get and what precise action
   produces it, stripped of context-specific details.
3. **Transfer** — apply that mechanism in another domain.

Worked example: stroking a stuffed animal's ears during sensory overload
isolates to "soft tactile input redirects attention"; the same mechanism
manages OCD (soft sensations on arms) and anxiety (rubbing something soft
while redirecting thoughts). Executive-function task breakdown learned for
ADHD — many short steps with frequent breaks — transfers to physical
pacing: grocery trips smaller and more frequent, prep broken down per
vegetable rather than "night before," bulk cooking to reduce repeated
physical aggravation.

Discarded social scripts can become stealth regulation tools: performing
poise in an overwhelming restaurant regulates emotional overload while
remaining socially legible. The technique itself — seeing the active
ingredient separate from the original diagnosis or identity context — is
described as among the most powerful tools late-diagnosed people develop
when formal supports were absent.

For capacity planning: when one dimension's pacing solution already works,
ask whether its mechanism (break size, sensory redirect, externalized
steps, reduced batch size) applies to another saturated dimension before
inventing from scratch. See [graded task decomposition for low
headroom](graded-task-decomposition-for-low-headroom.md) and [designing
around capacity limits](designing-around-capacity-limits.md).
