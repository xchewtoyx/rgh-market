---
type: concept
title: Sustainable Effort Margin
description: >
  Deliberately operating below maximum perceived effort, rather than at
  it, sustains higher output over time than an intermittent
  maximum-effort/crash cycle, because running without margin leaves no
  headroom to absorb variance or recover.
sources:
  - title: "The Canary Code: A Guide to Neurodiversity, Dignity, and Intersectional Belonging at Work"
    resource: "The Canary Code (Praslova), Chapter 7 (Work Organization — Productivity and Purpose)"
  - title: "Discipline Is Destiny: The Power of Self-Control"
    resource: "Discipline Is Destiny (Holiday), Chapter 6 (Manage the Load)"
---

"Maximum effort" and "maximum results" are not the same target: expecting
constant full-capacity output leads to burnout and, over any meaningful
timeframe, to *lower* aggregate output than pacing at a level with margin
to spare — the source cites roughly 85% sustained effort as more
productive over time than intermittent 100%. The mechanism is headroom:
running consistently at perceived maximum leaves nothing in reserve to
absorb the ordinary variance in daily demand or to recover between
periods of higher load, so any unplanned spike forces either an overshoot
(saturation, then a costly recovery debt) or a missed commitment.

The practical implication is that slack should be treated as a designed-in
part of a sustainable capacity plan, not as evidence of laziness or
underuse — a plan that looks less "efficient" by only counting output
during the tracked interval can still produce more total output over a
longer horizon, once repeated overshoot-and-recovery cycles are accounted
for. Steinbeck's phrase for the opposite mistake — the "indiscipline of
overwork" as "the falsest of economies" — captures why: chasing maximum
visible output now reliably trades away more total output later. This is a
margin held at the level of an ongoing operating rate,
distinct from [precommitted demand-shedding](precommitted-demand-shedding.md),
which is a one-off response used once a specific low-headroom period has
already arrived.
