---
type: concept
title: Action Before Motivation in Low Mood
description: >
  When mood is depressed, wanting to act often follows doing, not the
  other way around — so pacing plans must schedule small actions as
  experiments rather than waiting for felt readiness.
sources:
  - title: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition"
    resource: "Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression, 2nd Edition (Segal, Williams, Teasdale), Chapter 16 (Session 7 — Taking Care of Yourself)"
---

Normally people wait until they want to do something, then do it. In
depression the sequence reverses: **do something first, then discover
whether wanting returns.** Treat each attempt as an experiment without
prejudging the outcome; extra pressure for a dramatic mood shift adds
demand and often backfires.

Practical constraints:

- Break tasks into tiny steps — a few minutes with permission to stop, or
  one corner of a desk instead of the whole surface.
- Vary activities rather than relying on one favorite; novelty itself can
  counter withdrawal.
- Don't expect miracles; the win is modestly increased sense of control
  amid mood swings.

This is behavioral activation as capacity pacing, not a moral push through
pain. It pairs with [depressed fatigue calls for activity not
rest](depressed-fatigue-calls-for-activity-not-rest.md) when rest would
deepen lethargy, and with [graded task decomposition for low
headroom](graded-task-decomposition-for-low-headroom.md) for step sizing.

Guilt that self-care must wait until obligations are finished is a common
barrier — it treats nourishing activity as optional luxury rather than
capacity input; see [exhaustion funnel from dropping nourishing
activities](exhaustion-funnel-from-dropping-nourishing-activities.md).
