---
type: concept
title: Camouflaging Extends Demand Beyond the Interaction
description: >
  Masking load often begins before a social event (planning, rehearsal)
  and continues after it (recovery, decompression), so the true capacity
  cost spans a wider window than the visible interaction alone.
sources:
  - title: "Is This Autism? A Guide for Clinicians and Everyone Else"
    resource: "Is This Autism? (Henderson, Wayland, White), Chapter 2 (Camouflaging, Gender, and Culture)"
---

Camouflaging is not confined to the minutes of face-to-face contact. Many
autistic people spend effort beforehand — planning greetings, practicing
expressions, memorizing topics — and pay a recovery bill afterward:
extreme fatigue, shutdown, or days needed to recover from a multi-hour
"masking session," with anxious anticipation on the days before. A schedule
that books back-to-back social blocks as if only the event duration
matters will under-count load by ignoring this pre- and post-phase.

The mechanism is partly executive: navigating reciprocity, tone, and
context-dependent "secret rules" via deliberate analysis ("math in your
head all day") draws on the same [limited attention
budget](limited-attention-budget.md) and [working
memory](working-memory-as-cognitive-capacity-buffer.md) that would
otherwise support planning, organizing, and staying on-task during the
substantive work inside the interaction. Grace O.'s formulation captures
it: analyzing the social situation leaves less energy for the conversation
content itself.

When camouflaging intersects with other identity pressures — code
switching across cultural contexts, gendered expectations, or "double
camouflaging" for autistic people of color — the extended window and
executive overhead multiply. See [masking as a hidden capacity
drain](masking-as-hidden-capacity-drain.md) for the concurrent
suppression cost during the interaction itself, and [dimension-matched
recovery](dimension-matched-recovery.md) for why social recovery after
masking may need to be scheduled explicitly rather than assumed to happen
in unstructured downtime.
